# Level 4 Quiz: Character, Society & Family

## Part A: Multiple Choice Questions
1. How does Prophet Muhammad ﷺ define the standard of complete faith in Hadith 13 of Imam an-Nawawi?
   (A) By memorizing historical genealogies without error.
   (B) Loving for your brother what you love for yourself.
   (C) Accumulating significant financial wealth for travel.
   (D) Constructing large public buildings with tall arches.

2. What is the maximum time limit allowed for two Muslims to boycott or refuse to speak to one another following a dispute?
   (A) Three days.
   (B) Twenty-four hours only.
   (C) One full lunar year.
   (D) Indefinitely until an elder commands reconciliation.

3. According to authentic prophetic tradition, who is the superior party when two Muslims reconcile after a disagreement?
   (A) The one who remains completely silent until approached.
   (B) The one who hires a mediator to deliver a written apology.
   (C) The one who initiates the greeting of Salam first.
   (D) The one who pays a financial settlement to the other party.

4. What golden counsel did the Prophet ﷺ repeat three times to the man who asked for life guidance in Hadith 16 of Nawawi?
   (A) "Travel across foreign seas every summer!"
   (B) "Fast continuously without breaking your fast!"
   (C) "Abandon physical work and live in isolation!"
   (D) "Do not become angry!" (La Taghdab).

5. How did the Prophet ﷺ redefine "the truly strong person" (Al-Shadid) in Sahih al-Bukhari?
   (A) The warrior who can defeat twenty opponents in physical combat.
   (B) The one who controls his own soul and temper during moments of anger.
   (C) The merchant who commands the largest caravan in Arabia.
   (D) The runner who finishes first in endurance races.

6. What sequence of physical actions is recommended in the Sunnah when experiencing intense anger?
   (A) Pacing back and forth rapidly while breathing deeply.
   (B) Leaving the house immediately and running around the neighborhood.
   (C) Seeking refuge with Allah, sitting down if standing (or lying down), and making cool wudu.
   (D) Clenching fists and shouting into an empty room.

7. In Hadith 34 of Imam an-Nawawi, what are the three graduated tiers of changing an observed wrong or injustice?
   (A) By the hand (lawful authority), by the tongue (truth and counsel), and by the heart (inner rejection).
   (B) By public shaming, by legal lawsuits, and by economic embargoes.
   (C) By voting, by peaceful rallies, and by private boycotts.
   (D) By financial fines, by exile, and by silent disregard.

8. When is changing a wrong "with the hand" (physical intervention) legally permitted in Islamic jurisprudence?
   (A) By any passerby on the street who disagrees with someone's clothing.
   (B) Only during daylight hours on official holidays.
   (C) By anonymous youth groups enforcing social rules online.
   (D) Exclusively by those holding legitimate legal or custodial jurisdiction (such as parents at home or lawful civic authorities).

9. What advice did Imam Malik's mother give him when he began his pursuit of sacred knowledge?
   (A) "Study mathematics and commerce before touching religious books."
   (B) "Go to Rabi'ah and learn from his Adab (manners and reverence) before his knowledge!"
   (C) "Debate with scholars publicly so people recognize your intelligence."
   (D) "Never write down notes until you have memorized the entire text."

10. What is the correct student etiquette (Adab al-Talib) if a teacher explains a concept you have already studied?
    (A) Listen attentively with humility and reverence, recognizing that reviewing knowledge is a spiritual blessing.
    (B) Interrupt the teacher immediately and announce that you already understand the lesson.
    (C) Look at your mobile phone and whisper to a classmate while waiting for the next topic.
    (D) Stand up and leave the lecture hall to find advanced reading material.

## Part C: Matching Terms
* La Taghdab ─── The repeated prophetic counsel to master emotional anger
* Al-Shadid ─── The truly strong person who restrains his temper when provoked
* Ukhuwwah ─── Spiritual brotherhood and sisterhood rooted in love for Allah
* Hadith 34 ─── Changing injustice through hand, tongue, or heart
* Adab al-Talib ─── The sacred etiquette and humility of the student before teachers

## Part D: Reflection & Application
1. A classmate at school insults you in front of other students, tempting you to react with a physical fight or screaming insults. Walk through how you apply the prophetic anger protocol in that exact moment.
2. Analyze the three tiers of Hadith 34: Why did the Prophet ﷺ designate changing evil "in the heart" as the minimum standard of faith?
3. What is the danger of pursuing academic or Islamic knowledge without first mastering personal Adab (manners and humility)?
4. Explain how the Prophetic principle of loving for your brother what you love for yourself transforms peer competition into mutual cooperation.

## Answer Key & Marking Guide
1. B - Hadith 13 establishes empathy and selflessness as the benchmark of mature faith (Sahih al-Bukhari 13, Sahih Muslim 45).
2. A - Transgressing the three-day limit on anger boycotts is strictly forbidden in prophetic Hadith (Sahih al-Bukhari 6077).
3. C - The one who initiates the greeting of Salam breaks pride and earns greater reward.
4. D - The Prophet ﷺ repeated "La Taghdab" to emphasize emotional discipline as life's anchor (Sahih al-Bukhari 6116).
5. B - Spiritual emotional self-control requires far greater strength than mere physical wrestling (Sahih al-Bukhari 6114).
6. C - Seeking refuge, lowering physical posture, and cooling anger with wudu are proven Sunnah methods (Sunan Abi Dawud 4782).
7. A - Changing by hand, tongue, and heart represents the comprehensive framework of enjoining good (Sahih Muslim 49).
8. D - Direct physical intervention requires lawful authority to prevent chaotic vigilantism.
9. B - Imam Malik's mother insisted that character and reverence must precede theoretical learning (Tartib al-Madarik).
10. A - Classical Adab requires sitting with the attentiveness of someone hearing the lesson for the first time.
