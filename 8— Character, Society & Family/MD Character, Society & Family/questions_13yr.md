### Part B: Scenario-Based Questions (5 Questions)
*Read the scenario and analyze it based on Maliki jurisprudence and Ash'ari creed.*

11. **Tariq is 13 years old. He downloads an online messaging app that is popular in his school. However, he notices that the app's public channels frequently share inappropriate images and vulgar jokes. He tells himself, "I am strong enough to ignore the bad stuff and just chat with my friends." How does the Maliki legal principle of Sadd al-Dhara'i' apply here?**
    *   *Answer:* Tariq should apply **Sadd al-Dhara'i'** (blocking the means to evil). The Maliki school holds that if a permissible action (using a chat app) frequently or inevitably leads to a forbidden result (seeing inappropriate content, desensitizing the heart to vulgarity), the means themselves must be blocked. Tariq should delete the app or leave those channels rather than trusting his own strength, proactively protecting his heart.

12. **Yasmin is studying for her exams. She is naturally very intelligent. Her classmate asks her for help, but Yasmin refuses, thinking, "I worked hard for my grades; she should do the same instead of relying on my smart brain." Analyze Yasmin’s internal state using the concept of Tawadu' (humility).**
    *   *Answer:* Yasmin’s refusal and thoughts stem from a lack of **Tawadu'** (humility) and the presence of **Kibr** (pride). She is attributing her intelligence and grades solely to her own effort, forgetting that her intellect (*'Aql*) is a pure gift created by Allah. A humble person recognizes that blessings are from Allah and eagerly shares them to benefit others, rather than looking down on classmates.

13. **Khalid’s neighbor builds a massive wood-burning furnace directly adjacent to Khalid’s bedroom window. The furnace blows smoke into Khalid's room every night, making it hard to breathe. The neighbor says, "This is my yard, and I can build whatever I want on my property." What is the Maliki legal ruling in this scenario?**
    *   *Answer:* The neighbor is in violation of Maliki law. Under the legal principle of **Da'f al-Darar** (prevention of harm), which is derived from the Hadith *"La darar wa la dirar"* (no harming nor reciprocating harm), a property owner is legally restricted from using their property in a way that causes direct, active harm to their neighbor. A Maliki judge (*Qadi*) will legally force the neighbor to move or dismantle the furnace.

14. **Bilal is called to give testimony in court regarding a financial transaction. He is the best friend of the claimant, Sufyan. They eat together daily, travel together, and share their bank accounts. In Maliki fiqh, what will the judge rule regarding Bilal's testimony?**
    *   *Answer:* The judge will reject Bilal's testimony in favor of Sufyan. In the Maliki school, testimony in favor of a close friend (**Shahadat al-Sadiq li-Sadiqihi**) is disqualified. This is due to **Tuhmah** (suspicion of bias), as the legal system recognizes that the bond of close friendship (*Sadaqah*) is highly likely to compromise objectivity and lead the witness to favor their friend over absolute justice (*'Adl*).

15. **Sajida is trying to control her anger. Her friend insults her in front of a group. Sajida feels a sudden surge of rage. She instantly reminds herself of Qadar (Divine Decree). How does this theological reflection change her reaction?**
    *   *Answer:* By recalling the Ash'ari belief in **Qadar**, Sajida realizes that this insult was known, willed, and created by Allah as a spiritual test for her. Recognizing that the event is ultimately within Allah's decree allows her to separate her ego from the insult. It diffuses her anger, enabling her to respond with **Kadhm al-Ghaydh** (anger suppression) and **Hilm** (forbearance) for the sake of Allah.

### Part C: Essay-Based & Conceptual Questions (5 Questions)
*Provide detailed, analytical responses to the following prompts.*

16. **Explain the theological concept of Kasb (Acquisition) in the Ash'ari school, and contrast it with the idea of absolute predetermination (Jabr). Why is Kasb essential for personal accountability in character development?**
    *   *Answer:* Absolute predetermination (**Jabr**) teaches that humans are like feathers in the wind, having no choice or accountability. The Ash'ari school rejects this and teaches **Kasb** (Acquisition). In this view, Allah is the sole Creator of all actions, but He grants human beings free choice and the ability to intend an action. When a human directs their intent toward an action, they "acquire" it, and Allah creates the action. This is essential for character development because it means we are active agents who choose to be honest or angry, making us justly accountable before Allah for our moral decisions.

17. **Define the term Muru'ah (dignity/chivalry) within the context of Maliki jurisprudence. Give three examples of modern behaviors that would compromise a person's Muru'ah and explain why they have legal ramifications.**
    *   *Answer:* **Muru'ah** refers to the dignity, chivalry, and noble behavior of an individual in alignment with local customs (*'Urf*). Three modern behaviors that compromise it include: (1) posting foolish or humiliating pranks online for viral attention, (2) screaming and throwing public tantrums when frustrated, and (3) using vulgar or offensive language in social spaces. These compromise a person's *Muru'ah* because they demonstrate a lack of self-respect and emotional maturity. Legally, a person who lacks *Muru'ah* loses their **'Adalah** (legal integrity), and their testimony is rejected in a Maliki court.

18. **Explain the Maliki definition of a neighbor, and detail the legal rights of Shuf'ah (Pre-emption) and Da'f al-Darar (prevention of harm). How do these legal rules foster community cohesion?**
    *   *Answer:* In Maliki fiqh, a neighbor is defined as someone living within **forty houses** in all four directions. The right of **Shuf'ah** (Pre-emption) dictates that a partner must offer their share of co-owned property to their neighbor/partner first before selling it to an outsider. **Da'f al-Darar** legally bans a property owner from using their land to cause direct harm (e.g., blocking light or privacy) to a neighbor. These rules foster cohesion by preventing the intrusion of unwanted/harmful third parties into the local spatial community and ensuring that neighbors do not cause structural or privacy harm to one another, reducing social friction.

19. **How does Islamic law balance loyalty to family/friends with the universal obligation of 'Adl (justice)? Refer to Quranic concepts and Maliki court rules in your answer.**
    *   *Answer:* Islam commands absolute justice (**'Adl**), even if it is against oneself, parents, or kin (Surah an-Nisa: 135). The Maliki school balances this by disqualifying testimony in court that is highly suspected of bias. Children cannot testify *in favor* of parents, and intimate friends cannot testify *in favor* of close friends (**Shahadat al-Sadiq li-Sadiqihi**). By disqualifying these testimonies, the law prevents the temptation of lying for loved ones, ensuring that the legal standard remains objective while preserving the dignity of family and friend relationships from being compromised in court.

20. **Discuss how the Maliki principle of Maslahah Mursalah (Unrestricted Public Interest) can be applied to create digital ethics for young Muslims using social media.**
    *   *Answer:* **Maslahah Mursalah** allows the creation of rules that bring general benefit or prevent harm to society where there is no specific textual evidence. In the digital age, this principle can be applied to create rules such as screen-time limits, guidelines prohibiting the sharing of private chat screenshots (to protect honor/lineage, *Hifdh al-'Ird*), and rules against cyberbullying. While social media did not exist in the time of early scholars, establishing these digital ethics directly serves the public interest by protecting the mental well-being (*'Aql*) and social harmony of the Muslim community.
