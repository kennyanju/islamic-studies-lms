### Welcome, Young Scholar!
Welcome to your student guide for Unit 8. In this unit, we will explore how a Muslim is supposed to behave with others. We will look at what it means to be honest, how to stay humble, and how to control our anger. We will also learn about the rights of our parents, our family, and our neighbors, and how to choose good friends, navigate peer pressure, and understand justice.

We will learn these topics through the teachings of the **Maliki school** of Islamic law (*fiqh*) and the **Ash'ari school** of Islamic belief (*aqidah*). 

As you read, you will see special terms in bold. These are Arabic words that scholars use. Make sure you learn their meanings!

## Page 2: Creed Connect — Why Do We Choose Goodness?

Before we look at manners, we have to ask an important question: **Why do we do good deeds?** In our creed (*aqidah*), we learn two very special rules that explain this.

### 1. Good and Bad are Defined by Allah (Al-Husn wal-Qubh)
In the **Ash'ari** school of belief, we believe that we know what is good and what is bad because Allah told us in the Quran and through the Prophet (pbuh). This is called **Al-Husn wal-Qubh** (الْحُسْن وَالْقُبْح – Goodness and Badness). 
*   Why is honesty good? It is not just because people like it; it is because **Allah commanded it**.
*   Why is lying bad? It is because **Allah forbade it**.
*   Our minds can see that some things are helpful, but only Allah's law (*Al-Shar'*) decides what is truly a good deed that earns rewards in Jannah.

### 2. We Choose, and Allah Creates (Kasb)
Have you ever wondered: *Does Allah make me do good, or do I do it myself?*
*   Our creed teaches the concept of **Kasb** (كَسْب – Acquisition). 
*   Allah is the Creator (*Khaliq*) of everything, including our actions.
*   But Allah gave us a choice. When you decide in your heart to help your mom or tell the truth, you are "choosing" (*Kasb*) that action. Because you chose it, Allah creates the action for you, and He rewards you for your good choice!

> **Creed Focus Box: My Choice matters!**
> Since we choose (*Kasb*) our actions, we are fully responsible for our manners. We cannot say, *"I have a bad temper because Allah made me this way."* We must choose to be patient and polite!

## Page 3: The Three Jewels of Personal Character

Let's look at three powerful qualities that every young Muslim should wear like beautiful jewels.

### 1. Honesty (Sidq)
**Sidq** (صِدْق) means truthfulness and sincerity. The Prophet (pbuh) said that truthfulness leads us to goodness, and goodness leads to Jannah. 
To practice *Sidq*, we must be honest in three ways:
*   **Honest Words:** We never tell lies, even when we are joking or trying to get out of trouble.
*   **Honest Actions:** We do what we say. We don't pretend to be good when adults are watching and then act differently when they leave.
*   **Honest Intentions:** We do good things only to make Allah happy, not to show off.

### 2. Humility (Tawadu')
**Tawadu'** (تَوَاضُع) is humility. It means knowing that everything good about you—your smart brain, your athletic skills, your nice toys—is a gift from Allah. 
*   The opposite of humility is **Kibr** (كِبْر – Pride). Pride is looking down on others or refusing to accept the truth when you are wrong.
*   A humble person is kind to everyone, helps wash the dishes, and never brags about being better than others.

### 3. Controlling Anger (Kadhm al-Ghaydh)
Anger is like a hot spark from Shaytan. The Prophet (pbuh) taught us that a strong person is not someone who wins a physical fight, but someone who can control themselves when angry.
*   **Kadhm al-Ghaydh** (كَظْم الْغَيْظ) means locking up your anger inside and choosing to react with peace.
*   **Hilm** (حِلْم) is gentleness and patience that we build over time.

## Page 4: Character in the Law — What is Muru'ah?

In Islam, good character is not just a suggestion; it has real legal importance. In the **Maliki school of law**, there is a special concept called **Muru'ah**.

### What is Muru'ah?
**Muru'ah** (مُرُوءَة) translates to **chivalry, dignity, and honorable behavior**. It means acting in a way that is polite, respectful of others, and noble.

### How does Muru'ah affect us legally?
In an Islamic court of law, if someone wants to testify as a witness, the judge (*Qadi*) must check if they are an upright person. This quality is called **'Adalah** (عَدَالَة).
*   If a person lacks **Muru'ah**, they lose their **'Adalah**, and the judge will **reject their testimony**!
*   **What destroys Muru'ah for a 10-year-old?**
    *   Screaming and throwing temper tantrums in public.
    *   Playing mean or dangerous pranks on others.
    *   Lying to teachers or friends, even as a joke.
    *   Using bad language or acting foolishly for attention.

> **Law Check Box: The Dignified Muslim**
> Imam Malik taught that a Muslim should always carry themselves with quiet dignity. When we behave rudely, we are not just being impolite; we are showing that we cannot be trusted with important matters in our community.

## Page 5: My First Circle — Parents & Family

Our family is the most important group of people in our lives. Allah has given them special rights over us.

### 1. Loving and Obeying Parents (Birr al-Walidayn)
Allah says in the Quran that we must worship Him alone, and right after that, He commands us to show **Birr al-Walidayn** (بِرّ الْوَالِدَيْن – Righteous treatment of parents).
*   **No "Uff"!** Allah tells us in Surah al-Isra not to say even a small sigh of frustration, like *"Uff"* (أُفّ) or *"Argh!"*, when they ask us to do something.
*   **Obedience:** We must obey our parents in everything that is permissible (like cleaning our room, doing homework, or eating healthy food). The only time we do not obey them is if they command us to do something against Allah's law (like skipping Salah). But even then, we must speak to them with extreme kindness and love.

### 2. Legal Duty to Parents (Nafaqah)
In the Maliki school, when parents grow old and if they become poor, it is a **strict legal obligation** on their adult children to pay for their food, clothing, and home. This is called **Nafaqah** (نَفَقَة – Maintenance).

### 3. Connecting with Family (Silat al-Rahim)
**Silat al-Rahim** (صِلَة الرَّحِم) means connecting with your blood relatives (grandparents, aunts, uncles, cousins). 
*   The word *Rahim* means womb, showing that we are tied together by birth.
*   Cutting off family ties (*Qat' al-Rahim*) is a major sin. We must visit, call, or send cards to our relatives to keep our love alive!

## Page 6: My Neighbors — The 40-House Rule

Who is your neighbor? Is it just the family living next door? 

### The Maliki Definition of a Neighbor
According to **Imam Malik**, your neighbors are not just the people directly next to you. The neighborhood extends to **forty (40) houses in every direction**—in front, behind, to the left, and to the right! 

```
                    40 Houses North
                          ^
                          |
40 Houses West <--- [ YOUR HOUSE ] ---> 40 Houses East
                          |
                          v
                    40 Houses South
```

### The Rights of Neighbors (Huquq al-Jiwar)
Having neighbors means we must respect **Huquq al-Jiwar** (حُقُوق الْجِوَار – Rights of the Neighborhood). The Prophet (pbuh) said: *"Angel Jibril kept advising me to treat neighbors kindly until I thought he would make them my heirs."*

*   **Do Not Harm Them:** Never make loud noises that keep them awake, dump trash near their property, or let pets ruin their gardens.
*   **Be Generous:** The Prophet (pbuh) said that if you cook a warm soup, you should add extra water so you can share a bowl with your neighbor!
*   **Maliki Law on Neighbor Harm:** In our school, if a person does something on their own property that causes real harm to a neighbor (like blocking their light or window views), a judge can legally force them to change it.

## Page 7: True Friends vs. Peer Pressure

The friends you spend time with have a massive impact on who you become. 

### The Perfume Seller and the Blacksmith
The Prophet (pbuh) gave a beautiful example of friendship:
*   **The Good Friend** is like a perfume seller (**Misk**). Even if you don't buy any perfume, just standing near them makes you smell nice!
*   **The Bad Friend** is like a blacksmith working with fire. The sparks might burn your clothes, or at least you will walk away smelling like smoke.

### What is Peer Pressure?
Peer pressure is when friends try to push you to do things you know are wrong—like cheating on a test, skipping prayers, or being mean to someone. 
*   **The Khalil Rule:** The Prophet (pbuh) said: *"A person is on the religion of his close friend (*Khalil*), so let each of you look to whom he takes as a close friend."*
*   **Standing Strong:** A Muslim with **Muru'ah** (dignity) has the strength to say *"No"* when friends do bad things. If they don't stop, a true Muslim walks away and finds better company (**Suhbah Salihah** – Righteous companionship).

> **Maliki Court Rule: Friendship & Bias**
> In the Maliki school, if you are extremely close friends with someone, you cannot testify in court *in favor* of them. The law knows that your love for your friend might make it hard for you to be completely unbiased! This shows how powerful friendship is.

## Page 8: The Digital Screen — Social Media with a Clean Heart

Even though phones and tablets are fun, they present new challenges to our character. We must use them with an Islamic mindset.

### Protecting the Five Necessities (Al-Daruriyyat al-Khams)
Islamic law is designed to protect five important things: **Religion, Life, Intellect, Honor, and Wealth**. Social media can easily harm two of these:

1.  **Guarding the Intellect (*Hifdh al-'Aql*):** Doom-scrolling (watching short videos for hours) makes our brains tired and distracts us from Salah and schoolwork. We must control our screen time.
2.  **Guarding Honor (*Hifdh al-'Ird*):** It is very easy to write mean comments, share secrets, or make fun of people online. In Islam, guarding a person's honor (**'Ird** / عِرْض) is sacred.

### Maliki Rule: Sadd al-Dhara'i' (Blocking the Paths to Harm)
In the Maliki school, there is a legal rule called **Sadd al-Dhara'i'** (سَدّ الذَّرَائِع). It means: **"If something leads to sin, we must block it before the sin happens."**
*   If a certain video app always leads you to hear bad language or waste hours of time, the rule of *Sadd al-Dhara'i'* suggests you should delete it or set a strict limit.
*   If a group chat is full of gossip (*Gheebah*), you should leave it immediately.

## Page 9: Justice & Fairness — The Scales of Life

Islam teaches us to be fair to everyone, not just our friends or people we like.

### 1. Justice ('Adl) and Equity (Qist)
*   **'Adl** (عَدْل) means justice and giving everyone their rights.
*   **Qist** (قِسْط) means equity or fairness in how we treat people daily.
*   Allah says in the Quran: *"Be just! That is closer to piety."* (Surah al-Ma'idah: 8). We must be fair even if we are testifying against ourselves or our families.

### 2. Oppression (Zulm) is Haram
Oppression is called **Zulm** (ظُلْم). It means taking away someone's rights or treating them cruelly. Allah says in a Hadith Qudsi: *"I have forbidden oppression for Myself, and I have made it forbidden among you, so do not oppress one another."*

### 3. Serving the Public Interest (Maslahah Mursalah)
In Maliki fiqh, when there is no direct rule in the Quran about a modern problem, scholars use **Maslahah Mursalah** (مَصْلَحَة مُرْسَلَة) – the rule of **Public Interest**—to make decisions.
*   This means we support rules that keep people safe (like speed limits, school guidelines, and playground safety).
*   A good Muslim always acts in a way that brings benefit to the community and prevents harm.
