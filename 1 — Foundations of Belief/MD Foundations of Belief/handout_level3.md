## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
1. Define and differentiate the Three Judgments of the Intellect (Ahkam al-'Aql): Al-Wajib, Al-Mustahil, and Al-Ja'iz.
2. Explain how the Three Judgments protect a Muslim's faith from superstition and logical confusion.
3. Formulate the Sunni concept of Divine Transcendence (Tanzih) and the attribute of Mukhalafah lil-Hawadith.
4. Distinguish between the classical Sunni interpretive approaches of Tafwid and Ta'wil when reading apparent physical descriptions.
5. Memorize and explain the foundational creed lines from Matn Ibn 'Ashir.
6. Connect the Six Articles of Faith to personal accountability, moral duty, and the stages of the Hereafter.

## Section 1: Building an Analytical Faith
Faith in Islam (*Iman*) is not a blind emotional wish; it is firm conviction rooted in sound rational proof illuminated by divine revelation. At ages 10 to 12, your intellect is developing the ability to analyze arguments, categorize reality, and assess claims logically.

Classical Sunni theologians, such as Imam Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari and Imam Abu Bakr al-Baqillani, developed a logical framework known as the **Three Judgments of the Intellect** (*Ahkam al-'Aql*). Before studying specific theological texts, every student must understand how the human mind evaluates any statement:

| Judgment of the Intellect | Arabic Term | Definition | Universal Logical Example | Theological Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The Rationally Necessary** | *Al-Wajib al-'Aqli* | That which the sound mind cannot conceive of as nonexistent. | The whole of an object is larger than any of its parts. | The existence of one eternal, uncreated Creator (*Wujud Allah*). |
| **The Rationally Impossible** | *Al-Mustahil al-'Aqli* | That which the sound mind cannot conceive of as existing, because it contains an internal contradiction. | A shape that is simultaneously a square and a circle at the exact same moment. | Allah having a partner, beginning, physical body, or child. |
| **The Rationally Possible** | *Al-Ja'iz al-'Aqli* | That which the sound mind can conceive of as either existing or not existing without logical contradiction. | Whether it rains tomorrow, or whether an empire rises or falls. | Allah creating humanity, sending messengers, or forgiving a repentant believer. |

Understanding these categories equips a young Muslim to answer confused questions with clarity. For instance, when a skeptic asks: *"Can Allah create a rock so heavy He cannot lift it?"*, the student knows that an omnipotent Creator being unable to do something is a self-contradictory absurdity (*Al-Mustahil al-'Aqli*). Omnipotence (*Qudrah*) relates to all possibilities (*Al-Ja'izat*), not to logical nonsense.

## Section 2: Divine Transcendence: Tanzih vs. Anthropomorphism
Sunni theology is distinguished by **Tanzih** (تَنْزِيه), which means affirming that Allah is completely exalted above all created limits, flaws, parts, or physical dimensions.

### The Attribute of Mukhalafah lil-Hawadith (مُخَالَفَتُهُ لِلْحَوَادِثِ)
This attribute establishes that **Allah is entirely different from created things**:
* **Creation occupies space**: Allah created space and is not contained within it.
* **Creation is subject to time**: Allah created time and does not change across seconds, hours, or centuries.
* **Creation possesses physical forms and limits**: Allah has no physical body (*Jism*), organs, or boundaries.

Imam Dhu al-Nun al-Misri and Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal formulated the famous maxim of creed:
> *"Whatever comes to your mind, Allah is completely different from that."* (*Kullu ma khatara bi-balika fa-Allahu bi-khilafi dhalik*, cited by al-Qushayri in his *Risalah*).

### Understanding Apparent Bodily Descriptions in Classical Scholarship
When verses in the Qur'an use words that could be misunderstood physically (such as *Yad* or *Wajh*), Sunni scholars approached them with reverence and strict Tanzih through two recognized paths:
1. **The Method of the Early Predecessors (Tafwid)**: Affirming the text as revealed while completely denying any physical limb, bodily shape, or created resemblance (*Bila Kayf*).
2. **The Method of Later Scholars (Ta'wil)**: Explaining the word according to classical Arabic figurative eloquence (for example, understanding *Yad* to mean divine power, generosity, or protection, and *Wajh* to mean the Divine Essence).

Both paths share complete consensus on the core principle: **Allah is not a physical body and does not resemble anything in creation (Surah Ash-Shura 42:11).**

## Section 3: Classical Creed in Matn Ibn 'Ashir
Imam 'Abd al-Wahid Ibn 'Ashir (d. 1040 AH) summarized the necessary attributes of Allah in his famous educational poem *Al-Murshid al-Mu'in*:

> **فَصْلٌ: يَجِبُ لِلَّهِ الْوُجُودُ وَالْقِدَمْ ۝ كَذَا الْبَقَاءُ وَالْغِنَى الْمُطْلَقُ عَمْ**  
> **وَخُلْفُهُ لِخَلْقِهِ بِلاَ مِثَالْ ۝ وَوَحْدَةُ الذَّاتِ وَوَصْفٍ وَالْفِعَالْ**  
> *"Section: It is necessary for Allah to have Existence and Beginninglessness, likewise Endlessness and Absolute Independence encompassing all. His absolute difference from His creation without resemblance, and the Oneness of His Essence, Attributes, and Actions."* (Matn Ibn 'Ashir, Lines 11-13).

## Section 4: The Six Articles of Faith and Moral Responsibility
At Level 3, the Six Articles of Faith guide our daily choices and moral accountability:
1. **Belief in Allah**: Worshiping Him alone with reverence, love, and hope.
2. **Belief in the Angels**: Being mindful that the recording angels (*Kiraman Katibin*) record every word and deed (Surah Qaf 50:18).
3. **Belief in the Revealed Scriptures**: Following the final preserved guidance of the Noble Qur'an.
4. **Belief in the Messengers**: Honoring all 25 prophets named in the Qur'an, especially the resolute leaders (*Ulul-'Azm*: Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, 'Isa, and Muhammad ﷺ).
5. **Belief in the Last Day**: Understanding the stages of the Hereafter:
   * **The Intermediate Realm (*Al-Barzakh*)**: The state of the soul between death and resurrection.
   * **The Gathering (*Al-Hashr*)**: All humanity assembled before Allah on the Day of Resurrection.
   * **The Scale (*Al-Mizan*)**: Good and evil deeds weighed with perfect divine justice.
   * **The Traverse (*Al-Sirat*)**: The pathway over which people pass according to their faith and deeds.
   * **The Intercession (*Al-Shafa'ah*)**: The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ interceding by Allah's permission for believers.
6. **Belief in Divine Destiny (*Al-Qadar*)**: Trusting that everything happens by Allah's knowledge and wisdom, while using our free choice to perform good deeds.

## Section 5: Curriculum Anchors in Practice

### 5.1 Qur'an and Tafsir Anchor: Surah Al-Buruj (85:11-16)
> **إِنَّ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ لَهُمْ جَنَّاتٌ تَجْرِي مِن تَحْتِهَا الْأَنْهَارُ ۚ ذَٰلِكَ الْفَوْزُ الْكَبِيرُ ۝ إِنَّ بَطْشَ رَبِّكَ لَشَدِيدٌ ۝ إِنَّهُ هُوَ يُبْدِئُ وَيُعِيدُ ۝ وَهُوَ الْغَفُورُ الْوَدُودُ ۝ ذُو الْعَرْشِ الْمَجِيدُ ۝ فَعَّالٌ لِّمَا يُرِيدُ**  
> "Indeed, those who have believed and done righteous deeds will have gardens beneath which rivers flow. That is the great attainment. Indeed, the vengeance of your Lord is severe. Indeed, it is He who originates and repeats. And He is the Forgiving, the Loving, Owner of the Throne, the Glorious, Effecter of what He intends." (Surah Al-Buruj 85:11-16)

**Tafsir Insights**: Imam al-Qurtubi explains that *"Fa''alun lima yurid"* (Effecter of what He intends) shows that Allah's Will is executed throughout the cosmos without obstacle. He is *Al-Wadud* (The Loving), full of love for His righteous servants.

### 5.2 Tajwid Rule in Action: Rules of Nun Sakinah (Izhar and Idgham)
* **Izhar Halqi (Clear Pronunciation)**: When a Nun Sakinah (`نْ`) or Tanween is followed by one of the six throat letters (`ء, هـ, ع, ح, غ, خ`), the Nun is pronounced clearly without extra nasalization (e.g., *min tahtiha*).
* **Idgham (Merging)**: When followed by one of the six letters of *Yarmalun* (`ي, ر, م, ل, و, ن`), the Nun merges into the following letter:
  * With Ghunnah (2 counts): `ي, ن, م, و` (e.g., *Fa''alul-lima*).
  * Without Ghunnah: `ل, ر` (e.g., *Mir-Rabbika*).

### 5.3 Classical Text Anchor: Matn Ibn 'Ashir on Tanzih
> "His absolute difference from His creation without resemblance, and the Oneness of His Essence, Attributes, and Actions." (Matn Ibn 'Ashir, Line 13).

**Explanation**: This line teaches us that Allah's attributes (such as Knowledge, Power, and Will) do not resemble the limited knowledge, power, or will of human beings.

### 5.4 Prophetic Hadith Anchor: Hadith 2 of An-Nawawi (Iman and Conviction)
> "Tell me about Iman. He replied: 'It is to believe in Allah, His Angels, His Books, His Messengers, the Last Day, and to believe in Divine Destiny, both its good and its apparent adversity.'" (Sahih Muslim 8, Sahih; 40 Hadith Nawawi, Hadith 2)

**Living the Hadith**: When facing a difficult situation at school or home, remind yourself that Allah's decree contains hidden wisdom, and respond with patience (*Sabr*) and positive effort.

## Summary and Key Takeaways
* The intellect evaluates every statement into one of three categories: Necessary (*Wajib*), Impossible (*Mustahil*), or Possible (*Ja'iz*).
* Tanzih means recognizing that Allah is free from physical limits, shapes, parts, and locations.
* Classical scholars used either Tafwid or contextual Ta'wil when reading apparent bodily descriptions, while completely agreeing on Tanzih.
* Belief in the Last Day reminds us that our secret and open deeds will be weighed on the Mizan with perfect divine justice.
