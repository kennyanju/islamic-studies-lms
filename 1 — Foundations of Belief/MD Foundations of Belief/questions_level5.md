# Level 5 Quiz: Foundations of Belief

## Part A: Multiple Choice Questions
1. Which of the following accurately defines the three legitimate sources of knowledge (*Asbab al-'Ilm*) in classical Sunni epistemology?
   (A) Astrology, philosophical intuition, and secular consensus.
   (B) Sound Senses (*Al-Hiss al-Salim*), Pure Reason (*Al-'Aql al-Sarih*), and Veracious Report (*Al-Khabar al-Sadiq*).
   (C) Laboratory empiricism, dream interpretation, and governmental legislation.
   (D) Unrestricted skepticism, personal emotion, and ancestral mythology.

2. Why is the philosophical ideology of "Scientism" logically self-defeating?
   (A) Because modern scientists frequently disagree on experimental conclusions.
   (B) Because laboratory equipment is expensive and subject to mechanical malfunction.
   (C) Because the claim "only empirical science provides truth" cannot itself be empirically verified or measured.
   (D) Because scientific research papers are written in Latin rather than classical Arabic.

3. In the Kalam Cosmological Argument (*Dalil al-Huduth*), why is an "actual infinite regress of past temporal events" impossible?
   (A) Because the calendar would eventually run out of numbers to count past centuries.
   (B) Because if an infinite number of past moments had to elapse, history could never arrive at the present moment.
   (C) Because stars collapse into black holes after burning through their hydrogen fuel.
   (D) Because the legal schools limit historical jurisprudence to four generations.

4. What crucial deduction is made about the Creator from the fact that spacetime and physical matter had an absolute beginning?
   (A) The Creator must necessarily be timeless, non-spatial, immaterial, and supremely powerful.
   (B) The Creator must reside inside the physical core of expanding galactic superclusters.
   (C) The Creator must have evolved over billions of years from simpler cosmic energy forms.
   (D) The Creator is subject to the second law of thermodynamics and physical entropy.

5. How does the Ash'ari doctrine of **Kasb (Acquisition)** differ fundamentally from the Jabriyyah position?
   (A) Kasb denies that Allah has any knowledge of what humans will choose.
   (B) The Jabriyyah claimed humans have zero choice (fatalism), whereas Kasb affirms human intentional direction (*Qasd*) as the basis of accountability.
   (C) Kasb states that humans create their own physical bodies independently of divine decree.
   (D) The Jabriyyah permitted all sins, whereas Kasb prohibits travel outside one's hometown.

6. What major theological error did the Mu'tazilah commit regarding human actions?
   (A) They claimed that prayer should only be offered once a week on Friday.
   (B) They posited that humans create their own actions outside of Allah's power, effectively introducing multiple creators.
   (C) They denied that Prophet Muhammad ﷺ was a historical human being.
   (D) They prohibited believers from reading classical Arabic poetry and grammar.

7. In the theodicy of Sunni Islam, how is the existence of worldly suffering reconciled with Divine Mercy?
   (A) By claiming that Allah is unaware of suffering until humans pray to Him.
   (B) By understanding that Earth is a transient testing realm (*Dar al-Imtihan*), where suffering purifies, tests virtues, and is redressed in the Hereafter.
   (C) By declaring that evil is an independent deity warring against good.
   (D) By arguing that suffering is purely an optical illusion that has no actual existence.

8. Which Qur'anic narrative in Surah Al-Kahf serves as the primary theological paradigm for understanding hidden divine wisdom behind apparent tragedy?
   (A) The story of Prophet Yunus in the belly of the whale.
   (B) The narrative of Prophet Musa and the righteous servant Al-Khidr.
   (C) The dialogue between Prophet Yusuf and the prison inmates.
   (D) The description of Prophet Sulayman commanding the wind.

9. Why does Moral Relativism fail to provide a binding foundation for universal human rights?
   (A) Because international treaties are signed in multiple European languages.
   (B) Because without an objective, transcendent Divine Lawgiver, moral values reduce to subjective biological preferences or majority power.
   (C) Because ancient empires did not establish written legal constitutions.
   (D) Because scientific laboratories cannot synthesize ethical proteins.

10. What did Imam al-Ghazali demonstrate in *Tahafut al-Falasifah* regarding natural causality?
    (A) That observed physical correlation (e.g. fire touching cotton) does not prove that the physical fire is the ultimate independent creator of burning.
    (B) That medicine is useless and sick Muslims should refuse medical treatment.
    (C) That mathematics and logic should be banned from secondary school curricula.
    (D) That planets move in physical space purely by the mechanical push of ether.

## Part C: Matching Jurisprudential & Theological Terms
* Al-Hiss al-Salim ─── Sound empirical sense perception and physical observation
* Al-'Aql al-Sarih ─── Pure uncorrupted reason operating on necessary logical axioms
* Al-Khabar al-Sadiq ─── Veracious revelation and mass-transmitted historical reporting (Tawatur)
* Kasb ─── The human acquisition of responsibility through conscious intent
* Tahafut al-Falasifah ─── Al-Ghazali's masterwork critiquing philosophical causation dogmas

## Part D: Senior Seminar Case Study & Reflection
1. A university philosophy student asserts: "Since neurobiology can trace brain activity during prayer, spiritual experiences are simply biochemical tricks with no transcendent reality." How does classical epistemology deconstruct this reductionist fallacy?
2. Formulate a rigorous defense of the Kalam Cosmological Argument against the assertion that "the universe came from absolute nothing by a quantum fluctuation."
3. Using the narrative of Musa and Al-Khidr in Surah Al-Kahf, analyze why human beings cannot evaluate divine goodness solely based on immediate worldly outcomes.
4. Contrast the fatalism of secular determinism (genetic and neurological determinism) with the balanced Sunni doctrine of Kasb and moral accountability.

## Answer Key & Marking Guide
1. B - Classical kalam identifies sound senses, pure reason, and truthful report as the three channels of knowledge.
2. C - Scientism is a philosophical assertion that cannot meet its own empirical verification standard.
3. B - An actual infinite regress of past temporal moments leads to the logical impossibility of never reaching the present.
4. A - That which originates spacetime and matter cannot be composed of spacetime or matter; it must be transcendent and uncaused.
5. B - Kasb balances Allah's unshared creative power with genuine human intent and moral agency.
6. B - The Mu'tazilah attributed independent creative power (*Khalq*) to humans, compromising the absoluteness of Tawhid.
7. B - Suffering serves divine pedagogical and refining purposes within a transient testing realm, culminating in eternal justice.
8. B - The story of Al-Khidr in Surah Al-Kahf illustrates that apparent worldly misfortunes often prevent far greater hidden harms.
9. B - Without divine anchoring, moral imperatives reduce to cultural taste without objective binding force over individuals.
10. A - Al-Ghazali demonstrated that observed physical cause-and-effect is habitual conjunction created by Allah, not self-sufficient necessity.
