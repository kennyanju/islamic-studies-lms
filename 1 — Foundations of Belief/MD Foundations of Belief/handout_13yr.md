## Preface: Intellectual Responsibility & Faith

Dear Student,

As you reach the age of **Bulugh** (بُلُوغ - physical and mental maturity), your relationship with your faith transitions. You are no longer just practicing the habits taught by your parents; you are becoming an intellectually independent Muslim. 

Islam does not ask you to turn off your mind to believe. On the contrary, Sunni scholars have always maintained that sound intellect (*'Aql*) and divine revelation (*Naql*) work hand-in-hand to guide us to the truth. 

This manual is designed to introduce you to the deeper aspects of **Aqidah** (عَقِيدَة - theology/creed) and **Fiqh** (فِقْه - law/practice). We will explore these concepts through the lens of two classical, globally respected frameworks:
1. **The Ash'ari School of Aqidah:** Founded by Imam Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari (d. 324 AH), which provides a rational defense of the beliefs of the Quran and Sunnah.
2. **The Maliki School of Fiqh:** Founded by Imam Malik ibn Anas (d. 179 AH), which is built on the living Sunnah of the city of Madinah.

By studying these schools, you will develop **fiqh literacy**—understanding that the differences in how scholars interpret text or practice worship are not errors, but rather structured, logical conclusions based on valid methodologies.

## Section 1: Epistemology — How Do We Know the Truth?

Before we ask *what* we believe, we must ask *how* we know what is true. This is the study of **epistemology**. 

In the Ash'ari school, there are three primary sources of knowledge:
1. **Sound Senses (Al-Hawas al-Salimah):** Our sight, hearing, touch, taste, and smell, which tell us about the physical world.
2. **True Reports (Al-Khabar al-Sadiq):** Revelation (*Wahy*) from Allah through the Quran and the authentic statements of the Prophet ﷺ.
3. **Intellect (Al-'Aql):** Our mind's capacity to reason, deduce, and analyze.

```
                  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │          SOURCES OF KNOWLEDGE           │
                  └────────────────────┬────────────────────┘
          ┌────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────┐
          ▼                            ▼                            ▼
    Sound Senses                 True Reports                   Intellect
  (Physical World)               (Revelation)               (Logical Analysis)
```

### The Role of Intellect ('Aql) in Aqidah
Some people believe that religion is only about blind faith. The Ash'ari school argues otherwise. We believe that we can use our intellect to prove the existence of God. 

For example, using logic, we know that:
* The universe is **Hadith** (حَادِث - originated/created); it had a beginning and did not exist before.
* Everything that has a beginning must have a cause that brought it into existence (*The Cosmological Argument*).
* Therefore, the universe must have an uncaused Creator who is eternal.

Once logic leads us to realize there is a Creator, we look to **Revelation** to learn *Who* this Creator is, what His Names are, and how He wants us to live. Intellect establishes the foundation, and Revelation builds the structure.

## Section 2: The Divine Essence and the Necessary Attributes

Who is Allah? In Islamic theology, we study the **Dhat** (ذَات - Essence) of Allah and His **Sifat** (صِفَات - Attributes). 

Ash'ari theologians identify **20 Necessary Attributes** (*Sifat al-Wajibah*) that we must logically affirm for Allah. These are divided into four clear categories:

### 1. Nafsiyyah (نَفْسِيَّة) - The Personal Attribute
This is **Wujud** (وُجُود) – Existence. We must believe that Allah exists, and unlike us, His existence is necessary and has no cause.

### 2. Salbiyyah (سَلْبِيَّة) - The Divesting/Negative Attributes
These attributes deny qualities that are impossible for Allah to have. They clear Allah of any imperfections:
* **Qidam (قِدَم) - Beginninglessness:** Allah was not created and has no start.
* **Baqa' (بَقَاء) - Endlessness:** Allah will never cease to exist.
* **Mukhalafah lil-Hawadith (مُخَالَفَتُهُ لِلْحَوَادِثِ) - Absolute Difference from Creation:** Allah has no resemblance to created things.
* **Qiyamuhu bi-Nafsihi (قِيَامُهُ بِنَفْسِهِ) - Self-Sufficiency:** Allah does not need a creator, a place to exist, or anything else.
* **Wahdaniyyah (وَحْدَانِيَّة) - Oneness:** Allah is unique in His Dhat, His Sifat, and His actions.

### 3. Ma'ani (مَعَانِي) - The Existential Attributes
These are seven qualities that describe how Allah acts and interacts with the universe:
* **Qudrah (قُدْرَة) - Power:** The ability to bring things into existence or non-existence.
* **Iradah (إِرَادَة) - Will:** The choice to make things happen in a specific way.
* **'Ilm (عِلْم) - Knowledge:** Complete, eternal knowledge of all things.
* **Hayah (حَيَاة) - Life:** Allah is alive eternally.
* **Sam' (سَمْع) - Hearing:** Hearing all things without physical ears.
* **Basar (بَصَر) - Sight:** Seeing all things without physical eyes.
* **Kalam (كَلَام) - Speech:** Communicating without physical tongue, vocal cords, or sounds.

### 4. Ma'nawiyyah (مَعْنَوِيَّة) - The State Attributes
These are direct corollaries of the Ma'ani attributes (e.g., Allah *being* Powerful, *being* Willful, *being* Knowing, etc.).

## Section 3: Divine Transcendence and Hermeneutics

One of the most important concepts in Aqidah is **Tanzih** (تَنْزِيه), which means declaring Allah completely free from bodily attributes, spatial limits, or human emotions.

What happens when we encounter verses in the Quran that mention physical words, such as:
* The *"Hand"* (*Yad*) of Allah (e.g., Surah al-Fath, 48:10)
* Allah *"establishing Himself"* (*Istiwa'*) over the Throne (e.g., Surah Taha, 20:5)
* Allah *"coming"* (*Maji'*) on the Day of Judgment?

If we interpret these words literally, we fall into the dangerous error of **Tajsim** (تَجْسِيم - anthropomorphism / giving God a physical body). To prevent this, Sunni scholars developed two valid, orthodox approaches:

```
                  ┌─────────────────────────────────────────┐
                  │    HERMENEUTICAL APPROACHES TO TEXTS    │
                  └────────────────────┬────────────────────┘
          ┌────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────┐
          ▼                                                         ▼
    Tafwid (تَفْوِيض)                                         Ta'wil (تَأْوِيل)
  - Way of the Salaf (Early)                                - Way of the Khalaf (Later)
  - Affirm the word as revealed                             - Interpret metaphorically using
  - Deny physical meaning                                     rules of classical Arabic
  - Say: "Allah knows the meaning"                          - e.g., Yad = Power/Favor
```

### 1. Tafwid (تَفْوِيض) – Consignment / Delegating the Meaning
This was the primary method of the early scholars (*Salaf*). They affirmed the words of the Quran but completely denied any physical interpretation, saying: *"We read the text as it came, we believe in it, but we assert that Allah has no body, and we leave the exact definition of this word to Allah."*

### 2. Ta'wil (تَأْوِيل) – Metaphorical Interpretation
As the Muslim world expanded and met foreign philosophies, later scholars (*Khalaf*) used classical Arabic linguistics to explain these terms to prevent confusion. They argued that Arabic frequently uses metaphors:
* **Yad (Hand)** is interpreted as **Power** (*Qudrah*) or **Favor** (*Ni'mah*).
* **Wajh (Face)** is interpreted as the **Essence** (*Dhat*).
* **Istiwa' (Establishing)** is interpreted as **Control/Domination** (*Istila'*).

Both methods are fully accepted within Sunni Islam, and both agree on the core rule: **Allah exists without a body, without a direction, and without a physical place.**

## Section 4: The Angelic Realm — Metaphysical Reality

The second pillar of Iman is belief in the **Mala'ikah** (مَلَائِكَة - Angels). In Islamic metaphysics, angels represent a unique category of creation.

### The Metaphysical Nature of Angels:
* **Light (Nur):** They are created from light, making them subtle, non-physical entities that can manifest in physical forms (such as appearing as humans, as Jibril did in the Hadith of Jibril).
* **No Free Will:** Angels lack the faculty of *Shahwah* (شَهْوَة - desire/passion) and *Ghadab* (غَضَب - anger). They are programmed for absolute obedience.
* **Non-Bodily Functions:** They do not eat, drink, sleep, reproduce, or possess gender.

### Key Angels and Cosmic Order:
Sunni theology details that the universe is governed by Allah through angelic administration. The angels are not divine; they are servants carrying out natural laws.

```
                           ┌──────────────────┐
                           │   MAJOR ANGELS   │
                           └────────┬─────────┘
        ┌─────────────────┬─────────┴────────┬────────────────┐
        ▼                 ▼                  ▼                ▼
     Jibril            Mika'il            Israfil       Malik al-Mawt
  (Revelation)      (Sustenance)       (The Trumpet)       (Death)
```

### The Accompanying Angels:
As an individual, you are constantly interacting with the angelic realm:
* **Kiraman Katibin ( Honorable Recorders):** They record your moral choices. In Ash'ari texts, *Raqib* (Watcher) and *'Atid* (Ready) are not the specific proper names of the two angels, but rather adjectives describing their constant presence and vigilance.
* **Al-Hafazah (Guardian Angels):** They protect humans from unseen metaphysical forces (such as Jinn or spiritual harm) and physical disasters. They only step aside when a divine decree (*Qadar*) is set to take place.

## Section 5: The Holy Books and the Speech of Allah

When we say we believe in the **Kutub** (كُتُب - Holy Books), we are affirming that Allah communicated His guidance to mankind. However, the Ash'ari school makes a critical theological distinction regarding the nature of the **Speech of Allah** (*Kalam*).

### Kalam Nafsi vs. Kalam Lafzi

```
              ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
              │             THE SPEECH OF ALLAH (KALAM)          │
              └────────────────────────┬────────────────────────┘
            ┌──────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────┐
            ▼                                                     ▼
     Kalam Nafsi (كَلَام نَفْسِي)                            Kalam Lafzi (كَلَام لَفْظِي)
  - Allah's eternal, inner Speech                       - The created physical representation
  - Exists without letters, sounds,                     - Consists of Arabic letters, sound
    or languages (Arabic, Hebrew, etc.)                   waves, ink, and paper
  - Co-eternal with the Divine Essence                  - Points to the eternal Kalam Nafsi
```

1. **Kalam Nafsi (كَلَام نَفْسِي) - Inner/Essential Speech:**
   This is an eternal, uncreated attribute of Allah. It exists in the Divine Essence without letters, sounds, grammar, or languages (like Arabic or Hebrew). It is not subject to change.
2. **Kalam Lafzi (كَلَام لَفْظِي) - Uttered/Expressed Speech:**
   This refers to the words we read in the *Mushaf* (printed Quran), the sounds we hear during recitation, and the ink on the pages. These are created things that act as physical indicators of the eternal *Kalam Nafsi*.

### The Prophetic Scriptures:
We believe that Allah revealed scriptures to specific Messengers:
* **Suhuf** (Scrolls) to Ibrahim.
* **Tawrah** (Torah) to Musa.
* **Zabur** (Psalms) to Dawud.
* **Injil** (Gospel) to 'Isa.
* **Quran** to Muhammad ﷺ.

While we believe the original content of the prior books was divine truth, classical scholarship notes that their physical texts were corrupted (*Tahrif*) over time. The Quran remains the only book under direct divine protection from alteration.

## Section 6: Prophethood (Nubuwwah) & Infallibility

The fourth pillar of Iman is belief in the **Anbiya'** (أَنْبِيَاء - Prophets) and **Rusul** (رُسُل - Messengers).

### Nabi vs. Rasul
* **Nabi (Prophet):** A man chosen by Allah to receive revelation (*Wahy*). He is typically sent to implement and remind people of the law (*Shari'ah*) of the Messenger before him.
* **Rasul (Messenger):** A Prophet who is given a *new* divine law or scripture to deliver to a nation.
* *Rule:* All Messengers are Prophets, but not all Prophets are Messengers.

### The Doctrine of 'Ismah (Infallibility)
In Ash'ari theology, the Prophets must be **Ma'sum** (مَعْصُوم - infallible). This is a logical necessity: if a Prophet could commit sins, cheat, or lie, then we would be commanded by Allah to follow a sinner, which contradicts Allah’s justice.

Therefore, Prophets are protected from:
* Committing major sins (*Kaba'ir*).
* Committing minor sins that indicate low character or degrade their status.
* Deliberately lying, breaking contracts, or failing to deliver the message.

### The Four Essential Qualities of the Prophets:
To execute their duties, all Prophets are described as possessing:
1. **Sidq (صِدْق) - Truthfulness:** Complete honesty in speech and message.
2. **Amanah (أَمَانَة) - Trustworthiness:** Absolute moral integrity.
3. **Tabligh (تَبْلِيغ) - Transmission:** Conveying the message in its entirety.
4. **Fatanah (فَطَانَة) - Intelligence:** Sharp intellect and wisdom to guide people.

## Section 7: The Philosophy of Qadar: Decree and Choice

Belief in **Qadar** (قَدَر - Divine Decree) is the sixth pillar of Iman. It is often the most misunderstood concept. To understand Qadar, we must examine the relationship between Allah's **Knowledge**, **Will**, and **Power**.

```
    ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
    │           HOW THE DIVINE ATTRIBUTES ACT      │
    └──────────────────────┬───────────────────────┘
  ┌────────────────────────┼────────────────────────┐
  ▼                        ▼                        ▼
'Ilm (Knowledge)        Iradah (Will)            Qudrah (Power)
Allah knows what        Allah specifies how      Allah creates the
will happen.            it will happen.          action in reality.
```

### The Four Stages of Decree:
1. **'Ilm (Knowledge):** Allah eternally knows all things—past, present, future, and potential. His knowledge does not change or grow.
2. **Kitabah (Writing):** Allah ordered the Pen to write this knowledge on the **Lawh al-Mahfuz** (اللَّوْح الْمَحْفُوظ - Preserved Tablet) fifty thousand years before the creation of the heavens and earth.
3. **Mashi'ah (Will):** Nothing can exist or happen unless Allah wills it to happen.
4. **Khalq (Creation):** Allah is the sole Creator of all essences and actions.

### Addressing the Misconception:
A common error is believing in **Jabr** (جَبْر - determinism)—the idea that humans are forced to do things like robots. 

Another error is **Qadariyyah** (extreme free will)—the belief that humans create their own actions completely independent of Allah's power.

The Ash'ari school takes the middle path. We assert that **Allah knows what you will choose**, and He wrote it down. The writing is a record of your future choices, not a force that compels you. If you choose to study, you will pass. If you choose to skip study, you will fail. Allah knows your choice eternally, but the choice is yours.

## Section 8: The Concept of Kasb (Acquisition)

To explain human agency and accountability without compromising the fact that Allah is the *only* Creator of effects, Ash'ari theologians developed the theory of **Kasb** (كَسْب - Acquisition).

### Defining Kasb:
* **Khalq (Creation):** Allah creates the physical ability (*Istita'ah*) and the action itself.
* **Kasb (Acquisition):** The human directs their intention (*Niyyah*) and choice (*Ikhtiyar*) toward a specific action, and thus "acquires" the moral responsibility for it.

```
   ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │                       THE METAPHOR OF THE CAR                    │
   │                                                                  │
   │   - Engine, Fuel, and Wheels = Power created by Allah.           │
   │   - Turning the Steering Wheel = Intention of the Human.         │
   │                                                                  │
   │   The car moves because of the engine (Allah's Creation), but    │
   │   the driver is responsible for where the car goes (Kasb).       │
   └──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Scriptural Proof:
The Quran explicitly references this dual reality:
*"Allah does not charge a soul except with that within its capacity. It will have [the consequence of] what good it has acquired (Kasabat), and it will bear [the consequence of] what evil it has earned."* (Surah al-Baqarah, 2:286)

### Moral Implication:
Because you possess choice (*Ikhtiyar*), you cannot blame Qadar for your moral failures. If you commit a sin, you cannot say: *"Allah decreed it, so I am innocent."* You chose the action; therefore, you acquired the sin. 

Conversely, when you do a good deed, you thank Allah for creating the ability in you, while enjoying the reward of your positive intention.

## Section 9: Fiqh Literacy — The Maliki Methodology

As you study how to practice Islam, you must learn about **Fiqh** (Jurisprudence). Fiqh is the process of extracting practical rulings from the Quran and Sunnah.

Sunni Islam is preserved through four primary legal schools (*Madhhabs*). We focus on the **Maliki Madhhab**, founded by **Imam Malik ibn Anas** (d. 179 AH) in Madinah.

### The Sources of Maliki Law:
Imam Malik did not just look at written reports (Hadith). He looked at a unique source:
* **'Amal Ahl al-Madinah (عَمَل أَهْل الْمَدِينَة) - The Practice of the People of Madinah:**
  Madinah was the city where the Prophet ﷺ lived for ten years, established the Shari'ah, and died. Imam Malik argued that if the children and grandchildren of the Sahabah (Companions) in Madinah were all practicing a ruling publicly (like how they gave Zakat or prayed), this was a living Sunnah that was more reliable than a single written Hadith that might have been misunderstood or abrogated.

### The Case of Sadl (Praying with Arms at the Side)
A classic example of Fiqh diversity is the position of the hands in Salah:
* **Maliki Position (Sadl):** Standing in obligatory prayers with the arms resting naturally at the sides. 
* **Methodological Reason:** Imam Malik observed that the scholars and general population of Madinah prayed with their hands down in fard prayers. He concluded that this was the original, continuous practice of the Prophet ﷺ.
* **Other Schools (Qabd):** The Shafi'i, Hanafi, and Hanbali schools prefer folding the right hand over the left (*Qabd*), relying on written Hadiths.

Both are valid, Sunnah practices. Understanding *why* Imam Malik chose *Sadl* builds legal literacy and respect for scholarly methods.
