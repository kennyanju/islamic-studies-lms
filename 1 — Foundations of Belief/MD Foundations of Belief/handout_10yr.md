## Page 1: Who is Allah? Names of Mercy and Power

Have you ever looked up at the stars at night, or observed the perfect symmetry of a leaf? When we look at the world, we see that it is beautifully designed. A design needs a Designer. That Designer is Allah.

The core of our belief is **Tawhid** (تَوْحِيد), which means declaring and believing that Allah is One, Unique, and has no partners. 

### Knowing Allah through His Names
Allah has revealed to us many of His Names so that we can know Him, love Him, and worship Him. These are called **Al-Asma' al-Husna** (الأَسْمَاء الْحُسْنَى) – The Most Beautiful Names.

Here are a few Names that every Muslim should know:

1. **Al-Khaliq (الْخَالِق) – The Creator:** Allah made everything from nothing. Humans can build things out of wood or metal, but only Allah can create something out of absolutely nothing (*Ibadah*).
2. **Al-Rahman (الرَّزَّاق) – The Universally Merciful:** Allah is kind and gives blessings to every single creation in this world—humans, animals, trees, believers, and non-believers alike.
3. **Al-Raheem (الرَّحِيم) – The Specific Merciful:** Allah has a special, deep mercy reserved for the believers, especially in the hereafter.
4. **Al-Alim (الْعَلِيم) – The All-Knowing:** Allah knows everything. He knows what you say out loud, what you whisper, and even the thoughts that cross your mind when you are silent.

### Let's Reflect:
Think of a time when you saw something beautiful in nature. Which Name of Allah did it remind you of? Write it down or share it with your family.

## Page 2: What is Allah Like? (Divine Transcendence)

When we think about Allah, our minds might try to imagine what He "looks like." But there is a very important rule in our creed, called **Tanzih** (تَنْزِيه), which means keeping Allah free from any human or created qualities.

In the Ash'ari school of theology, we learn a special term:
**Mukhalafah lil-Hawadith (مُخَالَفَتُهُ لِلْحَوَادِثِ):** This means **Allah is completely different from created things.**

```
   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │                    THE GOLDEN RULE OF CREED                 │
   │                                                             │
   │  "Whatever you imagine in your mind, Allah is not like that."│
   │             — Imam Zu al-Nun al-Misri (d. 245 AH)           │
   └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Key Differences between Allah and Creation:
* **Creation:** We have bodies, we occupy space, we need food, we get tired, and we live inside of time (seconds, minutes, years).
* **Allah:** He does not have a physical body, He does not live inside space or time (because He created space and time!), He does not eat or sleep, and He never gets tired.

### How do we understand words like "Hand" or "Face" in the Quran?
Sometimes, the Quran uses words that sound like they describe physical body parts. For example, the Quran mentions the *"Hand of Allah"* or Allah *"establishing Himself over the Throne."* 

As Sunni Muslims following the Ash'ari path, we have two beautiful ways to understand these:
1. **Tafwid (تَفْوِيض) - Letting it be:** We say, *"I believe in this verse exactly as Allah revealed it, but I know it does not mean a physical human body part. Only Allah knows its true meaning."*
2. **Ta'wil (تَأْوِيل) - Metaphorical meaning:** We explain the word using the beauty of the Arabic language. For example, "Hand" means Allah's **Power** or **Blessing**, and "establishing over the throne" means Allah has absolute **Control** and **Authority** over the universe.

By understanding this, we protect ourselves from imagining Allah as a giant human, which is a major error called **Tashbih** (تَشْبِيه) (comparing Allah to creation).

## Page 3: The Six Pillars of Iman

What does it mean to have faith? In Arabic, the word for faith is **Iman** (إِيمَان). It is not just a feeling; it is a firm belief in the heart, spoken with the tongue, and shown through our actions.

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught us that Iman has **six pillars**. These are the six articles of belief that connect our hearts to the unseen world:

```
                  ┌───────────────────────────────┐
                  │      THE 6 PILLARS OF IMAN    │
                  └───────────────┬───────────────┘
         ┌────────────┬───────────┼───────────┬────────────┐
         ▼            ▼           ▼           ▼            ▼
     1. Allah    2. Angels    3. Books   4. Prophets  5. Last Day
                                  │
                                  ▼
                              6. Qadar
```

1. **Belief in Allah (اللّٰه):** Knowing He exists, is One, and has perfect attributes.
2. **Belief in His Angels (مَلَائِكَة):** Believing in these noble, unseen creations of light.
3. **Belief in His Books (كُتُب):** Believing in the original scriptures sent to guide humanity.
4. **Belief in His Messengers (رُسُل):** Believing in the noble men chosen to teach us the truth.
5. **Belief in the Last Day (الْيَوْم الْآخِر):** Believing in the Day of Judgment, Paradise, and Hellfire.
6. **Belief in Qadar (قَدَر):** Believing that everything happens according to Allah's decree and wisdom.

### Why do we call them "Pillars"?
Just like a building needs strong pillars to stand up, your heart needs these six beliefs to stand strong against doubts, sadness, and bad choices. If one pillar is missing, the whole structure of your faith can fall.

## Page 4: Angels: Creations of Light and Obedience

Imagine a creation that is incredibly powerful, made entirely of pure light (*Nur*), and has never committed a single sin. These are the **Mala'ikah** (مَلَائِكَة) – the Angels.

Unlike humans and Jinn, angels **do not have free will**. They do not get to choose between good and bad. They only do what Allah commands them to do, and they do it perfectly. They also do not have physical genders, they do not eat, sleep, or have families.

### The Four Archangels and Their Duties:
Allah has created millions of angels, but there are four major ones whose names and duties we must know:

| Angel | Arabic Name | Primary Duty / Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Gabriel** | Jibril (جِبْرِيل) | The Angel of Revelation. He carried Allah's words and books down to the Prophets. |
| **Michael** | Mika'il (مِيكَائِيل) | The Angel of Sustenance. He directs rain and helps plants grow by Allah's command. |
| **Raphael** | Israfil (إِسْرَافِيل) | The Blower of the Trumpet. He will blow the horn to end this world and start the Resurrection. |
| **Angel of Death** | Malik al-Mawt (مَلَكُ الْمَوْت) | The Angel of Death. He is responsible for taking the souls of people when their lifespan ends. |

### Did you know?
In many stories, the Angel of Death is called *Izra'il*. While that name is commonly used in history, the Quran simply calls him *Malik al-Mawt* (The Angel of Death). Both names point to the same noble angel!

## Page 5: Our Unseen Companions

Even though we cannot see angels with our eyes, they are close to us every single day! Allah has assigned specific angels to be with you as you walk, study, sleep, and play.

### 1. The Honorable Recorders: Kiraman Katibin (كِرَامًا كَاتِبِينَ)
Every day, two angels sit on your shoulders:
* **The Right-Shoulder Angel:** Writes down all your good deeds, like helping your parents, smiling at a friend, or saying your prayers.
* **The Left-Shoulder Angel:** Writes down any bad choices or sins you make.

**The Mercy of the Left Angel:**
In our theology, we learn that the angel on the left is very patient. If you make a mistake, the angel does not write it down immediately. He waits for a few hours. If you say, *"Astaghfirullah"* (I seek Allah's forgiveness) and feel sorry, the bad deed is erased and never written down! 

### 2. The Protectors: Al-Hafazah (الْحَفَظَة)
Have you ever had a moment where you almost tripped or got into a bad accident, but somehow you were completely fine? 
Allah sends guardian angels (*Hafazah*) to shield you from harm. They watch over you from the front and back. They only step aside if Allah has decreed that something must happen to teach you a lesson or test your patience.

```
   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │                     DAILY HABIT CHALLENGE                   │
   │                                                             │
   │   Before you sleep tonight, say: "Astaghfirullah" 3 times.  │
   │  This cleans your record before the left angel writes!     │
   └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Page 6: Our Holy Books: Allah's Guidance

Imagine you buy a very complex machine, like a spaceship. To operate it, you need an instruction manual. The human soul is complex, and the world is full of choices. To help us navigate life, Allah sent down **Holy Books** as instruction manuals for our hearts.

Sunni Muslims believe in **five major scriptures** revealed by Allah to different Prophets:

1. **Suhuf Ibrahim (صُحُف إِبْرَاهِيم) – The Scrolls of Abraham:** Revealed to Prophet Ibrahim. These were early scrolls containing wisdom.
2. **Tawrah (تَوْرَاة) – The Torah:** Revealed to Prophet Musa (Moses) for the Children of Israel.
3. **Zabur (زَبُور) – The Psalms:** Revealed to Prophet Dawud (David).
4. **Injil (إِنْجِيل) – The Gospel:** Revealed to Prophet 'Isa (Jesus).
5. **Quran (قُرْآن) – The Recitation:** Revealed to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ as the final, protected guide for all of humanity.

### How do we respect these books?
We believe that the original versions of the Torah, Psalms, and Gospel were the pure words of Allah. However, over time, people changed, added, or lost parts of those books. 

Only the **Quran** is perfectly preserved. Allah promised:
*"Indeed, it is We who sent down the message, and indeed, We will guard it."* (Surah al-Hijr, 15:9)

### A Deep Thought:
When we hold the physical copy of the Quran (called the *Mushaf*), we must wash our hands (*Wudu*) first. This is a special rule in the Maliki school. It shows that we respect the letters that point us to Allah's Speech.

## Page 7: The Prophets: The Best Examples

Allah did not just send books down from the sky; He sent **Prophets** to live out the books and show us how to be good people. 

### What is the difference between a Nabi and a Rasul?
* **Nabi (نَبِي) – Prophet:** A noble man who receives guidance from Allah to remind his people of the correct way. He usually follows the rules of the Prophet before him.
* **Rasul (رَسُول) – Messenger:** A Prophet who is given a brand-new book or a new set of laws (*Shari'ah*) to deliver.

> *Every Rasul is a Nabi, but not every Nabi is a Rasul!*

### The Infallibility of Prophets ('Ismah)
In our creed, we believe that all Prophets possess **'Ismah** (عِصْمَة), which means **infallibility**. This means Allah protected them from committing major sins, lying, or being untrustworthy.

Why did Allah make them infallible? Because if they made bad choices, we wouldn't know how to behave. They were chosen because they had the best characters:
1. **Sidq (صِدْق):** They always spoke the truth.
2. **Amanah (أَمَانَة):** They were completely trustworthy.
3. **Tabligh (تَبْلِيغ):** They delivered the message without hiding a single word.
4. **Fatanah (فَطَانَة):** They were highly intelligent and wise.

## Page 8: Qadar: Believing in Allah's Plan

Have you ever planned a perfect day outside, only for it to rain? Or have you ever lost something you loved, only to find something better later? This is related to **Qadar** (قَدَر) – Allah's Decree.

Belief in Qadar means we know that:
1. Allah knows everything that will happen before it happens.
2. He has written it all down in the **Lawh al-Mahfuz** (اللَّوْح الْمَحْفُوظ) – The Preserved Tablet.
3. Nothing happens in the universe unless Allah allows it to happen.

### The Story of the Seed
Imagine you plant a watermelon seed in the soil.
* You dig the dirt, put the seed in, water it, and make sure it gets sunlight.
* Who actually makes the seed crack open and grow into a big, green watermelon? You can't do that. Only Allah's power can create life.
* Your job was to plant and water (doing your best). Allah's job was to create the watermelon (decree).

When we understand this, we feel peace. If we try our best and fail, we don't get depressed. We say, *"Allah planned this, and He is the best of planners!"*

## Page 9: Making Choices and Being Responsible (Kasb)

If Allah has already written down everything that will happen, does that mean we don't have a choice? Can we do something bad and blame Qadar?

**No! Absolutely not!** 

To help us understand this, our scholars teach us a concept called:
**Kasb (كَسْب) – Acquisition.**

* **Allah's Role (Creation):** Allah creates the physical ability, the options, and the final action.
* **Your Role (Choice & Intention):** You choose which option to take. Your soul "acquires" (earns) the reward or the consequence.

### The School Test Analogy
Imagine your teacher gives you an exam. You have two choices: study hard, or play video games all night.
* You decide to play video games.
* On the exam day, you write down wrong answers. The teacher grades it and writes a red "F" on your paper.
* Can you scream at the teacher and say, *"You wrote 'Fail' on my paper! It's your fault!"*?
* Of course not! The teacher only wrote what *you* chose to earn.

In the same way, Allah knows what you will choose, and He creates the physical actions for you to do it. But because *you* directed your heart to make that choice, you are responsible. 

We can never use Qadar as an excuse to be lazy, skip our prayers, or be mean to others.

## Page 10: Fiqh Corner: Praying the Maliki Way

Have you ever visited a mosque and seen someone praying with their hands resting naturally at their sides instead of folded over their chest? 

If so, you were likely watching someone follow the **Maliki Madhhab** (school of law)!

### What is a Madhhab?
A *Madhhab* is like a path mapped out by a great Islamic scholar to help us practice our religion correctly. The Maliki school was founded by **Imam Malik ibn Anas** (d. 179 AH). 

Imam Malik lived in **Madinah**—the very city where the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ lived and died. Imam Malik based many of his rulings on the continuous practices of the people of Madinah (*'Amal Ahl al-Madinah*), because he knew that their parents and grandparents learned directly from the Prophet ﷺ.

### Special Maliki Practices in Salah:
1. **Sadl (سَدْل):** Standing in obligatory prayers with arms resting naturally at the sides. Malikis do this because it was the practice of the scholars and families of Madinah.
2. **Silent/No Basmalah:** In the obligatory prayers, we do not recite *"Bismillahir-Rahmanir-Rahim"* aloud or silently before Al-Fatihah. We start directly with *"Al-Hamdu lillahi Rabbil-'Alamin."*
3. **Sujud al-Sahw (Prostrations of Forgetfulness):**
   * If we *forgot* a sunnah part of prayer, we make two prostrations **before** the Taslim (called *Qabli*).
   * If we *added* an extra movement by mistake, we make two prostrations **after** the Taslim (called *Ba'di*).
