## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:
1. Explain the moral and legal significance of reaching Bulugh (puberty and moral accountability / Taklif).
2. Classify and articulate the 20 Necessary Attributes of Allah (Al-Sifat al-Wajibah) according to the Sanusi taxonomy.
3. Identify the 20 Impossible attributes (Al-Mustahilat) and the one Permissible attribute (Al-Ja'iz).
4. Define the four necessary attributes of the Messengers (Sidq, Amanah, Tabligh, Fatanah) and the doctrine of Prophetic Infallibility ('Ismah).
5. Compare the historical methodologies of the Ash'ari, Maturidi, and Athari schools within Ahl al-Sunnah.

## Section 1: The Threshold of Moral Accountability (Bulugh and Taklif)
As a young Muslim enters the years of **Bulugh** (puberty and physical maturity), Islamic jurisprudence recognizes them as a morally responsible believer (*Mukallaf*). Your religious obligations are no longer childhood practice; they are personal covenants between you and Allah, recorded directly by the angels.

Classical Sunni theologians, following the Qur'anic method, taught that the primary intellectual duty upon a discerning person is to attain grounded knowledge of Allah through reflective reasoning (*Ma'rifat Allah bi-Nazar*). This is not cold abstract philosophy; it means using the intellect (*'Aql*) that Allah created within you to observe the signs in the cosmos and recognize His unshared majesty, power, and mercy.

## Section 2: The Sanusi Framework: The 20 Necessary Attributes of Allah
Imam Muhammad ibn Yusuf al-Sanusi (d. 895 AH), a prominent North African Maliki scholar, organized the necessary attributes of Allah into four logical categories in his famous theological manual *Umm al-Barahin* (*The Mother of Proofs*):

| Category | Count | Divine Attributes | Theological Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Al-Sifah al-Nafsiyyah (Essential Attribute)** | 1 | **Al-Wujud** (Existence) | Allah exists necessarily by His own Essence, without beginning, end, or external cause. |
| **2. Al-Sifat al-Salbiyyah (Negative Attributes)** | 5 | **Al-Qidam** (Pre-Eternity)<br>**Al-Baqa'** (Endlessness)<br>**Mukhalafah lil-Hawadith** (Absolute Incomparability)<br>**Al-Qiyam bin-Nafs** (Self-Subsistence)<br>**Al-Wahdaniyyah** (Absolute Oneness) | These attributes negate all deficiencies:<br>1. *Qidam*: He has no beginning.<br>2. *Baqa'*: He never ceases to exist.<br>3. *Mukhalafah*: He has no physical body, shape, or location (Surah Ash-Shura 42:11).<br>4. *Qiyam bin-Nafs*: He needs no creator, location, or supporter.<br>5. *Wahdaniyyah*: One in Essence, Attributes, and Actions. |
| **3. Sifat al-Ma'ani (Qualitative Attributes)** | 7 | **Al-Qudrah** (Power)<br>**Al-Iradah** (Will)<br>**Al-'Ilm** (Knowledge)<br>**Al-Hayah** (Life)<br>**Al-Sam'** (Hearing)<br>**Al-Basar** (Sight)<br>**Al-Kalam** (Speech) | Eternal, living attributes subsisting with the Divine Essence:<br>1. *Qudrah*: Brings all possibilities into existence.<br>2. *Iradah*: Specifies what exists and when.<br>3. *'Ilm*: Encompasses everything without learning or forgetting.<br>4. *Hayah*: Eternal, uncreated life.<br>5. *Sam'*: Hears all sounds without physical ears.<br>6. *Basar*: Sees all existences without physical eyes.<br>7. *Kalam*: Divine speech without letters or vocal chords. |
| **4. Sifat al-Ma'nawiyyah (Active Relational States)** | 7 | **Kaunuhu Qadiran**<br>**Kaunuhu Muridan**<br>**Kaunuhu 'Aliman**<br>**Kaunuhu Hayyan**<br>**Kaunuhu Sami'an**<br>**Kaunuhu Basiran**<br>**Kaunuhu Mutakalliman** | The active state of being eternally All-Powerful, All-Willing, All-Knowing, Living, All-Hearing, All-Seeing, and Speaking. |

### The 20 Impossible Attributes (*Al-Mustahilat*) and the Permissible (*Al-Ja'iz*)
* **The 20 Impossible Attributes**: These are the direct opposites of the 20 necessary attributes. It is rationally impossible for Allah to: not exist (*'Adam*), have a beginning (*Huduth*), perish (*Fana'*), resemble creation (*Mumathalah*), need a location or cause (*Iftiqar*), have partners (*Ta'addud*), or be powerless, unwilling, ignorant, dead, deaf, blind, or unable to speak.
* **The One Permissible Attribute (*Al-Ja'iz*)**: For Allah, it is permissible to create any possible thing or leave it uncreated, to send messengers, and to reward believers by His grace (*Fadl*) or hold oppressors accountable by His justice (*'Adl*).

Together, these form the **41 Articles of Creed** (*'Aqa'id al-Iman*) systematized by Imam al-Sanusi to summarize the rational proofs of Tawheed.

## Section 3: Prophetic Infallibility ('Ismah) and the Qualities of Messengers
Prophets are the human ambassadors chosen by Allah to convey divine guidance. To ensure that revelation is transmitted with absolute reliability, Sunni theology establishes four necessary attributes for all prophets:

1. **Al-Sidq (Truthfulness)**: It is impossible for a prophet to tell a lie, whether in conveying revelation or in everyday personal speech.
2. **Al-Amanah (Trustworthiness and 'Ismah)**: Complete moral protection from committing major sins (*Kaba'ir*) or public minor sins (*Sagha'ir*), both before and after receiving prophethood.
3. **Al-Tabligh (Full Conveyance)**: They delivered every single command and verse entrusted to them by Allah, concealing nothing out of fear, hardship, or social pressure.
4. **Al-Fatanah (Intellectual Acuity)**: Exceptional wisdom, clarity, and discernment, enabling them to convey truth and debate opponents with compelling reasoning.

## Section 4: Comparative Sunni Theology: Schools of Ahl al-Sunnah
Throughout Islamic history, three main scholarly approaches developed within the broad tent of **Ahl al-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah**:

| School | Primary Methodology | Shared Sunni Consensus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The Ash'ari School** (Imam Abu al-Hasan al-Ash'ari, d. 324 AH) | Formulated systematic dialectical theology (*Kalam*) to defend orthodox creed against rationalist and materialist heresies. Prevalent in the Maliki, Shafi'i, and many Hanafi communities. | All three schools are united in **Tanzih**: Allah is not a physical body (*Jism*), does not occupy space, has no physical limbs, and does not resemble creation in any way (Surah Ash-Shura 42:11). |
| **The Maturidi School** (Imam Abu Mansur al-Maturidi, d. 333 AH) | Parallel Sunni dialectical tradition prevalent across Central and South Asian Hanafi scholars, sharing near-identical conclusions with the Ash'aris. | |
| **The Athari School** (Early Hadith scholars and Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal, d. 241 AH) | Affirmed the same divine transcendence, preferring to recite textual descriptions without formal philosophical categorization, affirming *Bila Kayf* (without attributing physical form). | |

## Section 5: Curriculum Anchors in Practice

### 5.1 Qur'an and Tafsir Anchor: Surah Al-Mulk (67:1-3)
> **تَبَارَكَ الَّذِي بِيَدِهِ الْمُلْكُ وَهُوَ عَلَىٰ كُلِّ شَيْءٍ قَدِيرٌ ۝ الَّذِي خَلَقَ الْمَوْتَ وَالْحَيَاةَ لِيَبْلُوَكُمْ أَيُّكُمْ أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًا ۚ وَهُوَ الْعَزِيزُ الْغَفُورُ ۝ الَّذِي خَلَقَ سَبْعَ سَمَاوَاتٍ طِبَاقًا ۖ مَّا تَرَىٰ فِي خَلْقِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ مِن تَفَاوُتٍ**  
> "Blessed is He in whose hand is dominion, and He is over all things competent, Who created death and life to test you as to which of you is best in deed, and He is the Exalted in Might, the Forgiving, Who created seven heavens in layers. You do not see in the creation of the Most Merciful any inconsistency." (Surah Al-Mulk 67:1-3)

**Tafsir Insights**: Imam Ibn Juzayy al-Kalbi explains in *Al-Tashil* that *"bi-yadihi al-Mulk"* is an Arabic eloquence signifying that absolute sovereignty and power belong solely to Allah. Notice the phrase *"best in deed"*: Allah tests the quality and sincerity of our actions, not merely their outward volume.

### 5.2 Tajwid Rule in Action: Rules of Meem Sakinah
When a Meem Sakinah (`مْ`) occurs in recitation, it follows one of three clear rules:
1. **Ikhfa' Shafawi (Labial Concealment)**: When followed by the letter Ba (`ب`), the lips close lightly with a 2-count nasal Ghunnah (e.g., *tarmihim bi-hijarah*).
2. **Idgham Shafawi (Labial Merging)**: When followed by another Meem (`م`), the two Meems merge with Ghunnah (e.g., *lakum ma kasabtum*).
3. **Izhar Shafawi (Labial Clarity)**: When followed by any of the remaining 26 letters, the Meem is pronounced clearly without extra elongation.

### 5.3 Classical Text Anchor: Sanusi's Umm al-Barahin
> "Know that the rationally necessary is that whose nonexistence cannot be conceived; the impossible is that whose existence cannot be conceived; and the permissible is that whose existence and nonexistence are both conceivable to the intellect." (Imam al-Sanusi, *Umm al-Barahin*).

**Explanation**: This foundational definition grounds Islamic belief in clear rational evidence, liberating the human mind from superstition and blind cultural conformism.

### 5.4 Prophetic Hadith Anchor: Hadith 2 of An-Nawawi (The Pillar of Faith)
> "He said: 'You have spoken the truth.' We were astonished that he asked him and confirmed him." (Sahih Muslim 8, Sahih; 40 Hadith Nawawi, Hadith 2)

**Living the Hadith**: Review the 20 divine attributes with your family. Reflect on how knowing Allah is *Al-Basir* (The All-Seeing) and *Al-Sami'* (The All-Hearing) inspires honesty in private and public life.

## Summary and Key Takeaways
* Bulugh marks the beginning of personal moral accountability (*Taklif*), where worship stems from reasoned conviction.
* Imam al-Sanusi classified the 20 necessary attributes of Allah into Essential, Negative, Qualitative, and Active Relational states.
* Prophetic Infallibility (*'Ismah*) guarantees that messengers are protected from major and disgraceful sins, ensuring the reliable transmission of revelation.
* The Ash'ari, Maturidi, and Athari schools represent authentic expressions of Ahl al-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah, united in affirming Divine Transcendence (Tanzih).
