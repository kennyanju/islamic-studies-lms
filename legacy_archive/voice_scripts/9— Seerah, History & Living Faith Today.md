### Seerah, History & Living Faith Today

* **Module 1:** The Later Years and Legacy of the Prophet ﷺ
* **Module 2:** The Rightly Guided Caliphs (*Al-Khulafā’ al-Rāshidūn*)
* **Module 3:** Imam Malik, the *Muwaṭṭa’*, and the Spread of Maliki Fiqh
* **Module 4:** Being a Muslim in the Modern World
* **Theme:** Building Fiqh Literacy & Faith-Identity Balance

---
#### Teacher Notes:
Assalāmu ‘alaykum wa raḥmatullāhi wa barakātuh. Welcome, respected teacher. This curriculum is designed to guide students through the transition from Prophetic history to the formalization of Islamic law (*Fiqh*), and finally to their own practical lives as modern Muslims. 

This slide deck serves as a visual aid. The core of the learning will come from your narrative delivery, guided by these notes. We will focus specifically on the Maliki school of jurisprudence (*Madhhab*) and the Ash'ari school of theology (*‘Aqīdah*). We aim to build a strong sense of intellectual heritage, illustrating how the practices of the Prophet ﷺ were preserved in Madinah, coded by Imam Malik, carried into Africa, and are lived by us today.

## Slide 2: Course Map & Objective
### Linking History to Living Practice

* **The Prophetic Legacy:** Understanding how the Prophet ﷺ completed his message.
* **The Golden Chain:** How the Companions (*Ṣaḥābah*) passed this light to the next generations.
* **The Madinah Connection:** Why Madinah is the cradle of Maliki Fiqh.
* **Living the Legacy:** Applying these timeless principles to your daily life.

---
#### Teacher Notes:
In this course, we are not just looking at dates and events as dead history. Instead, we are tracing a golden line of transmission. Students should realize that the way we pray, make decisions, and view our creator today is directly connected to the actions of the Prophet ﷺ, preserved by the scholars of Madinah, and structured into a system of jurisprudence. 

Make sure to emphasize that history is the soil from which our identity grows. By understanding how Islam was preserved and how it traveled to different parts of the world—especially North and West Africa—students will see Islam as a global, deeply rooted faith that is fully capable of addressing modern challenges.

## Slide 3: The Conquest of Makkah (*Fatḥ Makkah*)
### The Triumph of Mercy

* **Year of occurrence:** 8th Year of Hijrah.
* **The Entry:** The Prophet ﷺ entered Makkah on his camel, bowing his head in humility (*Tawāḍu‘*), not as a proud conqueror.
* **General Amnesty:** He ﷺ forgave his former enemies who had persecuted Muslims for twenty years.
* **The Saying:** *"Go, for you are free."*
* **Cleansing the Ka‘bah:** Removal of idols while maintaining the sacredness of the Sanctuary.

---
#### Teacher Notes:
We begin the historical narrative in the later years of the Prophet’s ﷺ life. The Conquest of Makkah (*Fatḥ Makkah*) is one of the greatest examples of political and spiritual victory in human history. Explain to the students that instead of seeking revenge against the Quraysh, who had tortured the early Muslims, boycotted them, and fought battles against them, the Prophet ﷺ showed unmatched mercy (*Raḥmah*). 

He quoted Prophet Yusuf (peace be upon him) saying, *"No blame is upon you today."* Point out the character of the Prophet ﷺ—he did not enter Makkah with trumpet blasts and a raised sword; rather, his chin touched the saddle of his camel out of humility and gratitude to Allah. This victory shows that the goal of Islam is the guidance of hearts, not the destruction of people.

---

## Slide 4: The Farewell Pilgrimage (*Ḥajjat al-Widā‘*)
### The Completion of the Message

* **Year:** 10th Year of Hijrah.
* **The Gathering:** Over 100,000 Companions gathered in the plains of ‘Arafāt.
* **The Final Sermon:**
  * Sanctity of life, property, and honor.
  * Prohibition of interest/usury (*Ribā*).
  * Equal status of all races: *"No Arab is superior to a non-Arab..."*
  * Good treatment of women.
* **The Verse of Completion:** Surah al-Mā’idah (5:3) was revealed.

---
#### Teacher Notes:
The Farewell Pilgrimage (*Ḥajjat al-Widā‘*) represents the peak of the Prophetic mission. The Prophet ﷺ delivered a historic speech at Mount ‘Arafāt. Highlight the key points of the sermon: the equality of human beings based on God-consciousness (*Taqwā*), the protection of human rights, and the elimination of pre-Islamic injustices such as tribalism and usury (*Ribā*). 

During this pilgrimage, Allah revealed the verse: *"Today I have perfected for you your religion and completed My favor upon you and have approved for you Islam as religion."* Explain to the students that this verse marked the completion of the revelation of rules, indicating that the Prophet's ﷺ time on Earth was drawing to a close.

---

## Slide 5: The Passing of the Messenger ﷺ
### The Loss of the Prophet and the Transition

* **Date:** 12th of Rabī‘ al-Awwal, 11th Year of Hijrah.
* **The Crisis:** The intense grief of the Companions (*Ṣaḥābah*).
* **Abu Bakr's Leadership:** Reassuring the community using Quranic verses.
* **The Eternal Truth:** Surah Al ‘Imran (3:144): *"Muhammad is not but a messenger. [Other] messengers have passed on before him."*
* **Preserving the Mission:** The physical presence of the Prophet ﷺ ended, but his guidance remains eternal.

---
#### Teacher Notes:
[Creed Focus: Ash'ari School]
In Ash'ari creed, we affirm that the Prophets of Allah are alive in their graves in a state of the *Barzakh* (the intermediate realm), praying and experiencing a spiritual life that suits their noble status, which is different from normal deceased people. However, their earthly life has ended, and their legal and physical leadership transitioned to the community.

Discuss the emotional weight of this event. The Companions were devastated; Sayyiduna ‘Umar could not believe it. Sayyiduna Abu Bakr showed immense stability. He stepped forward and delivered the famous statement: *"Whoever worshipped Muhammad, know that Muhammad has passed away. But whoever worships Allah, know that Allah is Ever-Living and never dies."* This lesson highlights that our worship is for Allah alone, and the Prophet ﷺ successfully delivered the message.

## Slide 6: The Prophetic Legacy
### Inheritance of Knowledge (*Warathat al-Anbiyā’*)

* **Prophets leave no wealth:** They do not leave behind gold or silver coins (*Dīnār* or *Dirham*).
* **The Real Inheritance:** Divine Knowledge (*‘Ilm*).
* **Who are the heirs?** The scholars (*‘Ulamā’*) who preserve and practice this knowledge.
* **The Core Legacy:**
  1. The Holy Quran (The Word of Allah).
  2. The Sunnah (The Prophetic Model).
  3. The Love of his Family (*Ahl al-Bayt*) and respect for his Companions (*Ṣaḥābah*).

---
#### Teacher Notes:
Explain the Hadith: *"The scholars are the heirs of the Prophets."* Make the students realize that knowledge is a sacred trust. When the Prophet ﷺ left this world, he did not leave a physical kingdom for his family to inherit. Rather, he left the ultimate gift: sacred knowledge. 

Highlight that this legacy must be handled with care. The preservation of the Sunnah was not a random project; it was a meticulous, scientific effort carried out by the Companions and the generations that followed them. Underline that loving the Prophet's family (*Ahl al-Bayt*) and respecting all his Companions (*Ṣaḥābah*) are key markers of the creed of Ahl al-Sunnah wal-Jama'ah.

---

## Slide 7: What is the Caliphate? (*Khilāfah*)
### The Rightly Guided Caliphs (*Al-Khulafā’ al-Rāshidūn*)

* **Definition:** *Khilāfah* means succession or stewardship of the community.
* **The Period:** The first four leaders after the Prophet ﷺ.
* **The Title:** "Rightly Guided" (*Rāshidūn*) because they followed the sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ closely in leadership.
* **Main Achievements:**
  * Maintaining unity during crises.
  * Preservation and compilation of the Quran.
  * Administration of justice and establishing public services.

---
#### Teacher Notes:
Define the word *Khilāfah* literally as "succession." The Caliph (*Khalīfah*) does not receive divine revelation—revelation ended with the Prophet ﷺ. Rather, the Caliph's job is to protect the religion and manage the worldly affairs of the community according to Islamic values. 

Introduce the term *Al-Khulafā’ al-Rāshidūn*. Explain that the Prophet ﷺ praised this era, stating that we should hold fast to their path. They faced unique, unprecedented issues and solved them using mutual consultation (*Shūrā*), showing us how to apply Islamic principles as the world changes.

---

## Slide 8: Caliph 1: Abu Bakr al-Siddiq (11–13 AH)
### The Pillar of Stability

* **Title:** *Al-Ṣiddīq* (The Truthful / The Affirmer of Truth).
* **Major Crises Handled:**
  * Grief over the Prophet's passing.
  * The Wars of Apostasy (*Riddah*): Groups refusing to pay Zakah or claiming fake prophethood.
* **Quran Preservation:** He ordered the first collection of the Quran into a single volume (*Ṣuḥuf*) under the guidance of Zayd ibn Thābit.
* **Character:** Soft-hearted yet incredibly firm in defending the faith.

---
#### Teacher Notes:
Sayyiduna Abu Bakr was the closest friend of the Prophet ﷺ. When the Prophet ﷺ passed away, Abu Bakr stood as the pillar of the community. Some Arab tribes thought that Zakah was only due to the Prophet ﷺ and refused to pay it to Abu Bakr. Others followed false prophets like Musaylimah. 

[Fiqh Focus: Maliki School]
In the Maliki school, we learn that Zakah is a compulsory social duty, not just a private charity. Abu Bakr’s stance proves that the state must ensure the financial rights of the poor are protected. Explain how he initiated the collection of the Quran after many Quran memorizers (*Huffāẓ*) were martyred in the battle of Yamamah, safeguarding the text for all future generations.

---

## Slide 9: Caliph 2: ‘Umar ibn al-Khattab (13–23 AH)
### The Architect of Justice

* **Title:** *Al-Fārūq* (The Distinguisher between Right and Wrong).
* **Administrative Genius:**
  * Established the Islamic Hijri calendar starting from the Migration (*Hijrah*).
  * Created ministries, departments (*Dīwān*), a postal system, and public treasuries (*Bayt al-Māl*).
* **Justice:** Known for his strict adherence to justice, refusing any favoritism.
* **Expansion:** Jerusalem opened peacefully under his rule, granting safety to the Christian population through the Covenant of ‘Umar.

---
#### Teacher Notes:
Sayyiduna ‘Umar was a visionary leader. He took the growing Muslim community and organized it into a fully functioning state. He established the Hijri calendar, choosing the Migration (*Hijrah*) as the starting point rather than the birth of the Prophet ﷺ, highlighting that Islam is defined by action and community movement. 

Focus on his opening of Jerusalem. He entered the city walking beside his servant, who was riding the camel because it was the servant’s turn on their travel schedule. He signed a peace treaty ensuring that Christian churches and lives were protected. This demonstrates that Islamic law values religious freedom and human dignity.

---

## Slide 10: Caliph 3: ‘Uthman ibn ‘Affan (23–35 AH)
### The Promoter of Unity

* **Title:** *Dhū al-Nūrayn* (Possessor of the Two Lights, having married two daughters of the Prophet ﷺ).
* **Major Achievement:** The Compilation of the Quran (*Al-Muṣḥaf al-‘Uthmānī*).
  * Standardized the pronunciation and script to prevent disputes.
  * Sent certified copies to all major Muslim provinces.
* **Generosity:** Funded the expansion of the Prophet's Mosque and bought the Well of Rūmah for public use.
* **Patience:** Faced rebellion with dignity, refusing to shed Muslim blood to defend himself.

---
#### Teacher Notes:
Sayyiduna ‘Uthman was famous for his modesty (*Ḥayā’*) and immense generosity. The biggest challenge during his rule was that as non-Arabs entered Islam, they began reciting the Quran with different regional accents, leading to arguments about which recitation was correct. 

‘Uthman acted decisively. He collected the original pages compiled by Abu Bakr and ordered copies to be written in the dialect of the Quraysh. This standard edition is called the *Muṣḥaf*. Emphasize his ultimate sacrifice: when rebels surrounded his house, he refused to let the Companions fight them, wanting to avoid bloodshed in the city of the Prophet ﷺ. He was martyred while reading the Quran.

---

## Slide 11: Caliph 4: ‘Ali ibn Abi Talib (35–40 AH)
### The Gate of Knowledge

* **Relationship:** Cousin and son-in-law of the Prophet ﷺ (married to Sayyidah Fāṭimah).
* **Key Attributes:** Famous for his bravery, wisdom, and deep knowledge of jurisprudence.
* **Rule:** A period of internal trial and tribulation (*Fitnah*).
* **Major Focus:**
  * Re-establishing law and order.
  * Fighting corruption and insisting on complete integrity in government.
* **Legacy:** His rulings in judicial matters serve as major foundations for Islamic law.

---
#### Teacher Notes:
[Creed Focus: Ash'ari School]
In Sunni theology (Ash'ari creed), we respect all the Companions (*Ṣaḥābah*). We hold that the conflicts between them (such as the battles of Jamal and Siffin) were based on differences in legal reasoning (*Ijtihād*). We do not curse or speak ill of any of them. Sayyiduna ‘Ali was correct in his political position, and those who opposed him were mistaken in their analysis, but all of them acted out of a desire to do what they thought was best for the Ummah.

Explain to students that Sayyiduna ‘Ali faced the most difficult political climate, yet he maintained high ethical standards. He did not compromise on justice. His deep understanding of the Quran and Sunnah was so respected that the previous Caliphs constantly consulted him on complex legal cases.

---

## Slide 12: The Golden Chain of Madinah
### How Knowledge Was Preserved

* **The Core Idea:** The practice of Islam was passed down visually, not just through texts.
* **The Madinan Legacy:** Madinah was where the Prophet ﷺ lived and died, and where the Quran was put into practice daily.
* **The Transmission Path:**
  1. The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ.
  2. The Companions in Madinah (e.g., Ibn Umar, Aisha, Zayd ibn Thabit).
  3. The Successors (The Seven Fuqaha of Madinah).
  4. Imam Malik ibn Anas.
  5. The Students of Imam Malik (carrying it to North and West Africa).

---
#### Teacher Notes:
How do we know how the Prophet ﷺ prayed, fasted, and lived? It was through this unbroken chain of transmission. This slide introduces the concept of *‘Amal Ahl al-Madīnah* (The Practice of the People of Madinah). 

Explain that when the Prophet ﷺ passed away, thousands of Companions remained in Madinah. They taught their children, who taught their children. This created a living, breathing model of Islam. Imam Malik was born in this environment. He argued that if thousands of people in Madinah did an action generation-after-generation, it was more reliable than a single person reporting a Hadith that contradicted it. This is a unique feature of the Maliki school.

---

## Slide 13: Imam Malik: The Star of the Scholars
### The Scholar of Madinah (93–179 AH)

* **Name:** Mālik ibn Anas ibn Mālik al-Aṣbaḥī.
* **Birthplace:** Madinah (lived and died there, rarely leaving).
* **Title:** *Imām Dār al-Hijrah* (The Leader of the Abode of Migration).
* **Respect for Hadith:**
  * He would take a bath, put on clean clothes, apply perfume, and sit with gravity before teaching Hadith.
* **Intellectual Humility:** Famous for saying *"I do not know"* (*Lā adrī*) to questions he was not certain about.

---
#### Teacher Notes:
Imam Malik is the founder of the Maliki school. He was born in the year 93 AH, meaning he was born during the time of the younger Companions and studied under the Successors (*Tābi‘ūn*). He was highly respected for his memory and intelligence. 

Tell the story of his respect for the words of the Prophet ﷺ: he never rode a horse or camel in Madinah, saying he felt ashamed to trample with animal hooves the soil where the Prophet ﷺ was buried. When asked questions, he was extremely cautious. Once, a man traveled for months to ask him forty questions, and Imam Malik answered "I do not know" to thirty-six of them. Teach the students that saying "I do not know" is a sign of true knowledge and sincerity.

---

## Slide 14: The *Muwaṭṭa’* of Imam Malik
### The First Great Book of Islam

* **Meaning of the title:** "The Approved/Smoothed Path."
* **Nature of the book:** A combined book of Hadith (sayings of the Prophet ﷺ) and Fiqh (legal rulings).
* **Content:**
  * Hadith of the Prophet ﷺ.
  * Sayings of the Companions (*Ṣaḥābah*).
  * Rulings of the Successors (*Tābi‘ūn*).
  * The Practice of Madinah (*‘Amal*).
* **Praise:** Imam al-Shāfi‘ī said: *"There is no book on earth after the Book of Allah that is more correct than the book of Malik."*

---
#### Teacher Notes:
The *Muwaṭṭa’* is a landmark in Islamic history. Before Imam al-Bukhari or Imam Muslim wrote their books of Hadith, Imam Malik compiled the *Muwaṭṭa’*. The Caliph of the time, Abu Ja'far al-Mansur, wanted to force all Muslims to follow the *Muwaṭṭa’* as the law of the land. 

[Fiqh Focus: Maliki School]
Imam Malik refused the Caliph's offer! He said: *"The Companions of the Prophet scattered to different lands, and they carry different practices. Leave the people to what they have chosen."* This shows Imam Malik's respect for diversity of opinion (*Ikhtilāf*) and his refusal to enforce a single legal code by state power.

---

## Slide 15: The Practice of Madinah (*‘Amal Ahl al-Madīnah*)
### The Core of Maliki Jurisprudence

* **Concept:** The daily practice of the people of Madinah.
* **Why it matters:**
  * The Prophet ﷺ lived there for ten years.
  * Thousands of Companions practiced Islam under his supervision.
  * This practice was passed down to the next generation collectively (*Tawātur*).
* **Maliki Rule Example: *Sadl* (Praying with arms at the side).**
  * While other schools hold that crossing the hands is sunnah, the dominant Maliki opinion is to pray with arms hanging naturally, because this was the continuous practice of the scholars of Madinah.

---
#### Teacher Notes:
[Fiqh Focus: Maliki School]
Let’s explain *‘Amal Ahl al-Madīnah* using a modern analogy: if you want to know how a game is played, you don’t just read a rulebook; you watch the people who invented the game play it. The people of Madinah saw the Prophet ﷺ pray every day. 

An example of this is *Sadl* (praying with the arms resting at the sides). In the Maliki school, this is the primary and preferred method in obligatory prayers (*Farā’iḍ*), as recorded in the *Mudawwanah* (the primary text of Maliki rulings). This is because the early scholars of Madinah, including the seven great jurists, did not cross their hands in their obligatory prayers. Point out to students that both methods (*Sadl* and *Qabḍ* - crossing hands) are valid, but they reflect different ways of analyzing the evidence.

---

## Slide 16: The Spread of Maliki Fiqh to North Africa
### The Caravan of Knowledge

* **How did it travel?** Students of Imam Malik returned to their home regions.
* **Key Scholars:**
  * **Ibn al-Qāsim:** Compiled Imam Malik's rulings into the *Mudawwanah*.
  * **Sahnūn:** A Tunisian scholar who organized and spread the school across North Africa.
  * **Asad ibn al-Furāt:** A scholar and military leader who opened Sicily.
* **Centers of Learning:**
  * The University of al-Qarawiyyīn in Fez, Morocco (founded by Fatima al-Fihri).
  * Kairouan in Tunisia.

---
#### Teacher Notes:
Explain how knowledge spreads. It is not through conquest, but through students who travel, study, and return to teach. Students of Imam Malik came from Egypt, North Africa (Ifriqiya), and Al-Andalus (Spain). They sat in his circle, memorized his rulings, and took them back home. 

Mention Fatima al-Fihri, a wealthy Muslim woman who founded the University of al-Qarawiyyīn in Fez, Morocco, in 859 CE. It is recognized by UNESCO as the oldest continuously operating university in the world, and it was a major center for Maliki jurisprudence. This shows the role of women in funding and building Islamic education.

---

## Slide 17: Maliki Fiqh in West Africa
### The Timbuktu Legacy

* **Trade Routes:** Islam spread to West Africa through peaceful trade and scholarly networks, not war.
* **The Empires:** Mali and Songhai Empires embraced the Maliki school.
* **Timbuktu:** A world-famous center of Islamic scholarship with millions of manuscripts.
* **Key Scholars:**
  * **Ahmad Bābā al-Timbuktī:** A great Maliki jurist who wrote over 40 books.
  * **Sheikh Usman dan Fodio:** A West African reformer who established the Sokoto Caliphate based on Maliki law.

---
#### Teacher Notes:
Many students do not know that West Africa has a rich, centuries-old Islamic history. Islam did not spread to West Africa by the sword, but by Muslim traders who traveled across the Sahara Desert. These traders were known for their honesty, which was shaped by Maliki rules of business. 

Timbuktu in modern-day Mali became a global hub of learning. Scholars from all over the Muslim world came to study there. Ahmad Baba of Timbuktu was so famous for his legal verdicts (*Fatāwā*) that scholars in North Africa regularly consulted him. Emphasize that West Africa produced massive libraries of manuscripts covering astronomy, medicine, math, and jurisprudence, all within the Maliki framework.

## Slide 18: Being a Muslim in the Modern World
### Fiqh as a Compass, Not a Cage

* **The Challenge:** Living in a society that doesn't always value faith.
* **The Solution:** Viewing *Fiqh* (understanding of law) as a guide for daily life.
* **Every action has a ruling:**
  * **Compulsory (*Farḍ*):** Five daily prayers, fasting.
  * **Recommended (*Mandūb*):** Smiling, extra charity.
  * **Permissible (*Mubāḥ*):** Sports, studying science.
  * **Disliked (*Makrūh*):** Wasting time, playing harmful games.
  * **Prohibited (*Ḥarām*):** Lying, cheating.

---
#### Teacher Notes:
Transitioning from history to the modern world, we must teach students how to live their faith. Many youth view Islamic law (*Sharī‘ah* and *Fiqh*) as a list of restrictions. We need to reframe this: Fiqh is a compass that helps you navigate life. 

Explain the five categories of human actions (*Al-Aḥkām al-Khamsah*). Show them that the vast majority of things in the world are *Mubāḥ* (permissible). Playing basketball, learning coding, and eating pizza are all permissible and can even become rewarding acts of worship if done with a good intention (*Niyyah*), such as keeping the body healthy or preparing to serve the community.

---

## Slide 19: Balancing Faith, Studies, and Identity
### The Scale of Priorities

* **Faith (*Dīn*):** Your connection to Allah.
* **Life (*Dunyā*):** Your schoolwork, physical health, and family duties.
* **Balancing the Scale:**
  * Pray on time (*Ṣalāt al-Waqt*). Plan your study sessions around your prayers.
  * Intention (*Niyyah*): Turn your school studies into worship by intending to help the Ummah.
  * Good Company (*Ṣuḥbah*): Surrounding yourself with friends who remind you of Allah.

---
#### Teacher Notes:
[Creed Focus: Ash'ari School]
In Ash'ari theology, we understand that everything in the universe happens by the will and power of Allah. However, Allah has given us choices and the ability to acquire our actions (*Kasb*). Therefore, we are responsible for how we manage our time and make decisions.

Teach the students how to schedule their day. Many students complain they don't have time to pray because of homework or sports. Tell them to change their mindset: do not fit prayer into your schedule; fit your schedule around your prayers. If you pray Dhuhr, Asr, and Maghrib on time, your time will be blessed (*Barakah*). Explain that studying biology, math, or history with the intention to benefit humanity is a form of worship.

---

## Slide 20: Faith in Action: Identity and Modesty
### Standing Strong in Your Identity

* **Modesty (*Ḥayā’*):** A core character trait of a Muslim.
* **Social Pressures:** Dealing with peer pressure, social media, and trends that contradict Islamic values.
* **The Prophet's Model:** Being kind, confident, and polite, without compromising on principles.
* **Proud but Humble:** Representing Islam through excellent character (*Akhlāq*).

---
#### Teacher Notes:
Modesty (*Ḥayā’*) is not just about clothing; it is a state of the heart that prevents a person from doing bad deeds. In the modern world, youth are bombarded with pressure to conform to un-Islamic values on social media, in school, and in entertainment. 

Teach them that a Muslim does not follow trends blindly. A Muslim is a leader, not a follower of fashion or online challenges that lack modesty or safety. Use the concept of *Akhlāq* (excellent character): the best way to defend your identity is to be the most honest, kindest, and most helpful student in your school. This is how the early merchants spread Islam in West Africa, and it is how we must live today.

---

## Slide 21: Conclusion & Reflection
### Your Legacy Starts Today

* **Review:**
  * The Prophet ﷺ completed his mission and left an inheritance of knowledge.
  * The Caliphs protected the community and compiled the Quran.
  * Imam Malik coded the living practice of Madinah.
  * Scholars carried this legacy across continents into Africa and then to us.
* **Your Part:** How will you preserve and pass on this legacy in your own life?

---
#### Teacher Notes:
Summarize the journey we have taken. We started in Makkah and Madinah, watched the message stabilize under the Rightly Guided Caliphs, saw it coded into jurisprudence by Imam Malik, watched it travel through the sands of the Sahara to West Africa, and finally brought it into our own classrooms and homes today. 

End the lesson by challenging the students: this legacy has survived for over 1,400 years and has been passed down by millions of scholars, teachers, and parents. Now, the baton is in their hands. What will they do with this inheritance of knowledge?
