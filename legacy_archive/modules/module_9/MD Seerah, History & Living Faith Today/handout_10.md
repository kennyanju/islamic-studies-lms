## Welcome to Your Journey!
Dear student, welcome! This handout is designed for you. In these pages, we will explore some of the most exciting and important parts of our history. We will travel back in time to the final years of the Prophet Muhammad’s ﷺ life, meet the four amazing leaders who took care of the Muslim community after him, discover a famous scholar named Imam Malik who lived in the city of Madinah, and find out how his teachings traveled across the desert into Africa. Finally, we will talk about how you can be a strong, confident Muslim right now in the modern world, balancing your school, your family, and your prayers.

Let’s begin this journey of knowledge!

---

## Chapter 1: The Sunset of Prophethood and the Eternal Legacy

### 1.1 The Conquest of Makkah (*Fatḥ Makkah*)
Imagine being forced to leave your home town, being treated badly for years, and then returning to that same city years later as a strong victor. How would you treat the people who hurt you?

In the 8th year of the Hijrah (the Migration), the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ returned to the city of Makkah. He did not enter like a proud king. Instead, he rode his camel with his head bowed so low in humility (*Tawāḍu‘*) that his chin almost touched the saddle. He was thanking Allah for His help.

When he stood before the chiefs of Quraysh—the very people who had fought against him, boycotted his family, and tried to harm the Muslims—they were terrified. They thought he would punish them. But the Prophet ﷺ looked at them and asked: *"What do you think I am going to do to you?"* They answered: *"You are a noble brother and the son of a noble brother."*

The Prophet ﷺ smiled and said: *"Go, for you are free."* 

This is known as the Conquest of Makkah (*Fatḥ Makkah*). It teaches us that Islam values mercy (*Raḥmah*) and forgiveness over revenge.

---
> ### Vocabulary Box:
> * **Tawāḍu‘** (تَوَاضُع): Humility. Showing humbleness before Allah and not acting better than other people.
> * **Raḥmah** (رَحْمَة): Mercy. Having a soft heart and showing kindness to others.
---

### 1.2 The Farewell Pilgrimage (*Ḥajjat al-Widā‘*)
Two years later, in the 10th year of the Hijrah, the Prophet ﷺ performed his last pilgrimage to Makkah. It is called the Farewell Pilgrimage (*Ḥajjat al-Widā‘*). Over 100,000 Muslims joined him. 

Standing on the plains of Mount ‘Arafāt, the Prophet ﷺ gave a beautiful and famous speech. He told us:
* Our lives, our money, and our honor are sacred. We must never hurt each other.
* We must treat women with kindness and respect.
* All human beings are equal. He said: *"An Arab is not better than a non-Arab, and a non-Arab is not better than an Arab. A white person is not better than a black person, and a black person is not better than a white person—except by God-consciousness (*Taqwā*)."*

During this journey, Allah sent down the final major verse of the Quran: 
> *"Today I have perfected for you your religion and completed My favor upon you and have approved for you Islam as religion."* (Surah al-Mā’idah, 5:3)

---

### 1.3 The Passing of the Prophet ﷺ and His Legacy
In the 11th year of the Hijrah, on the 12th day of Rabī‘ al-Awwal, the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ passed away. It was the saddest day in the history of Madinah. The Companions (*Ṣaḥābah*) were heartbroken. Some could not believe he was gone. 

His closest friend, Abu Bakr al-Siddiq, stood up and reminded everyone of a very important lesson. He read this verse from the Quran: 
> *"Muhammad is not but a messenger. [Other] messengers have passed on before him. So if he was to die or be killed, would you turn back on your heels?"* (Surah Al ‘Imran, 3:144)

Abu Bakr reminded the people: *"Whoever worshipped Muhammad, know that Muhammad has passed away. But whoever worships Allah, know that Allah is Ever-Living and never dies."*

---
> **[Creed Focus: Ash'ari School]**
> As Sunni Muslims, we believe that the Prophet ﷺ completed his work perfectly. Even though his earthly life ended, his teachings and guidance are preserved forever. In our creed, we believe the Prophets are given a special, noble spiritual life in their graves, but their work of bringing new rules is finished because the Quran is complete.
---

The Prophet ﷺ left behind no money, gold, or kingdoms. Instead, he left behind the greatest inheritance: **Knowledge (*‘Ilm*)**. The Prophet ﷺ said: *"The scholars are the heirs of the Prophets."* 

His legacy consists of:
1. **The Holy Quran:** The direct word of Allah.
2. **The Sunnah:** The living example of how the Prophet ﷺ acted, spoke, and behaved.
3. **His Family (*Ahl al-Bayt*) and Companions (*Ṣaḥābah*):** Whom we must love and respect.

---

## Chapter 2: The Four Rightly Guided Caliphs (*Al-Khulafā’ al-Rāshidūn*)

After the Prophet ﷺ passed away, the Muslim community needed a leader to keep them united. This leader was called a **Caliph (*Khalīfah*)**, which means "successor" or "steward." The first four leaders are called the "Rightly Guided Caliphs" (*Al-Khulafā’ al-Rāshidūn*) because they followed the path of the Prophet ﷺ very closely.

```
       ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
       │         THE FOUR RIGHTLY GUIDED CALIPHS                │
       ├──────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────┬───────────┤
       │   Abu Bakr   │    'Umar     │   'Uthman    │    'Ali   │
       │  al-Siddiq   │  ibn al-Khattab│ ibn 'Affan  │ ibn Abi Talib│
       │  (11-13 AH)  │  (13-23 AH)  │  (23-35 AH)  │ (35-40 AH)│
       └──────────────┴──────────────┴──────────────┴───────────┘
```

### 2.1 Abu Bakr al-Siddiq: The Pillar of Strength
Abu Bakr was the Prophet's ﷺ closest friend and was chosen by the Muslims to be the first Caliph. He was known for being gentle and soft-hearted, but when the community faced danger, he became as firm as a mountain.

* **What he did:** 
  * Some tribes refused to pay Zakah (the obligatory charity for the poor). Abu Bakr insisted that Zakah must be paid, because protecting the rights of the poor is a core part of Islam.
  * **Preserving the Quran:** During a battle, many Muslims who had memorized the Quran were killed. Abu Bakr realized we needed to compile the Quran into a single book so it would never be lost. He ordered the scribe Zayd ibn Thābit to gather the written pieces of the Quran and check them carefully.

---
> **[Fiqh Focus: Maliki School]**
> In the Maliki school, Zakah is not just a personal gift you give when you feel like it. It is a strict financial duty. If a person refuses to pay Zakah, they are breaking an essential rule of Islamic community life. Abu Bakr’s actions showed that Zakah is a fundamental pillar of our society.
---

### 2.2 ‘Umar ibn al-Khattab: The Fair Leader
‘Umar was the second Caliph. He was famous for his strict justice (*‘Adl*). He was so good at separating right from wrong that the Prophet ﷺ gave him the title *Al-Fārūq* (The Distinguisher).

* **What he did:**
  * He created the **Hijri Calendar** starting from the year the Prophet ﷺ migrated to Madinah.
  * He set up departments for the government, including a public treasury called the **Bayt al-Māl** to help orphans, widows, and the poor.
  * **The Story of Jerusalem:** When the city of Jerusalem opened to Muslims, ‘Umar traveled to sign the peace treaty. He shared a camel with his servant, taking turns to ride. When they entered the city, it was the servant’s turn to ride, so the Caliph ‘Umar walked on foot, holding the camel's reins! He signed the Covenant of ‘Umar, guaranteeing that Christians could practice their religion safely in their churches.

---

### 2.3 ‘Uthman ibn ‘Affan: The Generous Helper
‘Uthman was the third Caliph. He was a very wealthy businessman who spent almost all his money helping Islam. He was incredibly shy and modest (*Ḥayā’*).

* **What he did:**
  * **Standardizing the Quran:** As the Muslim lands grew, people from different countries began pronouncing the Arabic words of the Quran differently. ‘Uthman ordered that a master copy of the Quran be written down in the exact dialect of Makkah. He sent copies of this book (called the *Muṣḥaf*) to all the provinces. This is the exact Quran we read today!
  * **Patience in Trouble:** When troublemakers surrounded his house and threatened him, ‘Uthman refused to let his guards fight them. He did not want to cause bloodshed in the city of the Prophet ﷺ. He was martyred while reading the Quran.

---

### 2.4 ‘Ali ibn Abi Talib: The Gate of Wisdom
‘Ali was the cousin and son-in-law of the Prophet ﷺ (married to the Prophet's daughter, Fatima). He was the fourth Caliph and was famous for his bravery, deep knowledge, and wise rulings.

* **What he did:**
  * He ruled during a difficult time when there was division in the community.
  * He worked hard to restore justice, fight corruption, and ensure that government officials were honest.
  * Whenever the other Caliphs had a difficult legal question, they would ask ‘Ali because of his incredible understanding of Islamic law.

---
> **[Creed Focus: Ash'ari School]**
> Sometimes you might hear about disputes or wars that happened between the Companions during this time. In our creed, we respect **all** the Companions. We believe they were all sincere and trying their best to make the right choices for the community. We do not speak badly of any Companion of the Prophet ﷺ.

## Chapter 3: Imam Malik and the Journey of Fiqh

Have you ever wondered how scholars decide what is Halal (allowed) and Haram (forbidden)? This is called **Fiqh**, which means "deep understanding" of Islamic rules. To help us understand Fiqh, we have schools of law called **Madhhabs**. One of the most famous Madhhabs is the **Maliki school**.

### 3.1 The Star of the Scholars
Imam Malik ibn Anas was born in Madinah in the year 93 AH. He lived almost his entire life in the city where the Prophet ﷺ lived and died. He was called the *Imām Dār al-Hijrah* (The Imam of the Abode of Migration).

Imam Malik had immense love for the Prophet ﷺ. 
* He would never ride a horse or camel in Madinah, saying: *"I feel ashamed before Allah to ride an animal on the soil where the Messenger of Allah ﷺ is buried."*
* When someone came to ask him about a Hadith, he would take a bath (*Ghusl*), put on his best clothes, apply perfume, and sit with great respect before speaking.
* If he was not sure about a question, he would simply say: *"I do not know."* He taught his students that saying "I do not know" is a sign of true wisdom.

---

### 3.2 The *Muwaṭṭa’*: The Approved Path
Imam Malik wrote one of the first major books in Islamic history, called the **Muwaṭṭa’**. The name means "The Approved Path." 

This book contains:
* Sayings of the Prophet ﷺ (Hadith).
* Rulings of the Companions.
* The living practice of the people of Madinah (*‘Amal*).

Imam al-Shāfi‘ī said about it: *"There is no book on earth after the Book of Allah that is more correct than the book of Malik."*

---
> **[Fiqh Focus: Maliki School - The Practice of Madinah]**
> A unique rule of the Maliki school is **‘Amal Ahl al-Madīnah** (The Practice of the People of Madinah). Imam Malik argued that since thousands of Companions lived and died in Madinah, the general actions of the city's scholars were like a living Sunnah. 
> 
> For example: In the Maliki school, the primary way to pray obligatory prayers is with your arms resting naturally at your sides (*Sadl*), rather than folded over your chest. This is because Imam Malik observed that the major scholars of Madinah prayed this way. While both folding and resting the arms are valid, this shows how Fiqh decisions are based on different paths of evidence.

### 3.3 How Maliki Fiqh Traveled to Africa
How did Imam Malik’s teachings leave Madinah and travel thousands of miles? 

1. **The Caravans of Students:** Students traveled from Egypt, North Africa (Tunisia, Morocco), and Spain (Al-Andalus) to study with Imam Malik in Madinah. When they returned home, they taught what they had learned.
2. **Fatima al-Fihri & al-Qarawiyyīn:** In Morocco, a wealthy Muslim woman named Fatima al-Fihri used her inheritance to build a mosque and university called **al-Qarawiyyīn** in the year 859 CE. It became a global center for studying Maliki Fiqh.
3. **The Trade Routes to West Africa:** Muslim merchants traveled across the Sahara Desert to West Africa (countries like Mali, Senegal, and Nigeria). Because these merchants were honest, fair, and followed Maliki rules of business, the local kings and citizens embraced Islam.
4. **Timbuktu:** The city of Timbuktu in Mali became a legendary center of Islamic knowledge, producing thousands of hand-written books on Fiqh, math, and science. A famous Maliki scholar from there was Ahmad Baba, who wrote many books on law.

## Chapter 4: Being a Muslim Kid in the Modern World

Now that we have looked at our history, let's talk about **you**. How do you live as a Muslim today? You have school, sports, games, friends, and family. Sometimes, it can feel like a lot to balance.

### 4.1 Fiqh: Your Personal Compass
Think of Fiqh not as a list of "do nots," but as a guide or compass for your life. In Islam, every single action we do fits into one of five categories. This is called **Al-Aḥkām al-Khamsah**:

1. **Compulsory (*Farḍ*):** You must do it, and you get rewarded. If you skip it, you are committing a sin (e.g., the five daily prayers).
2. **Recommended (*Mandūb*):** You get rewarded if you do it, but there is no sin if you don't (e.g., using a miswak, smiling, or helping a neighbor).
3. **Permissible (*Mubāḥ*):** Neutral actions. You can do them or not. Most things in life are here (e.g., playing football, eating apples, studying science).
4. **Disliked (*Makrūh*):** It is better not to do it, but it is not a sin (e.g., wasting water while making Wudu, playing games all day).
5. **Forbidden (*Ḥarām*):** You must avoid it. Doing it is a sin (e.g., lying, stealing, cheating in exams).

*Tip:* You can turn a *Mubāḥ* (permissible) action into an act of worship by having a good intention (*Niyyah*). For example, if you eat dinner with the intention of gaining energy to pray, your dinner becomes a rewardable action!

---

### 4.2 The Balance: Dīn and Dunyā
* **Dīn** (دِين): Your religion and relationship with Allah.
* **Dunyā** (دُنْيَا): This worldly life, including your school, hobbies, and career.

As a Muslim, you do not have to choose between them! You can be a top student in your class and a practicing Muslim at the same time. 

Here is how:
* **The Prayer Pivot:** Never say, *"I will pray when I finish my homework."* Instead, plan your homework sessions around your prayers. If you pray Asr on time, take a break, do your homework, and then pray Maghrib, you will find that Allah puts **Barakah** (blessing) in your time.
* **Good Friends (*Ṣuḥbah*):** The Prophet ﷺ told us that we are like the friends we keep. If you hang out with people who are honest, kind, and pray, it will be easy for you to do the same. If your friends use bad words or skip prayers, it will eventually affect you.
* **Modesty (*Ḥayā’*):** Being proud of who you are. You don't need to fit into trends that go against Islamic values just to look "cool." True confidence comes from knowing you are trying to please Allah.

## Chapter 5: Reflection and Actions

### Summary of What We Learned:
1. **Mercy is Victory:** The Conquest of Makkah proved that forgiveness is stronger than anger.
2. **The Chain of Knowledge:** The Quran and Sunnah were preserved by the Caliphs and scholars, reaching us through an unbroken chain.
3. **Living Practice:** Imam Malik showed us that the daily practice of Madinah (*‘Amal*) is a key source of guidance.
4. **Active Faith:** Being a Muslim today means using Fiqh as a compass to balance your studies (*Dunyā*) and your faith (*Dīn*).
