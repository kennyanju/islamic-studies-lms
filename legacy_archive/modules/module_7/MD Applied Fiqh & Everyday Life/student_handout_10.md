# Everyday Fiqh: A Guide to Halal, Manners, and Sacred Journeys
## Student Handout (Age 10)

Welcome to the exciting study of **Applied Fiqh**! Fiqh means "deep understanding," and *Applied Fiqh* is about taking that understanding and putting it into action. This book is all about how you can turn your daily actions—like eating a snack, choosing what to wear, greeting your friends, and buying toys—into acts of worship that make Allah happy. 

In this book, we will explore the rules of daily life according to the **Maliki school of Islamic law** (*Madhhab*) and the **Ash'ari school of belief** (*‘Aqīdah*). Let’s begin our journey of learning together!

---

## Chapter 1: The Circle of Halal and Haram

Imagine you are playing in a beautiful, green park. The park is vast and open, full of swings, slides, and trees. But at the edge of the park, there is a fence with a sign that says: *"Warning: Deep Valley. Do Not Cross."* 

Why is the fence there? Is it to keep you trapped? No! The fence is there to protect you from falling and getting hurt. 

In Islam, we have a similar boundary:
*   ***Ḥalāl***: Permissible things. This is the big, open park. Most things in life are halal.
*   ***Ḥarām***: Forbidden things. This is the danger zone beyond the fence. Allah forbids these things only because they are bad for our bodies, minds, or souls.

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ gave us a beautiful example. He said that a person who hangs around the edges of the forbidden zone is like a shepherd who grazes his sheep right next to a private royal garden. It is very easy for a sheep to wander across the border by accident! To stay safe, we should stay well inside the *Ḥalāl* area.

### Transforming Your Daily Choices
Did you know that you can get reward (*Thawāb*) from Allah just for eating an apple or taking a nap? In Ash'ari creed, we learn that Allah is the Creator of all actions. If you make a good intention (*Niyyah*) in your heart before a normal activity, Allah turns that normal activity into worship!
*   **Normal eating**: Just filling your stomach. (No reward, but not a sin).
*   **Worship eating**: Eating a halal apple with the intention: *"I am eating this to give my body strength so I can pray Salah and help my parents."* (You get reward!).

### The Fiqh of Meat (*Dhakāh*)
When we eat chicken, beef, or lamb, the animal must be prepared in a clean and humane way called *Dhakāh* (ritual slaughter). The slaughterer must use a very sharp knife so the animal does not suffer, and they must cut the windpipe (*Ḥulqūm*), the gullet (*Marī’*), and the two jugular veins (*Wadajayn*). They must also say *Bismillāh* (In the name of Allah) as they do it.

> [!NOTE] **Maliki Ruling: Forgetting the Name of Allah**
> In the Maliki school, saying *Bismillāh* during slaughter is very important. 
> *   If a person remembers to say it but chooses not to, the meat becomes *Ḥarām* and we cannot eat it.
> *   But if the person is a Muslim and simply forgets (*Nisyān*), Allah excuses them! The meat is still *Ḥalāl* and clean to eat. This is a special mercy in the Maliki school.

### The Bounty of the Sea
What about fish, crabs, and shrimp? Do they need to be slaughtered this way? No! 
The Prophet ﷺ told us that the water of the sea is pure and its dead creatures are lawful to eat.

> [!NOTE] **Maliki Ruling: Everything from the Sea is Halal!**
> The Maliki school is very generous when it comes to seafood. According to Imam Malik, *every single animal* that lives entirely in the water is *Ḥalāl* to eat. It does not matter what shape it has, whether it is a fish, a crab, a lobster, a squid, or even a creature nicknamed "sea pig" (dolphin/porpoise). They are all gifts from Allah for us to eat, without any special slaughter required!

## Chapter 2: The Gift of Modesty (*Ḥayā’*)

Have you ever done something good and felt a warm, quiet feeling of peace? Or have you ever thought about doing something wrong and felt a sudden hesitation, like a soft voice in your heart telling you to stop? That beautiful feeling of restraint is called ***Ḥayā’*** (modesty or spiritual shyness).

The Prophet ﷺ said: *"Modesty is a branch of faith."* 
Modesty is not about feeling bad about ourselves. It is about realizing that Allah is *Al-Raqīb* (The All-Watchful) and *Al-Baṣīr* (The All-Seeing). Because we love Allah and know He is watching us, we want to behave, speak, and dress in a way that shows respect to Him.

### Modesty in Dress: What is the *‘Awrah*?
To help us practice modesty, Allah has defined the ***‘Awrah***—the parts of the body that we must keep covered in front of people who are not our close family members (non-mahrams) and during our prayers (*Ṣalāh*).

#### For Boys:
A boy's basic *‘Awrah* is the area between the navel (*Surrah*) and the knee (*Rukbah*). 

> [!NOTE] **Maliki Ruling: The Thigh**
> In the Maliki school, the navel and the knee themselves are not part of the *‘Awrah*. However, the thigh is classified as *‘Awrah Khafīfah* (light awrah). While the most private parts must always be covered, exposing the thigh in public is still strongly disliked (*Makrūh*) and considered bad manners. Boys should always wear clothing that covers past their knees to maintain true *Ḥayā’*.

#### For Girls:
In front of unrelated men and during prayer, a girl’s *‘Awrah* is her entire body, except for her face and her hands (from the wrists down). Her clothing should be loose (not tight) and thick (not see-through).

> [!NOTE] **Maliki Ruling: Uncovering the Feet in Prayer**
> If a girl is praying Salah and her feet accidentally become uncovered, what happens? In the Maliki school, the feet are considered part of the *‘Awrah Khafīfah* (light awrah). If her feet are exposed, her prayer is still valid and she does not have to restart it. However, it is disliked (*Makrūh*) to pray with uncovered feet. If she notices it, she should cover them, and if she finishes her prayer with uncovered feet, it is recommended (*Mustaḥabb*) for her to repeat that prayer before its time limit ends. This shows the balance between ease and striving for the best!

## Chapter 3: Sunnah Habits: Transforming Daily Manners

The Prophet ﷺ was sent to teach us the best of character. Fiqh helps us take the beautiful manners (*Adab*) of the Prophet ﷺ and turn them into daily habits. Let’s look at three key areas of daily manners.

### 1. Manners of Eating
When we sit down to eat, we are receiving a direct gift from Allah. We should behave like grateful guests at Allah’s table:
*   **Wash your hands** before eating to keep clean.
*   **Say *Bismillāh*** (In the name of Allah) out loud. This stops Shaytan from eating your food with you!
*   **Eat with your right hand**. The Prophet ﷺ taught us that the left hand is used for cleaning ourselves, while the right hand is for eating and clean tasks.
*   **Eat what is in front of you**. Don't reach across the plate to grab the best piece; show content (*Qanā‘ah*) with what is nearby.
*   **Say *Alḥamdulillāh*** (Praise be to Allah) when you finish.

### 2. Spreading the Greeting of Peace (*Salām*)
The greeting of Islam is a prayer: *As-Salāmu ‘Alaykum wa Raḥmatullāhi wa Barakātuh* (Peace, mercy, and blessings of Allah be upon you).
*   **The Rule:** Saying *Salām* first is a highly recommended action (*Sunnah Mu’akkadah*). Replying to a *Salām* is an obligation (*Farḍ*). If someone says *Salām* to you, you *must* say it back!
*   **The Order:** To keep things orderly, the Prophet ﷺ taught us:
    1.  The younger person should greet the older person first out of respect.
    2.  The person walking should greet the person sitting down.
    3.  A small group of people should greet a larger group.

### 3. Entering Homes (*Isti’dhān*)
When you visit a friend’s house, you must seek permission (*Isti’dhān*) before entering. 
*   **Knock up to three times**: Knock or ring the doorbell once, then wait. Knock a second time, and wait. Knock a third time. If no one answers after the third knock, you must turn around and walk away without getting upset.
*   **Stand to the side**: Do not stand directly in front of the open doorway. Stand to the left or right, so that if the door opens, you do not peer inside before the family is ready to welcome you.
*   **Say your name**: If someone inside calls out, *"Who is it?"*, do not just reply: *"It's me!"* Say your name clearly: *"It is Yusuf"* or *"It is Aminah."*

## Chapter 4: Fiqh of the Marketplace

Have you ever bought a snack at the corner store, or traded game cards with a friend? Every time you buy, sell, or trade, you are entering the world of business transactions (*Mu‘āmalāt*). 

Islam is so complete that it has rules for how we trade. A Muslim merchant must be known for two things: **Honesty** and **Fairness**.

### 1. Disclosing Defects
The Prophet ﷺ passed by a food seller and put his hand into a pile of grain. He felt wetness underneath. He asked, *"What is this?"* The seller said, *"It was rained on, O Messenger of Allah."* The Prophet ﷺ replied: *"Why didn't you put the wet grain on top so people could see it? Whoever deceives us is not of us."*

If you are selling a toy or a card, and it has a scratch or a missing piece, you *must* tell the buyer before they give you their money. Hiding a defect is a sin, and the money you make from it is not blessed (*Barakah*).

### 2. Fair Weights and Measures
When you buy a pound of candy, the scale must be accurate. Allah warns us in the Quran about people who demand a full measure when they are buying, but give less when they are selling. We must always be fair and honest.

### 3. When is the Deal Final?
Imagine you walk into a store, pick up a book, pay the clerk, and get your receipt. A few seconds later, while still standing in the store, you change your mind. Can you demand your money back just because you are still in the shop?

> [!NOTE] **Maliki Ruling: No "Option of the Session"**
> In some schools of law, you have a right to cancel a deal as long as you and the seller are still standing in the same room. This is called *Khiyār al-Majlis* (option of the session).
> However, **the Maliki school rejects this**. According to the Maliki school, once the offer and acceptance are made (you say "I'll buy this" and they agree and take the money), the contract is locked and final (*Lāzim*). You cannot force them to refund you just because you haven't walked out the door yet. This is based on the practice of the scholars of Madinah (*‘Amal Ahl al-Madīnah*), who valued clarity and immediate trust in business. (Of course, a nice seller can choose to refund you as a favor, but it is not a legal right!).

## Chapter 5: Hajj and Umrah — The Sacred Journey

Have you ever seen pictures of the big, black cube draped in gold-embroidered silk, surrounded by thousands of people dressed in white? That is the **Ka'bah** in the city of **Makkah**, the most sacred place on Earth. 

Every Muslim hopes to make the journey there at least once in their life.
*   ***Ḥajj***: The major pilgrimage that takes place only during specific days in the month of *Dhū al-Ḥijjah*. Hajj is one of the Five Pillars of Islam.
*   ***‘Umrah***: The minor pilgrimage that can be done at any time of the year.

### Who Must Go? (*Istiṭā‘ah*)
Hajj is only obligatory for a Muslim who has capability (*Istiṭā‘ah*). This means they are healthy enough to travel, the roads are safe, and they have enough money to travel and pay for their family's needs while they are gone.

> [!NOTE] **Maliki Ruling: Go as Soon as You Can!**
> In the Maliki school, Hajj is an immediate obligation (*‘Alā al-Fawr*). This means that if you grow up, become an adult, and have the money and health to go to Hajj, you must go during the next Hajj season. You are not allowed to delay it to a later year just because you want to wait until you are older. Delaying it without a good reason is considered a sin.

### Entering the Sacred State: *Iḥrām*
To start Hajj or Umrah, you must enter a state of special rules called ***Iḥrām***. 
*   **For boys**: You must change out of your normal clothes and wear two simple pieces of unstitched white cloth. You cannot cover your head with a hat.
*   **For girls**: You wear your normal, loose, modest clothing, but you must keep your face and hands uncovered.
*   **The Rules**: Once you are in *Iḥrām*, you cannot cut your hair, clip your nails, use scented perfume, or harm any animals or plants in the sacred area of Makkah.

### The Steps of the Journey
Once in Makkah, pilgrims perform several beautiful acts of worship:

#### 1. *Ṭawāf* (Circumambulating the Ka'bah)
Walking around the Ka'bah seven times counter-clockwise, starting and ending at the corner of the Black Stone (*Al-Ḥajar al-Aswad*).

> [!NOTE] **Maliki Ruling: Purity is Required for Tawaf**
> In the Maliki school, having Wudu is an absolute condition for *Ṭawāf*. If a pilgrim's Wudu breaks during the rounds—even on the last round—their *Ṭawāf* becomes invalid. They must exit, perform Wudu, and restart the entire *Ṭawāf* from the very first round. They must also perform the seven rounds continuously (*Muwālāh*) without long breaks.

#### 2. *Sa‘y* (Pacing between Safa and Marwah)
Walking seven times between the two hills of *Ṣafā* and *Marwah*. This reminds us of Hajar, who ran between these hills looking for water for her baby Ismail until the well of *Zamzam* miraculously bubbled up. The Maliki school considers *Sa‘y* to be a Pillar (*Rukn*) of Hajj—if you omit it, your Hajj is not valid.

#### 3. *Wuqūf ‘Arafah* (Standing at Arafat)
Pilgrims gather at the plain of Arafat on the 9th day of *Dhū al-Ḥijjah*. This is the most important day of Hajj. We spend the afternoon standing, praying, making Du'a, and asking Allah to forgive our sins.

#### 4. Stoning the *Jamarāt*
Pilgrims throw seven small pebbles at three stone pillars. This represents resisting the whispers of Satan and showing that we only obey Allah.

> [!NOTE] **Maliki Ruling: Precision in Stoning**
> In the Maliki school, you must be very careful when throwing the pebbles. Each pebble must land inside the collection basin around the pillar. If you throw a pebble and it misses completely or rolls away outside the basin, it does not count! You must throw an extra pebble to make sure you successfully land seven pebbles inside the target.

## Chapter 6: Glossary of Key Terms

Here are the important Arabic terms you learned in this unit:

*   ***‘Awrah***: The parts of the body that must be covered in front of others and in prayer.
*   ***Dhakāh***: The proper Islamic method of slaughtering an animal for food.
*   ***Gharar***: Uncertainty or trickery in business (which is forbidden).
*   ***Ḥalāl***: Permissible or lawful in Islam.
*   ***Ḥarām***: Forbidden or sinful in Islam.
*   ***Ḥayā’***: Modesty, shyness, and respect before Allah and His creation.
*   ***Iḥrām***: The sacred state of intention and dress for Hajj and Umrah.
*   ***Isti’dhān***: Seeking permission before entering someone's home.
*   ***Makrūh***: An action that is disliked, but not sinful if done.
*   ***Mandūb***: A recommended action that brings reward if done, but no sin if left.
*   ***Mubāḥ***: A neutral action that has no reward or sin by itself.
*   ***Ribā***: Usury or interest on money (which is strictly forbidden).
*   ***Sa‘y***: Pacing seven times between Safa and Marwah.
*   ***Ṭawāf***: Circling the Ka'bah seven times counter-clockwise.
