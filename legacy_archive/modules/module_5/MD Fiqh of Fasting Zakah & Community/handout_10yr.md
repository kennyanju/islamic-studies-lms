## Page 2: The Creed (Aqidah) Connection — Why Do We Fast?

Before we talk about rules, we must ask: **Why do we fast or give charity? Does Allah need us to do this?**

### 1. Allah is Al-Ghani (The Self-Sufficient)
In our creed, we learn that Allah is **Al-Ghani** (الْغَنِيّ). This means He has no needs.
*   Does Allah feel hungry? **No.**
*   Does Allah benefit if we stop eating or give away money? **No.**
*   Allah is the Creator. He is completely free of any need.

### 2. We are the Needy Ones (Al-Fuqara')
We, the humans, are **Al-Fuqara'** (الْفُقَرَاء) – the needy ones. We need food, water, air, and guidance to survive. We fast because it trains our hearts to remember how weak we are and how much we depend on Allah for everything.

### 3. Fasting and Charity are Mercy
Allah commanded us to fast and give charity purely out of mercy for *us*. 
*   **Fasting** cleans our hearts from bad behavior and helps us feel empathy for people who are hungry every day.
*   **Charity** cleans our money and helps everyone in our neighborhood live a good life.

> **Creed Focus Box: Shukr (Gratitude)**
> **Shukr** (شُكْر) means showing thanks to Allah. Fasting and giving Zakah are physical ways to say *"Thank you, Allah, for giving me life, food, and family."*

## Page 3: Moon Sighting — How Ramadan Begins

Ramadan is the 9th month of the Islamic calendar. But how do we know exactly when it starts?

### The Sighting of the Hilal (Crescent)
Islam uses a lunar calendar, which follows the phases of the moon. Ramadan begins when the new crescent moon, called the **Hilal** (هِلَال), is sighted in the sky.

*   **The Prophet’s Command:** The Prophet (pbuh) said: *"Fast when you see it [the crescent], and break your fast when you see it"* (Muwatta Malik).
*   **The Maliki Sighting Rule:** In the Maliki school, if the moon is sighted by two trustworthy people in one city, **everyone** who hears about it in other cities must start fasting too, as long as they share the night. This helps Muslims feel like one big family starting Ramadan together!
*   **Calculations vs. Sighting:** Malikis follow the Sunnah by looking at the sky. We do not use computer math calculations to replace looking for the moon, because the Prophet (pbuh) wanted us to physically search for it.

> **Fiqh Fact:** Other schools, like the Shafi'i school, say that if a city is very far away, they don't have to fast based on another city's sighting. But in the Maliki school, we prefer global unity whenever possible!

## Page 4: The Pillars of Fasting (Siyam) & The Intention

Fasting is called **Siyam** (صِيَام) in Arabic. For our fast to be correct and accepted by Allah, we must follow two core pillars.

### The Two Pillars of Siyam:
1.  **The Intention (Niyyah - نِيَّة):** Making a decision in your heart that you are fasting for Allah.
2.  **Abstinence (Imsak - إِمْسَاك):** Staying away from food, drink, and swallowing anything into your stomach from the first light of dawn (**Fajr**) until the sun sets (**Maghrib**).

### The Maliki Rule of the Single Intention (Niyyah Wahidah)
This is a very cool and special ruling in the Maliki school!
*   **The Rule:** Since Ramadan is one continuous month of fasting, you only need to make **one single intention** in your heart on the night before the first day of Ramadan. This intention covers the whole month!
*   **The Exception:** If you stop fasting during Ramadan (like if you get sick, travel, or have your period), you must make a new intention the night before you resume.
*   **Other Schools:** Other schools of law (like the Hanafis and Shafi'is) say you must make a brand new intention *every single night* of Ramadan.

## Page 5: What Breaks the Fast? (Qada' vs. Kaffarah)

If we do something that breaks our fast, we need to know what the law requires us to do. In the Maliki school, there are two kinds of corrections:

### 1. Qada' (قَضَاء) — Making Up a Day
This means you must fast an extra day after Ramadan is over to replace the broken one. 
*   **What requires Qada' only?**
    *   Eating or drinking **by accident** or because you forgot you were fasting. (Note: In the Maliki school, even if you forget, you must make the day up. Other schools say your fast is still valid).
    *   Getting sick or traveling, which makes fasting too hard.
    *   Vomiting on purpose.

### 2. Kaffarah (كَفَّارَة) — Expiation (A Penalty)
If someone breaks their fast **on purpose** during the day of Ramadan without a good excuse, they commit a sin and must perform **Kaffarah** to fix their relationship with Allah, along with doing *Qada'*.
*   **What requires Kaffarah in the Maliki school?** Deliberately eating, drinking, or engaging in intimacy. (The Maliki school is very strict about protecting the holy days of Ramadan!).
*   **How do you do Kaffarah?** The preferred Maliki way is **feeding 60 poor people** (giving each person a double-handful of food).

## Page 6: Zakat al-Fitr — The Charity of Fasting

At the end of Ramadan, before we celebrate Eid, Allah commanded us to give a special charity. This is called **Zakat al-Fitr** (زَكَاة الْفِطْر).

### Why do we give it?
1.  To clean our fast from any mistakes we made (like arguing or complaining).
2.  To make sure the poor people in our town have food to eat on Eid day.

### Who must pay it?
Every Muslim who has enough food for Eid day must pay it. The head of the house pays it for himself and for all dependents (like children).

### When do we give it?
*   **The Best Time:** After dawn on Eid morning, but *before* you go to the Eid prayer.
*   **The Allowed Time:** One or two days before Eid.
*   **The Disliked Time:** Delaying it until after the Eid prayer is completed.

---

## Page 7: Zakat al-Fitr — Measurements in Sa' and Mudd

How much food do we give for Zakat al-Fitr? The Prophet (pbuh) taught us to measure using hands!

### The Sa' and the Mudd
*   **Mudd (مُدّ):** The amount of food that fits into two hands cupped together.
*   **Sa' (صَاع):** Four *amdad* (plural of *mudd*). 

```
  [1 Mudd] + [1 Mudd] + [1 Mudd] + [1 Mudd] = 1 Sa' (One Zakat al-Fitr)
```

In the Maliki school, we must give **One Sa'** of the local staple food (like rice, wheat, or dates) per person. This is about **2.1 to 2.5 kilograms** of food.

### Can we give money instead of food?
*   **The Maliki View:** In the classical Maliki school, you **must** give actual food, not money. This keeps the Sunnah practice alive. (Other schools, like the Hanafis, allow giving cash).
*   **Modern Practice:** Today, many families give money to Islamic charity organizations. The charity then uses that money to buy actual rice or wheat and gives it to the poor on Eid day.

## Page 8: Zakah on Wealth — Purifying Our Money

While Zakat al-Fitr is paid at the end of Ramadan, there is another type of Zakah that is paid on our savings. This is **Zakah al-Mal** (زَكَاة الْمَال) – the third pillar of Islam.

### Why do we pay it?
Zakah means "purification" and "growth." We believe all our money belongs to Allah. Zakah is the portion of our savings that belongs to the poor. By giving it away, we purify the rest of our money.

### The Rules of Zakah:
*   **Nisab (نِصَاب):** The minimum amount of savings you must have before you owe Zakah. If your savings are below this line, you do not pay.
*   **Hawl (حَوْل):** A full lunar year. Your savings must stay above the *Nisab* for a whole year before Zakah is due.
*   **How much?** We pay **2.5%** of our savings (which is $2.50 for every $100).

> **Maliki Rule Box: Zakah on Children**
> In the Maliki school, Zakah is obligatory even on the money of **children** and **babies** if they have savings above the *Nisab* (like if they inherited money). Their parents pay it on their behalf. (Hanafis say children do not pay Zakah).

## Page 9: Sadaqah — Kindness and Community

Zakah is obligatory, but Muslims are also encouraged to give voluntary charity. This is called **Sadaqah** (صَدَقَة).

### The Meaning of Sadaqah
Sadaqah comes from the word *Sidq*, which means "truthfulness." Giving charity proves that your faith in Allah is true and sincere.

### Anything Can Be Sadaqah!
The Prophet (pbuh) taught us that charity isn't just about money:
*   Smiling at your brother or sister is Sadaqah.
*   Removing a sharp rock from the path is Sadaqah.
*   Helping someone carry their bags is Sadaqah.
*   Saying kind words is Sadaqah.

### Communal Duty (Fard Kifayah)
In our community, taking care of the poor is a **Communal Obligation** (**Fard Kifayah** - فَرْض كِفَايَة). This means if there are hungry people in our town, the whole town is responsible. If a few people feed them, the duty is done. But if everyone ignores them, everyone shares the sin!

---

## Page 10: Eid al-Fitr — Joy in Worship

After Ramadan ends, we celebrate **Eid al-Fitr** (عِيد الْفِطْر). Celebrating Eid is a way to thank Allah for helping us fast.

### Sunnahs of Eid Day:
1.  **Take a bath (Ghusl)** before going to the prayer.
2.  **Wear your best clothes** and look neat.
3.  **Eat an odd number of dates** (like 1, 3, or 5) before leaving the house to show that we are not fasting today.
4.  **Walk to the prayer** taking one road, and walk back home taking a different road.
5.  **Recite the Takbeerat** out loud on the way.

### Glossary of Key Arabic Terms
1.  **Al-Ghani (الْغَنِيّ):** The Self-Sufficient (Allah has no needs).
2.  **Iftiqar (اِفْتِقَار):** Humbleness and neediness before Allah.
3.  **Hilal (هِلَال):** The new crescent moon.
4.  **Siyam (صِيَام):** Fasting.
5.  **Imsak (إِمْسَاك):** Abstaining from food and drink.
6.  **Niyyah (نِيَّة):** Intention.
7.  **Qada' (قَضَاء):** Making up a missed day.
8.  **Kaffarah (كَفَّارَة):** An expiation/penalty for breaking a fast on purpose.
9.  **Sa' (صَاع):** A volumetric measure equal to 4 Amdad (approx. 2.1 - 2.5 kg).
10. **Mudd (مُدّ):** A double-handful scoop.
11. **Nisab (نِصَاب):** The minimum wealth threshold for Zakah.
12. **Hawl (حَوْل):** One lunar year.
13. **Sadaqah (صَدَقَة):** Voluntary charity.
14. **Fard Kifayah (فَرْض كِفَايَة):** A communal obligation.
