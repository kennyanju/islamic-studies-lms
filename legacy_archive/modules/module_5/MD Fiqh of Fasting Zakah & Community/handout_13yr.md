### Introduction: Developing Fiqh Literacy
As you mature in your study of Islam, it is time to look closely at the legal and spiritual systems that structure our faith. This is called **Fiqh** (فِقْه) – the deep understanding of Islamic law. 

In this unit, we will study **Siyam** (Fasting), **Zakat al-Fitr**, **Zakah al-Mal** (Zakah on Wealth), and the social rules of our community. We will explore these through the **Maliki school** of law, founded by Imam Malik ibn Anas (d. 179 AH) in Madinah, and the **Ash'ari school** of theology (*aqidah*), which provides the intellectual framework for our creed.

By understanding the logical paths that scholars used to derive these rulings, you will develop **fiqh literacy**. This means learning to appreciate the legal diversity (*ikhtilaf*) among schools as a mercy, rather than assuming there is only one "universal" way to practice every detail.

## Page 2: Theological Foundations — Divine Sovereignty and Ubudiyyah

To understand the rules of fasting and charity, we must first understand *why* we pray, fast, or spend from the perspective of our creed (*aqidah*).

### 1. Absolute Independence (Istighna')
In the Ash'ari school of creed, we study the necessary attributes of Allah. One of these attributes is **Qiyamuhu bi-Nafsihi** (قِيَامُهُ بِنَفْسِهِ) – Self-Existence. Allah is **Al-Ghani** (الْغَنِيّ), the absolutely Self-Sufficient.
*   **Worship does not benefit Allah:** If the entire universe fasted with perfect devotion, it would not increase Allah’s majesty.
*   **Disobedience does not harm Allah:** If the entire world refused to fast, it would not decrease His sovereignty.
*   **The Command is Mercy:** Allah commands us to worship Him solely for *our* spiritual growth. 

### 2. Utter Dependency (Iftiqar)
We are the creation, defined by **Iftiqar** (اِفْتِقَار) – complete neediness. We rely on Allah for every breath, every heartbeat, and our guidance. 
*   **Siyam as a Lesson in Iftiqar:** When we fast, the physical feeling of hunger is a reminder of our spiritual neediness before Allah.
*   **'Ubudiyyah (Servantship):** Fasting is the ultimate expression of **'Ubudiyyah** (عُبُودِيَّة), where we willingly submit our desires (eating, drinking) to the command of our Creator.

## Page 3: Moon Sighting — Communal Unity and Legal Evidence

The month of Ramadan begins when the new crescent moon, the **Hilal** (هِلَال), is sighted. 

### Sighting as the Legal Cause (Sabab)
In Ash'ari theology, we learn that physical markers in nature are set up by Allah as signs or causes (**Asbab**) for legal obligations. The visual sighting of the crescent is the legal cause for the obligation of fasting.
*   **The Prophet's Method:** The Prophet (pbuh) said: *"Fast when you see it [the crescent], and break your fast when you see it"* (Muwatta Malik). 
*   **Maliki Position on Astronomical Calculations:** The Maliki school holds that astronomical calculations (*Hisab*) cannot replace visual moon sighting. The command is tied to the physical observation (*ruyah*), which is democratic and accessible to everyone.

### Global vs. Local Horizons (Ikhtilaf al-Matali')
*   **The Maliki View (Global Sighting):** If the crescent is sighted reliably in any land, it becomes obligatory for all other lands to fast, provided they share the news and have overlapping nights. This ruling prioritizes communal unity across the Muslim world.
*   **The Shafi'i View (Local Sighting):** The Shafi'i school rules that a sighting in one city only binds those within a local horizon (approx. 83 km). 

Both views are based on valid legal interpretations of the Sunnah, showing the beauty of scholarly reasoning.

## Page 4: Pillars of Fasting & The Maliki Rule of Intention

Fasting (*Siyam*) contains two primary pillars (*Fara'id* / *Arkan*) in the Maliki school.

### The Two Pillars:
1.  **The Intention (*Niyyah*):** The mental resolve in the heart to fast.
2.  **Abstinence (*Imsak*):** Refraining from food, drink, and intimacy from true dawn (*Fajr*) until sunset (*Maghrib*).

### The Maliki Rule: Single Intention (Niyyah Wahidah)
A major distinctive feature of Maliki fiqh is the ruling on the **Niyyah** for continuous fasts:
*   **The Position:** For any fast that requires consecutive days of fasting (like the 30 days of Ramadan), **one single intention** made on the night before the first day is sufficient for the entire month.
*   **The Reasoning:** Imam Malik argued that since Ramadan cannot be divided (you cannot choose to fast only half of Ramadan; it is a single obligational block), it is treated as a single compound act of worship. Therefore, one intention at the beginning is enough.
*   **Interruption Exception:** If the fast is broken by travel, illness, or menstruation, the continuity is broken. A new intention must be made on the night before resuming.
*   **Contrast:** The Hanafi, Shafi'i, and Hanbali schools require a fresh intention *every single night* because they view each day of fasting as an independent act of worship.

## Page 5: Invalidators of the Fast — Qada' vs. Kaffarah

If a fast is broken, the legal consequences depend on *how* and *why* it was broken.

### 1. Qada' (Makeup Only)
**Qada'** (قَضَاء) is the obligation to make up a missed fast day-for-day after Ramadan.
*   **Maliki Forgetfulness Rule:** If you eat or drink because you **forgot** you were fasting, your fast is broken, and you must perform *Qada'*. (Imam Malik argued that the physical state of fasting is absent once food enters the stomach. Contrast: Hanafis and Shafi'is rule that the fast is still valid and no makeup is required).
*   **Other Qada' causes:** Illness, travel, or vomiting intentionally.

### 2. Kaffarah (Expiation + Makeup)
**Kaffarah** (كَفَّارَة) is a major expiation penalty required for deliberately violating the sanctity of Ramadan.
*   **The Maliki Scope:** Unlike the Shafi'i and Hanbali schools (who limit *Kaffarah* only to sexual intimacy), the Maliki school rules that **any deliberate, voluntary violation** (eating, drinking, or intimacy) without a legal excuse triggers *Kaffarah*.
*   **The Maliki Preference:** The three options for *Kaffarah* are:
    1.  Feeding 60 poor people. **(Highly Preferred by Malikis)**
    2.  Fasting two consecutive months.
    3.  Freeing a slave.
    *   *Why feed the poor first?* Imam Malik preferred this because it has a transitive, social benefit (*mutata'addi*) that helps the community, rather than a purely personal benefit.

## Page 6: Zakat al-Fitr — Purpose, Timing, and Staple Foods

At the end of Ramadan, before Eid prayer, we pay **Zakat al-Fitr** (زَكَاة الْفِطْر).

### The Timing Rulings
*   **Wujub (Obligation):** It becomes obligatory at sunset on the last day of Ramadan.
*   **Mustahabb (Recommended):** Between Fajr and the Eid prayer on Eid morning.
*   **Rukhsah (Permitted):** Up to two days before Eid.
*   **Disliked/Haram:** Delaying it past the Eid prayer is disliked (*Makruh*); delaying it past Eid day is forbidden (*Haram*). It remains a debt on your conscience.

### The Food-Only Rule vs. Cash Value
*   **The Maliki Position (Food Only):** In the classical Maliki school, Zakat al-Fitr must be paid in the **staple foods** (*Qut*) of the country (e.g., wheat, rice, dates). Giving cash value is invalid.
*   **The Hanafi Position (Cash Allowed):** The Hanafi school permits cash value, arguing that money is more flexible and useful for the poor in changing economic landscapes.
*   **Modern Workaround:** Many modern Malikis give cash to relief agencies, who act as representatives (*wakil*) to purchase food and distribute it to the poor on time.

## Page 7: Zakat al-Fitr — Volume and Weights (Sa' and Mudd)

To understand prophetic measurements, we look at the volumetric tools of Madinah.

### The Sa' and the Mudd
*   **Mudd (مُدّ):** The volume of food that fills two average-sized hands cupped together.
*   **Sa' (صَاع):** Exactly four *amdad* (plural of *mudd*). 

```
  1 Mudd (1 double-handful) ──> x 4 ──> 1 Sa' (Prophetic Measurement)
```

In the Maliki school, every Muslim must give **One Sa'** of food. 

### Why is Weight Different for Different Grains?
Because a cup of lead weighs more than a cup of feathers, the *weight* of one *Sa'* depends on the density of the food item:
*   One *Sa'* of **rice** is approximately **2.2 kg** (4.8 lbs).
*   One *Sa'* of **barley** is approximately **2.1 kg** (4.6 lbs).
*   One *Sa'* of **dates** is approximately **2.5 kg** (5.5 lbs).

### Historical Accuracy of the Maliki Sa'
Imam Malik lived in Madinah, where the families of the Companions preserved the actual measurement vessels used by the Prophet (pbuh). Therefore, the Maliki measurement of the *Sa'* is historically direct and highly accurate.

## Page 8: Zakah on Wealth — Nisab and the Hawl

The third pillar of Islam is **Zakah al-Mal** (Zakah on Wealth). It is a structural tax on savings designed to redistribute wealth.

### Key Conditions:
1.  **Nisab (نِصَاب):** The minimum wealth threshold. Zakah is only paid if savings exceed this amount.
    *   *Gold Nisab:* 20 gold Dinars (approx. **85 grams** of pure gold).
    *   *Silver Nisab:* 200 silver Dirhams (approx. **595 grams** of silver).
    *   *Currency:* Equivalent to the value of 85g of gold.
2.  **Hawl (حَوْل):** The requirement that the wealth must remain above the *Nisab* for one full lunar year. (Note: Crops do not require a *Hawl*; they are taxed at harvest).

### Zakah Rate:
For cash savings, gold, and silver, the rate is **2.5%** annually.

> **Comparative Fiqh: Zakah on the Wealth of Children and the Insane**
> *   **The Maliki Position:** Zakah is a right belonging to the poor that is linked to the wealth itself, not the owner's legal accountability. Therefore, children and insane individuals **must** pay Zakah. Their guardians calculate and pay it on their behalf.
> *   **The Hanafi Position:** Zakah is an act of physical-financial worship (*ibadah*), which requires intention. Since children and the insane are not legally accountable (*mukallaf*), they do not owe Zakah.

## Page 9: Sadaqah, Commutative Causality, and Fard Kifayah

Islamic law balances individual duties with social duties.

### The Theological Trust of Wealth
In Ash'ari Aqidah, we believe that humans do not have absolute, primary ownership of anything. Allah is the true Owner (*Al-Malik*). Humans are trustees (**Mustakhlafeen** - مُسْتَخْلَفِين). Therefore, we must spend the wealth in accordance with the Owner’s rules. 

### Sadaqah (Voluntary Charity)
Sadaqah comes from the root *Sidq* (truthfulness). It is a voluntary transfer of wealth that proves the truth of one's faith. Unlike Zakah, Sadaqah has no limits, no timing requirements, and can be given to anyone.

### Fard Kifayah (Communal Obligation)
*   **Definition:** An obligation that is binding on the community as a whole, rather than on individuals.
*   **Application to Poverty:** Eradicating hunger and poverty in a neighborhood is a **Fard Kifayah**. 
*   *If the community fails:* If a poor person dies of hunger, and the community had the means to save them but did not, **everyone** who knew shares in the sin. If a few step up and feed the poor, the sin is lifted from the rest.

## Page 10: Eid al-Fitr — Law, Sunnah, and Psychology

We celebrate **Eid al-Fitr** to mark the completion of Ramadan.

### The Legal Status of Eid Prayer
In the Maliki school, the Eid prayer is a **Confirmed Sunnah (*Sunnah Mu'akkadah*)**. It consists of 2 Rak'ahs:
*   **1st Rak'ah:** 6 extra Takbeers after the opening Takbeer (*Takbeer al-Ihram*).
*   **2nd Rak'ah:** 5 extra Takbeers before starting the recitation.

### The Sunnah Etiquettes of Eid:
*   Taking a bath (**Ghusl**) and using perfume before leaving.
*   Wearing your finest clothes.
*   Eating an odd number of dates before going to Eid al-Fitr prayer (showing obedience by breaking the fast).
*   Walking by one path and returning by another.
*   Reciting Takbeerat out loud while traveling to the prayer ground (*Musalla*).
