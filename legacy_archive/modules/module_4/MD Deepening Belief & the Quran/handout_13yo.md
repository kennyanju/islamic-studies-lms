## Section I: Intention, Sincerity, and the Ash‘arī Philosophy of Actions

At age 13, you are entering the stage of moral accountability (*Taklīf*). In the eyes of Islamic law, your choices are now recorded, and you are responsible for your relationship with Allah. This responsibility begins in the heart.

### The Spiritual Engine: *Niyyah* (نِيّة)
An intention (*Niyyah*) is defined legally as: "The resolve of the heart to perform an act of worship in seek of drawing closer to Allah." It serves two major functions:
1. **Cognitive Distinction of Worship:** It distinguishes ordinary physical habits (*‘Ādah*) from acts of worship (*‘Ibādah*).
2. **Hierarchy of Worship:** It distinguishes different levels of worship (e.g., praying a mandatory Dhuhr prayer vs. praying a voluntary Sunnah prayer).

The foundational text for this is the famous statement of the Prophet ﷺ:
> *"Actions are but by intentions, and every man shall have only that which he intended..."* (Bukhari & Muslim).

#### [Mālikī Fiqh Flag: The Legality of Niyyah]
In Mālikī jurisprudence, *niyyah* is a structural pillar (*Rukn*) for the validity of acts of purification (*Ṭahārah*) like Wudu, Ghusl, and Tayammum, as well as the prayer (*Ṣalāh*) and fasting (*Ṣiyām*). For instance:
* If you step into a shower to cool down on a hot day, and wash your entire body, this does *not* count as a ritual bath (*Ghusl*) that allows you to pray. 
* Even though the physical washing was completed, the lack of a specific *niyyah* to lift the state of ritual impurity (*Ḥadath*) means the action remains a mere habit (*‘ādah*) and is legally invalid for worship.

### The Danger of Ostentation: *Riyā’* (رِيَاء)
True belief requires **Ikhlāṣ** (إِخْلَاص) — absolute sincerity. Sincerity is the purification of one's motivation from any creaturely partnership. 
The primary threat to sincerity is **Riyā’** (spiritual show-off/ostentation). *Riyā’* occurs when a person performs an act of worship with the intention of gaining admiration, respect, or social status among people.
* The Prophet ﷺ warned: *"What I fear most for you is the minor shirk."* The Sahabah asked: "What is the minor shirk, O Messenger of Allah?" He replied: *"Riyā’."* (Ahmad).
* *Riyā’* is psychologically subtle. It can slip into our hearts unnoticed—such as lengthening our prayer because someone walked into the room, or speaking about our voluntary fasts to sound pious.

### [Ash‘arī Creed Flag: The Doctrine of *Kasb* (Acquisition)]
One of the most famous intellectual debates in Islamic history is the paradox of human free will and divine sovereignty. How do we balance the fact that Allah has determined all things with the fact that humans are held accountable for their sins?

The **Ash‘arī school of theology** resolved this debate through the Quranic concept of **Kasb** (Acquisition):

```
                                  ALLAH (The Creator)
                    Creates the physical body, the human will,
                   and the final action in the physical world.
                                          |
                                          | (Coincidence of Divine Creation)
                                          v
                                 HUMAN (The Acquirer)
                   Directs their intention/choice (al-Irādah al-Juz'iyyah)
                     toward a specific act. This choice is Kasb.
                                          |
                                          v
                             ACCOUNTABILITY & JUDGMENT
```

1. **Divine Creation:** Allah is the sole Creator of all existence, including actions. As the Quran states: *"And Allah created you and that which you do"* (Surah Aṣ-Ṣāffāt, 37:96). Nothing can happen in the universe without Allah's direct creation and power.
2. **Human Choice (*Kasb*):** Allah does not force humans to act. Instead, He created within us a limited choice (*al-Irādah al-Juz’iyyah*). When we intend to perform an action, Allah creates the power and the physical action at that exact moment. The human "acquires" (*yaksib*) the action by choosing to align their will with it.
3. **The Balance:** 
   * This rejects **Jabarism** (the belief that humans are forced to act and have no choices).
   * This also rejects **Qadarism** (the belief that humans create their own actions independently of Allah).
   * Because we have the freedom to choose our intentions, we are justly held accountable on the Day of Judgment, even though Allah is the ultimate Creator of the action itself.

## Section II: The Quran — Historical Preservation & The Nature of Divine Speech

The Quran is the foundational miracle of Islam. To understand its nature, we must examine both its theological definition and its historical compilation.

### [Ash‘arī Creed Flag: Eternal Speech vs. Created Recitation]
In Ash‘arī theology, one of the essential attributes of Allah is **Speech** (*Kalām*). To avoid comparing Allah to human beings, the Ash‘arī scholars explain the Quran using two key concepts:

1. **Al-Kalām al-Nafsī (الكَلَام النَّفْسِي - Speech of the Self):**
   * This is Allah's eternal, uncreated attribute of Speech. It exists within His Divine Self, has no beginning or end, and does not consist of sounds, letters, syntax, or human languages. It is a single, eternal attribute that cannot be divided.
2. **Al-Kalām al-Lafẓī (الكَلَام اللَّفْظِي - Written and Recited Speech):**
   * This refers to the physical words we read in the book, the sounds we produce when we recite, and the ink printed on the pages. These are created by Allah. The Arabic language itself is a created medium.
   * **The Union:** The recited Quran (*al-Kalām al-Lafẓī*) is a created representation that points directly to the uncreated, eternal Speech of Allah (*al-Kalām al-Nafsī*). Therefore, we hold the Mushaf in deep reverence as it conveys the uncreated Message of the Divine.

---

### The Dual Tracks of Preservation: Oral and Written
The preservation of the Quran was not left to chance; it was structurally preserved through a dual-track validation system.

```
                  +-----------------------------------------+
                  |  PROPHETIC REVELATION (23-Year Period)  |
                  +--------------------o--------------------+
                                       |
              +------------------------+------------------------+
              |                                                 |
              v                                                 v
    [ ORAL TRANSMISSION ]                            [ WRITTEN DOCUMENTATION ]
  * Immediate memorization by                      * Written in the presence of 
    the Prophet ﷺ and Sahabah.                       the Prophet ﷺ by scribes.
  * Recited in daily prayers.                      * Scribes used parchment, bone,
  * Audited annually by Jibreel.                     wood, and stones.
              |                                                 |
              +------------------------+------------------------+
                                       |
                                       v
                     +-----------------------------------+
                     |       THE CALIPHAL COMPILATIONS   |
                     +-----------------o-----------------+
                                       |
        +------------------------------+------------------------------+
        v                                                             v
[ ABU BAKR'S MANUSCRIPT (12 AH) ]             [ UTHMAN'S STANDARDIZED CODEX (30 AH) ]

* Led by Zayd ibn Thabit.                     * Standardized in the Qurayshi dialect.
* Required two physical written               * Written in the Rasm al-Uthmani.
  witnesses for every verse.                  * Dispatched official copies; burned variants.
```

#### 1. The Prophetic Era
* **Oral Transmission:** The Prophet ﷺ memorized the verses immediately upon revelation. Angel Jibreel would review the entire Quran with the Prophet ﷺ once every Ramadan, and twice in the final year of his life. The Sahabah memorized the verses, chanting them in their night prayers.
* **Written Documentation:** Although oral memory was primary, the Prophet ﷺ appointed official scribes (*Kuttāb al-Waḥy*), including Zayd ibn Thābit, Ubayy ibn Ka‘b, and Mu‘āwiyah. They wrote the text on camel shoulder bones (*Aktāf*), parchment (*Raq*), flat stones (*Likhāf*), and wood.

#### 2. The Abu Bakr Compilation (12 AH)
Following the Battle of Yamamah, where dozens of Quranic memorizers (*Ḥuffāẓ*) were martyred, Umar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb urged Caliph Abu Bakr to compile the Quran into a single book to prevent loss. 
* Zayd ibn Thābit was appointed to lead the project.
* Zayd was extremely rigorous: he did not rely solely on his own memory or the memory of other Sahabah. He established a rule that **no verse would be written down unless it was presented in written form and verified by two witnesses who saw it being written in the direct presence of the Prophet ﷺ.**
* This compiled text (*Ṣuḥuf*) was kept in the custody of Abu Bakr, then Umar, and finally Hafsah (the daughter of Umar and wife of the Prophet ﷺ).

#### 3. The Uthmanic Standardization (30 AH)
As the Islamic borders expanded into Persia, Syria, and North Africa, new non-Arab Muslims began reciting the Quran with varying regional dialects (*Aḥruf*). Fearing that these minor differences in pronunciation would lead to division and sectarianism, Caliph Uthman ibn Affan intervened.
* He borrowed the original manuscript from Hafsah.
* He commissioned a committee of four scholars, headed by Zayd ibn Thābit, to copy the text into standardized codices (*Muṣḥaf*).
* **The Qurayshi Dialect:** The spelling was adjusted to reflect the pronunciation of the tribe of Quraysh (the Prophet’s ﷺ tribe).
* **Rasm al-‘Uthmānī (رسم العثماني):** This specific spelling method allowed for the text to accommodate the valid, mutawātir recitations (*Qirā'āt*) while preventing unauthorized changes.
* **Standardization:** Uthman sent these official copies to the major cities of the empire and ordered all other personal, non-standard drafts to be burned to maintain unity of the text.

## Section III: Analytical Tafseer — Surahs Al-Ikhlāṣ and Al-‘Aṣr

Tafseer (Quranic Exegesis) is the systematic study of the meaning of the Quranic text. Let us analyze two foundational Surahs.

### Surah Al-Ikhlāṣ: The Manifesto of Tawheed
Revealed in response to the Makkah polytheists who demanded a description of Allah's lineage, this Surah is structurally equivalent to one-third of the Quran (*Thuluth al-Quran*) because it summarizes the entire science of theology (*‘Aqīdah*).

```text
قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ . اللَّهُ الصَّمَدُ . لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ . وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ
```

#### Lexical & Theological Analysis:
1. **Aḥad (أَحَد):** Unlike the word *Wāḥid* (one), *Aḥad* is a name of absolute singularity. It means Allah is uniquely one, with no partners, and He cannot be divided into parts or composites.
2. **Aṣ-Ṣamad (الصَّمَد):** A multi-dimensional word in classical Arabic:
   * *Al-Sīd al-Ladhī yuntahā ilayhi al-su’dad:* The Master whose sovereignty is absolute, to whom all creation turns for their needs.
   * *Al-Ghanī:* The Self-Sufficient who has no needs.
   * *La jawfa lahu:* The One who has no cavities (He does not eat, drink, digest, or need biological replenishment).
3. **Lam yalid wa lam yūlad (لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ):**
   * Refutes any concept of lineage, anthropomorphic offspring, or generation from a prior cause.

#### [Ash‘arī Creed Flag: The Concept of Tanzīh]
Ash‘arī theology is built on **Tanzīh**—declaring Allah completely free of any similarity to created things. 
* The verse *"Nor is there to Him any equivalent"* is a theological anchor.
* **The Rule of Anthropomorphism:** If any text in the Quran or Sunnah seems to attribute physical features (like hands, eyes, or sitting on a throne) to Allah, the Ash‘arī school applies two methodologies:
  * **Tafwīḍ (Consignment):** Believing in the words of the text but consigning their true meaning to Allah, while firmly denying physical body parts.
  * **Ta’wīl (Interpretation):** Explaining the terms linguistically (e.g., "Hand" means power, "Face" means divine existence/pleasure, "Establishment on the throne" means dominion and control), in accordance with the rules of classical Arabic grammar and metaphor.

---

### Surah Al-`Aṣr: The Capital of Time
This Surah outlines the existential conditions of human success.

```text
وَالْعَصْرِ . إِنَّ الْإِنسَانَ لَفِي خُسْرٍ . إِلَّا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالْحَقِّ وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالصَّبْرِ
```

#### Deep Analysis:
* **The Oath (*Wa-l-‘Aṣr*):** Swearing by time emphasizes that time is a non-renewable asset. Every passing second decreases a human's capital.
* **The State of Loss (*Khusr*):** The default state of human beings is spiritual bankruptcy, unless they develop four key qualities:
  1. **Īmān (Faith):** Cognitive assent and belief in the truth.
  2. **‘Amal Ṣāliḥ (Righteous Action):** Practical manifestation of faith, which must be accompanied by a clean *niyyah* to be valid.
  3. **Tawāṣī bi’l-Ḥaqq (Advising to Truth):** The social responsibility to spread knowledge, justice, and correct information.
  4. **Tawāṣī bi’ṣ-Ṣabr (Advising to Patience):** Building resilience within the community to endure trials, avoid sin, and remain steadfast in worship.

## Section IV: Hadith and Sunnah — The Science of Epistemic Transmission

To practice the Quran, we must follow the Prophet ﷺ. The lived model of the Prophet ﷺ is the **Sunnah**, and the textual reports that document this model are **Hadiths**.

### The Anatomy of Hadith Verification
Every Hadith consists of two elements:
1. **Isnād (إِسْنَاد) — The Chain of Narrators:** The genealogical line of transmission.
2. **Matn (مَتْن) — The Text:** The actual speech or description.

```
       [ HADITH TRANSMISSION SYSTEM ]
  Prophet Muhammad ﷺ
     |
     v
  Companion (Sahabi)      --> Verified for moral integrity ('Adalah)
     |
     v
  Successor (Tabi'i)      --> Checked for cognitive retention (Dabt)
     |
     v
  Hadith Compiler (e.g., Bukhari) --> Analyzed for structural continuity (Ittisal)
```

To classify a Hadith as **Ṣaḥīḥ** (Authentic), Hadith scholars required five conditions:
1. **‘Adālah (Moral Integrity):** Every narrator in the chain must be a practicing Muslim who avoids major sins and acts of public indecency.
2. **Ḍabṭ (Cognitive Accuracy):** Every narrator must have a flawless memory (either in their mind or in their written notebooks).
3. **Ittiṣāl al-Sanad (Connected Chain):** Every narrator must have heard the report directly from their teacher.
4. **‘Adam al-Shudhūdh (No Contradiction):** The Hadith must not contradict other narrations that are stronger.
5. **‘Adam al-‘Illah (No Hidden Defects):** The Hadith must not contain subtle, hidden mistakes in the chain or text.

## Section V: Legal Methodology — The Madhhab System and the Practice of Madinah

Why do we follow a Madhhab (school of law)? Why can we not just read a Hadith online and make our own decisions?

### The Necessity of Legal Schools
Extracting legal rulings from texts requires **Ijtihād** (scholarly effort). It requires mastery of Arabic grammar, rhetoric, theology, historical contexts, and the principles of jurisprudence (*Uṣūl al-Fiqh*). 
* For a non-specialist, attempting to perform Ijtihād directly from translation is highly dangerous. It often leads to taking verses out of context, misapplying legal principles, and creating chaos in practice.
* Following a Madhhab means following a structured, peer-reviewed legal system that has been refined by thousands of scholars over a thousand years.

The four Sunni Madhhabs are: Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali. We follow the **Mālikī Madhhab**.

### Imam Malik and the Epistemology of Madinah
**Imam Malik ibn Anas** (93–179 AH) was the grand jurist of Madinah. His legal school has a unique foundational source: **‘Amal Ahl al-Madīnah** (The practice of the people of Madinah).

Imam Malik’s reasoning was mathematically and historically elegant:
1. The Prophet ﷺ lived in Madinah for the final 10 years of his life.
2. He established the laws of Islam (Salah, Zakah, trade, marriage) in the presence of thousands of companions.
3. These companions lived out these laws daily.
4. When they passed away, their children and students (the Tābi‘ūn) continued these identical daily habits.
5. Therefore, the continuous, city-wide behavior of the people of Madinah is a **mass-transmitted physical transmission (*Mutawātir*)** of the Sunnah.

```
       [ EPISTEMIC WEIGHT COMPARISON ]
  
  * Khabar al-Wahid (Single-Narrator Hadith)
    [ Narrator A ] ---> [ Narrator B ] ---> [ Narrator C ] ---> Prophet ﷺ
    (Possibility of human error, forgetfulness, or local context)
  
  * 'Amal Ahl al-Madinah (Lived Practice of Madinah)
    [ Thousands of People ] ------------------------------> Prophet ﷺ
    (Zero possibility of collective fabrication; represents living Sunnah)
```

> **[Mālikī Fiqh Flag: Amal overrides Ahad Hadith]**
> In the Mālikī school, if a single Hadith narrated by one person (*Khabar al-Wāḥid*) contradicts the continuous, public practice of the people of Madinah, the practice of Madinah is prioritized. 
> Imam Malik argued that a single narrator can make a mistake, forget a detail, or narrate a ruling that was later abrogated. However, an entire city of scholars and companions practicing a rule daily cannot collectively be mistaken.

#### Practical Legal Application: Sadl (Praying with Arms at the Sides)
A famous example of this methodology is **Sadl** (praying with the arms hanging naturally at the sides) rather than **Qabḍ** (clasping the hands over the chest).
* While there are single Hadiths that mention the Prophet ﷺ clasping his hands in prayer, Imam Malik observed that the scholars and the general population of Madinah prayed with their arms at their sides (*Sadl*).
* Thus, the Mālikī school rules that *Sadl* is the preferred (*Mandūb*) position in mandatory prayers, as it represents the continuous, uninterrupted practice of the city of Madinah.

## Academic Glossary

1. **Taklīf (تَكْلِيف):** Moral accountability. The stage in life where a Muslim is legally responsible for their actions.
2. **Niyyah (نِيّة):** Intention. The resolve of the heart to perform an action.
3. **Ikhlāṣ (إِخْلَاص):** Sincerity. Doing actions solely for the sake of Allah.
4. **Riyā’ (رِيَاء):** Spiritual show-off. Seeking creation's praise through worship.
5. **Kasb (كَسْب):** Acquisition. The human choice of an action created by Allah.
6. **Al-Kalām al-Nafsī (الكَلَام النَّفْسِي):** The uncreated, eternal Attribute of Speech belonging to Allah.
7. **Al-Kalām al-Lafẓī (الكَلَام اللَّفْظِي):** The created Arabic text and recitation of the Quran.
8. **Rasm al-‘Uthmānī (رسم العثماني):** The standardized orthography of the Quran established under Caliph Uthman.
9. **Tanzīh (تَنْزِيه):** Transcendence. Purifying Allah of any resemblance to creation.
10. **Tafwīḍ (تَفْوِيض):** Consigning the exact meaning of anthropomorphic verses to Allah while denying physical likeness.
11. **Ta’wīl (تَأْوِيل):** Figurative interpretation of texts in accordance with classical Arabic grammar.
12. **Isnād (إِسْنَاد):** The chain of transmitters of a Hadith.
13. **Matn (مَتْن):** The text of a Hadith.
14. **‘Amal Ahl al-Madīnah (عَمَل أَهْل المَدِينَة):** The living practice of the people of Madinah.
15. **Sadl (سَدْل):** Praying with the arms naturally at the sides.
