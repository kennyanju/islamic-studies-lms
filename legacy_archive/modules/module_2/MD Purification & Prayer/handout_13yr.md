### Introduction: Developing Fiqh Literacy
As a young Muslim maturing in faith, it is time to move beyond simply memorizing actions and begin understanding the *legal framework* of Islam. This is called **Fiqh** (فِقْه) – the deep understanding of Islamic law. 

In this unit, we will study the rulings of **Taharah** (Purification) and **Salah** (Prayer) according to the **Maliki school** of jurisprudence, founded by Imam Malik ibn Anas (d. 179 AH) in the illuminated city of Madinah. We will also anchor our understanding in the **Ash'ari school** of theology (*aqidah*), which provides the logical framework of our beliefs. 

By understanding the logic behind different rulings, you will develop **fiqh literacy**—learning to appreciate the diversity of opinions (*ikhtilaf*) among scholars as a mercy, rather than assuming there is only one "universal" way to practice every detail.

## Page 2: The Theological Foundation — Worship and Divine Independence

To appreciate the rules of prayer, we must first understand *why* we pray from the perspective of Islamic creed (*aqidah*). 

### 1. The Independence of the Creator (Istighna')
In the Ash'ari school of creed, we study the necessary attributes of Allah. One of these attributes is **Qiyamuhu bi-Nafsihi** (قِيَامُهُ بِنَفْسِهِ) – Self-Existence or Self-Sufficiency. Allah is **Al-Ghani** (الْغَنِيّ), the absolutely Independent. 
*   **Allah does not benefit from our worship:** If every human and jinn on earth prayed with perfect devotion, it would not increase Allah’s majesty by a single atom.
*   **Allah does not suffer from our disobedience:** If the entire world refused to pray, it would not decrease His sovereignty.
*   **Why does He command us?** In His infinite wisdom and mercy, He commanded us to worship Him solely for *our* benefit. Worship is the path to our spiritual survival.

### 2. The Neediness of Creation (Iftiqar)
We are the creation, defined by **Iftiqar** (اِفْتِقَار) – utter dependency. We rely on Allah for our existence, our next breath, and our guidance. 
*   **Salah is a Rope:** Salah is the daily lifeline that connects the dependent servant (*'abd*) to the Independent Creator (*Al-Bari*).
*   **Salah as Gratitude (Shukr):** Cultivating *Shukr* is an intellectual and spiritual duty. Since Allah has given us life and everything we possess, regular prayer is the most direct physical expression of gratitude.

> **Key Term: 'Ubudiyyah**
> **'Ubudiyyah** (عُبُودِيَّة) is the state of slavery or servantship to Allah. It is not a state of forced oppression, but a state of loving submission where the human soul finds true freedom by serving the Creator rather than serving creation.

---

## Page 3: The Ash'ari Concept of Causality (Asbab)

A fundamental principle in the Ash'ari creed is our understanding of **causes and effects (*Asbab wa Musabbabat*)**. 

*   **No Inherent Power:** We believe that physical objects and actions have no independent power to cause effects. For example, fire does not burn by its own power; rather, Allah creates the burning *at the moment* the fire touches wood.
*   **Water and Purification:** Similarly, water does not possess an inherent, magical physical power to wash away a spiritual state like *hadath* (ritual impurity). 
*   **The Power of Command:** The purification occurs purely because we submit to **Allah’s command** to wash. When we do Wudu, we are using the physical means (*sabab*) out of obedience, but we believe that the true Creator of our spiritual purity is Allah.
*   **Tayammum Proof:** This is why, when water is missing, Allah commands us to wipe our faces with dry earth (*sa'id*). Physically, dirt doesn't clean us; but spiritually, it purifies us because we are obeying the Creator. The ritual itself is a training ground for submission.

## Page 4: The Historical Context of the Maliki School

To build your fiqh literacy, it helps to know how the Maliki school developed.

### 1. The Imam of Madinah: Malik ibn Anas
Imam Malik (93–179 AH) lived his entire life in Madinah, the city where the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh) established the first Islamic society and where he is buried. Imam Malik was a student of the *Tabi'un* (the generation after the Companions), who in turn learned directly from the Companions (*Sahabah*).

### 2. The Practice of the People of Madinah ('Amal Ahl al-Madinah)
Imam Malik did not rely solely on written texts or single, isolated narrations (*Hadith Ahad*). He argued that the continuous, daily, public practice of the thousands of people living in Madinah was a stronger proof of the Prophet's practice. 
*   *Why?* The Prophet (pbuh) prayed in public in Madinah five times a day for ten years, in front of thousands of Companions. They taught their children, who taught their children. 
*   Imam Malik argued: *"The narration of thousands from thousands is more reliable than the narration of one person from one person."* This is a cornerstone of Maliki legal thought.

## Page 5: The Concept of Purification (Taharah)

In Islamic law, **Taharah** is a prerequisite (*shart*) for the validity of prayer. You cannot stand before Allah in a state of impurity.

### Outer vs. Inner Taharah
*   **Taharah al-Batin (Inner Purity):** This is the purification of the heart (*qalb*) from spiritual diseases such as pride (*kibr*), envy (*hasad*), showing off (*riya'*), and doubt. This is achieved through repentance (*tawbah*) and remembering Allah (*dhikr*).
*   **Taharah al-Zahir (Outer Purity):** This is the physical removal of impurities from the body, clothes, and place of prayer.

### The Two Barriers to Salah
To prepare for prayer, a student of fiqh must understand the two types of barriers:
1.  **Najasah (Physical Impurity):** A physical substance that is legally ruled dirty (e.g., urine, blood, pus, wine, stool, or dog saliva). If *najasah* lands on your body or clothing, it must be physically washed away with water.
2.  **Hadath (Ritual Impurity):** A non-physical, spiritual barrier. You cannot see *hadath*. It is a state that a person enters when they perform certain actions.
    *   **Hadath Asghar (Minor Impurity):** Triggered by sleeping, losing consciousness, or passing wind/using the bathroom. It is removed by performing **Wudu**.
    *   **Hadath Akbar (Major Impurity):** Triggered by intimacy, emissions, or the end of menstruation/post-natal bleeding. It is removed by performing **Ghusl**.

## Page 6: Water — The Instrument of Purification

Water is the primary means of removing both *najasah* and *hadath*. However, not all liquids are legally valid for ritual purification. In the Maliki school, water is systematically classified into three distinct categories based on its attributes: **color**, **taste**, and **smell**.

### 1. Al-Ma' al-Mutlaq / Al-Tahur (الْمَاء الْمُطْلَق / الطَّهُور)
*   **Definition:** Water that remains in its original, natural state, retaining all its natural characteristics (color, taste, and smell).
*   **Examples:** Rainwater, river water, sea water, spring water, well water, and melted snow.
*   **Ruling:** It is pure in itself (*tahir fi nafsihi*) and purifying for others (*mutahhir li-ghayrihi*). This is the **only** type of water that can remove *hadath* or wash away *najasah*.
*   *Note:* Natural changes (such as water standing for a long time, or containing natural elements like mud, algae, or sulfur from the ground) do not strip the water of its *Mutlaq* status.

---

## Page 7: Mixed Water and the Maliki Distinction

### 2. Al-Ma' al-Tahir (الْمَاء الطَّاهِر)
*   **Definition:** Purifying water that has been mixed with a **pure** (*tahir*) substance (e.g., soap, vinegar, milk, tea, perfume), causing a significant change in its color, taste, or smell.
*   **The Litmus Test:** Does it change the *name* of the liquid? If a bystander looks at it and says, *"That is tea"* or *"That is rosewater"* instead of simply *"That is water"*, it has lost its *Mutlaq* (unrestricted) status.
*   **Ruling:** It is pure (you can drink it, cook with it, and it will not make your clothes dirty), but it is **non-purifying**. You **cannot** use it to perform Wudu or Ghusl.

### 3. Al-Ma' al-Najis (الْمَاء النَّجِس)
*   **Definition:** Water that has been altered in color, taste, or smell by an **impurity** (*najasah*).
*   **Ruling:** It is impure. It cannot be used for Wudu, Ghusl, or drinking.
*   **The Crucial Maliki Distinction (No Qullatayn Threshold):**
    *   *What if a small impurity falls into water, but does NOT change its color, taste, or smell?*
    *   **The Maliki Position:** The water remains **Tahur** (purifying). Imam Malik argued that water is purifying by nature and cannot be made impure unless the impurity actually alters its characteristics. However, if the water volume is small, it is deemed **Makruh** (disliked) to use it for Wudu if alternative water is available.
    *   **Contrast with Other Schools:** The Shafi'i school rules that if water is less than **Qullatayn** (approx. 200 liters), it automatically becomes *najis* when an impurity falls into it, even if the color, taste, and smell remain completely unchanged. Understanding this difference helps you see how scholars looked at the same texts but deduced different, logical rules!

## Page 8: Step-by-Step Wudu — The Obligations (Fara'id)

In the Maliki school, a valid Wudu depends on **seven obligatory pillars (*Fara'id*)**. If you omit one of these, your Wudu is invalid.

### The 7 Fara'id of Wudu:

1.  **Intention (Niyyah - نِيَّة):**
    *   The resolution in the heart to remove ritual impurity or make prayer permissible.
    *   *Rule:* It must coincide exactly with the start of the Wudu (i.e., when you wash the face, or when you wash the hands at the start if you intend that wash as part of the Wudu).
2.  **Washing the Face (Ghasl al-Wajh - غَسْل الْوَجْه):**
    *   From the normal hairline to the chin, and from earlobe to earlobe. This includes washing through a thin beard.
3.  **Washing the Arms (Ghasl al-Yadayn - غَسْل الْيَدَيْن):**
    *   Washing both arms from the fingertips up to and including the elbows.
4.  **Wiping the Entire Head (Mash al-Ra's - مَسْح الرَّأْس):**
    *   Wiping the **entirety** of the head, from the front hairline to the nape of the neck.
    *   *Why?* Malikis hold that the Quranic command *"Wipe your heads"* requires covering the entire area. The Shafi'i school, however, allows wiping just a few hairs, while the Hanafi school requires wiping a quarter of the head.
5.  **Washing the Feet (Ghasl al-Rijlayn - غَسْل الرِّجْلَيْن):**
    *   Washing both feet up to and including the ankles.
6.  **Rubbing (Dalk - دَلْك):**
    *   Passing the hand over the body part during or immediately after pouring water, before the water dries. Malikis hold that water running over a limb is not "washing" (*ghasl*) without physical contact.
7.  **Continuity (Muwalat - مُوَالَاة):**
    *   Performing the steps consecutively without a delay that would allow the previously washed limb to dry under normal weather conditions.

## Page 9: The Sunnahs and Recommended Acts of Wudu

To achieve the full reward of the Prophet's (pbuh) method, we incorporate the **Sunnahs** and **Fada'il** (recommendations).

### The Sunnahs of Wudu:
1.  Washing the hands to the wrists at the beginning of Wudu (before washing the face).
2.  Rinsing the mouth (**Madmadah** - مَضْمَضَة).
3.  Sniffing water into the nose (**Istinshaq** - اِسْتِنْشَاق) and expelling it (**Istinthar** - اِسْتِنْثَار).
4.  Returning the wipe of the head (from the nape of the neck back to the front hairline).
5.  Wiping the ears (both the inner canals and outer surfaces).
6.  Using fresh water to wipe the ears (not using the water left over from wiping the head).
7.  **The Order (Tarteeb - تَرْتِيب):**
    *   *Fiqh Detail:* In the Maliki school, keeping the obligations in order (washing face, then arms, then head, then feet) is a **Sunnah**, not a Fard. If a person accidentally washes their feet before wiping their head, their Wudu is technically valid, though they have missed a Sunnah. In contrast, the Shafi'i and Hanbali schools classify *Tarteeb* as an absolute obligation (*Fard*).

### The Recommended Acts (Fada'il):
*   Saying *Bismillah* at the start.
*   Using the tooth-stick (*Siwak*).
*   Washing the limbs twice or three times (the first is Fard, the rest are Fada'il).
*   Minimizing the use of water to avoid waste (*israf*).

## Page 11: Ghusl — The Ritual Bath

When a believer enters a state of major ritual impurity (**Hadath Akbar**), Wudu is insufficient. They must perform **Ghusl** (غُسْل) to restore their purity.

### When is Ghusl Required?
1.  **Janabah (Major Impurity):** Due to marital relations or emissions.
2.  **Hayd (Menstruation):** Once the menstrual flow ceases.
3.  **Nifas (Post-Natal Bleeding):** Once the post-childbirth bleeding ceases.
4.  **Death:** The ritual washing of a deceased Muslim.

### The Obligations (Fara'id) of Ghusl:
1.  **Intention (*Niyyah*):** Intending to lift the major impurity.
2.  **Continuity (*Muwalat*):** Performing the bath in one continuous session.
3.  **Rubbing (*Dalk*):** Rubbing the entire body with the hands.
4.  **Washing the Entire Body:** Ensuring water reaches every part of the skin, including the navel, underarms, and behind the knees.
5.  **Permeating the Hair (*Khilal al-Sha'r*):** Rubbing water to ensure it reaches the roots of all hair on the head, face, and body.
    *   *Note:* Unlike some other schools, the Maliki school does not require women to undo tight braids for Ghusl, *on the condition* that water can reach the scalp through the roots of the braided hair. If the braids prevent water from reaching the scalp, they must be undone.

## Page 12: Tayammum — Dry Purification

Islam is a practical religion designed to accommodate human weakness and changing circumstances. If water is unavailable or harmful to use, a believer performs **Tayammum** (تَيَمُّم).

### Conditions that Allow Tayammum:
*   **Absence of Water:** Having no water within a reasonable distance.
*   **Fear of Harm:** Using water would cause or worsen a severe medical condition, or the water is freezing and there is no way to heat it.
*   **Access Issues:** Water is present, but guarded by a dangerous animal or enemy, or the water is needed strictly for drinking to survive.

### The Legal Ground Substance: Sa'id (صَعِيد)
In the Maliki school, you must perform Tayammum on **Sa'id** (صَعِيد) – defined as the natural surface of the earth.
*   **Valid:** Soil, sand, solid stone, gravel, and natural dust.
*   **Invalid:** Any item that has been chemically altered, manufactured, or is organic but not part of the earth's crust (e.g., wood, carpets, textiles, plastic).

### The Steps of Tayammum (Maliki View):
1.  **Intention (*Niyyah*):** Intending to make prayer permissible (*istibahat al-salah*).
2.  **First Strike (*Al-Darbah al-Ula*):** Striking the *sa'id* with the palms of the hands.
3.  **Wiping the Face:** Wiping the entire face once.
4.  **Wiping the Hands and Arms:** Wiping the hands and forearms.
    *   *Fiqh Detail:* Wiping up to the **wrists** is the obligation (*Fard*). Striking the earth a second time and wiping up to the **elbows** is a recommended **Sunnah**.

## Page 13: Salah — The Spiritual Pillar and Cosmic Rhythms

Salah is not just a ritual; it is the center of a Muslim's day. It aligns our lives with cosmic cycles.

### The Concept of 'Imad al-Din (Pillar of Religion)
The Prophet (pbuh) taught us that Salah is the **'Imad al-Din** (عِمَاد الدِّين) – the pillar of the religion. In a physical structure, if the pillar is removed, the roof collapses. 
*   **In theology (Aqidah):** The Ash'ari creed teaches that Allah is independent of our prayers. However, our souls are created with a natural thirst to connect with the divine. Salah serves as the structural pillar of our spiritual life, protecting us from falling into heedlessness (*ghaflah*).

### The Cosmic Windows of Salah
The times of the five prayers are determined by the sun's position, linking our worship to the universe:
1.  **Subh (Fajr):** True dawn (*al-fajr al-sadiq*) until sunrise.
2.  **Zuhr:** From the sun's declination (*zawal*) from its meridian until the shadow of an object equals its height.
3.  **'Asr:** From the end of Zuhr shadow until the sun sets.
4.  **Maghrib:** From sunset until the red twilight (*al-shafaq al-ahmar*) disappears.
5.  **'Isha:** From the disappearance of the red twilight until true dawn.

### Preferred (Ikhtiyari) vs. Necessary (Daruri) Times
A unique feature of Maliki fiqh is the distinction between:
*   **Waqt Ikhtiyari (Preferred Time):** The window of time where a person is free to pray under normal conditions.
*   **Waqt Daruri (Necessary Time):** An emergency window (e.g., for someone who was asleep, ill, or traveling) that extends until the next prayer. Praying in this window is valid, but delaying it to this time without a valid excuse is sinful.

## Page 14: The Posture of Standing — Sadl in the Maliki School

When you pray in a congregation, you may notice different physical postures during the standing phase (**Qiyam** - قِيَام). 

### The Posture of Sadl (سَدْل)
In the Maliki school, the primary, dominant position for the arms during the standing portion of the obligatory prayer (*Fard*) is **Sadl** – letting the arms hang naturally and relaxed at the sides of the body.

### The Legal Evidences for Sadl:
1.  **'Amal Ahl al-Madinah (Practice of the People of Madinah):** 
    Imam Malik prioritized the inherited, continuous practice of the people of Madinah over isolated texts. He observed that the generation of the scholars, the children of the Companions, and the righteous of Madinah stood in prayer with their arms at their sides.
2.  **The Mudawwanah:** 
    In the *Mudawwanah al-Kubra*, the chief record of Maliki rulings, Ibn Qasim reports that Imam Malik said: *"I do not know of [clasping the hands] in the obligatory prayer,"* and he disliked it, although he permitted it in supererogatory (*nafl*) prayers to help rest the arms if the standing was long.

### The Posture of Qabd (قَبْض)
*   **Qabd** refers to clasping the right hand over the left hand/wrist on the chest or below the navel.
*   This is the preferred position in the Shafi'i, Hanafi, and Hanbali schools.
*   **Developing Fiqh Literacy:** 
    Both *Sadl* and *Qabd* are traced back to the Prophet (pbuh). The difference is simply in how scholars evaluated historical records. A Maliki standing in *sadl* and a Shafi'i standing in *qabd* are both performing authentic Sunnahs. Recognizing this prevents division and builds mutual respect.

## Page 15: Primary Sources & Bibliography

For students who wish to conduct further research, here is a list of the foundational texts used to compile this curriculum:

1.  **Al-Muwatta (الْمُوَطَّأ)** by Imam Malik ibn Anas (d. 179 AH). The primary source of Hadith and legal opinions of the people of Madinah.
2.  **Al-Mudawwanah al-Kubra (الْمُدَوَّنَة الْكُبْرَى)** by Imam Sahnun (d. 240 AH), containing the narrations of Ibn Qasim from Imam Malik. The ultimate textbook of Maliki jurisprudence.
3.  **Al-Risalah (الرِّسَالَة)** by Ibn Abi Zayd al-Qayrawani (d. 386 AH). A classic introductory manual summarizing Maliki creed and law.
4.  **Al-Kafi fi Fiqh Ahl al-Madinah (الْكَافِي فِي فِقْه أَهْل الْمَدِينَة)** by Ibn Abd al-Barr (d. 463 AH). A comprehensive manual on the legal rulings of Madinah.
5.  **Aqidah al-Murshidah (الْعَقِيدَة الْمُرْشِدَة)** and the works of Imam al-Sanusi (d. 895 AH), representing key manuals of the Ash'ari theological school.
