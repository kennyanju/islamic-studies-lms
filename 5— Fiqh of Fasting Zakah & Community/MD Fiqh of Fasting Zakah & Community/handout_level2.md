## 1. The Fiqh Pillars of Fasting (Sawm)

In Islamic jurisprudence, **Sawm** (fasting) is a major act of worship established upon **Two Essential Pillars (*Arkan*)**:
1. **The Intention (*Al-Niyyah*):** Dedicated in the heart purely for the sake of Allah before true dawn (*Fajr*).
2. **Abstinence (*Al-Imsak*):** Completely refraining from food, drink, and anything entering the body cavity from the break of dawn until the complete setting of the sun (*Maghrib*).

### The Monthly Intention in Maliki Jurisprudence

| Legal School | Rule for Forming the Intention in Ramadan | Evidence & Practical Benefit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Maliki School** (*Mashhur* Position) | A single comprehensive *Niyyah* formed on the first night of Ramadan is sufficient for the entire uninterrupted 30 days (*Al-Murshid al-Mu'in*). | Fasting Ramadan is viewed as one continuous, unified act of worship. (A new intention is only needed if the fast is interrupted by illness or travel). |
| **Hanafi, Shafi'i & Hanbali Schools** | A separate intention must be formed in the heart every night before dawn. | Each individual day is viewed as a separate act of worship requiring its own intention. |

## 2. Who is Granted a Concession (*Rukhsah*) from Fasting?

Allah is the Most Merciful and never places unbearable burdens on His servants (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:185). Islamic law provides clear concessions:
* **The Sick:** Anyone with an acute illness that would worsen with fasting may break their fast and make up the missed days later (*Qada'*).
* **The Traveler:** Anyone traveling a long distance (approximately 48 miles / 77 km or more) has the concession to break the fast and make it up later.
* **The Elderly and Chronically Ill:** Those who are permanently unable to fast do not make up days; instead, they provide a daily meal to a person in need (*Fidyah*).
* **Young Children:** Fasting is not legally obligatory until reaching puberty (*Bulugh*), but children aged 8-10 are gently encouraged to practice partial or full days to build stamina and love for worship.

## 3. Taraweeh: The Night Vigil of the Community

During the blessed nights of Ramadan, Muslims gather after the 'Isha prayer for special congregational night prayers called **Taraweeh**:
* **The Meaning:** *Taraweeh* is the plural of *Tarwihah*, meaning a brief rest, because worshippers took a short rest after every four rak'ahs while glorifying Allah.
* **The Prophetic Promise:** The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:  
  > *"Whoever stands in prayer during the nights of Ramadan with firm faith and seeking Allah's reward, all their past sins will be forgiven."* (Sahih al-Bukhari 37, Sahih Muslim 759).
* **Taraweeh in Madinah (20 and 36 Rak'ahs):**  
  * Sayyidina 'Umar ibn al-Khattab (RA) united the community in the Prophet's Mosque behind one imam for **20 rak'ahs** (*Al-Muwatta'*).  
  * Later, in the era of 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-'Aziz and Imam Malik, the scholars and residents of Madinah prayed **36 rak'ahs** (plus 3 Witr). They added extra units because the people of Makkah performed *Tawaf* around the Ka'bah after every four rak'ahs, so the people of Madinah added four rak'ahs in place of each Tawaf to match Makkah's reward! Both numbers are respected within the Maliki tradition.

## 4. Purifying Wealth: Zakah and Zakat al-Fitr

Economic justice in Islam ensures that wealth circulates throughout the entire community rather than staying concentrated in a few hands (Surah Al-Hashr 59:7).

| Dimension | Obligatory Zakah | Zakat al-Fitr (Charity of Fast-Breaking) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Legal Status** | Third Pillar of Islam (Mandatory on wealth) | Obligatory duty (*Wajib*) at the conclusion of Ramadan |
| **Who Must Pay** | Muslims whose qualifying surplus wealth exceeds the *Nisab* for one full lunar year. | The head of the household on behalf of themselves and every dependent they support (including children). |
| **What is Given** | 2.5% (one fortieth) of eligible savings and business assets. | One full **Sa'** (approximately 2.5 kg / four double-handfuls) of the local staple food (such as wheat, rice, dates, or barley). |
| **Due Time** | Once per lunar year on the owner's Zakah date. | Before the Eid al-Fitr prayer begins (Sahih al-Bukhari 1503, Sahih Muslim 984). |
| **Recipients** | Eight Quranic categories, primarily the poor and destitute (Surah At-Tawbah 9:60). | Needy Muslim families, ensuring everyone has food to celebrate Eid. |

## 5. The Sacred Journey: Introduction to Hajj and 'Umrah

The Fifth Pillar of Islam is **Hajj**, the sacred pilgrimage to the Holy City of Makkah. It is an obligation once in a lifetime for every Muslim who is physically and financially able (*Istita'ah*).

### The Core Rites of Pilgrimage:
1. **Ihram:** Entering the sacred state of consecration, wearing two simple unstitched white cloths for boys and men, and modest clothing for girls and women, symbolizing humility before Allah.
2. **Tawaf:** Circling the sacred **Ka'bah** seven times in a counter-clockwise direction, reciting prayers and glorifying the Lord of the House.
3. **Sa'i:** Walking seven times between the two hills of **Safa and Marwah**, honoring Lady Hajar's desperate search for water for her infant son, Prophet Isma'il, which Allah answered with the miraculous spring of Zamzam.
4. **The Day of 'Arafah (9th of Dhul-Hijjah):** Standing in supplication on the plain of 'Arafah from midday until sunset. The Prophet ﷺ said: *"Hajj is 'Arafah."* (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 889, Sahih).
5. **'Umrah (The Lesser Pilgrimage):** Consists of Ihram, Tawaf, and Sa'i, and can be performed at any time of the year.

## 6. Curriculum Anchors in Practice

1. **Setting Your Intention:** On the first night of Ramadan, join your family in setting a conscious, sincere intention in your heart to fast the month for the pleasure of Allah.
2. **Community Prayer:** Attend the mosque with your parents for 'Isha and Taraweeh, practicing standing quietly and listening to the recitation of the Quran.
3. **Helping with Zakat al-Fitr:** Assist your family in measuring or packing staple foods (like bags of rice or flour) before Eid, remembering that charity connects your heart to families in need.
