## 1. The Legal Maxim of Commerce: *Al-Aslu fil-Mu'amalati al-Ibahah*

In classical jurisprudence, human actions are divided into **'Ibadat (Ritual Worship)** and **Mu'amalat (Commercial & Civil Transactions)**.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   THE TWO POLAR JURISPRUDENTIAL MAXIMS                 │
│                                                                        │
│ • IN RITUAL WORSHIP ('IBADAT):                                         │
│   "Al-Aslu fil-'Ibadati al-Hazr wal-Man'"                              │
│   The default baseline is PROHIBITION. No act of worship can be       │
│   invented unless explicitly sanctioned by Divine Revelation.          │
│                                                                        │
│ • IN COMMERCE & TRANSACTIONS (MU'AMALAT):                              │
│   "Al-Aslu fil-Mu'amalati al-Ibahah"                                   │
│   The default baseline is PERMISSIBILITY. Any contract, trade, tech   │
│   platform, or business structure is halal unless it violates an       │
│   explicit Shari'ah prohibition!                                       │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 2. The Three Cardinal Prohibitions in Islamic Commercial Law

Islamic financial law protects human society by strictly prohibiting three structural evils:

```
       ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
       │             THE THREE PILLARS OF ECONOMIC HARM         │
       └───────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
              ┌────────────────────┼────────────────────┐
              ▼                    ▼                    ▼
       1. Al-Riba           2. Al-Gharar          3. Al-Maysir / Qimar
       (Usury / Interest)   (Deception / Hazard)  (Gambling / Speculation)
       Unearned surplus on  Contractual ambiguity Zero-sum wealth extraction
       loans or unequal     over existence,       based purely on chance;
       exchange of Ribawi   delivery, or price of derivatives, casinos,
       commodities.         the asset.            lotteries.
```

### A. Riba (Usury / Exploitative Surplus):
1. **Riba al-Duyun (Riba of Debts):** Any stipulated increase or premium charged over the principal of a loan in exchange for time. Whether 1% or 30%, simple or compounded, charging interest on debt is unanimously forbidden (*Haram Qat'i*) in Islam.
2. **Riba al-Buyu' (Riba of Sales/Exchange):** Unequal or delayed barter exchange of the **Six Ribawi Commodities** named by the Prophet ﷺ: **Gold, Silver, Wheat, Barley, Dates, and Salt**.  
   *Rule:* *"Like for like, equal for equal, hand to hand."*

┌──────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐

## 4. Contemporary Islamic Finance Contracts & Fintech

Modern Islamic banks do not lend money at interest; instead, they act as trading partners, asset managers, and equity participants using classical contract vehicles approved by AAOIFI (Accounting and Auditing Organization for Islamic Financial Institutions):

1. **Murabaha (Cost-Plus Sale):** The financier purchases an identified tangible asset (e.g. car or machinery) for cash, takes ownership risk, and resells it to the client at an agreed cost plus transparent markup paid in installments.
2. **Ijarah (Leasing):** Transferring the usufruct (use) of an asset (e.g. property, aircraft) for a specified rental fee while the bank retains asset ownership risk.
3. **Musharakah (Equity Partnership):** Co-ownership venture where partners contribute capital, share profits according to agreed ratios, and absorb financial losses strictly in proportion to their equity capital share.
4. **Mudarabah (Trust Financing):** Capital provider (*Rabb al-Mal*) partners with expertise/labor (*Mudarib*). Profits are shared by contract; capital losses are borne solely by the capital provider unless caused by manager negligence.
5. **Diminishing Musharakah (*Musharakah Mutanaqisah*):** The predominant modern model for **Islamic Home Financing**. The bank and client co-purchase a home. The client pays rent on the bank's share while gradually buying out the bank's equity units until 100% home ownership transfers to the client!
6. **Takaful (Islamic Mutual Insurance):** Replacing the Gharar and interest of commercial insurance with cooperative risk sharing. Policyholders make donations (*Tabarru'*) to a shared mutual indemnity pool to assist members facing losses.

1. **Macroeconomic Critique:** Write an analysis on how interest-based debt economies generate wealth concentration, inflation, and systemic financial crashes (e.g. the 2008 Subprime Mortgage Crisis), contrasting this with asset-backed Islamic risk-sharing.
