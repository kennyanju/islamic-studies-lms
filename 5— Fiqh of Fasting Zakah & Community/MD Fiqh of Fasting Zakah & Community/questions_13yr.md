### Part B: Case Studies & Scenario Analysis (6 Questions)
*Analyze the following cases and determine the rulings according to Maliki Fiqh.*

9.  **Case Study 1: The Forgotton Intention**
    *   Tariq was busy on the night before the first day of Ramadan and went to sleep without making any intention to fast. He woke up after Fajr had begun and realized he had not made the intention. He abstained from food and drink for the rest of the day. What is the status of his fast for that day, and what must he do?
    *   *Analysis:* Tariq's fast for that first day is invalid because the first pillar of fasting, the Intention (*Niyyah*), was missing before the start of the fast (Fajr). Tariq must perform **Qada'** (make up this day after Ramadan). He must also make a new intention that night for the rest of the month, which will then cover the remaining days of Ramadan.

10. **Case Study 2: The Dentist Visit**
    *   While fasting during Ramadan, Yasmine had an emergency dental appointment. During the procedure, some water from the dental tool went down her throat, and she swallowed it. She did not intend to swallow it and tried her best to avoid it. Is her fast valid?
    *   *Analysis:* In the Maliki school, Yasmine's fast is broken because a substance entered her stomach cavity during the day. Even though it was accidental and without intent, the physical reality of the fast was interrupted. She must perform **Qada'** (make up this day after Ramadan), but she is not sinful and does not owe *Kaffarah*.

11. **Case Study 3: The Currency Zakah**
    *   Zayd has $12,000 in cash savings. The Nisab threshold for the year (calculated based on 85g of pure gold) is $6,000. Zayd's savings remained above $6,000 for a full lunar year (*Hawl*). How does Zayd calculate his Zakah, and what is the final amount he must pay?
    *   *Analysis:* Zayd meets all conditions: he is Muslim, has full ownership, his wealth is above the *Nisab* ($12,000 > $6,000), and a full *Hawl* has passed. The Zakah rate is 2.5%.
    *   *Calculation:* $12,000 x 0.025 (or $12,000 / 40) = **$300**. Zayd must pay $300 to the eligible categories of Zakah recipients.

12. **Case Study 4: The Eid Delay**
    *   Aisha was preparing to pay Zakat al-Fitr for her family. She forgot to distribute the food on Eid morning before the prayer. She remembered it while walking home after the Eid khutbah (sermon) was completed. She immediately went and gave the food to a poor family. Did she perform her duty correctly?
    *   *Analysis:* Aisha's payment is still valid as Zakat al-Fitr, but because she delayed it until after the Eid prayer without a valid excuse, her action is classified as **Makruh** (disliked). She should make sure to distribute it before the prayer in the future to follow the recommended Sunnah timing.

13. **Case Study 5: The Crop Harvest**
    *   A farmer in Morocco harvests 1,000 kg of wheat. The wheat was irrigated entirely by natural rainfall without any artificial pumps or cost. Does the farmer need to wait for a lunar year (*Hawl*) to pass before paying Zakah on this crop? What is the Zakah rate?
    *   *Analysis:* No, agricultural crops are exempt from the *Hawl* requirement; Zakah is due immediately at harvest (*"And give its due on the day of its harvest"*). Since the crop was irrigated naturally by rain (without labor/pumps), the Zakah rate is **10%** (one-tenth) of the harvest, which is 100 kg of wheat. (If it had been irrigated artificially with pumps, the rate would be 5%).

14. **Case Study 6: The Hungry Neighbor**
    *   In a small suburban community, a family lost their income and had no food. Several neighbors knew about their situation but assumed "someone else would help." By the end of the week, the family had to go to a local shelter for emergency food. Did the neighbors commit a sin?
    *   *Analysis:* Yes, they committed a sin. Relieving hunger in the community is a **Fard Kifayah** (communal obligation). Because the obligation was not fulfilled by anyone in the immediate vicinity who knew about it, the sin of neglecting the poor falls upon all the neighbors who were aware of the need and had the means to help.

### Part C: Analytical Essay Questions (6 Questions)
*Provide a detailed, structured explanation for each prompt.*

15. **Explain the theological concept of Al-Ghani (The Self-Sufficient) and how it affects a Muslim's psychological attitude toward performing acts of worship like Siyam and Zakah.**
    *   *Suggested Outline:*
        *   Define *Al-Ghani* (Allah is free of all needs) and contrast it with human *Iftiqar* (complete dependence).
        *   Explain that acts of worship (like starving during fasting or giving up money in Zakah) do not benefit Allah or change His eternal state.
        *   Discuss the psychological shift: Worship is not a "favor" we do for Allah, nor is it a burden imposed by a needy deity. Instead, it is a spiritual medicine for our own souls. This realization fosters humility, sincerity (*Ikhlas*), and gratitude (*Shukr*).

16. **Analyze the legal reasoning behind the Maliki school's ruling that a single intention (*Niyyah Wahidah*) at the start of Ramadan is sufficient. Contrast this with the Shafi'i/Hanafi requirement for a daily intention.**
    *   *Suggested Outline:*
        *   Define the Maliki position: One intention on the night before the first day of Ramadan covers the entire month of continuous fasting.
        *   Explain the legal logic: Imam Malik viewed the 30 days of Ramadan as a single, indivisible package of worship (like a single prayer consisting of multiple rak'ahs). Therefore, a single intention at the start suffices.
        *   Contrast with the Shafi'i and Hanafi schools: They view each day of Ramadan as a distinct, independent act of worship because the days are separated by nights (when fasting is forbidden). Therefore, they require a daily *Niyyah*.
        *   Highlight how this comparative analysis builds fiqh literacy by showing how different, coherent legal models can be built from the same starting points.

17. **Evaluate the Maliki preference for feeding 60 poor people over fasting two months for *Kaffarah*. How does this reflect the social objectives (*Maqasid*) of Islamic law?**
    *   *Suggested Outline:*
        *   Define *Kaffarah* and the three options (feeding poor, fasting, freeing a slave).
        *   State the Maliki position: Feeding 60 poor people is the highly preferred option.
        *   Explain the legal/philosophical logic: Fasting for two months has a private, individual benefit (*Qasir*). Feeding 60 poor people has a communal, transitive benefit (*Mutata'addi*) that actively relieves hunger and strengthens social bonds.
        *   Discuss how this choice aligns with the broader *Maqasid al-Shariah* (objectives of Islamic law), which prioritize public benefit and social welfare.

18. **Discuss the historical and legal significance of the Prophet's *Sa'* and *Mudd* measurements in the Maliki school, referencing the concept of *'Amal Ahl al-Madinah* (Practice of the People of Madinah).**
    *   *Suggested Outline:*
        *   Define *Sa'* (4 Amdad) and *Mudd* (a double-handful scoop) as volumetric measures.
        *   Explain how the Maliki school verified these measurements: Imam Malik lived in Madinah, studying among the descendants of the Companions. He checked the actual physical measuring vessels preserved in Madinah, which had been blessed by the Prophet (pbuh).
        *   Explain *'Amal Ahl al-Madinah*: Imam Malik argued that the continuous, daily, public practice of the people of Madinah was a stronger proof than isolated individual narrations. The physical transmission of the *Sa'* vessel is a prime example of this methodology.

19. **Compare the Maliki and Hanafi views on Zakah being obligatory on the wealth of children and the insane. How does this reflect two distinct legal definitions of Zakah?**
    *   *Suggested Outline:*
        *   State the Maliki position: Zakah is due on the wealth of children and the insane (paid by their guardians).
        *   State the Hanafi position: Zakah is not due on their wealth.
        *   Analyze the legal definition of Zakah in each school:
            *   *Hanafis:* Define Zakah primarily as an act of personal physical-financial worship (*Ibadah*), which requires intention and legal accountability (*Taklif*). Since children are not *Mukallaf* (accountable), they are exempt.
            *   *Malikis:* Define Zakah primarily as a financial right belonging to the poor (*Hakk Mali*) that is attached to the wealth itself, not the owner. If the wealth exists and meets the threshold, the poor have a right to their share, regardless of who owns it.

20. **Outline the etiquettes of Eid al-Fitr and analyze how celebrating Eid functions as a form of spiritual worship rather than a secular holiday.**
    *   *Suggested Outline:*
        *   List the key etiquettes: Ghusl, fine clothing, eating dates before prayer, reciting Takbeerat, taking different paths.
        *   Explain that on Eid day, fasting is strictly forbidden (*Haram*) and eating is an act of obedience.
        *   Analyze the spiritual nature of Eid joy: It is not about self-indulgence, but about celebrating the grace of Allah (*Fadl Allah*) in completing the fast. The celebration is anchored by the Eid prayer and Takbeerat, showing that in Islam, joy and worship are intimately connected.
