## 1. Fasting (*Sawm*): The Shield of Taqwa

At ages 10–12, you are transitioning into observing full days of fasting during the blessed month of Ramadan. Fasting is not merely going hungry or thirsty; it is a profound spiritual academy designed to build **Taqwa** (God-consciousness, self-restraint, and empathy for the less fortunate).

Allah says in the Quran:  
*“O you who have believed, decreed upon you is fasting as it was decreed upon those before you that you may attain Taqwa.”* (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:183).

## 2. The Unique Maliki Position on Niyyah

One of the great practical mercies of the **Maliki school** is its ruling on the intention (*Niyyah*) for Ramadan:
* **The Ruling:** A person only needs to make a **single intention on the first night of Ramadan** to fast the entire month! Because Ramadan is an indivisible, unified period of consecutive worship, that single opening intention carries through all 29 or 30 days.
* **When Must Niyyah Be Renewed?** You only need to make a new intention if your consecutive fast is interrupted by a valid excuse—such as falling ill, traveling beyond 48 miles, or menses. Once you resume fasting, you make a fresh intention.

## 3. What Invalidates the Fast vs. What Does Not?

Understanding the precise rules protects you from unnecessary worry and preserves your worship:

### A. What Breaks the Fast (*Mubtilat*):
1. **Eating or drinking intentionally** between the true dawn and sunset.
2. **Anything reaching the stomach / digestive tract** through an open passage (e.g. swallowing toothpaste, taking oral pills, or swallowing phlegm intentionally).
3. **Intentional vomiting.**

### B. What Does NOT Break the Fast:
* **Eating or drinking out of pure forgetfulness:** If you forget you are fasting and drink a glass of water, your fast remains completely valid! The Prophet ﷺ said: *"Whoever forgets that he is fasting and eats or drinks, let him complete his fast, for it was Allah who fed him and gave him drink."* (Bukhari).
* **Using a dry toothbrush or natural Siwak:** Permissible throughout the entire day.
* **Tasting food on the tip of the tongue** for cooking without swallowing any of it.
* **Injections:** Non-nutritive medical injections (e.g. insulin, pain relief, vaccines) do not break the fast because they do not enter the stomach through a natural orifice.
* **Unintentional swallowing of dust, water droplets while rinsing wudu, or saliva.**

---

## 4. Valid Exemptions: Who May Break the Fast?

Islam never demands that which causes severe physical destruction:
1. **The Sick Person:** If fasting will worsen an acute illness or delay recovery. They make up the missed days (*Qada'*) when healthy.
2. **The Traveler:** Traveling beyond the shortening distance (48 miles / 80 km) in a lawful journey.
3. **The Chronically Ill & Elderly:** Those physically incapable of fasting pay **Fidyah** (feeding one poor person a Mudd of food for every missed day).
4. **Pregnant and Nursing Mothers:** If they fear harm for their own health or their infant's health.

## 5. Sunnahs and Social Joys of Ramadan

* **Al-Suhur (Pre-Dawn Meal):** The Prophet ﷺ said: *"Take Suhur, for indeed in Suhur there is blessing."* Delaying Suhur until shortly before Fajr is a confirmed Sunnah.
* **Hasten to Break the Fast (*Ta'jil al-Fitr*):** Breaking the fast immediately upon sunset with fresh dates, dried dates, or water before praying Maghrib.
* **Taraweeh Prayers:** Prayed in congregation after 'Isha. In the era of Sayyidina 'Umar in Madinah, the companions prayed 20 rak'ahs with beautiful, unhurried Quranic recitation.
