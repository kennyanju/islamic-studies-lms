# Level 3 Student Handout: Fiqh of Fasting, Zakah & Community

**Age Group:** Ages 10-12 (Level 3 Track - Years 5 & 6)  
**Topics:** The Pillars of Fasting (Sawm), The Maliki Ruling on Monthly Intention, Nullifiers & Exemptions, The Maliki Ruling on Forgetfulness, The Four Pillars of Hajj, and Zakah Basics  
**Classical Text Spine:** *Matn Ibn 'Ashir* (Fasting Chapter) & *Al-Risalah* of Ibn Abi Zayd al-Qayrawani  
**Riwayah & Tajweed:** Hafs 'an 'Asim and Warsh 'an Nafi' (Juz' 30: Surah Al-Mutaffifin and Al-Infitar)

---

## 1. Fasting (*Sawm*): The Shield of Taqwa

At ages 10-12, you are transitioning into observing full days of fasting during the blessed month of Ramadan. Fasting is not merely going hungry or thirsty; it is a profound spiritual discipline designed to build **Taqwa** (God-consciousness, moral self-restraint, and empathy for the less fortunate).

Allah says in the Quran:  
*“O you who have believed, decreed upon you is fasting as it was decreed upon those before you that you may attain Taqwa.”* (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:183).

| Pillar of Fasting (*Rukn*) | Juristic Definition | Time & Condition of Validity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Al-Niyyah (The Intention)** | Dedicating the fast purely for Allah in the heart. | Formed before the break of true dawn (*Fajr*). In Maliki Fiqh, a single intention on the first night of Ramadan suffices for the entire uninterrupted month. |
| **2. Al-Imsak (Abstention)** | Completely refraining from food, drink, and all physical nullifiers. | From the arrival of true dawn (*Fajr*) until the complete setting of the sun (*Maghrib*). |

---

## 2. The Maliki Position on the Monthly Intention (*Niyyah*)

One of the distinctive, practical rulings of the **Maliki school** is its ruling on the intention for Ramadan:
* **The Relied-Upon Ruling:** A person only needs to make a **single intention on the first night of Ramadan** to fast the entire month (*Matn Ibn 'Ashir*). Because Ramadan is an indivisible, unified period of consecutive worship, that single opening intention carries through all 29 or 30 days.
* **When Must the Intention Be Renewed?** You only need to make a new intention if your consecutive fasting is interrupted by a valid excuse, such as falling ill, embarking on a lawful journey beyond 48 miles, or menses. Once you resume fasting, you make a fresh intention.

| Legal School | Ruling on Ramadan Intention | Consequence of Forgetting Before Dawn |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Maliki School** | Single opening intention covers the full continuous month. | If a person sleeps through Suhur without a new intention, their fast is valid because the opening monthly intention remains active. |
| **Shafi'i & Hanbali Schools** | A separate intention must be made every night before dawn. | If a person forgets to intend before Fajr, the fast for that day is invalid and must be made up. |

## 3. What Invalidates the Fast vs. What Does Not?

Understanding the precise rules protects you from unnecessary worry and preserves your worship:

### A. What Breaks the Fast (*Mubtilat*):
1. **Eating or drinking intentionally** between true dawn and sunset.
2. **Anything reaching the stomach or digestive tract** through an open bodily entrance (such as swallowing oral medications or intentionally swallowing phlegm).
3. **Intentional vomiting (*Qay'*).**

### B. The Maliki Ruling on Forgetfulness (*Nisyan*):
* If a person eats or drinks purely out of forgetfulness during Ramadan, there is **no sin** upon them, because the Prophet ﷺ said: *"Whoever forgets that he is fasting and eats or drinks, let him complete his fast, for it was Allah who fed him and gave him drink."* (Sahih al-Bukhari 1933, Sahih Muslim 1155).
* In the relied-upon position of the Maliki school (*Al-Risalah*, *Matn Ibn 'Ashir* line 158), they must stop eating immediately, abstain for the remainder of the day out of respect for the sacred time, and **make up (*Qada'*) that single day after Ramadan** (with no *Kaffarah* penalty). In voluntary (*Nafl*) fasts, eating by mistake does not require *Qada'*.  
  *(Note: The Hanafi, Shafi'i, and Hanbali schools hold that eating out of forgetfulness does not require Qada' in either obligatory or voluntary fasts).*

### C. What Does NOT Invalidate the Fast:
* **Using a dry natural Siwak or toothbrush** without swallowing any paste or water.
* **Tasting food on the tip of the tongue** while cooking, provided none is swallowed.
* **Non-nutritive injections** (such as insulin, allergy injections, or vaccines) that enter the muscle or veins, because they do not enter through an open digestive passage.
* **Unintentional swallowing** of dust or water droplets while performing Wudu.

## 4. Valid Exemptions: Who May Break the Fast?

Islam never demands actions that cause severe bodily harm:
1. **The Sick Person:** If fasting will worsen an acute illness or significantly delay recovery. They make up the missed days (*Qada'*) when healthy.
2. **The Traveler:** Traveling beyond the shortening distance (approximately 48 miles / 80 km) on a lawful journey.
3. **The Chronically Ill and Elderly:** Those permanently unable to fast pay **Fidyah** (feeding one poor person a Mudd of staple food for each missed day of Ramadan - Surah Al-Baqarah 2:184).

## 5. The Four Pillars (*Arkan*) of Hajj in Maliki Fiqh

Hajj is the Fifth Pillar of Islam. In the Maliki legal tradition, Hajj rests upon **Four Indispensable Pillars**:
1. **Al-Ihram:** Entering the sacred state of consecration with the intention and reciting the Talbiyah (*Labbayk Allahumma Labbayk*).
2. **Tawaf al-Ifadah:** The obligatory seven circuits around the Ka'bah performed after descending from 'Arafah.
3. **Al-Sa'i:** Walking seven times between the hills of Safa and Marwah.
4. **Al-Wuquf bi-'Arafah:** Standing in prayer and supplication on the plain of 'Arafah from the afternoon of the 9th of Dhul-Hijjah until sunset.

*Juristic Principle:* If a pilgrim omits any of these four pillars, their Hajj is incomplete and cannot be repaired by sacrificing an animal (*Damm*); they must return to complete the missed pillar or repeat the pilgrimage.

## 6. Introduction to Zakah & Nisab

Zakah is the third pillar of Islam. It is a mandatory transfer of wealth from qualifying adults to support vulnerable members of the community (Surah At-Tawbah 9:60).
* **Nisab:** The minimum threshold of surplus wealth a Muslim must own before Zakah becomes due.
  * Gold Nisab: 20 gold dinars (approximately 85 grams of pure gold).
  * Silver Nisab: 200 silver dirhams (approximately 595 grams of pure silver).
* **Hawl:** The wealth must remain above the Nisab for one continuous lunar year (approximately 354 days).
* **Rate:** Exactly 2.5% (one fortieth) of eligible liquid assets and trade inventory.

## 7. Sunnahs and Social Traditions of Ramadan

* **Al-Suhur (Pre-Dawn Meal):** The Prophet ﷺ said: *"Take Suhur, for indeed in Suhur there is blessing."* (Sahih al-Bukhari 1923, Sahih Muslim 1095). Delaying Suhur until shortly before Fajr is a confirmed Sunnah.
* **Hastening to Break the Fast (*Ta'jil al-Fitr*):** Breaking the fast immediately upon sunset with fresh dates or water before praying Maghrib (Sahih al-Bukhari 1957).
* **Taraweeh Prayers:** Prayed in congregation after 'Isha. Sayyidina 'Umar unified the companions in Madinah for 20 rak'ahs (*Al-Muwatta'*), and during the era of Imam Malik, the community of Madinah prayed 36 rak'ahs to match the blessings of Makkah's Tawaf.

---

## 8. Curriculum Anchors in Practice

1. **Building Fasting Endurance:** Challenge yourself to complete full fasts during Ramadan, reminding yourself that thirst teaches empathy for the hungry.
2. **Safeguarding the Tongue:** Remember that fasting involves speech: avoid arguing, complaining, or harsh words, keeping your tongue moist with the remembrance of Allah.
3. **Practicing Generosity:** Participate in family charity by setting aside part of your savings for Ramadan food boxes or Eid gifts for orphans.
