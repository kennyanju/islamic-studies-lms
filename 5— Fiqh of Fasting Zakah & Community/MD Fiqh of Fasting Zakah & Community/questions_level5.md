# Level 5 Quiz: Fiqh of Fasting, Zakah & Community

## Part A: Multiple Choice Questions
1. What foundational legal maxim governs commercial transactions and business contracts (Mu'amalat) in Islamic jurisprudence?
   (A) All worldly contracts are strictly prohibited unless authorized by the local governor.
   (B) Commercial contracts must be drafted exclusively in classical Arabic to be legally binding.
   (C) Financial contracts require approval from international banking consortiums.
   (D) The default baseline in commercial dealings is permissibility (Al-Aslu fil-Mu'amalati al-Ibahah).

2. What defines "Riba al-Duyun" (Riba of Debt) in Islamic commercial law?
   (A) Any stipulated increment or interest charged above the principal of a loan in exchange for deferment or time.
   (B) A voluntary cash gift given freely by a borrower to a lender after full loan settlement.
   (C) The transparent profit margin earned by a merchant in a cash sale of clothing.
   (D) A standard banking administrative fee covering actual verified transaction expenses.

3. Which six specific commodities were named by Prophet Muhammad ﷺ in the foundational hadith of "Riba al-Buyu'"?
   (A) Camels, horses, sheep, leather, silk, and wool.
   (B) Paper currency, treasury bills, shares, bonds, corporate debentures, and real estate.
   (C) Gold, silver, wheat, barley, dates, and salt.
   (D) Olive oil, honey, vinegar, timber, copper, and iron.

4. In Ibn Rushd's *Bidayat al-Mujtahid*, what is the effective legal cause ('Illah) of Riba in foodstuffs according to the Maliki Madhhab?
   (A) Being sold by volume or weight measure.
   (B) Being a nourishing staple food that can be stored and preserved (Qut wa Iddikhar).
   (C) Being sweet and pleasant to the taste.
   (D) Being harvested from irrigated agricultural lands.

5. What constitutes the prohibited flaw of "Al-Gharar" (Hazardous Uncertainty) in contract law?
   (A) Offering seasonal promotional discounts on retail merchandise.
   (B) Utilizing digital electronic signatures on commercial invoices.
   (C) Storing excess inventory in regional commercial warehouses.
   (D) Contractual ambiguity where major terms regarding the existence, ownership, delivery, or price of the asset are unknown.

6. How does a genuine Shari'ah-compliant "Murabaha" (Cost-Plus Sale) contract operate in Islamic banking?
   (A) The bank lends money directly to the client at an interest rate fixed to central bank indices.
   (B) The client purchases an asset while the bank simply guarantees the manufacturer's warranty.
   (C) The bank purchases the asset, takes legitimate ownership title and inventory risk, and resells it to the client with a transparent markup paid in installments.
   (D) The bank and customer establish an equal equity joint venture with shared management.

7. In a classical "Mudarabah" partnership, who absorbs the financial capital loss if the venture suffers a loss without manager misconduct?
   (A) The capital provider (Rabb al-Mal) alone bears the financial loss, while the entrepreneur (Mudarib) loses their uncompensated time and effort.
   (B) The entrepreneur alone must repay all lost funds to the investor with interest.
   (C) The financial loss is divided equally between the local municipality and the investor.
   (D) The loss is subsidized entirely by the central reserve bank.

8. What contemporary financing structure is widely utilized by modern Islamic banks for Shari'ah-compliant residential home financing?
   (A) Conventional adjustable rate mortgages with compound interest.
   (B) Diminishing Musharakah (Musharakah Mutanaqisah) combined with an Ijarah (lease) agreement.
   (C) Speculative futures contracts tied to housing price indices.
   (D) Uncollateralized personal overdraft loans.

9. How does "Takaful" (Islamic Mutual Insurance) eliminate the prohibited element of Gharar found in commercial insurance?
   (A) By transferring all customer funds to offshore tax havens.
   (B) By excluding all natural disaster and vehicular coverage.
   (C) By investing all collected revenues into high-yield speculative bonds.
   (D) By structuring policy contributions as mutual charitable donations (Tabarru') to a cooperative assistance pool that pays claims.

10. In the jurisprudence of Hajj, what is the legal ruling regarding an individual who has due, immediate personal debts?
    (A) They may disregard their debts because Hajj always takes priority over financial contracts.
    (B) Their debts are automatically forgiven upon completing Tawaf al-Ifadah.
    (C) They cannot prioritize Hajj if doing so harms the creditor, and must either settle their debts or obtain explicit creditor consent.
    (D) They must transfer their personal debt obligations to a family member before traveling.

## Part D: Senior Seminar Case Study & Reflection
1. Analyze the macroeconomic destabilization caused by interest-based fractional reserve banking: How does the Islamic model of asset-backed trade prevent speculative asset bubbles?
2. Explain the juristic debate detailed in *Bidayat al-Mujtahid* regarding the 'Illah of Riba in commodities: Why did Malik restrict it to storable staples (*Qut wa Iddikhar*)?
3. Contrast a conventional home mortgage with a Diminishing Musharakah agreement: Detail how ownership, risk, and default are handled differently.
4. An individual wishing to perform Hajj has $12,000 saved, but also owes a personal debt of $10,000 that is immediately due. Explain the Islamic legal guidance regarding their pilgrimage.

## Answer Key & Marking Guide
1. D - Maxim: The default baseline in worldly commercial transactions is permissibility unless explicitly prohibited by revelation.
2. A - Riba al-Duyun covers any stipulated interest on debt, universally forbidden across all schools.
3. C - Gold, silver, wheat, barley, dates, and salt are the canonical six Ribawi commodities named in Sahih Muslim 1587.
4. B - Malik defined the 'Illah as food that sustains life (Qut) and can be preserved/stored (Iddikhar).
5. D - Gharar introduces hazardous contractual ambiguity that leads to conflict and unjust exploitation.
6. C - True Murabaha requires the financier to assume bona fide commercial ownership and inventory risk.
7. A - In Mudarabah, financial capital loss falls exclusively on the financier; the entrepreneur loses their time and effort.
8. B - Diminishing Musharakah with lease units is the global standard for Shari'ah-compliant home purchase.
9. D - Takaful transforms premiums into mutual cooperative donations (Tabarru'), removing speculative wagering.
10. C - Financial capability requires settling due debts or having creditor consent so rights are not violated.
