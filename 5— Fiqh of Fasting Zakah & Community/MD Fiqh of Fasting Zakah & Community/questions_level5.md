# Level 5 Quiz: Fiqh of Fasting, Zakah & Community

## Part A: Multiple Choice Questions
1. What foundational legal maxim governs commercial transactions and business contracts (*Mu'amalat*) in Islamic jurisprudence?
   (A) "The default state of all contracts is prohibition until proven permissible."
   (B) "The default state in commercial dealings is permissibility (*Al-Aslu fil-Mu'amalati al-Ibahah*)."
   (C) "All international commerce must be conducted in gold bullion."
   (D) "Trade is only valid if transacted within sixty seconds."

2. What constitutes "Riba al-Duyun" (Riba of Debt) in Islamic commercial law?
   (A) Exchanging identical sacks of grain hand-to-hand.
   (B) Any contractual increment or interest charged above the principal of a loan in exchange for time.
   (C) Charging a service fee for printing a paper bank statement.
   (D) Giving a voluntary gift to a creditor after repaying a debt.

3. Which six specific commodities were named by Prophet Muhammad ﷺ in the foundational hadith of "Riba al-Buyu'"?
   (A) Horses, camels, sheep, leather, silk, and wool.
   (B) Gold, silver, dates, wheat, barley, and salt.
   (C) Olive oil, honey, vinegar, pearls, timber, and copper.
   (D) Paper currency, cheques, shares, bonds, real estate, and crypto.

4. In Ibn Rushd's *Bidayat al-Mujtahid*, what is the effective legal cause (*'Illah*) of Riba in foodstuffs according to the Maliki Madhhab?
   (A) Being sweet and pleasant to the taste.
   (B) Being a nourishing staple that is storable (*Qut wa Iddikhar*).
   (C) Being sold by weight or liquid volume.
   (D) Being harvested during the spring season.

5. What is the fundamental commercial flaw of "Gharar" (Excessive Ambiguity/Deception)?
   (A) It requires the buyer to pay using electronic credit cards.
   (B) It involves selling an unknown, non-existent, or un-deliverable asset, turning trade into deceptive gamble.
   (C) It forces the merchant to discount their prices on holidays.
   (D) It limits the return policy to thirty days.

6. How does an Islamic bank execute a "Murabaha" (Cost-Plus Sale) contract?
   (A) The bank lends money at 5% interest to the customer to buy a car.
   (B) The bank purchases the vehicle, takes legitimate ownership title and risk, and resells it to the client with a transparent markup paid in installments.
   (C) The bank forces the customer to surrender their personal jewelry as collateral.
   (D) The bank borrows money from international bond markets.

7. In a classical "Mudarabah" partnership, who bears the financial capital loss if the venture fails without misconduct?
   (A) The working entrepreneur (Mudarib) alone.
   (B) The capital provider (Rabb al-Mal) alone.
   (C) The local municipal government.
   (D) The loss is split equally 50/50 between labor and capital.

8. What contemporary financing structure is widely utilized by modern Islamic banks for Shari'ah-compliant home mortgages?
   (A) Interest-only adjustable rate mortgages.
   (B) Diminishing Musharakah (Musharakah Mutanaqisah) combined with Ijarah (Lease).
   (C) Gambling lotteries for real estate deeds.
   (D) Government bond issuances.

9. How does "Takaful" (Islamic Insurance) eliminate the prohibited element of Gharar found in conventional insurance?
   (A) By requiring clients to pay double premiums.
   (B) By structuring policy contributions as mutual charitable donations (*Tabarru'*) to a cooperative assistance pool.
   (C) By investing all premiums into foreign equity markets.
   (D) By refusing to pay out claims for vehicular accidents.

10. What international regulatory body codifies global Shari'ah standards for Islamic banking and finance?
    (A) The World Trade Organization (WTO).
    (B) AAOIFI (Accounting and Auditing Organization for Islamic Financial Institutions).
    (C) The International Monetary Fund (IMF).
    (D) The Federal Reserve Board.

## Part C: Matching Financial Terms
* Riba al-Duyun ─── Contractual interest charged on loans or debt repayments
* Gharar ─── Hazardous uncertainty or deception regarding an asset's existence or delivery
* Murabaha ─── Cost-plus trade sale where profit margin is transparently fixed
* Mudarabah ─── Partnership between capital investor and expert entrepreneur
* Takaful ─── Mutual cooperative indemnity insurance eliminating commercial Gharar

3. Contrast a conventional home mortgage with a Diminishing Musharakah agreement: Detail how ownership, risk, and default are handled differently.
4. A fintech developer creates an app offering "crypto yield farming with 15% guaranteed return on stablecoin loans." Audit this structure using Islamic commercial jurisprudence.

## Answer Key & Marking Guide
1. B - Maxim: Everything in worldly transactions is halal unless explicitly prohibited by revelation.
2. B - Riba al-Duyun covers any stipulated interest on debt, universally forbidden across all schools.
3. B - Gold, silver, dates, wheat, barley, and salt are the canonical 6 Ribawi commodities.
4. B - Malik defined the 'Illah as food that sustains life (Qut) and can be preserved/stored (Iddikhar).
5. B - Gharar introduces toxic ambiguity that leads to conflict and unjust exploitation.
6. B - True Murabaha requires the financier to assume bona fide commercial ownership and inventory risk.
7. B - In Mudarabah, capital loss falls on the financier; the entrepreneur loses their time and effort.
8. B - Diminishing Musharakah with lease units is the global standard for Islamic home purchase.
9. B - Takaful transforms premiums into mutual cooperative donations (*Tabarru'*), removing speculative wagering.
