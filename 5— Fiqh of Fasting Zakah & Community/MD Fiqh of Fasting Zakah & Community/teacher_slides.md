# Presentation Slides: Ramadan, Zakah, & Community Responsibility
## A Guide for Teachers (Maliki Fiqh & Ash'ari Aqidah)

# Slide 1: Welcome & Course Overview
## Ramadan, Zakah, and the Community: The Social and Spiritual Pillars
*   **Subject:** Islamic Studies (Fiqh, Aqidah, and Social Ethics)
*   **Fiqh School:** Maliki (*Madhhab*)
*   **Creed School:** Ash'ari
*   **Core Focus:** Understanding fasting (*Siyam*) and obligatory charity (*Zakah*) not just as physical rituals, but as spiritual purifiers and tools for community justice.
*   **Key Themes:**
    *   The spiritual purpose of fasting and the theological concept of *Al-Ghani*.
    *   The Maliki jurisprudence of *Siyam* (intention, invalidators, expiation).
    *   Zakat al-Fitr: Purposes, timings, and weights (*Sa'* and *Mudd*).
    *   Zakah on Wealth (*Zakah al-Mal*): Purification of capital and communal rights.
    *   Sadaqah and social responsibility.
    *   Eid: Etiquette and joy in worship.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Welcome & Objective:** Welcome the students. Explain that this unit transitions from individual physical worship (Wudu and Salah) to communal, financial, and spiritual acts of worship (Fasting and Charity) that bind the Muslim community together.
*   **Pedagogical Note:** Remind students that we study the rulings according to the **Maliki school** of jurisprudence and the **Ash'ari school** of creed to build *fiqh literacy*. By seeing the rigorous, evidence-based differences between schools, they will learn that diversity of opinion (*ikhtilaf*) is a mercy and a product of scholarship.
*   **Discussion Starter:** Ask: *"When we fast or give charity, who benefits more: ourselves, the poor, or Allah?"* Let them reflect before moving to the next slide.

# Slide 2: Creed (Aqidah) Context: Why Do We Fast?
## Understanding Divine Independence (Istighna') and Human Need (Iftiqar)
*   **Allah is Al-Ghani (The Self-Sufficient):**
    *   Allah does not need our hunger, our thirst, or our money. 
    *   He is completely independent of all creation (*Mustaghni 'an al-khalq*).
*   **Humans are Al-Fuqara' (The Needy):**
    *   We are defined by **Iftiqar** (absolute dependence on Allah for every breath, heartbeat, and guidance).
*   **The Wisdom (*Hikmah*) of Command:**
    *   Allah commands us to fast and give charity purely out of mercy, to purify our souls and hearts.
*   **Fasting as a Secret Worship:**
    *   Unlike prayer, fasting has no outward physical actions. It is a secret between the servant (*'abd*) and the Creator (*Al-Khaliq*).

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Ash'ari Creed Integration:** Explain the concept of *Al-Ghani* (The Rich/Self-Sufficient). Under Ash'ari theology, Allah's attributes include absolute independence. Fasting does not "please" Allah in a way that changes His state (as He is exalted above changes of state), but rather He rewards it as a demonstration of our submission and gratitude (*Shukr*).
*   **Reflection:** Help students realize that fasting teaches us what it means to be needy (*Iftiqar*). When we feel hungry, we remember how weak we are and how much we depend on Allah for food and drink.
*   **Terminology:** Write *Al-Ghani* (الْغَنِيّ) and *Iftiqar* (اِفْتِقَار) on the board.

---

# Slide 3: The Spirit of Fasting (Siyam)
## Purification of the Heart (Taharah al-Batin)
*   **Fasting is a Shield (*Turs*):**
    *   The Prophet (pbuh) said: *"Fasting is a shield."* It protects us from sin in this world and the Fire in the next.
*   **Two Levels of Fasting:**
    1.  **Fasting of the Stomach and Private Parts:** Refraining from food, drink, and intimacy.
    2.  **Fasting of the Limbs and Heart:** Restraining the tongue from lying, the eyes from looking at haram, the ears from gossip, and the heart from jealousy and anger.
*   **Sincerity (*Ikhlas*):**
    *   Fasting is a training ground for *Ikhlas* because only Allah knows if you are truly fasting.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Connecting Taharah to Fasting:** Remind students of the concept of *Taharah* (purification) from the previous unit. Just as Wudu purifies the limbs, *Siyam* purifies the inner self (*tazkiyah*).
*   **Hadith Check:** Emphasize that the Prophet (pbuh) warned: *"Whoever does not give up false speech and evil actions, Allah has no need of his leaving his food and drink"* (Bukhari).
*   **Discussion:** Why is it easier to behave well when we are fasting? How does hunger train our self-control?

---

# Slide 4: Establishing Ramadan: Moon Sighting (Hilal)
## Sighting vs. Astronomical Calculations
*   **The Command to Sight:**
    *   The Prophet (pbuh) said: *"Fast when you see it [the crescent], and break your fast when you see it"* (Muwatta Malik).
*   **The Sighting Rule (*Ruyah*):**
    *   In Maliki fiqh, the start of Ramadan is established by either:
        1. Sighting the new crescent (*Hilal*) by two upright male witnesses.
        2. Sighting by a large crowd of people (*inshifad*) such that collusion to lie is impossible.
        3. Completing 30 days of the preceding month, Sha'ban.
*   **Calculations (*Hisab*):**
    *   The Maliki school (and classical consensus) does not adopt astronomical calculations as a substitute for visual sighting, maintaining the physical practice taught by the Prophet (pbuh).

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Fiqh Literacy Focus:** Explain the debate about astronomical calculations vs. actual sighting. Malikis adhere strictly to the visual sighting method because the Prophet (pbuh) made the ruling dependent on the *sight* (*ruyah*), which is accessible to all people, rich or poor, learned or unlearned.
*   **Communal Aspect:** Explain that moon sighting is a communal effort. It builds anticipation and connects the community to the cosmos and natural cycles created by Allah.

---

# Slide 5: The Maliki View on Global vs. Local Sighting
## Unity of Fasting Across Lands
*   **The Maliki Position: Sighting Transcends Distance**
    *   If the crescent (*Hilal*) is sighted in one land, it becomes obligatory for the people of all other lands to fast, *provided* the news reaches them through reliable channels.
*   **The Condition:**
    *   This global obligation applies to all lands that share the news, without restricting it to local horizons, unless the distance is so vast that their nights do not overlap at all.
*   **Contrast with Other Schools:**
    *   *Shafi'i School:* Advocates **Local Sighting (*Ikhtilaf al-Matali'*)**. If the moon is sighted in one city, it only binds those within a certain radius (approx. 83 km or sharing the same horizon).
*   **Communal Benefit:** The Maliki position favors global Muslim unity in starting and ending the holy month together.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Comparing Opinions:** Make sure students understand both views. Neither view is "wrong"—both are derived from legal interpretations of the same Hadiths.
*   **Key Concept:** *Ikhtilaf al-Matali'* (إِخْتِلَاف الْمَطَالِع - difference in moon horizons).
*   **Discussion:** How does the Maliki view on sighting help modern Muslim communities who want to fast together? What challenges arise when different countries start on different days?

---

# Slide 6: The Pillars of Fasting (Arkan al-Siyam)
## The Foundations of a Valid Fast
*   To have a valid fast, there are two primary pillars (*Rukn* / *Fard*) in Maliki fiqh:
    1.  **The Intention (*Niyyah*):** Deciding in the heart to fast for the sake of Allah.
    2.  **Abstinence (*Imsak*):** Refraining from food, drink, intimacy, and anything reaching the stomach cavity from the first light of dawn (*Fajr*) until sunset (*Maghrib*).
*   **Sanity (*'Aql*) & Separation from Menses:**
    *   Being sane is a condition for validity.
    *   Women must be free from menstruation (*Hayd*) and post-natal bleeding (*Nifas*).

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Explanation of Pillars:** Review what a *Rukn* (pillar) means: it is a core component of the act of worship itself. If it is missing, the act is completely invalid.
*   **Language Tip:** Introduce *Imsak* (إِمْسَاك - to hold back/abstain) and *Siyam* (صِيَام - fasting).
*   **Teaching Point:** Ensure students know the exact timing of *Imsak*: it starts at true dawn (*Al-Fajr al-Sadiq*), not when the sun rises, and ends at sunset (*Maghrib*).

---

# Slide 7: Deep-Dive: The Intention (Niyyah) in Maliki Fiqh
## The Ruling of a Single Intention (*Niyyah Wahidah*)
*   **The Maliki Rule:**
    *   For any fast that *must* be performed consecutively (like the 30 days of Ramadan), **one single intention** made on the night before the first day is sufficient for the entire month.
*   **The Exception:**
    *   If the continuous fasting is interrupted (e.g., due to travel, illness, menstruation, or breaking the fast), the student *must* make a new intention the night before they resume fasting.
*   **Contrast with Other Schools:**
    *   *Hanafi, Shafi'i, and Hanbali Schools:* Rule that each day of Ramadan is an independent act of worship. Therefore, a fresh intention **must** be made in the heart every single night of Ramadan.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Fiqh Literacy Check:** This is a major distinctive ruling of the Maliki school. Explain Imam Malik's reasoning: Ramadan is like a single package of worship (like a single unit of prayer). Just as you don't make a new intention for each Rak'ah of Salah, you don't need a new intention for each day of Ramadan, as long as it's not broken.
*   **Practical advice:** Even though a single intention at the start covers the month in our madhhab, it is highly recommended and good practice to renew the intention daily to gain extra rewards.
*   **Terminology:** *Niyyah Wahidah* (نِيَّة وَاحِدَة - single intention).

---

# Slide 8: Invalidators of Fasting: Qada' (Makeup Only)
## When the Fast is Broken Without Expiation
*   Certain actions break the fast but only require the student to make up that day after Ramadan (**Qada'** - قَضَاء).
*   **Examples of Qada'-Only Invalidators in Maliki Fiqh:**
    *   Eating or drinking **by mistake** or out of **forgetfulness** (*Nisyan*).
        *   *Contrast:* In the Hanafi and Shafi'i schools, eating/drinking out of forgetfulness does *not* break the fast at all. In the Maliki school, it breaks the fast, and you must make it up, but you are not sinful.
    *   Vomiting intentionally (*Al-Qay' al-'Amd*).
    *   Liquid or substances entering the stomach cavity through non-typical passages (e.g., nose drops, ear drops, or deep inhalation of steam/smoke).
    *   Failing to make the intention before Fajr.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Maliki Specific Detail on Forgetfulness:** Focus on the forgetfulness ruling. Imam Malik argued that the fast means actual abstinence; if you eat (even by mistake), the physical reality of the fast is broken, so it must be made up. However, the Prophet (pbuh) said the sin is forgiven, so the person has no sin.
*   **Class Activity:** Present a scenario: *"Zayd is fasting. He forgets he is fasting, walks to the kitchen, and drinks a glass of water. What is the status of his fast in the Maliki school versus the Shafi'i school?"*
    *   *Answer:* Maliki: He must make up the day (*Qada'*). Shafi'i: His fast is still valid; he doesn't need to make it up.

---

# Slide 9: Major Violations: Qada' and Kaffarah (Expiation)
## Deliberately Breaking the Ramadan Fast
*   **What is Kaffarah (كَفَّارَة)?**
    *   An expiation (penalty) performed to repair the spiritual gravity of deliberately violating the sacredness of Ramadan.
*   **The Model View on what triggers Kaffarah:**
    *   In the Maliki school, *Kaffarah* is required for **any** deliberate, voluntary breaking of an obligatory fast during the day of Ramadan, without a valid legal excuse (like travel or illness).
    *   This includes deliberately eating, drinking, or engaging in marital intimacy.
*   **Contrast with Other Schools:**
    *   *Shafi'i & Hanbali schools:* Limit *Kaffarah* **only** to breaking the fast through sexual intimacy. Deliberately eating a sandwich only requires *Qada'*.
    *   *Hanafi school:* Requires *Kaffarah* for eating, drinking, and intimacy, but limits it to nourishing foods/drinks.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Fiqh Literacy Focus:** Emphasize how serious the Maliki school treats the sanctity of the Ramadan day. Deliberately eating a single date or taking a sip of water without a valid reason triggers the massive penalty of *Kaffarah*, because you violated the sacred state of *Siyam*.
*   **Legal Excuse:** If a person is sick or traveling, they have a valid excuse to break the fast, so they only perform *Qada'* and do not owe *Kaffarah*.
*   **Terminology:** *Kaffarah* (expiation) and *Qada'* (making up a missed day).

---

# Slide 10: The Maliki Hierarchy of Kaffarah
## The Priority of Feeding the Poor
*   If a person triggers *Kaffarah*, they must choose one of three options. In the Maliki school, these options are ranked by preference, with **feeding the poor** being the most recommended option:
    1.  **Feeding 60 poor people (*It'am Sittin Miskin*):** Giving each poor person one *Mudd* (approx. 500-600g) of the staple food of the country. **(Highly Preferred in the Maliki school)**.
    2.  **Fasting two consecutive lunar months:** Without breaking the chain of days.
    3.  **Freeing a Muslim slave:** (Not applicable in modern times).
*   **Contrast with Other Schools:**
    *   The Shafi'i and Hanbali schools rule that the options must be done in a **strict order**: you *must* fast two months, and you can *only* feed 60 poor people if you are physically unable to fast. Imam Malik preferred feeding the poor because it directly helps the needy.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **The Maliki Logic:** Why did Imam Malik prefer feeding the poor over fasting two months? Because feeding the poor has a communal benefit (*mutata'addi* - transitive benefit), whereas fasting only benefits the individual (*qasir* - intransitive benefit). This shows the social justice focus of Maliki jurisprudence.
*   **Calculation:** Explain that 60 poor people must be fed. Each gets one *Mudd* (a double-handful scoop) of grain/staple food.

---

# Slide 11: Recommended & Disliked Acts of Fasting
## Maximizing the Reward of Siyam
*   **Recommended Acts (*Mandubat / Sunan*):**
    *   **Suhoor (سُحُور):** Eating the pre-dawn meal, and delaying it until just before Fajr.
    *   **Hastening Iftar (تَعْجِيل الْإِفْطَار):** Breaking the fast immediately when the sun sets (Maghrib).
    *   Breaking the fast with ripe or dry dates (*Tamar*) and water.
    *   Increasing charity (*Sadaqah*) and reciting the Quran.
*   **Disliked Acts (*Makruhat*):**
    *   Tasting food (unless necessary, like a cook, who must spit it out immediately).
    *   Excessive rinsing of the mouth or sniffing water deep into the nose during Wudu.
    *   Engaging in idle talk, arguing, or complaining about hunger.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Practical tips:** Teach students the Sunnah of breaking the fast: *"The people will remain in goodness as long as they hasten to break their fast"* (Muwatta Malik).
*   **Wudu during Fasting:** Explain that when we do Wudu while fasting, we should not sniff water too deeply (*istinshaq*) because water might go down the throat into the stomach, which would break the fast (requiring Qada').

---

# Slide 12: Zakat al-Fitr: The Charity of Fasting
## Cleaning the Fast and Feeding the Community
*   **What is Zakat al-Fitr (زَكَاة الْفِطْر)?**
    *   An obligatory charity paid at the end of Ramadan to purify the fasting person from idle speech and to feed the poor.
*   **Who Must Pay?**
    *   It is obligatory on **every Muslim** (man, woman, child) who possesses food in excess of their needs for themselves and their dependents for the day and night of Eid.
    *   A person must pay it on behalf of themselves and those they are legally obligated to support (spouse, poor children, poor parents).
*   **The Theological Purpose:**
    *   It ensures that no member of the Muslim community goes hungry on the day of Eid, shifting our focus from self-celebration to community care.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Connecting Fasting and Charity:** Explain that Zakat al-Fitr is linked to the body (*Zakat al-Badan*), unlike Zakah on wealth which is linked to assets (*Zakat al-Mal*).
*   **Communal Harmony:** Emphasize that Eid cannot be happy if some people are starving while others are celebrating. Zakat al-Fitr is the structural solution to this.
*   **Pronunciation:** Pronounce *Zakat al-Fitr* (Zah-kaht-ul-Fitr) clearly.

---

# Slide 13: Zakat al-Fitr: Timing & The Maliki Rules
## When is it Due?
*   **The Time of Obligation (*Wujub*):**
    *   It becomes obligatory at **sunset on the last day of Ramadan** (the night of Eid).
*   **The Best Time (*Mustahabb*):**
    *   After the dawn (*Fajr*) of Eid day, but **before** going out to perform the Eid prayer.
*   **The Permissible Time (*Rukhsah*):**
    *   One or two days before Eid. (Imam Malik permitted this to allow time for distribution).
*   **The Disliked & Forbidden Times:**
    *   It is **Makruh** (disliked) to delay it until after the Eid prayer.
    *   Delaying it past the day of Eid is **Haram** (sinful). It remains an outstanding debt (*Dayn*) that must be paid immediately, but the reward of Zakah is lost.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Timing Application:** Highlight how precise the timings are. Ask: *"Why is the best time between Fajr and the Eid prayer?"* (Answer: To ensure the poor receive the food fresh and ready to eat or cook on Eid morning).
*   **Debt Concept:** Explain that in Islamic law, missing an obligatory payment to the poor doesn't make it disappear; it becomes a debt to Allah and the poor that stays on your conscience until paid.

---

# Slide 14: Zakat al-Fitr: Amounts & Measurements
## Understanding the Sa' and the Mudd
*   **The Required Amount:**
    *   Exactly **One Sa'** (صَاع) of food per person.
*   **What is a Sa' in the Maliki School?**
    *   A **Sa'** is a volumetric measure containing exactly **four Amdad** (singular **Mudd** - مُدّ).
    *   A **Mudd** is the scoop of two average-sized hands held together.
*   **Modern Weight Equivalent:**
    *   Since different grains have different densities, one *Sa'* of wheat, rice, or dates weighs approximately **2.1 to 2.5 kilograms** (approx. 5 to 5.5 pounds).

```
   1 Mudd (Double Handful) x 4 = 1 Sa' (Approx. 2.1 - 2.5 kg)
```

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Physical Demo:** If possible, bring a cup or bowl and show what a "double handful" (*mudd*) looks like. Explain that the Prophet (pbuh) blessed these specific measurements.
*   **Historical Note:** Imam Malik lived in Madinah and used the actual measurement containers handed down from the families of the Companions, ensuring high accuracy of the prophetic *Sa'*.
*   **Terminology:** *Sa'* (صَاع) and *Mudd* (مُدّ).

---

# Slide 15: Zakat al-Fitr: Staple Foods vs. Cash
## The Fiqh Debate on Payment Methods
*   **The Maliki Ruling: Food Only**
    *   Zakat al-Fitr must be paid from the **common staple foods** of the country (e.g., wheat, barley, dates, raisins, millet, rice, or cheese).
    *   In the classical Maliki school, paying the cash equivalent is **not valid** because the prophetic command specified food.
*   **Contrast with Other Schools:**
    *   *Hanafi School:* Allows paying the value of the food in **cash** (currency). They argue this is more useful for the poor in modern societies.
*   **Modern Application:**
    *   Many modern Maliki scholars permit giving cash to charity organizations, who then purchase the food and distribute it to the poor on behalf of the donor.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Fiqh Literacy Check:** This is a very common topic of discussion every Ramadan. Help students understand the legal logic:
    *   Imam Malik's school focuses on adhering to the exact physical practice of the Sunnah (food distribution).
    *   Imam Abu Hanifah's school focuses on the ultimate purpose (*Maqsad*) of the charity (relieving the need of the poor, which cash might do better in certain settings).
*   **Synthesis:** Both views have strong textual and logical support.

---

# Slide 16: Zakah on Wealth (Zakah al-Mal)
## The Third Pillar of Islam: Purification of Wealth
*   **Meaning of Zakah:**
    *   Linguistically: Growth (*Numuw*) and Purification (*Taharah*).
    *   Lawfully: Giving a specific portion of one's wealth to specific categories of recipients.
*   **The Theological Foundation (Ash'ari Perspective):**
    *   **Wealth is a Trust (*Amanah*):** Allah is the true Owner (*Al-Malik*) of all things. Humans are temporary trustees (*mustakhlafeen*).
    *   **Right of the Poor:** Zakah is not "charity" in the sense of a voluntary gift; it is the legal right of the poor that Allah has placed inside our wealth. By paying it, we clean our money from the rights of others.
    *   **Purification of the Soul:** It cures the heart from the spiritual diseases of greed (*shuhh*) and materialism.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Theological Angle:** Connect this slide to Ash'ari Aqidah. If Allah is the Creator of all causes and properties, then our ownership of wealth is not absolute. We own it only because Allah gave us the legal right to use it. Therefore, we must follow His rules for its distribution.
*   **Language check:** Zakah comes from the root *Zaky* (to purify, grow, or sweeten). Explain that when we prune a plant, it looks smaller, but it grows back stronger. Zakah is the spiritual pruning of our wealth.

---

# Slide 17: Who Must Pay Zakah al-Mal?
## The Conditions of Obligation
*   Zakah on wealth is obligatory when five conditions are met:
    1.  **Islam:** The person must be Muslim.
    2.  **Freedom:** The person is not enslaved.
    3.  **Ownership (*Milk Tamm*):** Full, stable ownership of the assets.
    4.  **Nisab (نِصَاب):** The wealth must reach a minimum threshold.
    5.  **Hawl (حَوْل):** A full lunar year (354 days) must pass while the wealth remains at or above the Nisab.
*   **The Maliki Ruling on Age & Sanity:**
    *   In the Maliki school, Zakah is obligatory on the wealth of **children** and **insane individuals**. The guardian (*Wali*) is obligated to calculate and pay Zakah from their wealth on their behalf.
*   **Contrast with the Hanafi School:**
    *   *Hanafi View:* Zakah is only obligatory on sane, adult Muslims. Children and the insane do not owe Zakah, regardless of how wealthy they are.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Fiqh Literacy Focus:** Draw attention to the difference between Malikis and Hanafis on children's wealth. 
    *   The Hanafis view Zakah as an act of physical/spiritual worship (*ibadah*), which requires intention and accountability (*taklif*). Since children are not yet accountable (*mukallaf*), they do not owe Zakah.
    *   The Malikis view Zakah as a financial right (*hakk mali*) belonging to the poor, linked to the wealth itself, not the person. Therefore, if the money is there, the poor have a right to it, regardless of who owns it.
*   **Terminology:** *Nisab* (minimum threshold) and *Hawl* (one lunar year).

---

# Slide 18: Nisab, Hawl, & The Categories of Zakah
## Calculating the Purifying Share
*   **The Rate of Zakah:**
    *   For cash, gold, and silver, the rate is **2.5%** (one-fortieth) of the total wealth.
*   **The Nisab Thresholds (Gold & Silver equivalents):**
    *   **Gold Nisab:** 20 Dinars (approx. **85 grams** of pure gold).
    *   **Silver Nisab:** 200 Dirhams (approx. **595 grams** of silver).
    *   **Currency Nisab:** Calculated based on the cash equivalent of the gold Nisab (or silver Nisab in some applications).
*   **The Hawl Exception:**
    *   Agricultural crops, fruits, and mined minerals do **not** require the passage of a lunar year (*Hawl*). Their Zakah is paid immediately at harvest: *"And give its due on the day of its harvest"* (Quran 6:141).

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Math Application:** Help students calculate a basic Zakah amount. If someone has $10,000 savings that has sat in their account for a whole lunar year (*Hawl*), and the Nisab is $5,000:
    *   Do they pay Zakah? **Yes** (it is above Nisab).
    *   How much? $10,000 x 0.025 = **$250**.
*   **Agricultural Zakah:** Mention that the rate for crops is 10% if irrigated naturally by rain, and 5% if irrigated artificially with cost/labor. This shows how Islam balances the effort of the farmer.

---

# Slide 19: The Recipients of Zakah (Asnaf)
## Where Does the Money Go?
*   The Quran restricts Zakah distribution to **eight categories** (Surah at-Tawbah, Verse 60):
    1.  **Al-Fuqara' (الْفُقَرَاء):** The poor (who have nothing or less than half of their needs).
    2.  **Al-Masakin (الْمَسَاكِين):** The needy (who have more than half, but still cannot meet their basic needs).
    3.  **Al-'Amilina 'Alayha:** The Zakah collectors.
    4.  **Al-Mu'allafati Qulubuhum:** Those whose hearts are to be reconciled.
    5.  **Fi al-Riqab:** Freeing captives/slaves.
    6.  **Al-Gharimin:** Those in debt (incurred for halal reasons and unable to pay).
    7.  **Fi Sabilillah:** In the cause of Allah (historically, defending the Muslim lands; or communal projects).
    8.  **Ibn al-Sabil:** The stranded traveler.
*   **Zakah cannot be given to:** One's parents, grandparents, children, or spouse (since you are already legally obligated to spend on them).

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Social Structure:** Discuss how these eight categories cover all forms of social vulnerability: poverty, debt, displacement, and administrative overhead.
*   **Difference between Fuqara' and Masakin:** Malikis define *Fakir* as one who has nothing, while *Miskin* is slightly better off but still in need. Other schools swap these definitions, but the end result is the same: both deserve help.
*   **Discussion:** Why is it forbidden to give Zakah to your own parents? (Answer: Because you are supposed to support them from your regular wealth; using Zakah to support them would be like saving your own money using charity).

---

# Slide 20: Sadaqah: Voluntary Kindness & Social Responsibility
## The Communal Obligation (Fard Kifayah)
*   **What is Sadaqah (صَدَقَة)?**
    *   Voluntary charity given out of love and desire to please Allah. The word comes from *Sidq* (truthfulness), showing that charity proves the truth of one's faith.
*   **The Scope of Sadaqah:**
    *   The Prophet (pbuh) said: *"Every good deed is a charity."* (Smiling, removing a branch from the road, helping someone carry luggage).
*   **Fard Kifayah (Communal Obligation):**
    *   While Zakah is an individual obligation (*Fard 'Ayn*), ensuring that no one in the neighborhood goes hungry is a **Communal Obligation (*Fard Kifayah*)**.
    *   If a poor person in a town starves to death and the townspeople knew about him, the entire community shares the sin.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Distinguishing Terms:** Make sure students understand:
    *   *Zakah* is obligatory, strictly calculated, and limited to 8 categories.
    *   *Sadaqah* is voluntary, unlimited in amount, and can be given to anyone (including non-Muslims, animals, and public projects).
*   **Social Justice Discussion:** Explore how Islam structures society. We have personal responsibilities, but we also have collective responsibilities (*Fard Kifayah*). If a school, hospital, or well is needed, the community must build it.

# Slide 21: Eid al-Fitr: Etiquette and Celebration
## Joy as an Act of Worship
*   **What is Eid (عِيد)?**
    *   Eid literally means "a festival that returns." It is a gift from Allah to celebrate the completion of the spiritual marathon of Ramadan.
*   **The Eid Prayer (*Salat al-Eid*):**
    *   In the Maliki school, it is a **Confirmed Sunnah (*Sunnah Mu'akkadah*)**.
    *   It is 2 Rak'ahs, prayed in congregation:
        *   1st Rak'ah: 6 extra Takbeers (excluding the opening Takbeer).
        *   2nd Rak'ah: 5 extra Takbeers (excluding the rising Takbeer).
*   **Sunnahs and Etiquettes of Eid:**
    *   Performing Ghusl before leaving, wearing your finest clothes, and perfume.
    *   Eating an odd number of dates before going to Eid al-Fitr prayer.
    *   Walking to the prayer place (*Musalla*) by one path and returning by another.
    *   Reciting the Takbeerat out loud while walking.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Spiritual Joy:** Teach students that joy on Eid is not secular; it is an act of worship. Eating on Eid day is obligatory because fasting on Eid is strictly forbidden (*Haram*).
*   **The Takbeerat:** Recite the standard Takbeerat with the class: *"Allahu Akbar, Allahu Akbar, Allahu Akbar. La ilaha illa Allah. Allahu Akbar, Allahu Akbar, wa lillahi al-hamd."*
