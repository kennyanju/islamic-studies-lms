# Level 3 Quiz: Fiqh of Fasting, Zakah & Community

## Part A: Multiple Choice Questions
1. What is the fundamental spiritual purpose of fasting according to Surah Al-Baqarah (2:183)?
   (A) To lose weight before the annual Eid holidays.
   (B) To attain Taqwa (God-consciousness and righteous self-restraint).
   (C) To protest against food waste in supermarkets.
   (D) To sleep during daylight hours.

2. What are the two essential pillars (Arkan) of fasting in Islamic jurisprudence?
   (A) Sleeping eight hours and eating honey.
   (B) The Intention (Al-Niyyah) and Abstention (Al-Imsak) from dawn to sunset.
   (C) Reading thirty pages of history and attending the mosque.
   (D) Wearing white clothing and perfume.

3. In the Maliki Madhhab, what is the ruling regarding the intention (Niyyah) for Ramadan?
   (A) A person must announce their intention verbally with a microphone before Fajr.
   (B) A single intention formed on the first night of Ramadan suffices for the entire consecutive month.
   (C) An intention is only valid if stamped by a local religious judge.
   (D) If someone sleeps past midnight, they cannot fast the next day.

4. If a fasting person completely forgets they are fasting and drinks a cup of water, what should they do?
   (A) Break their fast for the rest of the day and eat lunch.
   (B) Spit out any remaining water, continue fasting, and their fast is completely valid without needing to make it up.
   (C) Pay a penalty of fifty dollars to a charity.
   (D) Fast forty consecutive days as an expiation.

5. Which of the following medical treatments does NOT invalidate the fast?
   (A) Swallowing painkiller tablets with fruit juice.
   (B) A non-nutritive insulin injection into muscle or bloodstream.
   (C) Drinking liquid antacid for heartburn.
   (D) Eating vitamin gummies.

6. Under what condition is a traveler legally permitted to break their fast in Ramadan?
   (A) Any trip that involves driving on a highway for twenty minutes.
   (B) A lawful journey exceeding the shortening distance (approx. 48 miles / 80 km).
   (C) Only if they are traveling on foot across a sandstorm.
   (D) Only if their journey crosses an international border.

7. What is the legal compensation required for an elderly person who is chronically unable to fast?
   (A) Fasting sixty consecutive days in the winter.
   (B) Paying Fidyah by feeding one poor person for each missed day of Ramadan.
   (C) Constructing a mosque well in an arid region.
   (D) Memorizing three chapters of the Quran.

8. What is the recommended prophetic Sunnah regarding the pre-dawn meal (Suhur)?
   (A) Skipping Suhur to show greater ascetic devotion.
   (B) Delaying Suhur until shortly before the true dawn (Fajr) to preserve strength.
   (C) Eating Suhur immediately after Maghrib prayer.
   (D) Consuming only salty meats during Suhur.

9. How is it recommended to break the fast at sunset (*Iftar*)?
   (A) Immediately at sunset with fresh or dried dates, or water, before praying Maghrib.
   (B) Waiting until the stars become clearly visible at midnight.
   (C) Eating a four-course feast before performing Maghrib prayer.
   (D) Drinking cold sodas while standing.

10. How many rak'ahs of Taraweeh prayer were established in congregation in Madinah during the caliphate of Sayyidina 'Umar ibn al-Khattab?
    (A) 2 rak'ahs.
    (B) 20 rak'ahs with unhurried recitation.
    (C) 50 rak'ahs.
    (D) 100 rak'ahs.

## Part B: Fill in the Blanks
Vocabulary Bank: Fidyah, Suhur, Taqwa, Al-Imsak, Niyyah
1. The supreme goal of fasting described in the Quran is the attainment of ______.
2. Abstaining from food, drink, and desires from dawn until sunset is termed ______.
3. In Maliki fiqh, a single ______ formed on Night 1 suffices for the entire month of Ramadan.
4. The blessed pre-dawn meal encouraged by the Prophet ﷺ is called ______.
5. The charitable food donation paid by someone permanently unable to fast due to chronic illness is ______.

## Part C: Matching Terms
* Al-Imsak ─── Refraining from food, drink, and nullifiers from true dawn to sunset
* Maliki Monthly Niyyah ─── A single intention on Night 1 covering the entire consecutive month
* Suhur ─── The blessed pre-dawn meal delayed until shortly before Fajr
* Fidyah ─── Feeding a poor person for each day missed due to chronic illness or old age
* Taraweeh ─── The special night congregational prayers prayed in Ramadan

## Part D: Reflection & Application
1. Explain the wisdom behind the Maliki ruling allowing a single monthly intention for Ramadan: How does it reflect the unity of the month?
2. A young student accidentally swallows water while vigorously brushing their teeth during Ramadan. How does classical fiqh evaluate whether their fast is broken?
3. What spiritual and social lessons can families learn from gathering together for Suhur and Iftar every day?
4. Distinguish between making up missed days (*Qada'*) and paying *Fidyah*: When does each ruling apply?

## Answer Key & Marking Guide
1. B - Surah Al-Baqarah 2:183 establishes that the ultimate purpose of fasting is achieving Taqwa.
2. B - Niyyah and Imsak are the two universal pillars of a valid fast.
3. B - Imam Malik held that consecutive obligatory fasting requires only an opening intention on night one.
4. B - Forgetful consumption does not invalidate the fast; the person must complete the day normally.
5. B - Non-nutritive injections that do not nourish via the digestive tract do not break the fast.
6. B - Travel exceeding 48 miles (80 km) qualifies for the concession of breaking the fast.
7. B - Fidyah provides daily nutritional compensation when physical fasting is permanently impossible.
8. B - The Prophet ﷺ recommended delaying Suhur as close to Fajr as possible to maintain physical vitality.
9. A - Hastening Iftar with dates and water follows the direct Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ.
10. B - Sayyidina 'Umar unified the companions behind Ubayy ibn Ka'b for 20 rak'ahs of Taraweeh.
