## Page 2: The Creed (Aqidah) Connection — Why Do We Worship?

Before we learn *how* to wash or *how* to pray, we must ask a very important question: **Why do we worship Allah?**

### 1. Allah is Al-Ghani (The Self-Sufficient)
In our creed, we learn that Allah is **Al-Ghani** (الْغَنِيّ). This means He is completely rich and has no needs. 
*   Does Allah need our prayers? **No.**
*   Does Allah become stronger if we pray, or weaker if we don't? **No.**
*   Allah is the Creator of everything. He does not need anything from His creation.

### 2. We are the Needy Ones (Al-Fuqara')
We, the human beings, are **Al-Fuqara'** (الْفُقَرَاء) – the needy ones. We pray because we need Allah's help, love, guidance, and peace. Without prayer, our hearts get dusty and tired. 

### 3. Prayer is Gratitude (Shukr)
Imagine someone gave you a beautiful house, amazing food, and a loving family. What is the first thing you say? *"Thank you!"* 
Worship is our way of saying *"Thank you"* – **Shukr** (شُكْر) – to Allah for the gift of life, our bodies, our families, and Islam.

> **Creed Focus Box: Ubudiyyah**
> **'Ubudiyyah** (عُبُودِيَّة) means showing our love and humbleness to Allah by being His willing, happy servants. Salah is the best way to show our *'Ubudiyyah*.

## Page 3: Who Was Imam Malik?
To understand our school of law (*fiqh*), we should meet the teacher who started it: **Imam Malik ibn Anas** (إِمَام مَالِك).

*   **Where did he live?** He lived in **Madinah** (الْمَدِينَة), the city of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him).
*   **When was he born?** He was born in the year 93 AH, which was about 80 years after the Prophet (pbuh) passed away.
*   **How did he learn?** Imam Malik studied with the children and grandchildren of the Companions (*Sahabah*). Because he lived in Madinah, he got to see how the whole city practiced Islam every day.
*   **His famous book:** He wrote a book called **Al-Muwatta** (الْمُوَطَّأ), which means "The Approved Path." It is one of the earliest collections of Hadith and fiqh rulings in Islam!

## Page 4: The Importance of Purity (Taharah)

In Islam, we cannot stand in prayer unless we are clean. This cleanliness is called **Taharah** (طَهَارَة).

### What are we cleaning?
We must clean two things:
1.  **Outer Cleanliness:** Washing away dirt or physical impurities from our skin, clothes, and the place we pray.
2.  **Inner Cleanliness:** Cleaning our hearts from bad thoughts, anger, pride, and jealousy. 

### Two Types of Cleanliness Problems:
To understand purity, we need to know the difference between two things:
*   **Najasah (نَجَاسَة) - Physical Filth:** This is actual dirty stuff you can see or smell, like urine, blood, or stool. If it gets on your shirt, you just wash it off with water.
*   **Hadath (حَدَث) - Ritual State:** This is not physical dirt. It is a spiritual state of being unready for prayer. For example, if you sleep or use the bathroom, you aren't physically dirty all over, but you have entered the state of *hadath*. To exit this state, you must do Wudu or Ghusl.

## Page 5: Water — Our Best Friend for Purity

Allah sent down rain from the sky so that we can purify ourselves. But in Islamic law, not all liquids are the same! The scholars of the Maliki school divide water into three types. 

### The Three Types of Water:

### 1. Al-Ma' al-Mutlaq / Al-Tahur (الْمَاء الْمُطْلَق / الطَّهُور)
This is natural water that has not changed. It is exactly as Allah created it. 
*   **Examples:** Rainwater, sea water, river water, well water, melted snow.
*   **Ruling:** It is **pure** in itself, and it has the power to **purify** other things. You **can** use it for Wudu and Ghusl.

---

## Page 6: Pure vs. Impure Water (Maliki Rulings)

### 2. Al-Ma' al-Tahir (الْمَاء الطَّاهِر)
This is water that was clean, but then a **pure** substance mixed with it and changed its color, taste, or smell completely. 
*   **Examples:** Water mixed with tea leaves (tea), water mixed with milk, chicken broth, or water mixed with a lot of soap.
*   **Ruling:** It is **pure** (tahir) – you can drink it or touch it – but it is **not purifying** (tahur). You **cannot** use it for Wudu or Ghusl!
*   **Maliki Detail:** If only a tiny bit of soap falls into water and doesn't change its look or smell, it is still *Tahur*. But if it changes the name of the water (so people call it "soap-water" instead of "water"), it becomes *Tahir* and cannot be used for Wudu.

### 3. Al-Ma' al-Najis (الْمَاء النَّجِس)
This is water that has been mixed with an **impurity** (*najasah*), like sewage, urine, or blood, and has changed its color, taste, or smell.
*   **Ruling:** It is dirty. You **cannot** drink it, touch it, or use it for Wudu.
*   **Important Maliki Distinction:** What if a tiny drop of blood falls into a bucket of water, but the water doesn't change color, taste, or smell? 
    *   In the Maliki school, if the water's qualities **do not change**, the water is still **Tahur** (purifying) and valid for Wudu. (However, if you have another clean bucket of water, it is disliked (*makruh*) to use this one). Other schools, like the Shafi'i school, say that if the water is a small amount, it automatically becomes impure even if it doesn't change.

## Page 7: Step-by-Step Wudu (Ablution)

To pray, we must make Wudu. In the Maliki school, Wudu has **seven obligations** (*Fara'id*). If you miss even one of these, your Wudu is invalid, and you must do it again.

### The 7 Pillars (Fara'id) of Wudu:
1.  **Intention (*Niyyah* - نِيَّة):** You make the intention in your heart to perform Wudu for prayer at the very moment you start washing.
2.  **Washing the Face (*Ghasl al-Wajh* - غَسْل الْوَجْه):** Washing from the top of the forehead to the bottom of the chin, and from ear to ear.
3.  **Washing the Arms (*Ghasl al-Yadayn* - غَسْل الْيَدَيْن):** Washing both arms from the fingertips up to and including the elbows.
4.  **Wiping the Entire Head (*Mash al-Ra's* - مَسْح الرَّأْس):** Wiping your wet hands over your **entire** head from the front hairline to the neck. Wiping the *entire* head is obligatory in the Maliki school (other schools allow wiping just a few hairs).
5.  **Washing the Feet (*Ghasl al-Rijlayn* - غَسْل الرِّجْلَيْن):** Washing both feet up to and including the ankles.
6.  **Rubbing (*Dalk* - دَلْك):** Gently rubbing each limb with your hand while washing it with water. (This is unique to the Maliki school! Simply letting water flow over your arms without rubbing is not enough).
7.  **Continuity (*Muwalat* - مُوَالَاة):** Doing all the steps of Wudu right after another without stopping. If you stop for so long that your skin dries, you must start over.

## Page 8: The Sunnahs and Recommended Acts of Wudu

Beside the obligations, there are actions that our beloved Prophet (peace be upon him) taught us to do. These are called **Sunnahs** (سُنَن) and **Fada'il** (فَضَائِل - virtues). 

### High Sunnahs of Wudu:
*   Washing your hands to the wrists at the very beginning (before washing the face).
*   Rinsing your mouth with water (**Madmadah** - مَضْمَضَة).
*   Sniffing water into your nose (**Istinshaq** - اِسْتِنْشَاق) and blowing it out (**Istinthar** - اِسْتِنْثَار).
*   Returning the wipe of your head (sliding your hands from the back of your neck back to the front).
*   Wiping your ears, inside and out.
*   **The Order (Tarteeb - تَرْتِيب):** Keeping the steps in order (washing face first, then arms, then head, then feet). 
    *   *Fiqh Check:* In the Maliki school, keeping the actions in order (*tarteeb*) is a highly recommended **Sunnah**, not an obligation. If you make a mistake and wash your feet before your face by accident, your Wudu is still valid, but you missed a Sunnah. You should try your best to always follow the correct order.

### Recommended Acts (Fada'il):
*   Saying *Bismillah* (In the name of Allah) at the start.
*   Using the tooth-stick (*Siwak*) to clean your teeth before Wudu.
*   Washing the limbs two or three times (the first wash is obligatory; the second and third are extra blessings).

## Page 9: Ghusl — The Ritual Bath

Sometimes, washing our hands and faces is not enough. We need to wash our entire body from head to toe. This ritual bath is called **Ghusl** (غُسْل).

### When is Ghusl Required?
Ghusl is required when a person enters a state of major impurity (*hadath al-akbar*). This happens in a few situations:
1.  **Major Impurity (*Janabah*):** Intimacy or physical emissions in sleep (like wet dreams).
2.  **Menstruation (*Hayd*):** When a woman's monthly bleeding ends.
3.  **Post-Natal Bleeding (*Nifas*):** When bleeding after childbirth ends.
4.  **Death:** When a Muslim passes away, we wash their body (*Ghusl al-Mayyit*).

### How to do Ghusl (The Obligations):
According to the Maliki school, there are **five obligations** for Ghusl:
1.  **Intention (*Niyyah*):** Intending to lift the major impurity.
2.  **Continuity (*Muwalat*):** Washing the whole body in one go without long breaks.
3.  **Rubbing (*Dalk*):** Rubbing your whole body with your hands while washing.
4.  **Washing the entire body:** Making sure water covers every single spot.
5.  **Permeating the hair (*Khilal al-Sha'r*):** Rubbing water down to the roots of all your hair.

## Page 10: Tayammum — Purification Without Water

What happens if you are in the desert and there is no water? Or what if you are very sick and water will make you worse? Allah is the Most Merciful, so He gave us a special way to purify ourselves using the earth. This is called **Tayammum** (تَيَمُّم).

### The Earth Substance: Sa'id (صَعِيد)
In the Maliki school, you can do Tayammum using anything that is a natural part of the earth's surface. This is called **Sa'id**.
*   **Allowed:** Clean soil, sand, rocks, stones, and natural dust.
*   **Not Allowed:** Wood, carpets, plastic, or anything man-made.

### How to Perform Tayammum:
1.  **Intention (*Niyyah*):** Intend in your heart to make prayer allowed.
2.  **First Strike:** Pat the clean earth or stone with both hands.
3.  **Wipe the Face:** Wipe your entire face once with your palms.
4.  **Wipe the Hands:** Wipe your hands and forearms. 
    *   *Fiqh Detail:* In the Maliki school, wiping to the wrists is obligatory (*Fard*), and wiping all the way to the elbows is a recommended **Sunnah**.

```
   [1. Intention] ──> [2. Pat the Earth] ──> [3. Wipe Face] ──> [4. Wipe Hands/Arms]
```

## Page 11: Salah — The Pillar of Religion

Once we are pure, we are ready for **Salah** (صَلَاة) – prayer. 

### Why is Salah the "Pillar of Religion"?
The Prophet (peace be upon him) said: *"Salah is the pillar of the religion (*'imad al-din* - عِمَاد الدِّين). Whoever establishes it has established the religion, and whoever destroys it has destroyed the religion."*
*   **The Tent Analogy:** Imagine a large tent. The center pole holding up the tent is the *'imad*. If you take away the center pole, the whole tent collapses. Similarly, prayer holds up our Islam. If we stop praying, our connection to Allah falls apart.

### The Five Daily Prayer Windows:
*   **Subh (Fajr):** Two units (*Rak'ahs*) at dawn.
*   **Zuhr:** Four units in the early afternoon.
*   **'Asr:** Four units in the late afternoon.
*   **Maghrib:** Three units immediately after sunset.
*   **'Isha:** Four units at night.

---

## Page 12: Standing in Prayer — The Position of the Arms (Sadl)

When we stand (*Qiyam*) during the obligatory daily prayers, where do we place our arms? 

### The Maliki Practice: Sadl (سَدْل)
In the Maliki school, the standard and most famous practice is **Sadl**. 
*   **Sadl** means letting your arms rest naturally and comfortably at your sides. You do not clasp your hands together on your chest.
*   *Why?* Imam Malik lived in Madinah. He watched the teachers, the descendants of the Companions, and the scholars of Madinah pray. Almost all of them prayed with their arms at their sides. This continuous practice is called **'Amal Ahl al-Madinah** (the practice of the people of Madinah).

### What about other schools?
*   Other Sunni schools (Shafi'i, Hanafi, and Hanbali) prefer **Qabd** (قَبْض) – clasping the right hand over the left hand on the chest or near the waist. 
*   **Fiqh Literacy Rule:** Both *Sadl* and *Qabd* are correct and sunnah. We must respect our brothers and sisters who clasp their hands, just as they respect us when we pray with our arms at their sides.
