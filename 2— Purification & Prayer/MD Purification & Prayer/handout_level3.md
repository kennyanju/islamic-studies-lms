## Section 1: The Fiqh of Purity: Approaching the Divine Presence

At ages 10 to 12, your daily prayer (*Salah*) transitions into a conscious, mature covenant with Allah. In Islamic jurisprudence, ritual purity (*Taharah*) is divided into two distinct dimensions:

1. **Purification from Minor Ritual Impurity (*Hadath Asghar*):** Lifted by performing complete Wudu with pure water.
2. **Purification from Major Ritual Impurity (*Hadath Akbar*):** Lifted by performing the complete ritual bath (*Ghusl*) with pure water.

Purity also requires the complete removal of physical filth (*Khabath*, such as urine, stool, or spilled blood) from three places: your body, your clothing, and the physical surface upon which you prostrate.

---

## Section 2: The Seven Obligations of Wudu in Matn Ibn 'Ashir

In his celebrated classical didactic poem *Al-Murshid al-Mu'in 'ala al-Daruri min 'Ulum al-Din*, Imam 'Abd al-Wahid ibn 'Ashir (d. 1040 AH) states lines 48-50 on the obligations (*Fara'id*) of Wudu:

> **فَصْلٌ فَرَائِضُ الْوُضُوءِ سَبْعُ *** وَهْيَ دَلْكٌ فَوْرٌ نِيَّةٌ فِي بَدْءِ**  
> **وَغَسْلُ وَجْهٍ غَسْلُهُ الْيَدَيْنِ *** وَمَسْحُ رَأْسٍ غَسْلُهُ الرِّجْلَيْنِ**  
> **وَالْفَرْضُ عَمَّ مَجْمَعَ الأُذْنَيْنِ *** وَالْمِرْفَقَيْنِ عَمَّ وَالْكَعْبَيْنِ**  
> *"Section: The obligations of Wudu are seven: rubbing (Dalk), continuity (Fawr), intention (Niyyah) at the beginning, washing the face, washing the two hands and arms, wiping the head, and washing the two feet. And the obligation encompasses the boundary meeting the two ears, and includes the two elbows and the two ankle-bones."*

### Detailed Explanation of the Seven Fara'id:
1. **Intention (*Al-Niyyah*):** Formed silently in the heart at the very first obligatory wash (washing the face), intending either to lift ritual impurity (*Raf' al-Hadath*) or to make prayer permissible (*Istibahat al-Salah*).
2. **Washing the Face (*Ghasl al-Wajh*):** From the normal top hairline to the bottom of the chin, and horizontally from earlobe to earlobe (the phrase *majma' al-udhnayn* refers to the facial boundary next to the ears; the ears themselves are washed as a Sunnah, not a Fard).
3. **Washing the Arms (*Ghasl al-Yadayn*):** Washing both hands and forearms from the fingertips up to and including the elbows (*Al-Mirfaqayn*).
4. **Wiping the Entire Head (*Mash Jami' al-Ra's*):** Moistening both hands and wiping the entire skull from the front hairline all the way back to the nape of the neck. (Returning the hands back to the front, *Radd al-Mash*, is an emphasized Sunnah).
5. **Washing the Feet (*Ghasl al-Rijlayn*):** Washing both feet up to and including the ankle-bones (*Al-Ka'bayn*), ensuring water penetrates between all toes.
6. **Gentle Rubbing (*Al-Dalk*):** Passing the hand over each limb while or immediately after pouring water. This ensures the water cleanses the skin directly rather than merely sliding off. Rubbing is a unique obligation in the Maliki school.
7. **Continuity (*Al-Muwalat* or *Al-Fawr*):** Washing consecutive limbs without allowing the preceding limb to dry under normal, moderate weather conditions. (Note: Continuity is obligatory in both the Maliki and Hanbali schools, while the Shafi'i and Hanafi schools classify it as a Sunnah).

## Section 3: Nullifiers of Wudu (Nawaqid al-Wudu)

The Maliki school categorizes things that break wudu into two clear divisions:

### 1. Actual Events (*Ahdath*):
* Direct discharges from the private passages: urine, stool, wind, pre-seminal fluid (*Madhy*), and post-urinary prostatic fluid (*Wady*).

### 2. Indirect Causes (*Asbab*):
* Loss of intellect or awareness:
  * Heavy, deep sleep where you lose complete awareness (dropping an item from your hand without noticing).
  * Fainting, intoxication, medication, or temporary loss of consciousness.
* Touching with sexual desire: In the Maliki school, touching an adult of the opposite gender breaks wudu only if it is accompanied by pleasure or intended for pleasure. Casual contact without desire (such as a child bumping into a relative or a doctor examining a patient) does not invalidate wudu.

### Wiping Over Leather Footwear (*Mash 'ala al-Khuffayn*):
If a person wears sturdy leather socks or boots that cover the ankles:
* The footwear must have been put on while in a complete state of purity established by washing with water.
* When performing wudu, the person may wipe over the top of the footwear with moistened hands instead of removing them to wash the feet.
* In the Maliki school, there is no fixed time limit for wiping over leather socks, provided the wearer does not enter a state of major ritual impurity (*Janabah*) and does not take them off.

## Section 4: Ghusl and Tayammum: Purification with Sa'id

### 1. The Fiqh of Ghusl (The Major Ritual Bath):
Ghusl is required after major ritual impurity (such as marital relations, nocturnal emission, or the completion of post-natal bleeding and menstruation).  
* **The Fara'id of Ghusl in Maliki Fiqh:**
  1. Intention (*Niyyah*) in the heart to lift major ritual impurity (*Raf' al-Hadath al-Akbar*).
  2. Continuity (*Al-Muwalat*).
  3. Rubbing (*Al-Dalk*) the entire body with the hand.
  4. Pouring water over every part of the body, including roots of hair.
  5. Picking through thick hair and skin folds (*Takhlil*) so water touches the skin beneath.

### 2. Tayammum (Dry Ablution with Earth):
When water is genuinely unavailable after searching, or when using water would cause medical harm or severe illness, Allah permits Tayammum:
* **The Substance (*Sa'id*):** Tayammum must be performed on pure, unadulterated natural earth (*Sa'id*), such as natural stone, rock, clean soil, or sand.
* **The Method in Maliki Fiqh:**
  1. Form the intention to render prayer permissible (*Istibahat al-Salah*).
  2. Strike the clean stone or earth once with both palms.
  3. Wipe the entire face once (*Fard*).
  4. Wipe both hands up to the wrists (*Fard*).
  5. It is an emphasized Sunnah to strike the stone a second time and wipe from the wrists up to the elbows.

## Section 5: The Call to Prayer: The Madinan Adhan with Tarji'

The Maliki Adhan preserves the historical call to prayer delivered by Sayyidina Bilal ibn Rabah in the Prophet's Mosque in Madinah:
* **The Opening Takbir:** The Madinan Adhan begins with **two** Takbirs (*Allahu Akbar, Allahu Akbar*), rather than four.
* **Al-Tarji' (التَّرْجِيع):** The Mu'adhin recites the two testimonies of faith (*Ash-hadu alla ilaha illallah*, *Ash-hadu anna Muhammadan Rasulullah*) softly to himself first, and then repeats them aloud with full vocal power. This brings the total count of the Maliki Adhan to 17 phrases.
* **The Iqamah:** The Maliki Iqamah consists of 10 concise phrases. Every sentence is uttered once, including the phrase *Qad qamat al-salah* (said once), while the opening and closing Takbirs are paired (*Allahu Akbar, Allahu Akbar*).

## Section 6: Sujud al-Sahw Clinic: Repairing Prayer Mistakes

Because human beings are prone to distraction or forgetfulness, Allah established a precise, logical legal mechanism to repair prayer errors:

### 1. Sujud al-Qabli (الْقَبْلِيُّ: Before the Final Salam):
* **When is it performed?** When you accidentally **omit (subtract / *Nuqsan*)** two or more confirmed sunnahs from the prayer (such as forgetting the middle Tashahhud and standing directly up for the third rak'ah).
* **The Exact Method in Relied-upon Maliki Fiqh (*Matn Ibn 'Ashir*, *Mukhtasar al-Akhdari*):**
  * Complete your normal final Tashahhud and blessings upon the Prophet ﷺ.
  * *Before* saying the Salam, make two prostrations (*Sajdatayn*), saying *Allahu Akbar* for each movement.
  * Sit up from the second prostration and **immediately say the Salam to conclude your prayer**.
  * **Critical Maliki Rule:** There is **NO second Tashahhud** recited after *Sujud al-Qabli*. You recite the Tashahhud once before the prostrations, prostrate twice, and immediately give the final Salam.

### 2. Sujud al-Ba'di (الْبَعْدِيُّ: After the Final Salam):
* **When is it performed?** When you accidentally **add (*Ziyadah*)** an extra action to the prayer (such as an extra bowing, an extra prostration, or standing for a fifth rak'ah by mistake).
* **The Exact Method:**
  * Complete the prayer normally, make the final Salam, and sit.
  * Say *Allahu Akbar* and perform two prostrations of forgetfulness.
  * Sit up, recite a second Tashahhud, and say Salam a second time to complete the rectification.

### 3. Combining Omission and Addition:
If you both omitted a sunnah and added an extra action during the same prayer, **the omission takes priority**, and you perform **Sujud al-Qabli** before the Salam.

### 4. Handling Doubt (*Shakk*):
If you doubt whether you have prayed three or four rak'ahs, apply the classical legal maxim: *"Certainty is not dispelled by doubt"* (*Al-Yaqinu la yazulu bish-Shakk*).  
* Build upon certainty (the lower number: 3 rak'ahs).
* Pray the remaining fourth rak'ah.
* Because you may have actually prayed five rak'ahs, complete the prayer, say the final Salam, and perform **Sujud al-Ba'di** after the Salam.

## Section 7: Curriculum Anchors in Practice

### 1. Qur'an and Tafsir Anchor: Surah Al-Inshiqaq (84:1-6)
Allah warns us of our ultimate meeting:

> **يَا أَيُّهَا الْإِنسَانُ إِنَّكَ كَادِحٌ إِلَىٰ رَبِّكَ كَدْحًا فَمُلَاقِيهِ**  
> *Ya ayyuha al-insanu innaka kadihun ila rabbika kadhan fa-mulaqih.*  
> *"O human being! Indeed you are laboring toward your Lord with strenuous labor, and you will surely meet Him."* (Surah Al-Inshiqaq 84:6).

**Classical Tafsir (*Tafsir al-Qurtubi*):** Imam al-Qurtubi writes that every soul is striving toward its Creator day by day. Prayer is the scheduled spiritual checkpoint where we stand before Allah in this world before our ultimate accounting on the Day of Judgment.

### 2. Tajweed Rule in Action: The Four Rules of Noon Sakinah and Tanween
When a Noon Sakinah (نْ) or Tanween (ً ٍ ٌ) is followed by an Arabic letter, one of four rules applies:
1. **Izhar Halqi (Clear Pronunciation):** Pronounced clearly before the six throat letters: Hamzah, Ha, 'Ayn, Ha, Ghayn, Kha. Example: **مَنْ آمَنَ** (*man amana*).
2. **Idgham (Merging):** Merged into the six letters of *Yarmalun* (ي ر م ل و ن):
   * *Idgham bi-Ghunnah* (with nasal tone) for letters: Ya, Noon, Meem, Waw. Example: **مَن يَقُولُ** (*may-yaqul*).
   * *Idgham bila Ghunnah* (without nasal tone) for letters: Lam and Ra. Example: **مِن رَّبِّهِمْ** (*mir-rabbihim*).
3. **Iqlab (Conversion):** Converted into a hidden Meem with a nasal hum before the letter Ba. Example: **مِن بَعْدِ** (*mim-ba'd*).
4. **Ikhfa' Haqiqi (Concealment):** Pronounced with a light nasal hum before the remaining 15 letters. Example: **مِن قَبْلِ** (*min-qabl*).

### 3. Classical Maliki Text Anchor: *Matn Ibn 'Ashir*
Lines 117-118 of *Al-Murshid al-Mu'in* summarize the prostration of omission:
> **قَبْلَ السَّلاَمِ سَجْدَتَانِ يَسْجُدُ *** إِنْ نَقَصَتْ سُنَّتُهُ لاَ يَشْهَدُ**  
> **وَبَعْدَهُ يَسْجُدُ إِنْ تَزِيدُ *** مُتَشَهِّدًا لِخَتْمِهِ يُعِيدُ**  
> *"Before the Salam two prostrations are made if a Sunnah was decreased, and he does not recite Tashahhud. And after it he prostrates if he added, reciting Tashahhud and repeating the concluding Salam."*

### 4. Prophetic Hadith Anchor: Hadith 3 of Nawawi's 40
The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> **بُنِيَ الْإِسْلَامُ عَلَى خَمْسٍ: شَهَادَةِ أَنْ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَأَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا رَسُولُ اللَّهِ، وَإِقَامِ الصَّلَاةِ، وَإِيتَاءِ الزَّكَاةِ، وَحَجِّ الْبَيْتِ، وَصَوْمِ رَمَضَانَ**  
> *"Islam is built upon five: testifying that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, establishing prayer, giving Zakah, performing pilgrimage to the House, and fasting Ramadan."* (Sahih al-Bukhari 8, Sahih Muslim 16, Sahih).

**Family Application:** Establishing prayer (*Iqamat al-Salah*) means more than praying quickly; it means perfecting wudu, learning the rulings of repair (*Sujud al-Sahw*), and maintaining concentration (*Khushu'*). When children learn how to fix mistakes in prayer, they gain confidence and peace of mind before Allah.
