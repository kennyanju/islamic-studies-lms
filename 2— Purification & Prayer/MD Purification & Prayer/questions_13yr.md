### Part A: Advanced Multiple Choice (8 Questions)
*Select the single best answer.*

1.  **Which of the following best describes the Ash'ari theological understanding of our relationship with Allah in worship?**
    *   a) Allah is dependent on our actions to manifest His power.
    *   b) Worship is a tool we use to earn blessings from a Creator Who requires our assistance.
    *   c) Allah is Al-Ghani (absolutely Self-Sufficient) and commands worship solely for our spiritual growth, as we are Al-Fuqara' (dependent).
    *   d) Worship is optional since Allah does not need it.

2.  **A large container of well water is mixed with pure laundry soap. The water now looks cloudy and smells strongly of soap. Legally, what is its status in Maliki Fiqh?**
    *   a) It remains Mutlaq/Tahur because soap is clean.
    *   b) It is Tahir (pure but non-purifying); it cannot be used for Wudu.
    *   c) It is Najis (impure) because its qualities changed.
    *   d) It is Makruh to use, but Wudu is still valid.

3.  **In the Maliki school, if a drop of urine falls into a large pool of water but does NOT change the color, taste, or smell of the water, what is the status of the water?**
    *   a) It becomes Najis immediately because urine is impure.
    *   b) It remains Tahur (purifying) and is valid for Wudu, without being disliked.
    *   c) It remains Tahur (purifying), but using it is disliked (Makruh) if other water is available.
    *   d) It is Tahir (pure but non-purifying).

4.  **How does the Maliki school differ from the Shafi'i school regarding the water volume threshold for impurities?**
    *   a) Shafi'is have no volume threshold, while Malikis use the Qullatayn (approx. 200 liters) rule.
    *   b) Shafi'is rule that water under Qullatayn becomes impure automatically upon contact with filth, whereas Malikis focus on whether the filth actually altered the water's attributes.
    *   c) Malikis require water to be boiled before Wudu if a drop of filth falls into it.
    *   d) There is no difference; both schools require Qullatayn.

5.  **Which of the following represents an obligatory pillar (Fard) of Wudu in the Maliki school that is NOT a Fard in the Hanafi school?**
    *   a) Washing the face.
    *   b) Wiping the entire head (*Mash al-Ra's*).
    *   c) Washing the arms to the elbows.
    *   d) Rinsing the mouth.

6.  **What is the definition of "Dalk" in the context of Maliki purification?**
    *   a) Pouring water three times over a limb.
    *   b) Passing the hand over the body part during or immediately after pouring water.
    *   c) Sniffing water deep into the nasal cavity.
    *   d) Wiping the ears with fresh water.

7.  **If a person performs Wudu but forgets to follow the standard order (e.g., they wipe their head before washing their arms), what is the status of their Wudu in the Maliki school?**
    *   a) The Wudu is invalid, and they must repeat it.
    *   b) The Wudu is valid because Tarteeb (order) is a Sunnah, not an obligation.
    *   c) The Wudu is valid only if they perform Tayammum immediately.
    *   d) The Wudu is invalid only if they did it intentionally.

8.  **Why does the Maliki school prioritize the posture of Sadl (arms at the sides) during Qiyam in obligatory prayers?**
    *   a) Because there are no Hadiths supporting clasping the hands.
    *   b) Based on the continuous, inherited practice of the people of Madinah (*'Amal Ahl al-Madinah*).
    *   c) Because crossing the arms is disliked in all prayers.
    *   d) Because it is easier for elderly people.

### Part B: Case Studies & Scenario Analysis (6 Questions)
*Analyze the following cases and determine the rulings according to Maliki Fiqh.*

9.  **Case Study 1: The Braided Hair**
    *   Fatima has her hair tied in tight, intricate braids. She enters a state of major ritual impurity and needs to perform Ghusl. She knows that she must wet her hair. Must she undo her braids to perform Ghusl according to the Maliki school?
    *   *Analysis:* In the Maliki school, Fatima does **not** have to undo her braids *provided* that the water can permeate the roots and reach her scalp. If the braids are so tight that water cannot reach the scalp, they must be undone.

10. **Case Study 2: The Delayed Wudu**
    *   Khalid begins Wudu. He washes his face and arms, but then notices that the water faucet has stopped running. He walks to another room to get a bottle of water, which takes about 30 seconds. His arms and face are still wet. He continues his Wudu by wiping his head and washing his feet. Is his Wudu valid?
    *   *Analysis:* Yes. Khalid's Wudu is valid because he maintained **Muwalat** (continuity). The brief delay did not cause his previously washed limbs to dry under normal conditions.

11. **Case Study 3: The Earth Substance**
    *   Sarah is in an office building and needs to perform Tayammum due to a medical skin condition that prevents her from using water. She cannot go outside, but she finds a polished, decorative marble slab in the lobby. Can she use this polished marble slab for Tayammum?
    *   *Analysis:* Yes. In the Maliki school, natural stone (including marble) is considered **Sa'id** (natural surface of the earth) and is valid for Tayammum, even if it is polished, as long as it is free of artificial coatings like wax or paint.

12. **Case Study 4: The Soup Mistake**
    *   Anas has a bucket of pure rainwater. While cooking, a small cup of chicken soup falls into the bucket. The water changes color slightly and smells like chicken soup. Anas uses this water to perform Wudu. Is his Wudu valid?
    *   *Analysis:* No. The water has been changed by a pure substance (soup) to the extent that it has lost its name and characteristics as plain water. It is now classified as **Ma' Tahir** (pure but non-purifying). Wudu cannot be performed with it.

13. **Case Study 5: The Forgotton Limb**
    *   Yasmine completes her Wudu and begins to pray. Mid-way through her prayer, she looks down and realizes she forgot to wash her left foot. What is the status of her prayer, and what must she do according to Maliki Fiqh?
    *   *Analysis:* Her prayer is invalid because washing the feet is a Fard of Wudu. She must stop her prayer immediately. Since a long time has probably passed and her limbs have dried, she must repeat her entire Wudu (due to loss of *Muwalat*) and then repeat her prayer.

14. **Case Study 6: The Emergency Window**
    *   Tariq fell into a deep sleep due to exhaustion and woke up 10 minutes before Maghrib. He had not prayed 'Asr. The sun is yellow and about to set. He performs Wudu quickly and prays 'Asr. Did he pray on time, and is he sinful?
    *   *Analysis:* Tariq prayed in the **Waqt Daruri** (necessary/emergency time) of 'Asr. Because he was asleep (which is a valid legal excuse), his prayer is valid and he is not sinful. However, if he had delayed it to this time deliberately without an excuse, it would have been sinful.

### Part C: Analytical Essay Questions (6 Questions)
*Provide a detailed, structured explanation for each prompt.*

15. **Explain the theological concept of Al-Ghani (The Self-Sufficient) and how it affects a Muslim's psychological attitude toward performing Salah.**
    *   *Suggested Outline:* 
        *   Define *Al-Ghani* (Allah has no needs).
        *   Explain that Allah is the Creator and does not benefit from our worship, nor does disobedience harm Him.
        *   Discuss how this shifts our mindset: we do not pray to "do Allah a favor" or as a burden. Rather, we pray because we are *Al-Fuqara'* (the needy ones) who require spiritual connection, guidance, and peace. It fosters humility and sincere gratitude (*Shukr*).

16. **Why is the act of "Dalk" (rubbing) considered a logical requirement of washing in the Maliki school? Contrast this with the view of other schools.**
    *   *Suggested Outline:*
        *   Define *Dalk* (rubbing the skin during or immediately after pouring water).
        *   Explain the Maliki linguistic and legal analysis: Imam Malik argued that simply letting water flow over a surface is called "pouring" (*sabb*), not "washing" (*ghasl*). Since the Quran commands us to "wash" our faces and arms, we must perform *Dalk* for it to count as washing.
        *   Contrast this with schools like the Shafi'i or Hanafi, which hold that letting water cover the limb is sufficient to satisfy the command, even without physical rubbing.

17. **Discuss the textual and historical reasons why the Maliki school prefers the posture of "Sadl" (arms at the sides) during the standing phase of prayer.**
    *   *Suggested Outline:*
        *   Explain that Imam Malik lived in Madinah, the city where the Prophet (pbuh) lived and taught.
        *   Introduce the concept of *'Amal Ahl al-Madinah* (the practice of the people of Madinah) as a primary source of law. Imam Malik valued the continuous, generational practice of thousands of residents of Madinah over isolated written reports (*hadith ahad*).
        *   Reference the *Mudawwanah*, where Imam Malik stated that he disliked clasping the hands in obligatory prayers, preferring the natural hanging of the arms, which was the visible practice he inherited from the top scholars of the Prophet's city.

18. **Compare the rulings of the Maliki and Shafi'i schools on wiping the head (Mash al-Ra's) during Wudu. How does this differences showcase the concept of Fiqh Literacy?**
    *   *Suggested Outline:*
        *   Define the Maliki position: Wiping the *entire* head is an obligation (*Fard*).
        *   Define the Shafi'i position: Wiping even a few hairs is sufficient.
        *   Explain the linguistic root of the difference: the interpretation of the Arabic particle *Ba'* in the verse *"Wamsahu bi-ru'usikum"*. Shafi'is treat it as *tab'eed* (partitive - "some of"), while Malikis treat it as *za'idah* (extra emphasis - "wipe your heads").
        *   Discuss how this teaches us Fiqh Literacy: it shows that differences are not due to scholars making things up, but rather are the result of systematic, sincere, linguistic, and legal interpretations of the same Quranic text.

19. **Explain the wisdom behind the allowance of Tayammum. How does it connect to the Ash'ari creed concerning the relationship between means (asbab) and the Creator?**
    *   *Suggested Outline:*
        *   Explain the practical wisdom: mercy, easing hardship, and ensuring that the connection of prayer is never broken.
        *   Connect to Aqidah: Under Ash'ari creed, physical elements like water do not possess inherent, independent power to purify. Rather, Allah is the only Creator of effects. Water is simply a physical cause/means (*sabab*) that Allah usually uses. When He commands us to use earth (*sa'id*) instead, He demonstrates that the spiritual purity comes from obedience to His command, not from the physical properties of the elements.

20. **What is the distinction between Waqt Ikhtiyari (preferred time) and Waqt Daruri (necessary time) in Maliki Fiqh, and why is this distinction practically important?**
    *   *Suggested Outline:*
        *   Define *Waqt Ikhtiyari*: the normal time window for a prayer when one has free choice.
        *   Define *Waqt Daruri*: the emergency time window, extending beyond the preferred window, meant for people with legitimate excuses (illness, sleeping, menstruation ending).
        *   Discuss practical importance: It helps Muslims understand the severity of delaying prayers without an excuse, while offering reassurance and a clear legal path for those who face unavoidable emergencies.
