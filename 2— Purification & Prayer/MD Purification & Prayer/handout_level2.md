## Section 1: Water Categories and Pure Water (Ma' Tahur)

In Islamic law (*Fiqh*), all acts of purification begin with understanding the substance we use to clean ourselves: **water**. Classical jurists classify water into three clear categories:

1. **Purifying Water (*Al-Ma' al-Tahur*):**  
   Water that remains in its natural, original state as created by Allah (such as rainwater, seawater, river water, spring water, and melted snow).  
   *Ruling:* It is pure in itself (*Tahir*) and purifies other things (*Mutahhir*). It removes physical impurities (*Khabath*) and lifts ritual impurity (*Hadath*) through Wudu and Ghusl.
2. **Clean but Non-Purifying Water (*Al-Ma' al-Tahir*):**  
   Water that was originally pure, but has mixed with a clean, permissible substance that altered its color, taste, or smell (such as tea, fruit juice, rose water, or soapy dishwater).  
   *Ruling:* It is clean to drink or use for everyday cooking, but it cannot be used for Wudu, Ghusl, or removing ritual impurities.
3. **Impure Water (*Al-Ma' al-Mutanajjis*):**  
   Water that has been contaminated by physical filth (*Najasah*, such as urine or blood), altering its taste, color, or smell.  
   *Ruling:* It is strictly prohibited to drink or use for Wudu and prayer.

## Section 2: Fara'id vs. Sunnahs vs. Fada'il of Wudu in Maliki Fiqh

A student of knowledge learns to distinguish between three categories of actions in worship:
* **Obligation (*Fard* / Plural: *Fara'id*):** An essential pillar without which wudu is legally invalid.
* **Confirmed Sunnah (*Sunnah Mu'akkadah*):** A practice consistently performed and emphasized by Prophet Muhammad ﷺ. Leaving it intentionally is blameworthy, but wudu remains valid.
* **Meritorious Act (*Fadilah* / Plural: *Fada'il*):** A recommended virtue that brings additional reward.

| Category | Actions in the Maliki School |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The Seven Fara'id (Obligations)** | 1. Intention (*Al-Niyyah*) in the heart at the beginning.<br>2. Washing the whole face (hairline to chin, ear to ear).<br>3. Washing the arms to the elbows, including hands.<br>4. Wiping the entire head from hairline to nape.<br>5. Washing the feet up to and including the ankles.<br>6. Gentle rubbing (*Al-Dalk*) with the wet hand.<br>7. Continuity (*Al-Muwalat*) without excessive delay. |
| **The Eight Confirmed Sunnahs** | 1. Washing hands to the wrists before dipping into the vessel.<br>2. Rinsing the mouth (*Madmadah*).<br>3. Sniffing water into the nostrils (*Istinshaq*).<br>4. Blowing water out from the nostrils (*Istinthar*).<br>5. Returning the wipe of the head from nape to forehead (*Radd al-Mash*).<br>6. Wiping the inside and outside of both ears.<br>7. Renewing fresh water for wiping the ears.<br>8. Maintaining the established sequence (*Al-Tartib*). |
| **Meritorious Acts (*Fada'il*)** | 1. Saying *Bismillah* before starting.<br>2. Washing limbs three times (*Al-Tathlith*).<br>3. Brushing teeth with a siwak or toothbrush.<br>4. Starting with the right side before the left (*Al-Tayamun*).<br>5. Minimizing water waste (*Iqtisad fi al-Ma'*). |

### Scholarly Differences on Wiping the Head:
* **Maliki and Hanbali Schools:** The worshipper must wipe the entire head from the front hairline all the way to the nape of the neck.
* **Shafi'i School:** Wiping even a small part of the head (even three hairs) fulfills the minimum obligation.
* **Hanafi School:** Wiping at least one-quarter of the head is the obligatory minimum.  
*We respect all valid schools of Sunni scholarship, while following the relied-upon Maliki position of wiping the entire head with care.*

## Section 3: What Breaks Wudu (Nawaqid al-Wudu)

Knowing what breaks wudu prevents doubt and hesitation (*Waswas*):

### Things that Definitely Break Wudu:
1. Natural bodily emissions from the private exits (urine, stool, passing gas, pre-seminal fluid).
2. Deep, heavy sleep where a person loses conscious awareness of sounds and movement around them.
3. Complete loss of consciousness (fainting, deep dizziness, or anesthesia).

### Things that DO NOT Break Wudu in Maliki Fiqh:
* Eating food of any kind, including cooked camel meat (the Hanbali school views camel meat as breaking wudu, whereas Malikis, Hanafis, and Shafi'is hold that it does not).
* Minor bleeding from cuts, scratches, or nosebleeds.
* Light drowsiness or nodding off where you can still hear people speaking around you.
* Touching clean animals or everyday objects.

## Section 4: The Five Conditions (Shurut) for a Valid Prayer

Before raising your hands for the opening Takbir, five mandatory conditions must be fulfilled:

1. **Purity (*Taharah*):** Being pure from minor ritual impurity (through Wudu) and major ritual impurity (through Ghusl), and ensuring body, clothing, and prayer space are free from physical filth (*Khabath*).
2. **Covering the 'Awrah:** Boys must cover from the navel to the knee with loose, non-transparent clothing; girls cover their entire body except for the face and hands.
3. **Facing the Qiblah:** Aligning your chest toward the Sacred Ka'bah in Makkah.
4. **Entry of the Prayer Time:** Praying within the prescribed time window. A prayer performed before its time enters is completely invalid.
5. **Intention (*Al-Niyyah*):** Knowing with certainty in your heart which specific prayer you are about to perform (such as Dhuhr today for the pleasure of Allah).

## Section 5: The Five Daily Prayers and the Maliki Posture of Standing

The five daily prayers form the spiritual rhythm of our day:

| Prayer Name | Prayer Time | Number of Rak'ahs | Recitation Method |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fajr** | Dawn before sunrise | **2 Rak'ahs** | Recited aloud |
| **Dhuhr** | Midday, after the sun passes its zenith | **4 Rak'ahs** | Recited quietly |
| **'Asr** | Late afternoon before sunset | **4 Rak'ahs** | Recited quietly |
| **Maghrib** | Immediately after sunset | **3 Rak'ahs** | First 2 rak'ahs aloud, 3rd rak'ah quiet |
| **'Isha** | Nighttime after twilight fades | **4 Rak'ahs** | First 2 rak'ahs aloud, last 2 rak'ahs quiet |

### Standing in Prayer: Sadl and Qabd
In the standing position (*Qiyam*) of obligatory prayers, the relied-upon position in the Maliki school is **Sadl** (resting the arms naturally and gently at the sides).  
* **Why do Malikis practice Sadl?** Imam Malik lived and taught in Madinah, the city of the Prophet ﷺ. He observed that the scholars, elders, and descendants of the Companions in Madinah stood with their arms naturally at their sides during obligatory prayers. This continuous living communal practice (*'Amal Ahl al-Madinah*) was a strong proof of the Sunnah.
* **What about Qabd?** Clasping the right hand over the left wrist or forearm (*Qabd*) is also an authentic Sunni practice narrated in hadith. In the Maliki school, *Qabd* is permitted in voluntary prayers. Both *Sadl* and *Qabd* are respected, valid Sunnahs, and Muslims should pray together with love, unity, and mutual respect in every mosque.

### The Reward of Congregational Prayer (Salat al-Jama'ah)
The Prophet ﷺ said:
> *"Prayer in congregation is twenty-seven times superior to praying alone."* (Sahih al-Bukhari 645, Sahih Muslim 650, Sahih).

## Section 6: Curriculum Anchors in Practice

### 1. Qur'an and Tafsir Anchor: Surah Al-Bayyinah (98:5) and Al-Ma'idah (5:6)
Allah states:

> **وَمَا أُمِرُوا إِلَّا لِيَعْبُدُوا اللَّهَ مُخْلِصِينَ لَهُ الدِّينَ حُنَفَاءَ وَيُقِيمُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَيُؤْتُوا الزَّكَاةَ ۚ وَذَٰلِكَ دِينُ الْقَيِّمَةِ**  
> *Wa ma umiru illa li-ya'budu Allaha mukhlisina lahu al-dina hunafa'a wa yuqimu al-salata wa yu'tu al-zakata wa dhalika dinu al-qayyimah.*  
> *"And they were not commanded except to worship Allah, being sincere to Him in religion, upright, and to establish prayer and give Zakah. And that is the correct religion."* (Surah Al-Bayyinah 98:5).

**Classical Tafsir (*Tafsir Ibn Juzayy al-Kalbi*):** Ibn Juzayy explains that this verse proves that physical actions of prayer and purity are worthless without sincere inner intention (*Ikhlas*). Purity of the heart must accompany the washing of the body.

### 2. Tajweed Rule in Action: The Echo Letters (Qalqalah)
*Qalqalah* is a bouncing, echoing sound produced when one of the five letters of Qalqalah carries a Sukun (quiescent letter) or when stopping at the end of a word:  
* The five letters are grouped in the phrase: **قُطْبُ جَدٍّ** (*Qaf, Ta, Ba, Jim, Dal*).
* Examples:
  * In Surah Al-Bayyinah (98:8): **تَجْرِي** (*taj-ri*): The letter Jim has a gentle bounce (*Qalqalah Sughra*).
  * In Surah Al-Falaq (113:1): **الْفَلَقِ** (*al-falaq*): When stopping on Qaf, it receives a clear bounce (*Qalqalah Kubra*).
  * In Surah Al-Ikhlas (112:1): **أَحَدٌ** (*ahad*): When stopping on Dal, it bounces clearly.

### 3. Classical Maliki Text Anchor: *Matn al-'Ashmawiyyah*
Imam 'Abd al-Bari al-'Ashmawi writes in *Matn al-'Ashmawiyyah*:
> *"Know that water which is valid for purification is pure water (Ma' Tahur), which has not been altered in color, taste, or odor by anything that separates from it. The obligations of wudu are seven: intention, washing the face, washing the arms to the elbows, wiping the whole head, washing the feet to the ankles, rubbing, and continuity."*

### 4. Prophetic Hadith Anchor: Hadith 2 of Nawawi's 40 (Hadith of Jibril)
Sayyidina 'Umar ibn al-Khattab (RA) narrated that Angel Jibril asked the Prophet ﷺ about Islam, and the Prophet ﷺ replied:

> **الْإِسْلَامُ أَنْ تَشْهَدَ أَنْ لَا إِلَهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ وَأَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا رَسُولُ اللَّهِ، وَتُقِيمَ الصَّلَاةَ، وَتُؤْتِيَ الزَّكَاةَ، وَتَصُومَ رَمَضَانَ، وَتَحُجَّ الْبَيْتَ إِنِ اسْتَطَعْتَ إِلَيْهِ سَبِيلًا**  
> *"Islam is to testify that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, to establish prayer, to give Zakah, to fast Ramadan, and to perform the pilgrimage to the House if you have the means."* (Sahih Muslim 8, Sahih).

**Family Application:** Prayer is the visible pillar of our Islam. Setting a family prayer routine where parents and children pray together in congregation fills our home with light, strengthens bonds between siblings, and instills lifelong discipline.
