## Section 1: The Conditions of Prayer: Obligation versus Validity

As young Muslims reach adolescence and physical maturity (*Bulugh*), the sacred law (*Shari'ah*) addresses them as morally accountable individuals (*Mukallafun*). In classical Maliki jurisprudence, the prerequisites of prayer are divided into two categories:

### 1. Conditions of Obligation (*Shurut al-Wujub*):
These conditions dictate who is legally required to pray:
* **Islam:** Being a Muslim.
* **Puberty (*Bulugh*):** Reaching physical maturity through biological signs or reaching fifteen lunar years.
* **Sanity (*'Aql*):** Possessing sound mental capacity.
* **Freedom from Menstruation and Post-Natal Bleeding (*Al-Naqa' min al-Hayd wal-Nifas*):** For young women, prayer is neither obligatory nor permitted during active bleeding; once bleeding completely ceases, Ghusl is performed and prayers resume immediately without making up missed prayers.
* **Entry of the Prayer Time (*Dukhul al-Waqt*):** An obligation only attaches once its prescribed time begins.

### 2. Conditions of Validity (*Shurut al-Sihhah*):
These conditions dictate whether a prayer is accepted as legally valid:
* **Purity from Ritual Impurities (*Taharat al-Hadath*):** Minor impurity removed via Wudu, major impurity removed via Ghusl (or Tayammum when legally justified).
* **Purity from Physical Filth (*Taharat al-Khabath*):** Ensuring body, clothes, and praying surface are free from unexcused impurities.
* **Covering the 'Awrah (*Satr al-'Awrah*):**
  * *For Men:* The minimum area from the navel to the knee.
  * *For Women:* The entire body except the face and the hands.
* **Facing the Qiblah (*Istiqbal al-Qiblah*):** Directing the chest toward the Ka'bah.
* **The Opening Intention (*Al-Niyyah*):** Explicitly intending the specific prayer concurrent with the opening Takbir.

## Section 2: Travel Concessions: Shortening (Qasr) and Combining (Jam')

Islam balances devotion with divine ease (*Rukhsah*). Allah states in the Qur'an:  
*"Allah intends for you ease and does not intend for you hardship."* (Surah Al-Baqarah 2:185).

| Concession | Legal Rulings in the Maliki School |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Shortening (*Al-Qasr*)** | Shortening the four-rak'ah obligatory prayers (Dhuhr, 'Asr, and 'Isha) to **two rak'ahs**. |
| **Distance Requirement** | The journey must be at least **four Barids** (approximately 48 Arabian miles, equal to 77-80 kilometers or 48 miles) one way. |
| **Lawful Journey** | The journey must be for a permissible purpose (such as education, work, family visitation, or pilgrimage). |
| **Departure Boundary** | Shortening begins only after passing beyond the outermost residential buildings of your home town or city. |
| **Length of Stay** | If you intend to stay at your destination for **four full days or more** (20 obligatory prayers), you pray complete prayers (*Itmam*) upon arrival. If you intend to stay less than four days, you shorten throughout your stay. |
| **Prayers Not Shortened** | **Fajr** (2 rak'ahs) and **Maghrib** (3 rak'ahs) are never shortened under any circumstance. |
| **Combining Prayers (*Al-Jam'*)** | Combining Dhuhr with 'Asr, or Maghrib with 'Isha (either early at the first prayer time, *Jam' Taqdim*, or late at the second prayer time, *Jam' Ta'khir*). Permitted while actively traveling on the road, during severe rain and mud at the mosque, and at 'Arafah and Muzdalifah during Hajj. |

## Section 3: Wiping Over Leather Footwear (Khuffayn) and Bandages (Jaba'ir)

### 1. Wiping Over Leather Footwear (*Mash 'ala al-Khuffayn*):
Wiping over footwear is an established Sunnah confirmed by dozens of Companions of the Prophet ﷺ:
* **Conditions of Validity:**
  1. The socks or boots must be made of pure leather or durable, water-impermeable material that can withstand continuous walking.
  2. They must cover the feet completely up to and including the ankle-bones.
  3. They must be put on while in a state of **complete ritual purity established by washing with water** (not Tayammum).
* **The Method of Wiping:** Place the right moistened hand on the top of the foot and the left hand beneath the sole, drawing the hands across the foot once from the toes toward the ankle.
* **Duration in Maliki Fiqh:** Unlike other schools that restrict wiping to 24 hours for residents and 72 hours for travelers, Imam Malik held that a person may continue wiping without a fixed time limit, provided they do not remove the footwear and do not enter a state of major ritual impurity (*Janabah*).

### 2. Medical Bandages and Splints (*Al-Jaba'ir*):
If a person has a bone fracture, cast, surgical stitches, or open wound covered by a bandage:
* The patient does not remove the cast or bandage if doing so would cause pain, harm, or delay healing.
* When performing wudu, they wash all healthy limbs, moisten their hands, and gently wipe over the exterior of the bandage (*Al-Mash 'ala al-Jaba'ir*).
* This concession is valid even if the cast was applied while not in a state of wudu, based on the Islamic legal maxim: *"Harm must be eliminated"* (*Al-Dararu Yuzal*).

## Section 4: The Maliki Tradition of Sadl and 'Amal Ahl al-Madinah

In the Maliki school, the relied-upon and prevalent position for standing in obligatory prayers is **Sadl** (allowing the arms to hang naturally at the sides).

### Why do Malikis practice Sadl?
1. **The Proof of 'Amal Ahl al-Madinah (Communal Practice):** Imam Malik witnessed thousands of the grandchildren and senior students of the Companions in Madinah standing before Allah with their arms naturally draped at their sides. In Maliki jurisprudence, an uninterrupted, mass communal practice (*Tawatur 'Amali*) in the Prophet's city is a stronger proof of the Sunnah than an isolated individual transmission (*Khabar al-Ahad*).
2. **The Posture of Natural Humility:** Letting the limbs rest freely at the sides embodies quiet dignity, ease, and total physical submission.
3. **Respecting Scholarly Diversity (*Adab al-Ikhtilaf*):** Clasping the right hand over the left (*Qabd*) is also an authentic Sunni practice supported by authentic hadiths. In the Maliki school, *Qabd* is permitted in voluntary prayers. All four Sunni Imams agree that both *Sadl* and *Qabd* are valid, acceptable Sunnahs. Neither posture invalidates prayer, and Muslims must always pray side-by-side with brotherhood, respect, and mutual love.

## Section 5: Congregational Prayer, Friday Prayer, and Special Circumstances

### 1. Congregational Prayer (*Salat al-Jama'ah*):
Praying in congregation is an emphasized Sunnah for men in the mosque.  
* **Catching a Rak'ah (*Idrak al-Rak'ah*):** A latecomer (*Masbuq*) is counted as having caught a rak'ah if they join the Imam and achieve steady, tranquil bowing (*Ruku'*) alongside him before the Imam begins rising from bowing.
* **Completing Missed Units:** After the Imam says the final Salam, the latecomer stands up without an additional Takbir (unless they missed an entire rak'ah) and completes missed units according to the rule:
  * In recitation: they treat what they pray now as the beginning (*Qada'* in words: reciting Fatihah and a Surah aloud or quietly as appropriate).
  * In physical movements: they treat what they already prayed with the Imam as the beginning (*Bina'* in actions).

### 2. Friday Prayer (*Salat al-Jumu'ah*):
Friday prayer is an individual obligation (*Fard 'Ayn*) upon adult, resident, healthy Muslim men:
* Replaces the Dhuhr prayer with two rak'ahs recited aloud preceded by two sermons (*Khutbatayn*).
* Listening to the Khutbah silently is obligatory; speaking during the sermon is strictly prohibited.

### 3. Prayer of the Sick (*Salat al-Marid*):
A Muslim never abandons prayer due to illness. A patient prays standing if able; if unable to stand, they pray sitting; if unable to sit, they pray lying on their right side facing the Qiblah, gesturing with their head for bowing and prostration.

## Section 6: Curriculum Anchors in Practice

### 1. Qur'an and Tafsir Anchor: Surah Al-Haqqah (69:13-24)
Allah describes the Day when our deeds will be presented:

> **يَوْمَئِذٍ تُعْرَضُونَ لَا تَخْفَىٰ مِنكُمْ خَافِيَةٌ**  
> *Yawma'idhin tu'raduna la takhfa minkum khafiyah.*  
> *"On that Day you will be exhibited; not a single secret of yours will remain hidden."* (Surah Al-Haqqah 69:18).

**Classical Tafsir (*Tafsir al-Qurtubi*):** Imam al-Qurtubi states that the believer who regularly stands in prayer with physical and internal purity will find their record of deeds delivered into their right hand with joy on that Day.

### 2. Tajweed Rule in Action: The Rules of Meem Sakinah
When a Meem Sakinah (مْ: a Meem with a Sukun) meets another Arabic letter, three rules apply:
1. **Ikhfa' Shafawi (Lip Concealment):** When Meem Sakinah meets the letter Ba (ب). The lips close gently with a two-count nasal hum (*Ghunnah*). Example: **تَرْمِيهِم بِحِجَارَةٍ** (*tarmihim bi-hijarah*).
2. **Idgham Shafawi / Mithlayn (Lip Merging):** When Meem Sakinah meets another Meem (م). The two merge with a complete nasal hum. Example: **لَهُم مَّا يَشَاءُونَ** (*lahum-ma yasha'un*).
3. **Izhar Shafawi (Lip Clarity):** When Meem Sakinah meets any of the remaining 26 letters. The Meem is pronounced clearly without extra nasal stretch. Example: **أَلَمْ تَرَ** (*alam tara*). Extra care is taken before Waw (و) and Fa (ف) so the Meem is not mistakenly hidden.

### 3. Classical Maliki Text Anchor: *Al-Risalah* of Ibn Abi Zayd al-Qayrawani
Imam Ibn Abi Zayd al-Qayrawani (d. 386 AH) writes in his celebrated manual *Al-Risalah*:
> *"Shortening the prayer during travel is a confirmed Sunnah. A person shortens four-rak'ah prayers to two units over a distance of four Barids, which is forty-eight miles. Shortening begins when one leaves behind the residential buildings of one's city. If one intends to reside at one's destination for four days, one completes the prayers upon arrival."*

### 4. Prophetic Hadith Anchor: Hadith 10 of Nawawi's 40
The Prophet ﷺ said:

> **إِنَّ اللَّهَ طَيِّبٌ لَا يَقْبَلُ إِلَّا طَيِّبًا، وَإِنَّ اللَّهَ أَمَرَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ بِمَا أَمَرَ بِهِ الْمُرْسَلِينَ**  
> *"Indeed, Allah is Pure, and He accepts only what is pure. And Allah has commanded the believers with that which He commanded the Messengers."* (Sahih Muslim 1015, Sahih).

**Family Application:** Purity applies to everything we touch: our bodies through wudu, our wealth through honest work and avoiding interest, and our family interactions through truthfulness and respect. When youth practice travel concessions with confidence, they realize the beauty and flexibility of Islamic law in daily life.
