# Level 4 Quiz: Purification & Prayer

## Part A: Multiple Choice Questions

1. What is the minimum travel distance required to qualify for shortening the four-rak'ah prayers (*Al-Qasr*) in classical Maliki jurisprudence?
   (A) Four Barids, approximately 48 Arabian miles (equal to 77 to 80 kilometers)
   (B) Ten kilometers from one's front door
   (C) Any journey that requires taking a motorized vehicle
   (D) Exactly 500 nautical miles

2. Which prayers are eligible for shortening (*Al-Qasr*) during a qualifying journey?
   (A) Fajr, Dhuhr, and 'Asr
   (B) Maghrib and 'Isha only
   (C) All five daily prayers including Fajr and Maghrib
   (D) The four-rak'ah prayers: Dhuhr, 'Asr, and 'Isha

3. Under Maliki fiqh, if a traveler intends to stay at their destination for four full days (20 obligatory prayers) or longer, how should they pray upon arrival?
   (A) Continue shortening for up to one full year
   (B) Pray voluntary prayers only and skip the obligatory units
   (C) Pray complete prayers (*Itmam*) of four rak'ahs from the moment of arrival
   (D) Shorten the first three days and complete on the fourth day

4. At what exact geographical point does a traveler begin shortening their prayers when departing on a journey?
   (A) The moment they pack their bags inside their home
   (B) As soon as they pass beyond the outermost residential boundaries of their city or town
   (C) Exactly halfway between their hometown and their destination
   (D) Only upon arriving at their final destination city

5. What prerequisite condition must be fulfilled before a Muslim is permitted to wipe over leather footwear (*Khuffayn*) during Wudu?
   (A) The footwear must have been put on while in a complete state of ritual purity established with water
   (B) The footwear must be purchased from an authorized leather merchant in Madinah
   (C) The wearer must have fasted the preceding day
   (D) The footwear must be colored black or dark brown

6. In the Maliki school, how is the physical wipe over leather footwear (*Mash 'ala al-Khuffayn*) performed?
   (A) Washing the outer leather surface three separate times from the ankle down to the tips of the toes
   (B) Wiping only the back heel and upper ankle cuff of both leather socks with a single wet hand
   (C) Sprinkling water lightly over the top of each shoe without passing the palm across the leather
   (D) Placing the right moistened hand on the top of the foot and the left hand beneath the sole, wiping across from toes toward the ankle

7. What is the primary evidentiary foundation for the practice of *Sadl* (resting arms at the sides) during prayer in the Maliki school?
   (A) An isolated textual report prohibiting raising the hands during prayer
   (B) The living, mass communal practice of the scholars and descendants of the Sahabah in Madinah (*'Amal Ahl al-Madinah*)
   (C) A desire by later jurists to make the physical standing movements of the prayer faster
   (D) An administrative decree issued by the Umayyad governors in Damascus to standardize prayer postures

8. How is a rak'ah legally counted as caught by a latecomer (*Masbuq*) joining a congregational prayer?
   (A) By entering the mosque before the Imam finishes reciting Surah Al-Fatihah
   (B) By touching the shoulder of the person in the final row
   (C) By achieving calm, stable bowing (*Ruku'*) alongside the Imam before the Imam begins rising from bowing
   (D) By catching the final Tashahhud sitting with the Imam

9. Which two prayers are legally permitted to be combined (*Al-Jam'*) during a qualifying travel journey or severe rain at the mosque?
   (A) Dhuhr with 'Asr, and Maghrib with 'Isha
   (B) Fajr with Dhuhr, and 'Asr with Maghrib
   (C) 'Isha with Fajr, and Dhuhr with Maghrib
   (D) Any three prayers of the worshipper's choosing

10. What is the scholarly consensus among the four Sunni schools of law regarding Muslims who pray with *Sadl* (arms at sides) and those who pray with *Qabd* (hands clasped)?
    (A) A follower must always abandon their own school's hand posture to match the Imam's posture or repeat the prayer
    (B) Congregational prayer is only valid when the Imam and all followers belong to the exact same legal school
    (C) Performing either posture invalidates the standing pillar of prayer unless compensated by two prostrations of forgetfulness
    (D) Both Sadl and Qabd are authentic, recognized Sunni postures; prayers behind either are completely valid, and mutual brotherhood is maintained

## Part C: Matching Terms

* Al-Qasr ─── Shortening four-rak'ah prayers to two units during travel
* Al-Jam' ─── Combining Dhuhr with 'Asr, or Maghrib with 'Isha under valid concessions
* Masah 'ala al-Khuffayn ─── Wiping over sturdy leather footwear in place of washing feet
* Sadl ─── Leaving arms to rest naturally at the sides during Qiyam
* 'Amal Ahl al-Madinah ─── The continuous living practice of Madinah used as legal proof

## Part D: Reflection & Application

16. A family travels 120 miles to attend a 3-day family gathering. Detail how they should pray Dhuhr, 'Asr, Maghrib, and 'Isha during their stay.
17. Outline the conditions required for footwear to be eligible for wiping (*Mash*). Why do classical jurists distinguish sturdy leather footwear from thin, absorbent cotton socks?
18. Explain how the principle of *Adab al-Ikhtilaf* (respecting scholarly differences) applies when Muslims praying with *Sadl* and *Qabd* pray together in the same congregation.

## Answer Key & Marking Guide

1. A - 48 Arabian miles (approximately 77-80 km or four barids) is the classical threshold for travel concessions.
2. D - Only four-rak'ah prayers (Dhuhr, 'Asr, and 'Isha) are shortened; Fajr (2) and Maghrib (3) are never shortened.
3. C - Intending to reside at a destination for four full days (20 prayers) or more establishes residency, requiring complete prayers (Itmam) upon arrival.
4. B - Travel concessions commence only once the traveler has physically exited beyond the outermost residential boundaries of their city.
5. A - Purity established through complete water washing must be achieved before donning the leather socks.
6. D - The Sunnah method involves simultaneously drawing the right hand across the top and the left hand across the sole from toes toward ankles.
7. B - Imam Malik grounded Sadl in the unbroken, mass living consensus of Madinah, where thousands prayed with Sadl without objection.
8. C - Achieving stable, tranquil bowing (Ruku') alongside the Imam before he begins straightening is the legal criterion for catching a rak'ah.
9. A - Concessional combination is legally restricted to Dhuhr with 'Asr, and Maghrib with 'Isha.
10. D - Sunni orthodoxy universally validates both Sadl and Qabd, viewing diversity in secondary postures as a source of mercy rather than division.
