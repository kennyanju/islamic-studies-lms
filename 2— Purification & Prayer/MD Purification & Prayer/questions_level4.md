# Level 4 Quiz: Purification & Prayer

## Part A: Multiple Choice Questions
1. What is the minimum travel distance required in classical Maliki fiqh to qualify for shortening prayers (Al-Qasr)?
   (A) 10 miles (16 km).
   (B) 48 Arabian miles (approximately 77–80 km).
   (C) 250 miles (400 km).
   (D) Any distance that requires boarding an airplane.

2. Which of the daily obligatory prayers are shortened from four rak'ahs down to two during travel?
   (A) Fajr, Dhuhr, and 'Asr.
   (B) Dhuhr, 'Asr, and 'Isha.
   (C) Maghrib and 'Isha.
   (D) All five daily prayers.

3. Under what condition does a traveler cease shortening prayers upon reaching their destination?
   (A) As soon as they enter a hotel room.
   (B) If they intend to stay for four full days (20 prayers) or more.
   (C) If the weather turns sunny and warm.
   (D) If they find a supermarket selling halal food.

4. When does the legal permission to shorten prayers begin for an outgoing traveler?
   (A) While still packing luggage inside their bedroom.
   (B) As soon as they cross beyond the outermost residential buildings of their home city.
   (C) Only after arriving at the destination airport terminal.
   (D) After driving for at least three consecutive hours.

5. What is an essential prerequisite before wiping over leather socks (Khuffayn)?
   (A) The socks must be purchased during the sacred month of Ramadan.
   (B) The socks must be put on while in a complete state of purity from water ablution.
   (C) The socks must be colored black or dark brown.
   (D) The socks must be wiped with pure rose water.

6. How is the physical act of wiping over leather footwear performed?
   (A) Washing the inside of the socks with three cups of boiling water.
   (B) Wiping simultaneously with the right hand over the top and left hand under the sole.
   (C) Submerging the entire foot in a basin while wearing the socks.
   (D) Spraying aerosol mist across the laces.

7. What is the primary evidentiary foundation for the practice of *Sadl* (leaving arms at the sides) in the Maliki school?
   (A) Ancient Persian diplomatic customs.
   (B) The uninterrupted living normative practice of the people of Madinah ('Amal Ahl al-Madinah).
   (C) A decision reached by modern educational committees in North Africa.
   (D) A translation error in early English Islamic dictionaries.

8. How does a worshipper know they have successfully caught a rak'ah with the Imam in congregation?
   (A) If they enter the mosque before the Adhan is called.
   (B) If they join the Imam in tranquil Ruku' before the Imam raises his head.
   (C) If they shake hands with the person standing on their right side.
   (D) If they hear the Imam recite the first verse of Surah Al-Fatihah.

9. Which two prayers may be combined together during valid journey circumstances?
   (A) Fajr with Dhuhr, and 'Asr with Maghrib.
   (B) Dhuhr with 'Asr, and Maghrib with 'Isha.
   (C) 'Isha with Fajr.
   (D) Jumu'ah with the following day's Fajr.

10. What is the consensus of Sunni scholars regarding a person who prays with arms at their sides (Sadl) behind an Imam who folds them (Qabd)?
    (A) The prayer is invalid and both must repeat it.
    (B) The prayer is completely valid, as both postures are recognized within the Sunnah.
    (C) The person praying with Sadl must pay a penalty to the mosque.
    (D) The Imam must restart the prayer and command everyone to copy him.

## Part B: Fill in the Blanks
Vocabulary Bank: Sadl, Qasr, Khuffayn, Idrak, Jam'
1. Shortening a four-rak'ah obligatory prayer to two rak'ahs during qualified travel is termed ______.
2. Combining two prayers together at the time of the earlier or later prayer is known as ______.
3. Sturdy, ankle-covering leather footwear permitted for wiping during ablution are called ______.
4. The Maliki posture of allowing the arms to hang naturally during the standing position of prayer is ______.
5. Successfully joining the Imam in bowing to catch a rak'ah is called ______ al-Rak'ah.

## Part C: Matching Terms
* Al-Qasr ─── Shortening four-rak'ah prayers to two units during travel
* Al-Jam' ─── Combining Dhuhr with 'Asr or Maghrib with 'Isha
* Masah 'ala al-Khuffayn ─── Wiping over sturdy leather footwear in place of washing feet
* Sadl ─── Leaving arms to rest naturally at the sides during Qiyam
* 'Amal Ahl al-Madinah ─── The continuous living practice of Madinah used as legal proof

## Part D: Reflection & Application
1. A family travels 120 miles to attend a 3-day family reunion. Explain how they should pray Dhuhr, 'Asr, Maghrib, and 'Isha during their stay.
2. Outline the conditions required for footwear to be eligible for wiping (*Masah*). Why are thin cotton athletic socks viewed with caution by classical jurists?
3. Explain why the Maliki school prioritizes *'Amal Ahl al-Madinah* when establishing the posture of *Sadl* in prayer.
4. If a worshipper arrives late to the mosque and finds the Imam in the final Tashahhud, what should they do, and how do they complete their prayer?

## Answer Key & Marking Guide
1. B - 48 Arabian miles (approx. 77–80 km / 4 barids) is the classical threshold for travel concessions.
2. B - Only the 4-rak'ah prayers (Dhuhr, 'Asr, 'Isha) are shortened; Fajr and Maghrib are never shortened.
3. B - An intention to reside for 4 full days (20 prayers) establishes residency, terminating shortening upon arrival.
4. B - Shortening begins as soon as the traveler departs beyond the residential boundaries of their city.
5. B - Purity from water washing must be established prior to donning the Khuffayn.
6. B - The classical method involves wiping top and bottom simultaneously with moistened hands.
7. B - Imam Malik relied on the living consensus of Madinah, where thousands prayed with Sadl without objection.
8. B - Catching the Imam in stable bowing (Ruku') before he begins rising counts as catching that rak'ah.
9. B - Only Dhuhr/'Asr and Maghrib/'Isha can be combined under valid concessions.
10. B - All Sunni legal authorities agree on the validity of both Sadl and Qabd across all schools.
