# Year 4 Syllabus: Evidence, Law, & Prophetic Mission

> **Target Learner**: Level 4 Track: Adolescent & Puberty (Ages 12-14)  
> **Weekly Schedule**: **3 extended sessions × 70 minutes** = 210 min/week  
> **Annual Instructional Volume**: 36 Weeks (3 Terms of 12 Weeks) = 126 hours  
> **ICO Benchmarks Absorbed**: Grade 7, Grade 8, and Grade 9 (analytical theology, systematic worship, Madinan battles, Caliphate)  
> **Classical Text Spine**: *Ibn 'Ashir* (Salah, Zakah, Fasting, Hajj), Sanusi's *Umm al-Barahin* (The 20 Necessary Attributes), *Risalah* of Ibn Abi Zayd (selected chapters), and Qadi 'Iyad's *Al-Shifa*  
> **Riwayah**: Hafs 'an 'Asim & Warsh 'an Nafi' (Juz' 29: Surah Al-Mulk to Surah Al-Mursalat, systematic Tajweed rules: Noon Sakinah, Meem Sakinah, Madd)

## Term 1: Advanced 'Aqidah & The Madinan Transformation (Weeks 1–12)

### Unit 4.1: The 20 Necessary Attributes of Allah (The Sanusi Framework) (Weeks 1–4)
* **Strand**: 'Aqidah (Ash'ari)
* **Session Allocation**: 12 extended sessions × 70 min = 14 hours
* **Classical Reference**: *Umm al-Barahin* (Imam Muhammad ibn Yusuf al-Sanusi) / *Ibn 'Ashir* (Lines 15–25).
* **Core Outcomes (Age 9–10)**:
  1. The student groups the 20 Necessary Attributes (*Al-Sifat al-Wajibah*) of Allah into the 4 classical categories:
     * **Nafsiyyah (Personal)**: *Al-Wujud* (Existence).
     * **Salbiyyah (Negative/Divesting - 5 attributes)**: *Al-Qidam* (Beginninglessness), *Al-Baqa'* (Endlessness), *Al-Mukhalafah lil-Hawadith* (Absolute difference from creation), *Al-Qiyam bin-Nafs* (Self-Subsistence), *Al-Wahdaniyyah* (Absolute Oneness).
     * **Ma'ani (Existential/Qualitative - 7 attributes)**: *Al-Qudrah* (Power), *Al-Iradah* (Will), *Al-'Ilm* (Knowledge), *Al-Hayah* (Life), *Al-Sam'* (Hearing), *Al-Basar* (Sight), *Al-Kalam* (Speech).
     * **Ma'nawiyyah (State attributes - 7 attributes)**: Being Powerful, Willing, Knowing, Living, Hearing, Seeing, Speaking.
  2. The student explains what is Impossible (*Mustahil*) for Allah: The direct opposites of these 20 attributes (non-existence, having a beginning, dying, resembling creation, needing anything, weakness, ignorance, deafness, blindness, muteness).
  3. The student explains what is Permissible (*Ja'iz*) for Allah: Creating or not creating any possible thing.
* **Extension Outcomes (13+)**:
  1. The Ash'ari doctrine of *Ta'alluqat* (functional attachments of attributes): How Power and Will attach to all possible things (*Al-Ja'izat*), while Knowledge attaches to the Necessary, Impossible, and Possible.
  2. Hermeneutical reconciliation of ambiguous scriptural texts (*Ayat al-Mutashabihat*): The two valid Sunni methodologies:
     * **Al-Tafwid** (Consigning the ultimate reality to Allah while affirming *Tanzih*, the way of the Salaf).
     * **Al-Ta'wil al-Tafsili** (Linguistic contextual interpretation to safeguard layman faith, the way of the Khalaf).
* **Ash'ari Creed Flag**: Affirming that Allah's Speech (*Kalam*) is an eternal, uncreated attribute of His Essence without letters, sounds, sequence, or silence.
* **🌍 Other Scholars Say (Reasons Box)**:
  > *Topic: How do Sunni Scholars Explain Apparent Spatial Texts (e.g. Istiwa' over the Throne)?*
  > **All Sunni schools agree**: Allah is completely unlike creation (*Laysa ka-mithlihi shay'*). Allah does not sit, stand, have physical weight, or occupy physical space, because space was created by Allah!
  > *Ash'ari & Maturidi scholars*: Held that *Tafwid* (saying: "Allah knows what He meant, and He is free of all physical traits") is safe and sound, while allowing *Ta'wil* (understanding *Istiwa'* as divine subjugation and majesty, like a king asserting sovereign rule).
  > *Athari / Hanbali scholars*: Affirmed the word *Istiwa'* exactly as revealed, firmly asserting *Bila Kayf* (without asking how, without physical resemblance).
  > *Why the difference?* Both sought to protect monotheism: The Atharis feared altering scriptural words, while the Ash'aris feared unlearned people imagining Allah as a physical person on a chair. Both are within the safe pale of *Ahl al-Sunnah*.
* **Qur'an & Hadith Evidence**: Surah Ash-Shura (42:11); Surah Taha (20:5); Imam Malik's golden rule: *"Al-Istiwa' ma'lum, wal-kayf majhul, wal-su'al 'anhu bid'ah"* (The establishment is known, the 'how' is incomprehensible, and interrogating it is an innovation).
* **Pedagogical Activities**: Designing an interactive mind map: "The 20 Jewels of Divine Perfection"; seminar debate on how understanding *Al-Qidam* protects the heart from existential anxiety.
* **Hifz & Memorisation**: *Ibn 'Ashir* (Creed section lines 15–25). Surah Al-Mulk (first 15 verses) with tajweed.

### Unit 4.2: Al-Isra' wal-Mi'raj & The Great Hijrah to Madinah (Weeks 5–8)
* **Strand**: Seerah & Islamic History
* **Session Allocation**: 12 extended sessions × 70 min = 14 hours
* **Classical Reference**: *Al-Fusul fi Seerat ar-Rasul* / *Al-Shifa* of Qadi 'Iyad (Miracles of the Prophet ﷺ).
* **Core Outcomes (Age 9–10)**:
  1. The student narrates the miraculous Night Journey (**Al-Isra'**) from Makkah to Jerusalem on the Buraq, leading all the Prophets in prayer at Al-Masjid al-Aqsa.
  2. The student describes the Heavenly Ascension (**Al-Mi'raj**) through the seven heavens, meeting the prophets (Adam, Yahya, 'Isa, Yusuf, Idris, Harun, Musa, Ibrahim), reaching the Lote Tree of the Utmost Boundary (*Sidrat al-Muntaha*), and receiving the supreme gift of the 5 daily prayers.
  3. The student recounts the two Pledges of 'Aqabah (*Bay'at al-'Aqabah*) and the perilous Hijrah journey with Sayyidina Abu Bakr: Hiding in Cave Thawr, the spider web, the miraculous milk of Umm Ma'bad, and the triumphant welcome in Madinah (*Tala'a al-Badru 'Alayna*).
* **Extension Outcomes (13+)**:
  1. The vision of Allah on the night of Mi'raj: Examining the scholarly discussion between Sayyiduna Ibn 'Abbas (who affirmed spiritual vision of the Divine) and Sayyidah 'A'ishah (who denied physical eye sight), and how Ash'ari theology reconciles both.
  2. Strategic planning of the Hijrah: Analyzing prophetic reliance on divine promise coupled with meticulous worldly preparation (hiring a skilled non-Muslim desert guide, using covert intelligence, reversing routes).
* **Qur'an & Hadith Evidence**: Surah Al-Isra' (17:1); Surah An-Najm (53:1–18); Surah At-Tawbah (9:40: *"Do not grieve; indeed Allah is with us."*).
* **Pedagogical Activities**: Mapping the Topography of Hijrah: Tracing the southern route to Cave Thawr and the coastal trek to Madinah; timeline construction of the 13 Makkan years.
* **Hifz & Memorisation**: Surah Al-Mulk (completion). Nawawi Hadith 21 (*"Say: I believe in Allah, and then remain steadfast"*).

### Unit 4.3: The Fiqh of Zakah: Wealth, Nisab, & Social Justice (Weeks 9–12)
* **Strand**: Fiqh (Maliki)
* **Session Allocation**: 12 extended sessions × 70 min = 14 hours
* **Classical Reference**: *Ibn 'Ashir* (Kitab al-Zakah) / *Al-Risalah* of Ibn Abi Zayd al-Qayrawani.
* **Core Outcomes (Age 9–10)**:
  1. The student defines **Zakah**: The mandatory annual purification of specific categories of wealth given to 8 Qur'anically prescribed recipients.
  2. The student calculates Zakah on contemporary monetary wealth:
     * Condition 1: Reaching the **Nisab** threshold (equivalent to 85 grams of pure gold or 595 grams of silver).
     * Condition 2: Passing of one full lunar year (**Al-Hawl**) in possession of that wealth.
     * The Rate: **2.5%** (one-fortieth).
  3. The student lists the 8 eligible recipients from Surah At-Tawbah (9:60): The poor (*Fuqara'*), the destitute (*Masakin*), administrators of zakah, those whose hearts are reconciled, freeing captives/slaves, debtors (*Gharimin*), in the cause of Allah, and the stranded traveler.
* **Extension Outcomes (13+)**:
  1. Zakah on livestock (*An'am*) and agricultural produce (*Harth*): Differentiating between naturally watered crops (10% / *'Ushr*) and artificially irrigated crops (5% / *Nisf al-'Ushr*).
  2. The Maliki ruling on permissible worn jewelry: Gold and silver jewelry owned by women for regular lawful adornment is **exempt** from Zakah in the relied-upon Maliki position (in stark contrast to the Hanafi school where worn gold is strictly zakatable).
* **Maliki Fiqh Flag**: Permissible worn gold jewelry is not subject to annual Zakah, as it is classified as a personal utility rather than growing wealth (*Mal Namin*).
* **🌍 Other Scholars Say (Reasons Box)**:
  > *Topic: Zakah on Women's Worn Gold Jewelry*
  > **All agree**: Gold bullion, coins, and hoarded investment gold are 100% subject to Zakah.
  >
  > | School | Position on Worn Personal Jewelry | Legal Proof / Reason |
  > |---|---|---|
  > | **Maliki** | **No Zakah due** | Followed the practice of Sayyiduna Ibn 'Umar and 'A'ishah who did not take zakah from their daughters' personal jewelry. |
  > | **Hanafi** | **Zakah is strictly due** (2.5%) | Based on general hadiths warning against wearing bracelets of fire if zakah is not paid. |
  > | **Shafi'i** | **No Zakah due** | Analogized personal jewelry to riding horses and clothing (non-growing assets). |
  > | **Hanbali** | **No Zakah due** | Shared the Maliki and Shafi'i deduction. |
  > *Why the difference?* Hanafis prioritized the general wording of hadiths on gold, while Malikis, Shafi'is, and Hanbalis prioritized the specific lived practice of the Sahabah in Madinah.
* **Qur'an & Hadith Evidence**: Surah At-Tawbah (9:60 and 9:103: *"Take from their wealth a charity by which you purify them and cause them increase"*).
* **Pedagogical Activities**: "The Islamic Treasury (Bayt al-Mal) Workshop": Students are given sample family financial portfolios (cash, gold, business inventory, debts) and must calculate the exact Zakah payable.
* **Hifz & Memorisation**: Surah Al-Qalam (Verses 1–25). Nawawi Hadith 22 & 23 (*"Purification is half of faith, and Alhamdulillah fills the scale"*).

## Term 2: The Social Shari'ah: Fasting, Travel, & The Defensive Battles (Weeks 13–24)

### Unit 4.4: In-Depth Fasting: Kaffarah, Fidyah, & Medical Exemptions (Weeks 13–16)
* **Strand**: Fiqh (Maliki)
* **Session Allocation**: 12 extended sessions × 70 min = 14 hours
* **Classical Reference**: *Ibn 'Ashir* (Bab al-Siyam) / *Mukhtasar Khalil* (Selections on Kaffarah).
* **Core Outcomes (Age 9–10)**:
  1. The student differentiates between what merely requires making up a missed fast (**Qada'** alone) vs what triggers both making up the day **and** paying an expiation (**Kaffarah**).
  2. The student identifies what triggers Kaffarah in the Maliki school: Intentionally, deliberately, and without a valid excuse breaking a Ramadan fast by eating, drinking, or intimate relations.
  3. The student describes the Maliki Kaffarah hierarchy: Feeding 60 poor people (which Imam Malik preferred as the most beneficial charity for society) or fasting two consecutive months.
  4. The student applies the rulings of sickness, travel, pregnancy, and elderly chronic weakness.
* **Extension Outcomes (13+)**:
  1. The legal nature of *Ibtal al-Ibadah* (invalidating an act of worship): In the Maliki school, voluntarily breaking *any* commenced act of worship (even a voluntary fast or prayer) without a valid excuse is prohibited and requires making it up (*Qada'*).
  2. Modern medical issues in fasting: Eye drops, ear drops, asthma inhalers, injections (nutritive vs non-nutritive), and blood draws according to classical Maliki principles.
* **Maliki Fiqh Flag**:
  * **Preference for feeding 60 poor people**: While other schools enforce fasting two consecutive months first, Imam Malik held that the options of Kaffarah are either equal or that feeding 60 poor people (*It'am Sittina Miskina*) is superior because its benefit reaches the wider community.
* **Qur'an & Hadith Evidence**: Surah Al-Baqarah (2:184–185); Hadith of the Bedouin who broke his fast in Ramadan (Bukhari & Muslim).
* **Pedagogical Activities**: Case-study diagnostic clinic: 8 real-world scenarios (e.g. an asthmatic child, an elderly grandmother, someone who accidentally drank water forgetting they were fasting) with student legal verdicts.
* **Hifz & Memorisation**: Surah Al-Qalam (completion). Nawawi Hadith 24 (Hadith Qudsi: *"O My servants, I have forbidden oppression for Myself"*).

### Unit 4.5: Travel Concessions: Shortening, Combining, & Khuffs (Weeks 17–20)
* **Strand**: Fiqh (Maliki)
* **Session Allocation**: 12 extended sessions × 70 min = 14 hours
* **Classical Reference**: *Mukhtasar al-Akhdari* (Kitab al-Salah: Salat al-Musafir) / *Ibn 'Ashir*.
* **Core Outcomes (Age 9–10)**:
  1. The student explains the legal concession of travel (**Rukhsah**): Shortening 4-rak'ah prayers (Dhuhr, 'Asr, 'Isha) to **2 rak'ahs** (**Qasr**). Note: Fajr (2) and Maghrib (3) are never shortened.
  2. The student identifies the conditions for shortening:
     * Minimum distance: Approximately **48 miles (approx. 77–80 km)** or 4 *Buruds*.
     * The journey must be for a lawful purpose (*Safar Mubah*).
     * The traveler has left the outskirts of their home city.
  3. The student explains combining prayers (**Jam'**): Joining Dhuhr with 'Asr, and Maghrib with 'Isha, during actual travel or torrential rain/mud at the mosque.
  4. The student demonstrates wiping over leather socks (**Masah 'ala al-Khuffayn**) and over medical bandages/casts (*Al-Jaba'ir*).
* **Extension Outcomes (13+)**:
  1. Duration of travel status: In the Maliki school, if a traveler resolves to stay in a destination for **4 full days (or 20 fard prayers)** or more upon arrival, they immediately become a resident (*Muqim*) and must pray full rak'ahs.
  2. The Maliki ruling on wiping over socks: Unlike the 24-hour resident / 72-hour traveler limits of the three other schools, Imam Malik held that there is **no fixed time limit** for wiping over leather socks, provided they were put on in a state of pure wudu and have not been removed (though weekly washing for Jumu'ah is recommended).
* **🌍 Other Scholars Say (Reasons Box)**:
  > *Topic: Combining Prayers while Traveling (Jam')*
  >
  > | School | Shortening (Qasr) | Combining (Jam') |
  > |---|---|---|
  > | **Maliki** | Sunnah Mu'akkadah | Permitted when actively journeying on the road. |
  > | **Hanafi** | Strictly **Obligatory** (Wajib) | **Not permitted** at all during travel (except at 'Arafah and Muzdalifah during Hajj). Prayers must be prayed in their own time! |
  > | **Shafi'i** | Permissible (concession) | Permitted (either early *Taqdim* or delayed *Ta'khir*). |
  > | **Hanbali** | Recommended | Permitted with broad flexibility. |
  > *Why the difference?* Imam Abu Hanifa prioritized the Qur'anic ayah *"Indeed prayer is decreed at specific times"*, while the other three Imams followed explicit hadiths where the Prophet ﷺ combined prayers on the expedition to Tabuk.
* **Qur'an & Hadith Evidence**: Surah An-Nisa (4:101: *"And when you travel throughout the land, there is no blame upon you for shortening the prayer"*); Hadith of Ibn 'Umar on the Prophet's travel prayer.
* **Pedagogical Activities**: "The Travel Itinerary Planner": Plotting a family trip from London to Manchester (or New York to Philadelphia), marking exactly where Qasr begins and ends on a map.
* **Hifz & Memorisation**: Surah Al-Haqqah (Verses 1–25). Nawawi Hadith 25 & 26.

### Unit 4.6: Defending the Sanctuary: Badr, Uhud, & The Trench (Weeks 21–24)
* **Strand**: Seerah & Islamic History
* **Session Allocation**: 12 extended sessions × 70 min = 14 hours
* **Classical Reference**: *Al-Sirah al-Nabawiyyah* (Ibn Hisham) / *Zad al-Ma'ad*.
* **Core Outcomes (Age 9–10)**:
  1. The student explains why Muslims fought: Permission was granted only to defend against brutal expulsion and religious persecution (Surah Al-Hajj 22:39).
  2. The student analyzes the three decisive defensive campaigns:
     * **Battle of Badr (2 AH)**: 313 ill-equipped Muslims vs 1,000 Qurayshi warriors; night of crying in du'a by the Prophet ﷺ; miraculous victory; noble treatment of captives (teaching Muslim children to read in exchange for freedom).
     * **Battle of Uhud (3 AH)**: Early victory reversed when the 50 archers abandoned Mount 'Aynayn for war spoils; martyrdom of Sayyiduna Hamzah; the Prophet ﷺ injured yet forgiving; critical lesson on obeying prophetic orders.
     * **Battle of the Trench / Al-Ahzab (5 AH)**: 10,000 confederate troops siege Madinah; Salman al-Farsi's Persian military innovation of digging a wide trench; psychological terror and starvation; victory through divine wind and cold.
  3. The student articulates the strict Islamic rules of engagement: Prohibiting killing women, children, monks, non-combatants, cutting trees, or destroying crops.
* **Extension Outcomes (13+)**:
  1. Just War Theory in classical Shari'ah: Differentiating defensive warfare (*Daf' al-Sa'il*) from imperial aggression, terrorism, or forced religious conversion (*La ikraha fid-din*).
  2. Theological reflections on Uhud: Why Allah allows believers to experience defeat (testing faith, purifying ranks from hypocrites, honoring martyrs).
* **Qur'an & Hadith Evidence**: Surah Al-Hajj (22:39: *"Permission has been given to those who are being fought, because they were wronged"*); Surah Ali 'Imran (Uhud commentary, 3:152–159); Surah Al-Ahzab (33:9–25).
* **Pedagogical Activities**: Sand-table terrain modeling of Mount Uhud and Mount 'Aynayn, demonstrating the strategic military angles and tactical consequences of the archers' withdrawal.
* **Hifz & Memorisation**: Surah Al-Haqqah (completion) and Surah Al-Ma'arij. Nawawi Hadith 27 & 28 (*"Adhere to my Sunnah and the sunnah of the rightly guided caliphs"*).

## Term 3: Shama'il, The Sacred Journey, & Legal Foundations (Weeks 25–36)

### Unit 4.7: Shama'il: The Sublime Person & Character of the Prophet ﷺ (Weeks 25–28)
* **Strand**: Seerah, Shama'il & Akhlaq
* **Session Allocation**: 12 extended sessions × 70 min = 14 hours
* **Classical Reference**: *Al-Shama'il al-Muhammadiyyah* (Imam at-Tirmidhi) / *Al-Shifa bi-Ta'rif Huquq al-Mustafa* (Qadi 'Iyad).
* **Core Outcomes (Age 9–10)**:
  1. The student describes the physical appearance of the Prophet ﷺ from classical narrations (Sayyidina 'Ali, Hind ibn Abi Halah, and Umm Ma'bad):
     * Neither tall nor short, balanced in stature; luminous face radiant like the full moon.
     * Wavy black hair resting above the shoulders; wide brown eyes with natural kohl; slightly curved nose.
     * Broad shoulders, large palms, walked with purpose leaning forward as if walking down an incline.
  2. The student describes his daily habits: Smiling constantly, never laughing boisterously, speaking concise words of profound wisdom (*Jawami' al-Kalim*), mending his own sandals, milking his goat, and never seeking vengeance for personal insults.
  3. The student explains our duties toward the Prophet ﷺ: Loving him more than oneself, sending frequent Salawat upon him, and protecting his honor.
* **Extension Outcomes (13+)**:
  1. Qadi 'Iyad's *Al-Shifa*: The legal obligation of loving the Prophet ﷺ (*Fard 'Ayn*) and the ruling on honoring his relics (*Tabarruk* with hair, sweat, cloak, and touch verified in Sahih hadith).
  2. Prophetic perfection: Infallibility from moral defect, spiritual illness, or vulgarity, and the cosmic gift of the Intercession (*Al-Shafa'ah al-'Uzma*).
* **Qur'an & Hadith Evidence**: Surah Al-Qalam (68:4: *"And indeed, you are of a great moral character"*); Surah Al-Ahzab (33:56: *"Indeed, Allah and His angels confer blessings upon the Prophet"*); Hadith of Anas: *"I served the Prophet ﷺ for ten years, and he never once said 'Uff' to me."* (Bukhari).
* **Pedagogical Activities**: Assembling an illustrated "Book of Prophetic Beauty" containing calligraphy of descriptions from *Al-Shama'il*; choral singing of traditional praise poetry (e.g. *Qasidat al-Burdah* selections).
* **Hifz & Memorisation**: The opening 10 lines of Imam al-Busiri's *Burdah*. Surah Nuh.

### Unit 4.8: The Fiqh of Hajj & 'Umrah (The Journey of a Lifetime) (Weeks 29–32)
* **Strand**: Fiqh (Maliki)
* **Session Allocation**: 12 extended sessions × 70 min = 14 hours
* **Classical Reference**: *Ibn 'Ashir* (Kitab al-Hajj) / *Al-Risalah* of Ibn Abi Zayd.
* **Core Outcomes (Age 9–10)**:
  1. The student explains the Fifth Pillar: Hajj is obligatory once in a lifetime for every Muslim who has the financial and physical capacity (**Al-Istita'ah**).
  2. The student identifies the **4 Arkan (Pillars)** of Hajj in the Maliki Madhhab:
     1. **Al-Ihram** (The sacred state entered at the Miqat with the intention and Talbiyah).
     2. **Al-Tawaf** (Tawaf al-Ifadah / Ziyarah around the Ka'bah 7 circuits).
     3. **Al-Sa'i** (Walking between Safa and Marwah 7 times).
     4. **Wuquf bi-'Arafah** (Standing at 'Arafat on the 9th of Dhul-Hijjah).
  3. The student distinguishes between the 3 modes of Hajj: **Ifrad** (Hajj alone, the most preferred in the Maliki school), **Qiran** (combining Hajj and 'Umrah together), and **Tamattu'** ('Umrah followed by Hajj).
* **Extension Outcomes (13+)**:
  1. Prohibitions of Ihram: Perfume, cutting hair/nails, covering head for men, stitching clothes to fit limbs for men, marital intimacy, and hunting land animals.
  2. The spiritual symbolism of Hajj: Shedding worldly status, universal equality of all races, rehearsing the Day of Resurrection at the plain of 'Arafat.
* **Maliki Fiqh Flag**:
  * **Preference for Ifrad**: Imam Malik held that *Ifrad* (performing Hajj on its own, and 'Umrah in another journey) is superior to Tamattu' and Qiran, because it preserves the singular sanctity of each pilgrimage.
* **Qur'an & Hadith Evidence**: Surah Ali 'Imran (3:97: *"And [due] to Allah from the people is a pilgrimage to the House - for whoever is able to find thereto a way"*); The Talbiyah: *"Labbayk Allahumma Labbayk, Labbayka la sharika laka Labbayk..."*.
* **Pedagogical Activities**: "The Hajj Map Simulation": Marking out stations on the classroom floor (Makkah, Mina, 'Arafat, Muzdalifah, Jamarat); students dressed in white cloth walking the rites step-by-step chanting the Talbiyah.
* **Hifz & Memorisation**: The complete Talbiyah with perfect pronunciation. Surah Al-Jinn (first 15 verses).

### Unit 4.9: The Rightly Guided Caliphs: Leadership & Legacy (Weeks 33–36)
* **Strand**: Islamic History & Government
* **Session Allocation**: 12 extended sessions × 70 min = 14 hours
* **Classical Reference**: *Tarikh al-Khulafa* (Imam al-Suyuti) / *Al-Bidayah wan-Nihayah* (Ibn Kathir).
* **Core Outcomes (Age 9–10)**:
  1. The student details the lives and distinctive contributions of the Four Rightly Guided Caliphs (*Al-Khulafa' al-Rashidun*):
     * **Abu Bakr As-Siddiq (RA)** (11–13 AH): Preserving unity during the Riddah crisis; commissioning the first compilation of the Qur'an.
     * **'Umar ibn Al-Khattab (RA)** (13–23 AH): Unsurpassed justice (*Al-Faruq*); liberation of Jerusalem; establishing the Hijri calendar, public treasury, and welfare state.
     * **'Uthman ibn 'Affan (RA)** (23–35 AH): Generosity; funding the Tabuk army; standardization of the Uthmanic Mushaf sent to the major provincial capitals.
     * **'Ali ibn Abi Talib (RA)** (35–40 AH): Courage, profound legal wisdom; resolving complex civil disputes; the gate of the city of prophetic knowledge.
  2. The student articulates the Sunni principle of revering all Companions (*Fadl al-Sahabah*) and avoiding sectarian hatred.
* **Extension Outcomes (13+)**:
  1. Political philosophy of the Caliphate: Governance by mutual consultation (*Shura*), accountability of leaders before the law, and protection of religious minorities (*Ahl al-Dhimmah*).
  2. Historiographical analysis of the First Fitnah: Navigating historical differences among the Sahabah with *Husn al-Zann* (positive assumption) and academic integrity according to the Ash'ari creed.
* **Qur'an & Hadith Evidence**: Surah Al-Fath (48:29: *"Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, and those with him are forceful against the disbelievers, merciful among themselves"*); Hadith: *"Hold fast to my Sunnah and the sunnah of the Rightly Guided Caliphs after me."* (Abu Dawud).
* **Pedagogical Activities**: Creating a biographical matrix comparing the leadership qualities of the Four Caliphs; historical document analysis: Reading the Covenant of 'Umar granted to the people of Jerusalem.
* **Hifz & Memorisation**: Surah Al-Jinn (completion) and Surah Al-Muzzammil. Nawawi Hadith 29 & 30.

## Year 4 Assessment & Review Framework
* **Bi-Weekly Essay / Legal Case Briefs**: A 2-page written case analysis answering a complex Fiqh dilemma (e.g. Zakah calculation or travel prayer).
* **Term Practicums**:
  * *Term 1*: Oral recitation of the 20 Necessary Attributes of Allah with definitions and mental categories.
  * *Term 2*: Complete mock exam on Fasting Kaffarah, Travel distance calculation, and Masah rulings.
  * *Term 3*: Hajj Map oral walk-through explaining the 4 Arkan and the timeline from 8th to 12th Dhul-Hijjah.
* **Annual Milestone**: Awarding the **"Intermediate Scholar (Year 4) Certificate"**, signifying mastery of advanced Ash'ari theology, systematic Maliki worship jurisprudence, and the historical epoch of the Caliphate.
