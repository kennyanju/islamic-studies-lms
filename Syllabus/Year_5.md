# Year 5 Syllabus: Analysis, Contemporary Issues, & Synthesis

> **Target Learner**: Level 5 Track: Senior High & Capstone (Ages 14-16+)  
> **Weekly Schedule**: **3 seminar sessions × 90 minutes** = 270 min/week  
> **Annual Instructional Volume**: 36 Weeks (3 Terms of 12 Weeks) = 162 hours  
> **ICO Benchmarks Absorbed**: Grade 10, Grade 11, and Grade 12 (epistemology, commercial law, modern ideologies, usul al-fiqh, bioethics, inheritance)  
> **Classical Text Spine**: *Jawharat al-Tawhid* (Imam al-Laqani), *Bidayat al-Mujtahid* (Ibn Rushd), *Al-Waraqat* (Imam al-Juwayni), and *Ibn 'Ashir* (Tazkiyah section)  
> **Riwayah**: Hafs 'an 'Asim & Warsh 'an Nafi' (Juz' 29 completion: Surahs Al-Muddaththir, Al-Qiyamah, Al-Insan, Al-Mursalat; analytical study of Surah Al-Kahf and Surah Yasin)

## Term 1: Epistemology, Prophethood, & The Quranic Sciences (Weeks 1–12)

### Unit 5.1: Epistemology: How We Know Truth & The Philosophy of Qadar (Weeks 1–4)
* **Strand**: 'Aqidah (Ash'ari)
* **Session Allocation**: 12 seminar sessions × 90 min = 18 hours
* **Classical Reference**: *Jawharat al-Tawhid* (Lines 20–45) / *Al-Iqtisad fil-I'tiqad* (Imam al-Ghazali).
* **Core Outcomes (Age 10–11)**:
  1. The student outlines the 3 legitimate sources of knowledge in Islamic epistemology:
     * **Al-Hiss al-Salim (Sound Senses)**: Observation, empirical science, experimentation.
     * **Al-'Aql al-Sarih (Uncorrupted Intellect)**: Logical reasoning, necessary truths (e.g. the principle of non-contradiction, causality).
     * **Al-Khabar al-Sadiq (Veracious Transmitted Report)**: Divine Revelation (*Wahy*) and mass-transmitted prophetic reports (*Tawatur*).
  2. The student dismantles the modern dogma of **Scientism** (the false belief that only physical science produces truth) by demonstrating that empirical science itself rests on non-empirical philosophical premises.
  3. The student articulates the Ash'ari doctrine of **Kasb (Acquisition)** in Divine Decree (*Al-Qadar*): Allah is the sole Creator of all actions, while human beings freely choose and acquire moral responsibility for their deeds, steering between the fatalism of the *Jabriyyah* and the deification of human power in the *Mu'tazilah*.
* **Extension Outcomes (13+)**:
  1. The 4 Stages of Qadar: *Al-'Ilm* (Eternal Knowledge), *Al-Kitabah* (Preserved Tablet / *Al-Lawh al-Mahfuz*), *Al-Mashi'ah* (All-Encompassing Will), and *Al-Khalq* (Direct Creation of Action).
  2. Ash'ari critique of natural necessity (*Iqtiran* vs *Ta'thir*): Causality as a divine customary habit (*'Adah*) rather than inherent mechanical power in physical objects (fire does not burn by itself; Allah creates burning at the moment of contact).
* **Ash'ari Creed Flag**: The doctrine of *Kasb*: *"Kullu shay'in bi-qada'illahi wa qadarih"*: Everything occurs by divine decree, yet human intention (*Qasd*) ensures full ethical and legal accountability.
* **🌍 Other Scholars Say (Analysis Box)**:
  > *Topic: Human Will, Action, and Divine Decree*
  > **All Sunni schools agree**: Allah knows everything that was and will be; nothing happens in the universe without His Will; human beings are genuinely responsible for their moral choices and will be held accountable.
  >
  > | School | Explanation of Human Action | Historical Context |
  > |---|---|---|
  > | **Ash'ari (our school)** | **Kasb (Acquisition)**: Allah creates the power and the act; the human directs their will toward it. | Refuted both the fatalist Jabriyyah and the rationalist Mu'tazilah. |
  > | **Maturidi** | Affirms Kasb, adding that human choice has an inclination (*Ikhtiyar*) created by human agency. | Very close nuance to the Ash'ari school. |
  > | **Athari** | Affirms that humans truly act with will given by Allah, without engaging in scholastic terminology. | Emphasized literal scriptural affirmations. |
  > *Heretical extremes*: The *Jabriyyah* said humans are feathers in the wind (erasing morals); the *Mu'tazilah* said humans create their own acts independently of Allah (limiting Allah's sovereignty). Ahl al-Sunnah stood firmly in the radiant middle!
* **Qur'an & Hadith Evidence**: Surah As-Saffat (37:96: *"While Allah created you and that which you do"*); Surah Al-Kahf (18:29: *"The truth is from your Lord, so whoever wills - let him believe; and whoever wills - let him disbelieve"*).
* **Pedagogical Activities**: Epistemology mock trial: Defending how we know historical figures existed (e.g. Julius Caesar, George Washington) through *Tawatur* (mass transmission) without having physically seen them, proving the validity of revelation.
* **Hifz & Memorisation**: *Jawharat al-Tawhid* Lines 20–25. Surah Al-Qiyamah.

### Unit 5.2: 'Ulum al-Qur'an: Preservation, Compilation, & Inimitability (Weeks 5–8)
* **Strand**: Qur'an & 'Ulum al-Qur'an
* **Session Allocation**: 12 seminar sessions × 90 min = 18 hours
* **Classical Reference**: *Al-Itqan fi 'Ulum al-Qur'an* (Imam al-Suyuti) / *Al-Burhan* (Imam al-Zarkashi).
* **Core Outcomes (Age 10–11)**:
  1. The student narrates the three historical stages of the compilation of the Noble Qur'an:
     * **Prophetic Era**: Oral memorisation by thousands of Sahabah; written fragments on parchment, leather, and palm stalks checked under the direct supervision of the Prophet ﷺ.
     * **Caliphate of Abu Bakr (RA)**: Commissioning Zayd ibn Thabit to gather the fragments into one single master volume (*Suhuf*) following the Battle of Yamamah.
     * **Caliphate of 'Uthman (RA)**: Standardizing the official orthography (*Rasm al-Uthmani*), producing master codices, and sending them to Kufa, Basra, Damascus, and Makkah with master reciters.
  2. The student differentiates between Makki and Madani revelations:
     * **Makki**: Focuses on Tawheed, dismantling shirk, the afterlife, moral fortitude, poetic grandeur (prior to Hijrah).
     * **Madani**: Focuses on community legislation, commercial law, family structures, statecraft, treaties (after Hijrah).
  3. The student defines the **I'jaz (Inimitability)** of the Qur'an: Linguistic, historical prophecies, legal perfection, and psychological impact.
* **Extension Outcomes (13+)**:
  1. The science of **Qira'at**: The Seven / Ten mutawatir readings (including Hafs and Warsh); understanding that variations reflect divine facilitation for tribal dialects without altering theological meaning.
  2. Understanding **Asbab al-Nuzul** (occasions of revelation) and the principle: *"Al-'Ibratu bi-'umum al-lafzi la bi-khususi al-sabab"* (Legal consideration is granted to the generality of the wording, not merely the specific incident of revelation).
* **Qur'an & Hadith Evidence**: Surah Al-Baqarah (2:23: The Challenge of the Qur'an: *"Produce a surah like thereof"*); Surah Al-Hijr (15:9).
* **Pedagogical Activities**: Examining high-resolution photographs of the Topkapi and Birmingham Qur'an parchment manuscripts; identifying Makki vs Madani stylistic traits in sample chapters.
* **Hifz & Memorisation**: Surah Al-Insan (complete). Nawawi Hadith 31 (*"Renounce the world and Allah will love you; renounce what people possess and people will love you"*).

### Unit 5.3: Usul al-Fiqh: The Architecture of Islamic Jurisprudence (Weeks 9–12)
* **Strand**: Usul al-Fiqh (Maliki Methodology)
* **Session Allocation**: 12 seminar sessions × 90 min = 18 hours
* **Classical Reference**: *Al-Waraqat* (Imam al-Haramayn al-Juwayni) / *Tartib al-Madarik* (Qadi 'Iyad).
* **Core Outcomes (Age 10–11)**:
  1. The student defines **Usul al-Fiqh**: The principles and methodology by which jurists extract detailed legal rulings from primary scriptural sources.
  2. The student categorizes human actions into the **5 Legal Rulings (Al-Ahkam al-Taklifiyyah)**:
     * **Wajib / Fard** (Obligatory): Rewarded for doing, punished for leaving (e.g. 5 daily prayers).
     * **Mandub / Mustahabb** (Recommended): Rewarded for doing, not punished for leaving (e.g. siwak, voluntary charity).
     * **Mubah** (Permissible): Neither rewarded nor punished inherently (e.g. eating an apple, walking).
     * **Makruh** (Disliked): Rewarded for leaving, not punished for doing (e.g. wasting water in wudu).
     * **Haram** (Prohibited): Rewarded for leaving, punished for doing (e.g. lying, consuming riba, theft).
  3. The student details the unique hierarchy of sources in the **Maliki Madhhab**:
     1. The Noble Qur'an (Textual proof and universal principles).
     2. The Prophetic Sunnah (Mutawatir and Mashhur).
     3. **'Amal Ahl al-Madinah** (The living practice of the community of Madinah).
     4. **Ijma'** (Consensus of Muslim jurists).
     5. **Qiyas** (Analogical deduction via shared effective cause / *'Illah*).
     6. **Al-Maslahah al-Mursalah** (Unspecified public interest).
     7. **Sadd al-Dhara'i'** (Blocking the means to harm).
     8. **'Urf** (Valid local customary practice that does not contradict scripture).
* **Extension Outcomes (13+)**:
  1. The authority of *'Amal Ahl al-Madinah*: Why Imam Malik gave precedence to the continuous generational transmission of thousands of Madinan scholars over isolated solitary hadiths (*Khabar al-Ahad*).
  2. The **Maqasid al-Shari'ah** (Higher Objectives of Divine Law): Preserving the 5 Universal Necessities (*Al-Daruriyyat al-Khams*): Faith (*Din*), Life (*Nafs*), Intellect (*'Aql*), Lineage/Honor (*Nasl*), and Wealth (*Mal*).
* **Maliki Fiqh Flag**: *'Amal Ahl al-Madinah* serves as an unmatched oral and practical mutawatir transmission of the prophetic way of life, demonstrating that Islam is a lived tradition, not merely dead ink on paper.
* **Qur'an & Hadith Evidence**: Surah An-Nisa (4:59: *"Obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you"*); Imam Malik's letter to Al-Layth ibn Sa'd defending the consensus of Madinah.
* **Pedagogical Activities**: "The Shari'ah Court Case": The teacher presents a new technological development (e.g. self-driving vehicles or cryptocurrency), and students apply the 5 Maqasid to evaluate its potential harms and benefits.
* **Hifz & Memorisation**: *Al-Waraqat* (Opening definitions of Fiqh, Usul, and the 5 Ahkam). Surah Al-Mursalat.

## Term 2: Mu'amalat: Commercial Law, Ethics, & Family (Weeks 13–24)

### Unit 5.4: Foundations of Mu'amalat: Contracts & The Ban on Riba, Gharar, and Maysir (Weeks 13–16)
* **Strand**: Fiqh al-Mu'amalat (Maliki)
* **Session Allocation**: 12 seminar sessions × 90 min = 18 hours
* **Classical Reference**: *Bidayat al-Mujtahid* (Kitab al-Buyu') / *Al-Risalah* of Ibn Abi Zayd.
* **Core Outcomes (Age 10–11)**:
  1. The student explains the foundational legal maxim of Islamic business: **"Al-Aslu fil-mu'amalati al-ibahah"** (The default ruling in commercial transactions is permissibility, unless a clear prohibition exists).
  2. The student analyzes the three absolute prohibitions that invalidate any business contract:
     * **Riba (Usury / Interest)**: Unjustified gain without counter-value. Differentiating between *Riba al-Duyun* (interest charged on loans/debt) and *Riba al-Buyu'* (unequal exchange of commodities like gold for gold).
     * **Gharar (Excessive Ambiguity / Deception)**: Selling things whose delivery or nature is unknown (e.g. selling fish still swimming wild in the sea, selling an unopened mystery box).
     * **Maysir / Qimar (Gambling & Speculation)**: Zero-sum arrangements where one party gains purely at the financial ruin of another through chance (e.g. lotteries, roulette, derivative financial betting).
  3. The student articulates the economic harms of interest-based banking: Wealth concentration in few hands, inflation, predatory exploitation of the poor.
* **Extension Outcomes (13+)**:
  1. Detailed analysis of the 6 Ribawi commodities (Gold, Silver, Wheat, Barley, Dates, Salt): The Maliki effective cause (*'Illah*) for food commodities (being staple and storable: *Al-Iqqiyāt wal-Iddikhar*) contrasted with the Hanafi and Shafi'i views.
  2. Options in contracts (**Al-Khiyar**): The Maliki rejection of *Khiyar al-Majlis* (option of the physical session) in favor of the binding nature of clear verbal agreement, upholding *'Amal Ahl al-Madinah*.
* **🌍 Other Scholars Say (Analysis Box)**:
  > *Topic: The Option of Canceling a Sale at the Counter (Khiyar al-Majlis)*
  > **All agree**: Both buyer and seller must deal with complete honesty, disclosure of defects, and mutual consent (*'An Taradin*).
  >
  > | School | Position on Khiyar al-Majlis | Reasoning & Source |
  > |---|---|---|
  > | **Maliki** | **Not recognized**. Once the offer and acceptance are voiced, the contract is completely binding. | Followed the established practice of Madinah (*'Amal*), interpreting the hadith *"until they part"* as parting in speech. |
  > | **Shafi'i & Hanbali** | **Fully recognized**. Either party can cancel the sale as long as they are still standing in the same room. | Prioritized the literal wording of the hadith: *"The two parties have the option as long as they have not separated."* |
  > | **Hanafi** | Does not recognize physical separation as a requirement; binding upon acceptance. | Shared the Maliki outcome based on Quranic textual absoluteness. |
  > *Reflective thought*: Ibn Rushd in *Bidayat al-Mujtahid* points out that both camps used authentic texts; their difference was purely a methodological distinction between Madinan continuous practice and isolated textual phrasing!
* **Qur'an & Hadith Evidence**: Surah Al-Baqarah (2:275: *"Allah has permitted trade and has forbidden interest"*); Surah An-Nisa (4:29: *"Do not consume one another's wealth unjustly but only in lawful business by mutual consent"*).
* **Pedagogical Activities**: Business Ethics Boardroom: Students analyze simulated business contracts (e.g. subscription models, delayed payment plans, gaming loot-boxes) to detect hidden Gharar or Riba.
* **Hifz & Memorisation**: Nawawi Hadith 32 (*"There shall be no harm, nor reciprocal harm"*, *La darara wa la dirar*). Surah Yasin (Verses 1–32).

### Unit 5.5: Contemporary Islamic Finance & Halal Livelihoods (Weeks 17–20)
* **Strand**: Applied Fiqh & Mu'amalat
* **Session Allocation**: 12 seminar sessions × 90 min = 18 hours
* **Classical Reference**: *Bidayat al-Mujtahid* (Partnerships & Leasing) / AAOIFI Islamic Shari'ah Standards.
* **Core Outcomes (Age 10–11)**:
  1. The student explains how Islamic finance operates without interest: Replacing debt-exploitation with asset-backed trade, risk-sharing, and equity partnerships.
  2. The student defines the major Islamic financial contracts used today:
     * **Murabaha (Cost-Plus Sale)**: The financier purchases an asset (e.g. a house) and sells it to the client at cost plus a transparent, agreed-upon profit margin paid in instalments.
     * **Mudarabah (Trustee Financing)**: One partner provides capital (*Rabb al-Mal*) and the other provides expertise and labor (*Mudarib*); profits are shared by agreed ratio, while financial loss is borne by capital provider alone unless caused by negligence.
     * **Musharakah (Joint Venture)**: Both partners contribute capital and management, sharing profits and losses proportionally.
     * **Ijarah (Leasing)**: Transferring the usufruct (use) of a tangible property for an agreed rental fee over a specified time.
  3. The student discusses ethical professions: Seeking halal income (*Kasb al-Halal*), fair treatment of employees, paying workers before their sweat dries, and avoiding bribery (*Rishwah*).
* **Extension Outcomes (13+)**:
  1. Legal mechanisms of Islamic Home Financing: Diminishing Musharakah (*Musharakah Mutanaqisah*) combined with Ijarah.
  2. The concept of **Takaful** (Islamic Mutual Insurance): Cooperatively pooling funds on the basis of *Tabarru'* (donation) to protect against catastrophe without the Gharar and interest of conventional insurance.
* **Qur'an & Hadith Evidence**: Surah Al-Ma'idah (5:1: *"O you who believe, fulfill all contracts"*); Hadith: *"The truthful and trustworthy merchant will be with the prophets, the truthful, and the martyrs."* (Tirmidhi).
* **Pedagogical Activities**: "The Islamic Entrepreneur Pitch": Students work in teams to design an ethical, halal business venture (e.g. ethical organic farming, educational software, or zero-interest micro-lending) and pitch it to the class.
* **Hifz & Memorisation**: Surah Yasin (Verses 33–58). Nawawi Hadith 33 & 34 (*"Whoever of you sees an evil, let him change it with his hand..."*).

### Unit 5.6: The Sanctuary of Family: Marriage, Rights, & Inheritance (Weeks 21–24)
* **Strand**: Family Fiqh & Society
* **Session Allocation**: 12 seminar sessions × 90 min = 18 hours
* **Classical Reference**: *Al-Risalah* of Ibn Abi Zayd (Kitab al-Nikah and Bab al-Fara'id) / *Ibn 'Ashir*.
* **Core Outcomes (Age 10–11)**:
  1. The student articulates the divine purpose of marriage (**Nikah**) in Islam: Creating mutual love, spiritual tranquility (*Sakinah*), affection, and mercy (*Mawaddah wa Rahmah*), rather than mere legal transaction.
  2. The student outlines the essential pillars of a valid Islamic marriage:
     * Mutual consent and acceptance (*Ijab wa Qabul*).
     * The bridal gift (**Mahr**) owned exclusively by the bride.
     * The presence of the bride's guardian (**Wali**) ensuring her protection and rights.
     * Public proclamation (**I'lan**) and two upright witnesses.
  3. The student identifies the mutual rights and duties of spouses: Kind companionship, financial maintenance (*Nafaqah*), emotional care, and shared responsibility in raising righteous children.
  4. The student learns the introductory wisdom of Islamic Inheritance (**Mirath**): Allah Himself distributed wealth across generations to prevent dynastic accumulation, honor parents, protect widows, and care for orphans.
* **Extension Outcomes (13+)**:
  1. Conflict resolution and dissolution of marriage: The Islamic process of arbitration (Surah An-Nisa 4:35), the prescribed waiting period (*'Iddah*), and the differences between **Talaq** (initiated by husband), **Khul'** (initiated by wife returning mahr), and **Faskh** (judicial annulment by a Qadi due to abuse, harm, or non-maintenance).
  2. Basic Mirath math: Understanding the primary Qur'anic shares (*Al-Furud al-Muqaddarah*): 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 2/3, 1/3, and 1/6, and the primary non-excludable heirs (Father, Mother, Husband/Wife, Son, Daughter).
* **Maliki Fiqh Flag**:
  * **Public Announcement of Marriage**: Imam Malik famously held that public proclamation (*I'lan*) and celebration of a marriage is so critical that a secret wedding performed with two secret witnesses is legally invalid and must be annulled.
* **Qur'an & Hadith Evidence**: Surah Ar-Rum (30:21: *"And of His signs is that He created for you from yourselves mates that you may find tranquility in them; and He placed between you affection and mercy"*); Hadith: *"The best of you are those who are best to their families, and I am the best among you to my family."* (Tirmidhi).
* **Pedagogical Activities**: "The Family Budget & Inheritance Tree": Mapping out the generational care tree of a household; calculating simple Islamic inheritance allocations for a model family.
* **Hifz & Memorisation**: Surah Yasin (completion). Nawawi Hadith 35 (*"Do not envy one another, do not inflate prices, do not hate one another... be servants of Allah as brothers"*).

## Term 3: Contemporary Thought, Tazkiyah, & Living the Faith (Weeks 25–36)

### Unit 5.7: Deconstructing Materialism, Scientism, & Modern Atheism (Weeks 25–28)
* **Strand**: Contemporary 'Aqidah & Apologetics
* **Session Allocation**: 12 seminar sessions × 90 min = 18 hours
* **Classical Reference**: *Tahafut al-Falasifah* (Imam al-Ghazali) / *Kitab al-Tamhid* (Qadi Abu Bakr al-Baqillani).
* **Core Outcomes (Age 10–11)**:
  1. The student deconstructs modern philosophical materialism: The false claim that only physical matter exists, demonstrating that thoughts, mathematics, moral laws, consciousness, and love are non-physical realities.
  2. The student applies the classical cosmological argument (**Dalil al-Huduth**):
     * Premise 1: The universe had a beginning (finite time, expanding cosmos, Big Bang).
     * Premise 2: Whatever begins to exist must have a cause (things cannot produce themselves out of nothingness).
     * Conclusion: Therefore, the universe has a transcendent, uncaused, eternal Creator of supreme Will and Power.
  3. The student addresses the "Problem of Evil and Suffering": Evil and pain are not signs of Allah's absence, but are necessary dimensions of an earthly testing ground (*Dar al-Imtihan*), serving as catalysts for human spiritual growth, justice in the Hereafter, and appreciation of divine mercy.
* **Extension Outcomes (13+)**:
  1. Critiquing philosophical Naturalism and Darwinian evolution: Distinguishing between biological adaptation within species vs metaphysical claims of purposeless, unguided macro-evolution excluding divine teleology.
  2. The moral argument for God: Showing that objective moral values (e.g. torturing innocent children is evil everywhere and always) cannot exist in a purely godless, accidental universe.
* **Qur'an & Hadith Evidence**: Surah At-Tur (52:35–36: *"Were they created by nothing, or were they themselves the creators? Or did they create the heavens and the earth? Nay, they have no firm belief"*); Surah Al-Mulk (67:2: *"He who created death and life to test you as to which of you is best in deed"*).
* **Pedagogical Activities**: "Socratic Seminar": Students role-play a respectful public dialogue between a Muslim youth and a modern skeptic asking: "Why does God allow suffering in the world?"
* **Hifz & Memorisation**: Surah Al-Kahf (Verses 1–20). Nawawi Hadith 36 (*"Whoever relieves a believer of a distress from the distresses of this world, Allah will relieve him of a distress from the distresses of the Day of Resurrection"*).

### Unit 5.8: The Intellectual Radiance of Islamic Civilization (Weeks 29–32)
* **Strand**: Islamic History & Civilisation
* **Session Allocation**: 12 seminar sessions × 90 min = 18 hours
* **Classical Reference**: *Muqaddimah* of Ibn Khaldun / *Al-I'tibar* (Usamah ibn Munqidh).
* **Core Outcomes (Age 10–11)**:
  1. The student tracks the flourishing of Islamic thought across historical golden eras:
     * **Umayyads**: Architectural wonders (Dome of the Rock, Umayyad Mosque of Damascus), Arabization of public registers.
     * **Abbasids & Baghdad**: Bayt al-Hikmah (House of Wisdom), paper manufacturing, universal translation of world knowledge.
     * **Al-Andalus (Islamic Spain)**: Cordoba's paved illuminated streets, 70 libraries, coexistence (*Convivencia*), gardens, and universities where Christian and Jewish scholars studied Arabic.
     * **North & West Africa**: The great universities of Al-Qarawiyyin in Fez (founded by Fatima al-Fihri - world's oldest degree-granting university), Al-Azhar in Cairo, and the Sankore University in Timbuktu (Mali).
     * **Ottoman Empire**: Institutional architecture (Sinan), preservation of Islamic borders, Waqf endowment networks.
  2. The student identifies major polymaths who synthesized faith and empirical science:
     * **Al-Khwarizmi**: Pioneer of Algebra (*Al-Jabr*) and algorithms.
     * **Ibn Sina (Avicenna)**: Author of *The Canon of Medicine* and pioneer of clinical medicine.
     * **Ibn al-Haytham (Alhazen)**: Pioneer of modern optics and empirical testing.
     * **Ibn Khaldun** (Maliki judge of Tunis/Cairo): Founder of sociology and historical analysis.
* **Extension Outcomes (13+)**:
  1. The Islamic concept of **Waqf** (Perpetual Charitable Endowments): How free hospitals, universities, soup kitchens, and animal sanctuaries were funded autonomously without government interference.
  2. The legacy of Muslim scholarship in preserving and transmitting classical learning to spark the European Renaissance.
* **Qur'an & Hadith Evidence**: Surah Al-Mujadila (58:11: *"Allah will raise those who have believed among you and those who were given knowledge, by degrees"*); Hadith: *"Seeking knowledge is an obligation upon every Muslim."* (Ibn Majah).
* **Pedagogical Activities**: "The Muslim Inventors Exhibition": Each student curates a museum display detailing the invention of optics, surgical tools (Al-Zahrawi), astrolabes, or water clocks.
* **Hifz & Memorisation**: Surah Al-Kahf (Verses 21–45). Nawawi Hadith 37 & 38 (*"Whosoever shows enmity to a wali of Mine, I have declared war upon him..."*).

### Unit 5.9: Tazkiyah: Purifying the Heart & Confident Living (Weeks 33–36)
* **Strand**: Akhlaq, Tazkiyah & Contemporary Living
* **Session Allocation**: 12 seminar sessions × 90 min = 18 hours
* **Classical Reference**: *Ibn 'Ashir* (The final section on Sufism/Tazkiyah) / *Matharat al-Qulub* (Purification of the Heart - Sidi Muhammad Mawlud).
* **Core Outcomes (Age 10–11)**:
  1. The student diagnoses the major spiritual diseases of the heart and their prophetic remedies:
     * **Hasad (Envy)**: Remedied by realizing that feeling bitter over another's blessing is objecting to Allah's distribution of bounty; wishing well for them.
     * **Kibr (Arrogance)**: Remedied by remembering one's humble origin from dust and water; the definition: *"Rejecting the truth and looking down upon people."*
     * **'Ujb (Self-righteous vanity)**: Remedied by knowing that every good deed is purely a gift of divine enabling grace (*Tawfiq*), not one's own cleverness.
     * **Hubb al-Jah (Love of fame/prestige)**: Remedied by anonymous charity and sincerity.
  2. The student outlines the 3 conditions of sincere repentance (**Al-Tawbah al-Nasuha**): Immediate cessation of the sin, deep heartfelt remorse (*Nadam*), and firm determination never to return to it (plus restoring rights to victims if property was stolen).
  3. The student formulates a personal manifesto for living as a confident, educated, practicing Muslim in the contemporary Western world.
* **Extension Outcomes (13+)**:
  1. The methodology of Imam al-Junayd: *"Our path of spiritual purification is bound by the Qur'an and the Sunnah."* Reconciling Law (*Shari'ah*) and Spiritual Reality (*Haqiqah*).
  2. Contemporary civic engagement: The theology of being an ethical witness over humanity (*Shuhada' 'ala an-Nas*) through charitable excellence, neighborly service, and political integrity.
* **Qur'an & Hadith Evidence**: Surah Ash-Shams (91:9–10: *"He has succeeded who purifies it, and he has failed who instills it [with corruption]"*); Hadith: *"Truly, in the body there is a morsel of flesh; if it is sound, the whole body is sound, and if it is corrupted, the whole body is corrupted. Truly, it is the heart."* (Bukhari & Muslim).
* **Pedagogical Activities**: "The Spiritual Physician Clinic": Diagnosing real-life case studies of jealousy, arrogance, and gossip, and prescribing daily adhkar, physical charity, and behavioral exercises to cure them.
* **Hifz & Memorisation**: *Ibn 'Ashir* (Closing lines on Tazkiyah). Surah Al-Kahf (Verses 46–60). Nawawi Hadith 39, 40, 41, and 42 (*"Be in this world as though you were a stranger or a traveler passing on a path"*).

## Year 5 Assessment & Review Framework
* **Capstone Research Thesis & Oral Defense**: A 1,500-word written monograph exploring a contemporary Islamic ethics topic (e.g. "Islamic Finance vs Conventional Debt", "Bioethics of Organ Donation", or "The Ash'ari Rebuttal of Materialism") defended before a teacher panel.
* **Term Practicums**:
  * *Term 1*: Usul al-Fiqh Case Defense: Deriving rulings using the 5 Maqasid and Maliki *'Amal*.
  * *Term 2*: Mu'amalat Contract Analysis: Auditing an investment contract for Riba and Gharar.
  * *Term 3*: Classical Matn Recitation: Delivering 50 lines of *Ibn 'Ashir* and selected lines of *Jawharat al-Tawhid* from memory.
* **Annual Milestone**: Awarding the **"Graduate of Islamic Studies Diploma"**, conferring full foundational Islamic literacy across the 7 Curricular Strands, certified readiness for intermediate madrasah / classical study, and complete K–12 ICO equivalency!
