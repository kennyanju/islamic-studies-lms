## Page 1: Welcome, Young Historian!

Welcome to your study guide for Unit 6! In this unit, we are going to travel back in time to the year 622 CE. We will follow our beloved Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) as he leaves his hometown of Makkah and travels to the city of Madinah. 

In Madinah, the Prophet (pbuh) didn't just build a place to pray; he built a beautiful, peaceful community where people of different tribes and religions could live together safely. 

As we study this journey, we will explore:
1.  **The Hijrah:** The secret escape and lessons in trust and sacrifice.
2.  **The Constitution of Madinah:** The peace treaty that united different groups.
3.  **Badr and Uhud:** Lessons in patience, discipline, and peaceful rules during war.
4.  **The Farewell Sermon:** The Prophet's final speech about equality and kindness.
5.  **The Golden Chain of Knowledge:** How knowledge traveled from the Companions to **Imam Malik** in Madinah.

### Fiqh & Creed Literacy

*   **Law Focus (Fiqh):** Explaining our actions according to the **Maliki** school.

## Page 2: The Command to Leave Makkah (What is Hijrah?)

For thirteen years in Makkah, the Muslims were treated very badly by the leaders of the Quraysh tribe. The Muslims were boycotted, teased, and even hurt just because they believed in One Allah. Yet, they stayed patient. 

Finally, the Quraysh leaders met at a place called *Dar al-Nadwah* (the assembly house) and made a terrible plan: they decided to choose one young man from every family to attack the Prophet (pbuh) at the same time. That way, the Prophet’s family wouldn’t be able to fight back against all the tribes at once.

But Allah is the Best of Planners. He sent the Angel Jibril (Gabriel) to tell the Prophet (pbuh) that it was time to migrate.

### What is Hijrah?
**Hijrah** (*Hijrah* - هِجْرَة) means migration or moving from one place to another for the sake of Allah to protect your faith. 

The Prophet (pbuh) chose a city named **Yathrib** as the new home. Yathrib was located about 280 miles north of Makkah. Later, this city would be named **Al-Madinah al-Munawwarah** (*Al-Madīnah al-Munawwarah* - الْمَدِينَة الْمُنَوَّرَة), which means "The Illuminated City."

To make this journey, the Muslims had to make huge sacrifices. They had to leave behind their houses, their wealth, and sometimes even their family members who did not believe. They did all of this because their faith was the most valuable thing they owned.

## Page 3: Doing Your Best & Trusting Allah (The Story of the Cave)

Before leaving Makkah, the Prophet (pbuh) made a very careful plan. He did not just walk out into the desert. He hired a skilled guide named Abdullah ibn Urayqit to show him secret paths. He and his best friend, Abu Bakr, decided to travel south instead of north to trick the Quraysh search parties. They also hid in a dark cave on Mount Thawr for three days.

This careful planning teaches us a very important lesson about how a Muslim should live.

> ### Creed Focus: Asbab (Causes) & Tawakkul (Trust)
> In the **Ash'ari** creed, we learn two major concepts:
> 1.  **Asbab** (*Asbāb* - أَسْبَاب): The physical means or steps we take (like wearing a seatbelt, studying for a test, or locking a door).
> 2.  **Tawakkul** (*Tawakkul* - تَوَكُّل): Placing our absolute trust and reliance on Allah alone for the final result.
> 
> **The Golden Rule:** We must use our bodies to take the necessary steps (*Asbab*), but our hearts must only rely on Allah. We must never believe that the physical steps *create* the result. Only Allah creates the result. 
> 
> If we ignore the steps and say, *"I trust Allah, so I won't study,"* we are being foolish. But if we study and say, *"My studying is what got me the A,"* we are forgetting that Allah is the Creator of our intelligence and our success.

While hiding in the cave, the Quraysh trackers walked right up to the entrance. Abu Bakr was terrified for the Prophet's safety. He whispered: *"If one of them looks down at his feet, he will see us."*
The Prophet (pbuh) calmly replied: *"O Abu Bakr, what do you think of two, of whom Allah is the third?"* (Quran 9:40). 

Allah sent a spider to spin a web across the cave entrance and a wild dove to lay eggs in a nest there. When the Quraysh saw this, they thought, *"No one could have entered this cave recently,"* and they left. Allah used a tiny spider web to protect His messenger!

## Page 4: Welcome to Madinah!

When the Prophet (pbuh) finally arrived in Quba', on the outskirts of Yathrib, the people were waiting for him with great joy. They sang songs of welcome. 

### 1. The Prophet's Camel and the Orphans' Land
As the Prophet (pbuh) entered the city, every tribe wanted him to live with them. They held the reins of his camel, **Al-Qaswa'** (*Al-Qaswā'* - الْقَصْوَاء). The Prophet (pbuh) said: *"Let her go, for she is commanded."* 
The camel walked and finally knelt down on a flat, empty piece of land. 

*   This land belonged to two young orphan brothers named Sahl and Suhayl.
*   The Prophet (pbuh) insisted on buying the land from them at a fair price, refusing to take it as a gift. 
*   **Fiqh Lesson:** In Islamic law, we must protect the property of orphans (*Aytām*) and never take advantage of them.

### 2. Building Al-Masjid al-Nabawi
On this land, they built the Prophet's Mosque (*Al-Masjid al-Nabawi* - الْمَسْجِد النَّبَوِيّ). It was simple, built from mud-bricks and palm trees. The Prophet (pbuh) worked right alongside the companions, carrying heavy bricks. The mosque was not just for prayer; it was a school, a community hall, and a place where the poor could sleep.

### 3. The Great Brotherhood (Al-Muwakhah)
The Muslims in Madinah were now divided into two groups:
*   **Al-Muhajirun** (*Al-Muhājirūn* - الْمُهَاجِرُونَ): The Emigrants who left Makkah with nothing.
*   **Al-Ansar** (*Al-Anṣār* - الأَنْصَار): The Helpers of Madinah who welcomed them.

The Prophet (pbuh) paired one Muhajir with one Ansari as brothers. The Ansar were so generous that they offered to share half of their homes, fields, and food with their new brothers. The Muhajirun accepted this love but immediately worked hard to support themselves, showing that a Muslim should always be independent and industrious.

## Page 5: The Constitution of Madinah

Now that different groups were living in the same city, the Prophet (pbuh) did something amazing. He wrote a document called the **Constitution of Madinah** (*Wathīqat al-Madīnah* - وَثِيقَة الْمَدِينَة). This was the first written constitution in the history of the world!

It was a treaty signed by the Muslims and the Jewish tribes of Madinah. It set down rules so everyone could live together in peace.

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------+
|                THE CONSTITUTION OF MADINAH                  |
|                        Key Pillars:                         |
+-------------------------------------------------------------+
|  1. ONE COMMUNITY: Muslims and Jews are part of one city,   |
|     promising to defend it together if attacked.            |
|                                                             |
|  2. FREEDOM OF FAITH: "The Jews have their religion and     |
|     the Muslims have theirs." No one is forced to convert.  |
|                                                             |
|  3. JUSTICE FOR ALL: Helping the oppressed and stopping     |
|     criminals, even if the criminal is a close relative.    |
|                                                             |
|  4. PEACE COVENANT: No citizen is allowed to help the       |
|     enemies of the city (like the hostile Quraysh).         |
+-------------------------------------------------------------+
```

This constitution teaches us that Muslims are builders of peace. We must respect the rights of our neighbors, even if they pray differently than we do.

## Page 6: Fiqh of Treaties and Safety (Maliki Rules)

The Constitution of Madinah was not just a political agreement; it became a part of Islamic law. In the **Maliki** school of jurisprudence, keeping agreements and protecting others are sacred duties.

Let's look at two important terms that Imam Malik explained in his books:

### 1. Aman (Safety / Protection)
**Aman** (*Amān* - أَمَان) means granting safety to someone. In the Maliki school, if *any* Muslim—even a normal citizen, a woman, or a young person—gives their word to a non-Muslim traveler that they are safe, the entire Muslim community and the government must respect that safety. 
Imam Malik wrote that even if a Muslim just makes a friendly gesture that looks like safety to a non-Muslim, that person is legally protected under *Aman*. This shows how much Islam values human life and the word of a believer.

### 2. 'Ahd (Covenant / Treaty)
**'Ahd** (*'Ahd* - عَهْد) is a sacred promise or treaty. 
*   **Rule:** It is strictly forbidden (*Harām*) to break a treaty.
*   **Treachery (Ghadr):** If we sign a peace treaty with another community, we cannot attack them or trick them while the treaty is active. The Prophet (pbuh) said: *"For every betrayer, a banner will be raised on the Day of Resurrection to show their treachery."*

## Page 7: Badr & Uhud: Lessons in Patience

For many years, the pagan Quraysh continued to threaten the Muslims, stealing the property they left behind in Makkah and blocking them from trading. To stop this aggression, Allah finally gave the Muslims permission to defend themselves.

### 1. The Battle of Badr (2 AH)
The Quraysh marched toward Madinah with 1,000 well-armed soldiers. The Muslims had only 313 men and very few weapons. 
Before the battle, the Prophet (pbuh) did not boast. Instead, he spent the night weeping and praying to Allah, asking for help. The next day, the Muslims won a miraculous victory. 

> ### Creed Focus: Where Does Victory Come From?
> In the **Ash'ari** creed, we believe that Allah is **Al-Nasir** (*Al-Nāṣir* - النَّاصِر) — The Giver of Victory. 
> At Badr, the Quran says: *"And you threw not when you threw, but it was Allah who threw..."* (Quran 8:17). 
> The Prophet (pbuh) threw a handful of sand toward the enemy (the physical cause), but Allah made that sand enter the eyes of the enemy army (the ultimate effect). The lesson is: victory does not come from having more weapons or money; it comes only from Allah when we obey Him.

### 2. The Battle of Uhud (3 AH)
One year later, the Quraysh returned with 3,000 soldiers. The Prophet (pbuh) placed 50 archers on a hill and gave them a strict command: *"Do not leave this hill, even if you see birds eating our bodies, until I send for you."*
During the battle, the Muslims were winning. The archers saw the physical spoils of war (*Ghanīmah* - غَنِيمَة) on the ground. Forgetting the Prophet's command, most of them ran down to collect the items. 
Because they left their post, the enemy cavalry swept around the hill and attacked the Muslims from behind, causing a major setback where many companions were killed. 

**The Lesson:** Disobeying the Prophet's commands because we want worldly things (*Dunyā*) always leads to failure.

## Page 8: Rules of Peace and Battle

In many ancient cultures, war meant destroying everything: burning houses, killing families, and ruining fields. But the Prophet (pbuh) taught that conflict must never be glorified. It is only a last resort to stop oppression.

Imam Malik recorded the strict rules of combat that the first Caliph, Abu Bakr, gave to his army based on the Prophet's teachings:

```
                  +--------------------------------+
                  |    MALIKI ETHICS OF WARFARE    |
                  +--------------------------------+
                                  |
         +------------------------+------------------------+
         |                                                 |
         v                                                 v
+------------------+                              +------------------+
| PROTECT PEOPLE   |                              | PROTECT NATURE   |
| * No killing of  |                              | * No cutting of  |
|   women/children |                              |   fruitful trees |
| * No hurting the |                              | * No burning of  |
|   elderly/sick   |                              |   crops/fields   |
| * No attacking   |                              | * No slaughter of|
|   monks/priests  |                              |   animals except |
|   in temples     |                              |   for food       |
+------------------+                              +------------------+
```

These rules were written 1,300 years before modern international treaties like the Geneva Conventions! They show that even when Muslims are forced to defend themselves, they must be kind, fair, and protect the environment.

## Page 9: The Farewell Sermon (Khutbat al-Wada')

In the tenth year after the Hijrah, the Prophet (pbuh) performed his final pilgrimage to Makkah. He stood on Mount Arafat and gave a beautiful, final speech to over 100,000 Muslims. This speech is called **Khutbat al-Wada'** (*Khutbat al-Wadā'* - خُطْبَة الْوَدَاع).

In this sermon, the Prophet (pbuh) summarized the core values of Islam:

1.  **Sanctity of Life and Property:** The life and wealth of every human being is sacred. It is a major sin to steal or hurt someone.
2.  **No Racism:** The Prophet (pbuh) declared: *"An Arab has no superiority over a non-Arab, nor does a non-Arab have any superiority over an Arab. A white person has no superiority over a black person, nor does a black person have any superiority over a white person, except by piety (Taqwa)."*
3.  **Kindness to Women:** He commanded men to treat women with gentleness and respect, calling them a trust (*Amānah*) from Allah.
4.  **Economic Justice:** He abolished **Riba** (*Ribā* - رِبًا), which is usury or lending money at high interest. This system kept poor people in debt while making rich people richer.

## Page 10: How Knowledge Traveled to Madinah

Have you ever wondered how we know all these details today? It is because of a beautiful chain of teachers and students that started in Madinah.

1.  **The Source:** The Prophet Muhammad (pbuh) lived, taught, and passed away in Madinah.
2.  **The Companions:** Great Sahabah like **Abdullah ibn Umar** (the son of Umar) and **Aisha** (the Prophet's wife) lived in Madinah and taught thousands of students.
3.  **The Tabi'un:** The next generation of scholars, including the **Seven Fiqh Scholars of Madinah**, collected these teachings. A famous teacher among them was **Nafi'**, who was the personal student of Ibn Umar.
4.  **Imam Malik:** Imam Malik ibn Anas was born in Madinah. He studied under Nafi' and Rabi'at al-Ra'y. Because he lived in the very city where the Prophet walked, he wrote down the city's continuous practices (*'Amal*) in his book, **Al-Muwatta** (*Al-Muwaṭṭa'* - الْمُوَطَّأ). 

This is why the Maliki school is so unique: it is built on the living inheritance of the people of Madinah.
