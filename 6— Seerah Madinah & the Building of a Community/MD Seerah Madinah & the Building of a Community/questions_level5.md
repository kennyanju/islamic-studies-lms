# Level 5 Quiz: Seerah: Madinah & Building a Community

## Part A: Multiple Choice Questions
1. In what year did Prophet Muhammad ﷺ draft the Constitution of Madinah (*Sahifat al-Madinah*)?
   (A) 570 CE.
   (B) 610 CE.
   (C) 622 CE (1 AH).
   (D) 632 CE (11 AH).

2. How did Clause 25 of the Constitution of Madinah define the civic relationship of the Jewish clan of Banu 'Awf with the Muslim community?
   (A) As an enslaved group without property rights.
   (B) As required to abandon their customs within thirty days.
   (C) As exempt from obeying civil laws.
   (D) As a community (*Ummah*) alongside the believers, retaining their faith and property.

3. Who authored the foundational 8th-century treatise *Kitab al-Siyar al-Kabir*, establishing the first systematic code of Islamic international law?
   (A) Imam Muhammad ibn al-Hasan al-Shaybani.
   (B) Imam Ibn Khaldun.
   (C) Imam al-Shafi'i.
   (D) Imam al-Bukhari.

4. In classical Islamic jurisprudence (*Fiqh al-Siyar*), how are sovereign non-Muslim nations that maintain peaceful diplomatic treaties and trade accords classified?
   (A) Dar al-Harb (Abode of Belligerence).
   (B) Dar al-'Ahd / Dar al-Sulh (Abode of Treaty and Peace).
   (C) Dar al-Jahiliyyah.
   (D) Dar al-Kufr al-Mutlaq.

5. What solemn warning did Prophet Muhammad ﷺ give regarding someone who betrays, burdens, or harms a non-Muslim treaty-partner (*Mu'ahid*) (Sunan Abi Dawud 3052)?
   (A) He will be required to pay ten dinars to the municipal treasury.
   (B) He must fast three consecutive days in winter.
   (C) The Prophet ﷺ himself will stand as his prosecuting adversary on the Day of Resurrection.
   (D) He must donate three camels to public charity.

6. Why did Imam Malik prioritize *'Amal Ahl al-Madinah* (The Living Practice of Madinah) over solitary isolated Hadiths (*Ahad*)?
   (A) Because a living practice observed and transmitted continuously by thousands from thousands represents mass-transmitted certainty (*Tawatur*).
   (B) Because people in Madinah spoke a different dialect of Arabic from the rest of Arabia.
   (C) Because Madinah was the only city with paper libraries at the time.
   (D) Because the Abbasid governor prohibited writing solitary narrations.

7. How do modern jurists (such as the ECFR and Shaykh Abdallah bin Bayyah) understand a Muslim's legal citizenship, visa, or residency in a contemporary constitutional society?
   (A) As a non-binding convenience that can be abandoned at will.
   (B) As an invalid secular contract with no Islamic standing.
   (C) As a barrier preventing adherence to personal worship.
   (D) As a binding contract of mutual security and citizenship (*'Aqd al-Aman*) that must be fulfilled with ethical fidelity.

8. What pre-Islamic justice pact did the Prophet ﷺ praise in Madinah, declaring he would join such an alliance for human rights even after Islam?
   (A) The Council of Makkah.
   (B) Hilf al-Fudul (The League of the Virtuous).
   (C) The Roman Senate Accord.
   (D) The Treaty of Basra.

9. In classical Siyar, what is the legal ruling regarding diplomatic immunity for foreign emissaries and ambassadors visiting a Muslim state?
   (A) They may be held as political hostages during negotiations.
   (B) They must renounce their political allegiance before entering the city.
   (C) They possess diplomatic immunity (*Aman*) and cannot be harmed, detained, or executed.
   (D) They are prohibited from meeting with local community representatives.

10. What does the concept of "Muwatanah" signify in contemporary Islamic political ethics?
    (A) Shared civic citizenship based on mutual rights, public duties, and constructive contributions to civil society.
    (B) The restriction of legal residency and commercial trade exclusively to members of a single tribal lineage.
    (C) The replacement of constitutional public treaties with informal clan arbitration outside state jurisdiction.
    (D) The exemption of religious minorities from civic defense agreements and municipal neighborhood obligations.

## Part C: Matching Juristic Concepts
* Sahifat al-Madinah ─── The 622 CE charter establishing a pluralistic civic commonwealth
* Dar al-'Ahd ─── Sovereign nations bound by peaceful diplomatic accords and treaties
* Mu'ahid ─── A non-Muslim ally or citizen holding a sacred covenant of protection
* Hilf al-Fudul ─── The inter-tribal human rights alliance praised by the Prophet ﷺ
* Kitab al-Siyar al-Kabir ─── Al-Shaybani's masterwork codifying early international law

## Part D: Senior Seminar Case Study & Reflection
1. Analyze the constitutional genius of the Constitution of Madinah: How did it create a unified federal commonwealth (*Ummah Wahidah*) while preserving religious and cultural pluralism?
2. A contemporary extremist group claims: "All non-Muslim nations today are Dar al-Harb, and Muslims are not bound to honor Western laws." Deconstruct this claim using classical *Fiqh al-Siyar* and the hadiths of the *Mu'ahid*.
3. Detail how the concept of *Hilf al-Fudul* provides an Islamic mandate for modern Muslims to collaborate with non-Muslim civil rights, humanitarian, and environmental organizations.
4. Explain why Imam Malik's reliance on *'Amal Ahl al-Madinah* represents a sophisticated sociological epistemology that protects religious law from isolated textual misinterpretations.

## Answer Key
1. C - Drafted in 622 CE immediately following the Hijrah to Madinah.
2. D - Clause 25 designated Jewish clans as a constituent community with religious autonomy.
3. A - Imam Muhammad al-Shaybani pioneered classical international law in *Al-Siyar al-Kabir*.
4. B - Bilateral diplomatic relations and UN charters place modern nations into Dar al-'Ahd.
5. C - The Prophet ﷺ promised to personally oppose anyone who oppresses or betrays a Mu'ahid.
6. A - Living communal consensus (*Tawatur*) carries definitive epistemic weight over solitary reports.
7. D - Visas and citizenship constitute mutual security contracts (*'Aqd Aman*) binding under Shari'ah.
8. B - The Prophet ﷺ endorsed Hilf al-Fudul for its noble commitment to defending the vulnerable.
9. C - Diplomatic immunity is an established inviolable principle in prophetic practice.
10. A - Muwatanah establishes shared civic rights and responsibilities within pluralistic societies.
