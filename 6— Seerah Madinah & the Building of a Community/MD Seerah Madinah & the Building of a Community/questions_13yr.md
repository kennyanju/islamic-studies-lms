### Part B: Scenario & Case-Study Questions (5 Questions)
*Analyze the following cases based on what you have learned of Ash'ari creed and Maliki jurisprudence.*

11. **A student studies for his final exams but gets a low grade. He becomes angry and says, "Islamic creed says Allah is the Creator of all actions. If Allah wanted me to get an A, He would have created it. Therefore, studying is useless." How do you analyze his statement using Ash'ari theology?**
    *   *Answer:* The student has a flawed understanding of **Asbab** (causes) and **Qadar** (destiny). While it is true that Allah alone creates the final effect (the grade), human beings are legally commanded to utilize the physical means (*Asbab*) out of obedience. The student's failure is a trial (*fitnah*) that tests his patience (*Sabr*). Denouncing the means because of a setback is a rejection of Allah's wisdom and natural laws. He must align his physical body with actions while keeping his heart surrendered to the decree.

12. **A Muslim nation signs a peace treaty with a neighboring non-Muslim nation. A group of citizens within the Muslim nation decides to launch a secret hack on the neighbor's network to steal data, arguing, "They are non-Muslims, so the treaty doesn't apply to digital property." Identify the Maliki fiqh rulings that refute this argument.**
    *   *Answer:* This action violates the prohibition of **Ghadr** (treachery/betrayal). In Maliki fiqh, a covenant (*'Ahd*) is sacred and applies to all aspects of safety, including life, honor, and property (physical or digital). Muslims cannot act treacherously against signatories of a treaty. If a treaty is active, it must be honored in full. Engaging in secret attacks is classified as a major sin (*Harām*).

13. **During a civil debate, a speaker argues that Islam was spread by the sword and that warfare is glorified in Islamic history. Using the Maliki code of military ethics, write a legal defense that refutes this claim.**
    *   *Answer:* Islamic warfare (*Qital*) is defensive, aimed at repelling oppression, not forced conversion. The Maliki code of ethics forbids targeting non-combatants (women, children, elderly, monks) and bans the destruction of nature (cutting fruitful trees, ruining crops, poisoning wells). If the goal were forced conversion or destruction, these protections of life, religion, and ecology would not exist. War is a last resort to establish peace, not a tool for expansion.

14. **A local government proposes building a highway that will cut through a historical forest and displace a local orphanage. The community is deciding how to respond. How can a Muslim use the Farewell Sermon and the Five Necessities (Al-Daruriyyat al-Khams) to build a moral argument against this proposal?**
    *   *Answer:* The proposal directly threatens two of the Five Necessities:
        1. **Preservation of the Vulnerable / Wealth (*Hifz al-Mal*):** Taking or ruining the space of orphans (*Aytām*) violates their property rights, which the Prophet (pbuh) strictly guarded at Madinah.
        2. **Preservation of Nature / Life (*Hifz al-Nafs*):** Ruining ecosystems that sustain human and animal life violates the ethics of preservation. The Farewell Sermon establishes that justice and safety are universal rights.

15. **A scholar finds a Hadith that has a single chain of narrators (Khabar al-Wahid) which says the Prophet (pbuh) raised his hands at every movement in prayer. However, the scholar observes that all the descendants of the Sahabah in Madinah pray without raising their hands in those movements. How would Imam Malik resolve this conflict using 'Amal Ahl al-Madinah?**
    *   *Answer:* Imam Malik would prioritize the **'Amal Ahl al-Madinah** over the single-narrator Hadith. His reasoning is that the continuous, mass practice of an entire city of scholars who inherited the prayer from their fathers (who saw the Sahabah and the Prophet) is equivalent to a mass-transmitted (*Mutawatir*) Sunnah. A single report (*Khabar al-Wahid*) is subject to mistakes in memory or context, whereas an entire community cannot collectively make a mistake in their daily, public ritual of prayer.

### Part C: Short Answer & Critical Essay Prompts (5 Questions)
*Answer the questions with depth and critical analysis.*

16. **Explain the difference between Jihad and Qital, and why this distinction is important for building fiqh literacy.**
    *   *Answer:* *Jihad* (هِجَاد) means "striving" or exerting effort in the path of Allah, which includes spiritual struggles against one's ego (*Jihad al-Nafs*), studying, and charity. *Qital* (قِتَال) is the specific legal term for physical combat or warfare. Confusing the two leads to the false belief that all Jihad is violent conflict, whereas warfare in Islam is a restricted, defensive sub-category of the broader concept of Jihad.

17. **How does the Ash'ari creed explain the relationship between human actions, divine creation, and moral responsibility (Kasb)?**
    *   *Answer:* The Ash'ari school teaches the concept of **Kasb** (acquisition). Allah is the sole Creator of all actions and effects, but He grants human beings the free will to choose and intend a particular action. When the human chooses, Allah creates the action. Because the human chose and intended the action, they are held morally responsible for it, even though Allah is the physical Creator of the deed.

18. **Detail how the Farewell Sermon protected the economic rights of the poor by banning Riba (interest/usury).**
    *   *Answer:* *Riba* creates an unfair system where wealth is extracted from those in need through compounding debt, creating a cycle of poverty. By banning *Riba*, the Prophet (pbuh) protected the necessity of wealth (*Hifz al-Mal*) for the masses, ensuring that transactions were based on real trade and mutual benefit rather than economic exploitation.

19. **Outline the Seven Fiqh Scholars of Madinah (Al-Fuqaha' al-Sab'ah) and explain their role in consolidating the Sunnah.**
    *   *Answer:* They were the prominent Tabi'un scholars of Madinah: Sa'id ibn al-Musayyib, Urwah ibn al-Zubayr, Qasim ibn Muhammad, Kharijah ibn Zayd, Abu Bakr ibn Abd al-Rahman, Sulayman ibn Yasar, and Ubaydullah ibn Abdillah. They collected, debated, and unified the legal judgments of the Sahabah (like Umar and Ibn Umar), transforming them into a structured legal code that Imam Malik later inherited and codified.

20. **Why is the preservation of religion (Hifz al-Din) placed first among the Five Necessities, and how does it relate to the preservation of other human rights?**
    *   *Answer:* *Hifz al-Din* provides the ultimate moral and theological framework that makes life, intellect, family, and wealth sacred in the first place. Without a divine framework, human rights are subject to changing political opinions. By establishing the sanctity of religion, Islam establishes the absolute, unchanging divine protection for all other human rights.
