## Part 3: Scenario & Critical Reflection (Questions 16–20)
*Analyze the scenarios below and write a short essay response (3-5 sentences) for each.*

### 16. The Modern Career vs. Prayer Schedule
* **Scenario:** Kareem has a busy high school schedule. He is joining the school debate club and has basketball practice immediately after classes. He tells his mom: *"I will just combine my Dhuhr, Asr, and Maghrib prayers at night because my schedule is too packed to pray at school."*
* **Question:** How would you advise Kareem using the concept of Fiqh as a compass and the concept of *Barakah* (blessing in time) when organizing his daily schedule?

### 17. Evaluating the Logic of *‘Amal Ahl al-Madīnah*
* **Scenario:** A student asks: *"Why did Imam Malik prefer the practice of the people of Madinah over a solitary Hadith report? Isn't a written Hadith always more reliable than what a crowd of people are doing?"*
* **Question:** Explain the legal logic of *‘Amal*. Why is the collective, continuous action of a city like Madinah considered a strong, living transmission of the Sunnah?

### 18. Standardization and Unity
* **Scenario:** Imagine if Caliph ‘Uthmān had not standardized the written text of the Quran and had allowed every region to write the Quran in their local dialects and pronunciations.
* **Question:** What could have been the consequences for the unity of the global Muslim community and the preservation of the Quranic text?

### 19. Media Consumption and the Five Rulings
* **Scenario:** Yasmin spends two hours every day watching random videos on social media. She argues: *"Watching videos is not Haram, so I am not doing anything wrong."*
* **Question:** Help Yasmin analyze her activity using the five categories of action (*Al-Aḥkām al-Khamsah*). How can she use her intention (*Niyyah*) to change how she uses her screen time?

### 20. Theology in Action: Preparing for Exams
* **Scenario:** A teen student says: *"Everything is written by Allah. If Allah has decreed that I will fail my exam, studying won't change anything. If He has decreed I will pass, I will pass anyway without studying. So I will just play games."*
* **Question:** Correct this student's understanding using the Ash'ari creedal concepts of human agency (*Kasb*), cause and effect, and relying on Allah (*Tawakkul*).

## Answer Key & Explanations

### Part 1: Multiple Choice
1. **B) Divine mercy (*Raḥmah*) and humility (*Tawāḍu‘*).** (His action broke the historical pattern of revenge and established a precedent of mercy in victory).
2. **B) To protect the poor from financial exploitation.** (Usury creates a system where wealth accumulates only among the rich, which violates Islamic social justice).
3. **B) He enjoys a special, noble spiritual life in the intermediate realm (*Barzakh*)...** (Ash'ari creed balances high respect for the Prophet's continuing spiritual existence with the finality of his earthly legal mission).
4. **C) To prevent the Quran from being lost after many memorizers died.** (The loss of memorizers in the Yamamah campaign created an urgent administrative need to write down the text).
5. **B) The safety of Christian citizens, their property, and their churches.** (This covenant is a foundational text for religious tolerance in Islamic law).
6. **B) To resolve rising disputes over differing regional dialects.** (As Islam expanded to non-Arab regions, standardization prevented divisions over how the Quran should be recited).
7. **B) All sides acted out of a sincere effort to find the truth through legal reasoning (*Ijtihād*)...** (Sunni creed emphasizes holding a good opinion of all Sahabah, recognizing their status while acknowledging they were not infallible in political judgments).
8. **C) The continuous, living practice of the scholars and community of Madinah.** (It represents a mass-transmitted, visual sunnah that Imam Malik prioritized).
9. **C) Because it was the continuous living practice (*‘Amal*) of the scholars and people of Madinah.** (Imam Malik observed that the major successors did not cross their hands in obligatory prayers, indicating this was the original practice of the Prophet ﷺ).
10. **B) Through trade routes, where local populations observed the honesty of Maliki merchants.** (The ethical practice of Islamic trade laws by Muslim merchants was the primary vehicle for the peaceful spread of Islam in West Africa).

### Part 3: Scenario & Critical Reflection (Sample Answers)
16. **Explanation:** Kareem should realize that prayer (*Ṣalāt*) is a compulsory duty (*Farḍ*) that must be performed within its designated window. Combining prayers without a valid excuse (like travel or illness) is not permitted in the Maliki school. He should use Fiqh as a compass to navigate his day, scheduling his debate and basketball practices around his prayers, which will bring *Barakah* (spiritual blessing) and efficiency to his time.
17. **Explanation:** The logic is that a solitary Hadith (*Khabar al-Wāḥid*) is passed down through a single chain of narrators and is subject to error or misunderstanding. On the other hand, the continuous practice (*‘Amal*) of the entire city of Madinah was witnessed and performed by thousands of people generation after generation. It is highly unlikely that an entire city would collectively fabricate or alter a daily, public action like prayer posture or market weights.
18. **Explanation:** If ‘Uthmān had not acted, the Quranic text might have fractured into multiple regional versions with different words and meanings. This would have caused deep divisions (*Fitnah*) in the community, similar to how previous scriptures were altered, and would have undermined the unified preservation of the text.
19. **Explanation:** While watching videos is generally permissible (*Mubāḥ*), doing it for two hours daily without purpose wastes time and can border on disliked (*Makrūh*). If it leads her to delay prayers or neglect her studies, it can become forbidden (*Ḥarām*). Yasmin can change her intention by dedicating some of that time to educational or spiritual videos, or by limiting entertainment to healthy relaxation, thus turning it into a positive action.
20. **Explanation:** In Ash'ari creed, we believe that Allah has created the world with causes and effects. Studying is the cause, and passing is the effect. Although Allah knows and decrees the outcome, He commands us to take the necessary means. The student's choice to study is their "acquisition" (*Kasb*), and they are responsible for it. Real trust in Allah (*Tawakkul*) means tying the camel first (studying hard) and then relying on Allah for the results.
