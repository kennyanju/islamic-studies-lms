# Level 5 Quiz: Seerah, History & Living Faith Today

## Part A: Multiple Choice Questions
1. In his epochal treatise *Al-Muqaddimah*, what sociological concept did Ibn Khaldun identify as the foundational driving engine of civilizational rise and state formation?
   (A) High maritime taxation.
   (B) 'Asabiyyah (Group solidarity and shared social cohesion).
   (C) The discovery of silver mines.
   (D) The construction of royal palatial gardens.

2. How did the development of empirical natural sciences in Islamic civilization differ fundamentally from the European scientific revolution?
   (A) Islamic science was banned by religious judges in all centuries.
   (B) Islamic science was seen as an organic act of divine worship (*'Ibadah*) and Quranic reflection (*Tafakkur*), flourishing inside religious universities and mosques.
   (C) Muslim scientists only translated ancient Babylonian mythology.
   (D) Islamic science focused exclusively on astrology.

3. Which 11th-century polymath authored *Kitab al-Manazir*, refuting ancient Greek theories and founding the modern experimental science of optics?
   (A) Al-Khwarizmi.
   (B) Al-Hasan Ibn al-Haytham (Alhazen).
   (C) Ibn Rushd.
   (D) Al-Farabi.

4. What practical religious and legal need directly motivated Al-Khwarizmi to invent the mathematical discipline of **Algebra (*Al-Jabr*)**?
   (A) Calculating the trajectory of military catapults.
   (B) Solving complex Islamic inheritance (*Mirath*) distribution formulas and land survey measurements.
   (C) Calculating the exact distance to foreign trading ports in China.
   (D) Designing decorative tile patterns in Isfahan.

5. What historic institution founded by Fatima al-Fihri in 859 CE in Fes, Morocco is recognized by UNESCO as the oldest continuously operating university in the world?
   (A) Oxford University.
   (B) The University of al-Qarawiyyin.
   (C) The Sorbonne.
   (D) Harvard College.

6. What distinguished the intellectual and cultural life of Muslim Cordoba in Andalusia during the 10th century?
   (A) Paved and illuminated streets, running water, 70 libraries, and religious coexistence (*Convivencia*) while Europe was in the Dark Ages.
   (B) Total isolation from foreign trade and travel.
   (C) The complete prohibition of studying medicine.
   (D) Constructing stone fortresses without any books.

7. What historic reality dismantles colonial myths regarding sub-Saharan West Africa prior to European arrival?
   (A) The rediscovery of Roman roads in Nigeria.
   (B) The vibrant manuscript tradition of Timbuktu and Sankore University, housing hundreds of thousands of complex texts on law, astronomy, math, and optics.
   (C) The complete absence of any trade routes across the Sahara.
   (D) The discovery of ancient Egyptian obelisks in Mali.

8. In Ibn Khaldun's four-stage civilizational cycle, what factor triggers the inevitable decline of an imperial dynasty?
   (A) The sudden cooling of regional climate.
   (B) Luxury (*Taraf*), moral decay, oppressive taxation, and the loss of shared ethical solidarity (*'Asabiyyah*).
   (C) The migration of horse herds into foreign lands.
   (D) The invention of paper manuscripts.

9. What are the four core pillars of the "Western Muslim Leadership Manifesto" for contemporary senior graduates?
   (A) Political isolation, financial speculation, military training, and ascetic withdrawal.
   (B) Theological Certitude (Yaqin), Methodological Jurisprudence (Asalah), Civic Engagement (Ihsaan), and Professional Excellence.
   (C) Abandoning madhhabs, imitating secular trends, changing prayer times, and renaming holidays.
   (D) Moving permanently to uninhabited desert regions.

10. What did Imam Malik declare regarding the historical continuity of the Ummah?
    (A) "Every new generation must invent an entirely new religion."
    (B) "Nothing will rectify the end of this nation except that which rectified its beginning!"
    (C) "Knowledge will disappear completely after the third century."
    (D) "The Ummah should adopt the laws of neighboring empires."

## Part D: Senior Seminar Case Study & Reflection
1. Synthesize Ibn Khaldun's theory of *'Asabiyyah* and civilizational decline: How does his analysis apply to modern secular nation-states experiencing social atomization, luxury, and hyper-individualism?
2. Analyze the synthesis of faith and scientific inquiry in the Golden Age: Why did Islamic civilization view empirical laboratory science as a sacred pursuit of *Tafakkur* rather than an enemy of religion?
3. What is the historical and psychological significance of the Timbuktu manuscript tradition in deconstructing racist colonial narratives regarding African intellectual history?
4. Formulate your personal **Leadership Manifesto**: How do you plan to combine deep theological certitude (*Yaqin*), ethical integrity, and professional brilliance in your future academic or career field?
