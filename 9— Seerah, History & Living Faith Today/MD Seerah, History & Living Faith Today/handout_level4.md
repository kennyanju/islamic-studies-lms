## 1. The Rightly Guided Caliphate (Al-Khilafah al-Rashidah)

Following the passing of the Prophet ﷺ, the Muslim Ummah was guided by four exceptional companions known collectively as the **Four Rightly Guided Caliphs (Al-Khulafa' al-Rashidun)**. Their governance represented the closest practical application of prophetic justice, ethical leadership, and communal consultation (*Shura*).

The Prophet ﷺ instructed:  
*"Adhere firmly to my Sunnah and the sunnah of the rightly guided caliphs after me; hold fast to it and bite onto it with your molar teeth!"* (Sunan Abi Dawud 4607; Jami' at-Tirmidhi 2676, Sahih).

| Caliph | Period | Core Focus | Enduring Milestone |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sayyidina Abu Bakr al-Siddiq** | 11-13 AH (2 years) | Unity and Preservation | Preserved the unity of Arabia during the Riddah crisis and commissioned the first compiled Quranic sheets (*Suhuf*). |
| **Sayyidina 'Umar ibn al-Khattab** | 13-23 AH (10 years) | Justice and Civil Institutions | Established the Hijri calendar, the public treasury (*Bayt al-Mal*), independent courts, and signed the Treaty of Jerusalem. |
| **Sayyidina 'Uthman ibn 'Affan** | 23-35 AH (12 years) | Generosity and Standardization | Standardized the authoritative Uthmanic Master Codex (*Al-Mushaf al-Imam*) and expanded civic infrastructure. |
| **Sayyidina 'Ali ibn Abi Talib** | 35-40 AH (5 years) | Knowledge and Principle | Governed with unyielding judicial integrity, Arabic eloquence, and supreme moral steadfastness during civil discord. |

## 2. The Four Caliphs and Their Enduring Legacies

### 1. Sayyidina Abu Bakr al-Siddiq (11-13 AH / 2 Years)
* **The Challenge:** Following the Prophet's death, several tribes rebelled and false claimants to prophethood arose in the Apostasy Wars (*Hurub al-Riddah*).
* **The Achievement:** With resolute courage, Abu Bakr preserved the unity of Arabia, declared that not a single obligatory obligation of Zakah would be abandoned, and commissioned the **first compilation of the Holy Quran** under the direction of Zayd ibn Thabit.

### 2. Sayyidina 'Umar ibn al-Khattab (13-23 AH / 10 Years)
* **The Embodying of Justice (Al-Faruq):** Renowned for rigorous personal accountability and justice, walking the streets of Madinah at night carrying sacks of flour on his own shoulders to relieve hungry families.
* **State Institutions:** Established the Islamic Hijri calendar, the public state treasury (*Bayt al-Mal*), social welfare stipends for children and the elderly, independent judicial courts, and urban planning.
* **The Entry into Jerusalem (16 AH):** Entered Jerusalem peacefully on foot, taking turns riding a single mount with his servant. He declined to pray inside the Church of the Holy Sepulchre so Muslims would not later convert it into a mosque, granting the Christians of Jerusalem full religious freedom, safety of person, and protection of churches through the **Covenant of 'Umar** (*Al-'Uhdah al-'Umariyyah*).

### 3. Sayyidina 'Uthman ibn 'Affan (23-35 AH / 12 Years)
* **Modesty and Generosity (Dhun-Nurayn):** Renowned for profound humility and generosity. He purchased the Well of Rumah to ensure free drinking water for the residents of Madinah and equipped the entire army for the Expedition of Tabuk.
* **The Standardized Quranic Codex:** As the borders of the Muslim world expanded across diverse language communities, he authorized the transcription and distribution of the **Uthmanic Master Codex (Al-Mushaf al-Imam)**, unifying all regions upon a single recitation text.
* **Patience in Tribulation:** Besieged in his home by armed agitators, he refused to allow Muslim defenders to shed blood on his behalf, meeting martyrdom while reciting the Holy Quran.

### 4. Sayyidina 'Ali ibn Abi Talib (35-40 AH / 5 Years)
* **Preeminent Knowledge and Chivalry:** The Prophet ﷺ said to him: *"You are to me in the position that Harun was to Musa, except that there is no prophet after me"* (Sahih al-Bukhari 3701; Sahih Muslim 2404). Sayyidina 'Umar affirmed: *"The most decisive of us in legal judgments is 'Ali"* (Sahih al-Bukhari 4481).
* **Principled Steadfastness:** Governed during a period of complex civil upheaval with exemplary legal integrity, chivalry (*Futuwwah*), and devotion to spiritual truth, refusing to sacrifice divine principles for short-term political convenience.

## 3. Sunni Creed: Revering the Companions (Adab ma'a al-Sahabah)

A cornerstone of Sunni orthodoxy and Ash'ari creed is **loving, honoring, and respecting all the Companions of the Prophet ﷺ ('Adalat al-Sahabah)**:
* Allah praised them in the Holy Quran: *"And the first forerunners [in the faith] among the Muhajirun and the Ansar and those who followed them with good conduct: Allah is pleased with them and they are pleased with Him"* (Surah At-Tawbah 9:100).
* The Prophet ﷺ warned: *"Do not insult my companions! For by Him in whose Hand is my soul, if one of you were to spend the equivalent of Mount Uhud in gold, it would not reach a handful of their deeds, nor half of it!"* (Sahih al-Bukhari 3673; Sahih Muslim 2540).
* Sound belief entails maintaining clean hearts and tongues regarding the historical disagreements that occurred among them, recognizing that they were sincere scholars striving for truth (*Mujtahidun*).
