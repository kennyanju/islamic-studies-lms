## Part 3: Scenario & Short Answer (Questions 16–20)
*Read the scenarios and write 2-3 sentences to answer the questions.*

### 16. The Science Project (*Niyyah*)
* **Scenario:** Yusuf is studying for his science exam. He thinks: *"This is just a school science project, it has nothing to do with my religion."*
* **Question:** How can Yusuf change his intention (*Niyyah*) to turn his school studies into a rewardable act of worship?

### 17. The Zakah Duty
* **Scenario:** A wealthy person says: *"I don't need to pay Zakah to the community. I will just give a small amount of charity directly to my friend when I feel like it."*
* **Question:** Based on the actions of Sayyiduna Abu Bakr and the Maliki school, why is Zakah a compulsory community duty rather than just a personal choice?

### 18. Sharing the Camel
* **Scenario:** When entering Jerusalem, Caliph ‘Umar walked on foot while his servant rode the camel, because it was the servant's turn on their travel schedule.
* **Question:** What does this story teach us about how Islamic leaders treat other people and view justice?

### 19. Respect for Knowledge
* **Scenario:** A student is sitting in a classroom, talking to their friends while the teacher is reading a Hadith of the Prophet ﷺ.
* **Question:** How does this student's behavior compare to how Imam Malik prepared himself to study and teach Hadith? What should the student change?

### 20. The Five Categories of Action
* **Scenario:** Layla wants to spend her entire weekend playing video games and skipping her homework.
* **Question:** Using the five categories of action (*Al-Aḥkām al-Khamsah*), what is the difference between a permissible (*Mubāḥ*) action like playing games, and a disliked (*Makrūh*) action like wasting all her time?

## Answer Key & Explanations

### Part 1: Multiple Choice
1. **C) 8th Year.** (Fatḥ Makkah took place in Ramadan, 8 AH).
2. **B) Bowing his head in deep humility (*Tawāḍu‘*) on his camel.** (He showed gratitude to Allah instead of pride).
3. **B) "Go, for you are free."** (The Prophet ﷺ granted a general amnesty, showing mercy instead of revenge).
4. **C) Mount ‘Arafāt.** (This is where the Farewell Sermon was delivered).
5. **C) Sayyiduna Abu Bakr al-Ṣiddīq.** (He kept the community stable and firm in their faith).
6. **B) Successor or Steward.** (A Caliph manages the political and social affairs of the community, succeeding the Prophet’s ﷺ leadership).
7. **A) Sayyiduna Abu Bakr al-Ṣiddīq.** (He ordered the first collection of the Quran onto sheets of parchment after many memorizers died in battle).
8. **B) Sayyiduna ‘Umar.** (He chose the Migration (*Hijrah*) as the starting point of the calendar).
9. **C) The Muwaṭṭa’.** (This is Imam Malik’s compilation of Hadith, sayings of the Companions, and practices of Madinah).
10. **C) Letting their arms rest naturally at their sides (*Sadl*).** (This is the dominant opinion in the Maliki school, representing the continuous practice of the people of Madinah).

### Part 2: Fill in the Blanks
11. **Tawāḍu‘**
12. **Al-Fārūq**
13. **Muṣḥaf**
14. **Sadl**
15. **Timbuktu**

### Part 3: Scenario & Short Answer (Sample Answers)
16. **Explanation:** Yusuf can intend to study science to understand Allah's creation, and to use his knowledge to build technology or medicine that helps the Muslim community and humanity. By having this intention (*Niyyah*), his study hours become rewardable acts of worship (*‘Ibādah*).
17. **Explanation:** Zakah is a compulsory social obligation that belongs to the poor. Based on Abu Bakr’s stance and Maliki fiqh, it must be collected systematically to ensure the rights of the poor are protected, and cannot be replaced by personal, random charity.
18. **Explanation:** It teaches us that in Islam, all human beings are equal, and leaders are servants of the people. Justice means respecting agreements and treating subordinates with fairness and dignity.
19. **Explanation:** Imam Malik would make Wudu, apply perfume, and sit with absolute respect before speaking about the Prophet ﷺ. The student should stop talking, sit up straight, and listen with respect when the Hadith of the Prophet ﷺ is being read.
20. **Explanation:** Playing games is permissible (*Mubāḥ*) in moderation, but wasting an entire weekend and neglecting duties (like homework or helping parents) turns it into a disliked (*Makrūh*) or even prohibited (*Ḥarām*) action if she misses her obligatory prayers.
