## Chapter 1: The Secret Power of Intention (*Niyyah*)

Have you ever wished you had a superpower? What if I told you that you already have one? It is called **Niyyah** (نِيّة), which is the Arabic word for **intention**. 

### What is an Intention?
An intention is the reason *why* you do something. It is a quiet decision made in your heart before you act. No one else can see your intention—only you and Allah know it!

The Prophet Muhammad ﷺ taught us:
> *"Actions are judged only by intentions, and everyone will get what they intended."* (Bukhari & Muslim)

This means that two people can do the exact same physical action, but get completely different rewards from Allah because of what was in their hearts.

```
                  +--------------------------+
                  |    Physical Action:      |
                  |     Eating Dinner        |
                  +-------------+------------+
                                |
        +-----------------------+-----------------------+
        |                                               |
        v                                               v
+----------------------------+             +----------------------------+
|  Intention A:              |             |  Intention B:              |
|  "I am greedy and just     |             |  "I want to strengthen my   |
|  want to stuff myself."    |             |  body to perform my Salah."|
+-------------+--------------+             +-------------+--------------+
              |                                          |
              v                                          v
      [ Ordinary Habit ]                        [ Reaped Reward / ]
      [ (No Reward)    ]                        [ Act of Worship  ]
```

### Let’s Look at an Example:
Imagine two brothers, Yusuf and Zayd. Both of them are kicking a football in the garden.
* Yusuf is playing just because he is bored and wants to pass the time.
* Zayd is playing because he knows his body is a trust (*Amānah*) from Allah, and he wants to stay healthy and strong so he can pray, help his parents, and be an active Muslim. He also wants to make his brother Yusuf happy.

Physically, Yusuf and Zayd are doing the exact same thing: kicking a ball. But spiritually, Zayd's play is transformed into an act of worship (*‘Ibadah*) that earns him rewards, while Yusuf's play is just a normal daily habit (*‘Adah*).

> **[Mālikī Fiqh Flag: The Rule of Intention]**
> In the Mālikī school of law, intention (*niyyah*) is not just a nice detail—it is a strict pillar (*Rukn*) for worship. If you pray, make Wudu, or fast, you must make a specific intention in your heart *before* you start. If you perform the actions without this intention, your worship is not legally valid!

### Pure Sincerity (*Ikhlāṣ*) vs. Showing Off (*Riyā’*)
To make our actions count, we need **Ikhlāṣ** (إِخْلَاص) — **sincerity**. This means doing things *only* to please Allah, not to get praise from people. 

The opposite of sincerity is **Riyā’** (رِيَاء) — **showing off**. If you clean your room only because you want your parents to say, "Wow, you are the best child in the world!" instead of doing it to please Allah by being clean and helpful, that is *riyā’*. Showing off ruins the spiritual reward of our good deeds!

### [Ash‘arī Creed Insight: Allah is the Creator of Our Actions]
Have you ever wondered: "If I choose to do something, did I create that action, or did Allah?"
According to the **Ash‘arī school of belief**, we believe that:
1. **Allah is the Creator of everything**, including our ability to choose and the actions we perform. 
2. However, Allah gave us a free will. When we choose to do something, we "acquire" the action. The Arabic word for this is **Kasb** (كَسْب) — **acquisition**.
3. Therefore, because we choose to do good or bad, we are responsible for our choices, even though Allah is the One who created the actions and the power inside us to perform them.

## Chapter 2: The Quran — The Story of Revelation and Preservation

The Quran is the speech of Allah. It is the guide sent to lead humanity out of darkness and into light. Let's learn how it came to us and how it remained exactly the same for over 1,400 years.

### How the Quran was Revealed
The Quran was not sent down as a physical book all at once. Instead, it was revealed in pieces over **23 years** to the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ.
* The revelations began in the Cave of Hira in Makkah when the Prophet ﷺ was 40 years old.
* The Angel Jibreel (Gabriel) would bring the verses, recite them to the Prophet ﷺ, and the Prophet ﷺ would instantly memorize them.
* Some verses were revealed in **Makkah** (about belief in Allah, Paradise, and Hell), and others were revealed in **Madinah** (about laws, community, and charity).

### [Ash‘arī Creed Insight: The Two Understandings of Allah's Speech]
In our creed, we learn a beautiful distinction about the Speech of Allah (*Kalām*):
1. **Al-Kalām al-Nafsī (الكَلَام النَّفْسِي - Eternal Speech):** Allah's attribute of Speech is eternal and has no beginning or end. It does not consist of sounds, voices, or human languages like Arabic. It is an attribute of Allah, just like His Knowledge.
2. **Al-Kalām al-Lafẓī (الكَلَام اللَّفْظِي - Created Letters/Sounds):** To allow humans to understand His eternal message, Allah created the physical Arabic words, letters, and ink. When we read the Arabic Mushaf or listen to its recitation, we are interacting with the created letters and sounds that point directly to Allah’s eternal, uncreated Speech.

### How the Quran was Preserved
In ancient Arabia, most people did not know how to read or write. Instead, they had incredibly strong memories. The Quran was preserved in two main ways:

#### 1. In the Hearts (Oral Preservation)
As soon as the Prophet ﷺ received verses, he recited them to his companions (the Sahabah). Thousands of Sahabah memorized the verses perfectly. They recited them in their daily prayers. This made it impossible for anyone to change a single letter, because thousands of people would immediately notice the mistake!

#### 2. On Pieces of Material (Written Preservation)
The Prophet ﷺ had special scribes, like Zayd ibn Thābit, who wrote down the verses on whatever materials they could find:
* Pieces of leather or parchment (*Raq*)
* Flat stones (*Likhāf*)
* Camel shoulder bones (*Aktāf*)
* Palm tree branches (*‘Usub*)

Whenever a verse was written, it was verified directly in front of the Prophet ﷺ.

```
       +---------------------------------------------+
       |           Dual Preservation Tracks          |
       +----------------------o----------------------+
                              |
              +---------------+---------------+
              |                               |
              v                               v
     [ Oral Track ]                  [ Written Track ]
   Memorized by thousands           Scribes wrote verses on
  of Sahabah and recited            leather, bones, and stones
   in daily prayers.                in front of the Prophet ﷺ.
              |                               |
              +---------------+---------------+
                              |
                              v
                +---------------------------+
                |    Standardized Mushaf    |
                |   (The Uthmanic Codex)    |
                +---------------------------+
```

### The Compilation of the Mushaf
After the Prophet ﷺ passed away, two major steps were taken to keep the Quran safe:

1. **Under Caliph Abu Bakr:** Many Sahabah who memorized the Quran died in a battle. Umar suggested to Abu Bakr that they should collect all the scattered written pieces into one single, master manuscript. Zayd ibn Thābit was put in charge. He was extremely careful: he only accepted a written verse if two witnesses swore they saw it written in the presence of the Prophet ﷺ.
2. **Under Caliph Uthman:** As Islam spread to new countries, people began pronouncing the Quran in different regional dialects. To keep the Muslim community united, Uthman ordered the master copy to be multiplied, and sent official copies to all major cities. These copies used a specific, standardized spelling style called **Rasm al-‘Uthmānī** (رسم العثماني). This is the exact same text we read in our Mushafs today!

## Chapter 3: Exploring Tafseer — Surah Al-Ikhlāṣ and Surah Al-`Aṣr

What is **Tafseer** (تَفْسِير)? It is the science of explaining the meanings of the Quran. We cannot just guess what a verse means; we must study what the Prophet ﷺ and his companions explained. Let's look at two short, powerful Surahs in depth.

### Surah Al-Ikhlāṣ (The Sincerity)
This Surah was revealed when the polytheists of Makkah asked the Prophet ﷺ: "Tell us about the lineage of your Lord." Allah revealed:

> **قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ**  
> *Qul Huwa-Llāhu Aḥad*  
> Say, "He is Allah, [who is] One.
>
> **اللَّهُ الصَّمَدُ**  
> *Allāhu-ṣ-Ṣamad*  
> Allah, the Self-Sufficient Besought of all.
>
> **لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ**  
> *Lam yalid wa lam yūlad*  
> He neither begets nor is born,
>
> **وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ**  
> *Wa lam yakul-lahū kufuwan aḥad*  
> Nor is there to Him any equivalent.

#### Key Theological Meanings:
* **Aḥad (أَحَد):** Allah is absolutely One. He does not have partners, He is not divided into different parts, and there is no other god besides Him.
* **Aṣ-Ṣamad (الصَّمَد):** This is a unique name of Allah. It means He is the Supreme Master whom everyone needs, but He does not need anyone. He does not eat, drink, sleep, or decay. He is completely perfect and self-sufficient.
* **Tanzīh (تَنْزِيه - Transcendence):** 
  * *[Ash‘arī Creed Flag]* We believe that Allah is completely different from everything He created. The verse *"Nor is there to Him any equivalent"* means He has no likeness. He does not look like a human, He does not have physical body parts (like hands or eyes in a physical sense), and He does not live inside space or time because He created space and time!

---

### Surah Al-`Aṣr (The Time)
Imam al-Shāfi‘ī famously said: *"If Allah had revealed only this Surah to mankind, it would have been enough for them."* Let's read it:

> **وَالْعَصْرِ**  
> *Wa-l-‘Aṣr*  
> By time,
>
> **إِنَّ الْإِنسَانَ لَفِي خُسْرٍ**  
> *Inna-l-insāna lafī khusr*  
> Indeed, mankind is in loss,
>
> **إِلَّا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالْحَقِّ وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالصَّبْرِ**  
> *Illa-l-ladhīna āmanū wa ‘amilu-ṣ-ṣāliḥāti wa tawāṣaw bi-l-ḥaqqi wa tawāṣaw bi-ṣ-ṣabr*  
> Except for those who have believed and done righteous deeds and advised each other to truth and advised each other to patience.

#### The Four Steps to Success:
Allah swears an oath by **Time** (*Al-‘Aṣr*). Time is slipping away like ice melting in the sun. To avoid losing our life capital, we must do four things:
1. **Belief (*Īmān*):** Knowing the truth about Allah, His prophets, and the Quran.
2. **Righteous Actions (*‘Amal Ṣāliḥ*):** Doing good deeds with a pure intention (*Niyyah*).
3. **Advising to Truth (*Tawāṣī bi’l-Ḥaqq*):** Helping our friends and family learn the truth and stand up for what is right.
4. **Advising to Patience (*Tawāṣī bi’ṣ-Ṣabr*):** Reminding each other to stay strong when things get tough, and to keep doing good even when we feel lazy.

## Chapter 4: Hadith and Sunnah — The Prophet’s ﷺ Footsteps

To practice Islam, we need the Quran, but we also need the **Sunnah** (سُنَّة) — the path and practice of the Prophet ﷺ. The reports that tell us about the Sunnah are called **Hadiths** (أَحَادِيث).

### How a Hadith is Structured
Every Hadith has two parts. Think of it like a train: the engine that pulls it (the chain of people) and the carriages behind it (the message).

```
   +-------------------------------------------------------------+
   |                        HADITH STRUCTURE                     |
   +-------------------------------------------------------------+
   |  ISNĀD (Chain of Narrators)  --->  MATN (The Actual Text)    |
   |                                                             |
   |  "Yusuf heard from Malik,   |     "Actions are judged       |
   |   who heard from Nafi',     |      only by intentions..."   |
   |   who heard from Ibn Umar,  |                               |
   |   who heard from the        |                               |
   |   Prophet ﷺ..."             |                               |
   +-----------------------------+-------------------------------+
```

1. **Isnād (إِسْنَاد) — The Chain:** This is the list of people who passed the story along. For example: *"Malik narrated from Nafi‘, who narrated from Ibn ‘Umar, that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said..."*
2. **Matn (مَتْن) — The Text:** This is the actual statement, action, or description of the Prophet ﷺ. For example: *"Actions are judged by intentions..."*

### The Hadith Detective Work
Hadith scholars were like master detectives. They traveled for months just to verify one Hadith! They checked the life of every narrator in the chain:
* Was this person honest?
* Did they have a great memory?
* Did they actually meet the teacher they claimed to learn from?

If the chain was perfect and all narrators were trustworthy, the Hadith was classified as **Ṣaḥīḥ** (صَحِيح) — authentic. If there was a gap or a narrator was weak, it was called **Ḍa‘īf** (ضَعِيف) — weak.

## Chapter 5: Why Do We Follow a Madhhab?

If you are sick, you go to a qualified doctor who has studied medicine for years. You do not just read a medical website and try to perform surgery on yourself! 

In Islamic law, we do the same thing. Rulings are extracted from the Quran and Hadith by master scholars called **Mujtahids**. The schools of law they founded are called **Madhāhib** (مَذَاهِب, sing. **Madhhab**). Following a Madhhab keeps us safe from making wrong guesses about our religion.

There are four famous, correct Sunni Madhhabs: Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, and Hanbali. We follow the **Mālikī Madhhab**.

### Imam Malik and the City of Light
Our school of law is named after **Imam Malik ibn Anas** (93–179 AH). He lived his entire life in Madinah, the city where the Prophet ﷺ lived and died. 

Imam Malik loved the Prophet ﷺ so much that he refused to ride a horse in Madinah, because he did not want the hooves of the animal to stamp on the ground where the Prophet ﷺ was buried!

```
                  +--------------------------+
                  |  Prophet Muhammad ﷺ      |
                  |  teaches the Sahabah     |
                  +-------------+------------+
                                |  (Lived and practiced together)
                                v
                  +--------------------------+
                  |  Sahabah pass practice   |
                  |  to their children       |
                  +-------------+------------+
                                |  (Continuous city-wide tradition)
                                v
                  +--------------------------+
                  |  Imam Malik observes     |
                  |  the Living Practice     |
                  |  ('Amal Ahl al-Madinah)  |
                  +--------------------------+
```

### The Mālikī Secret: ‘Amal Ahl al-Madīnah
Imam Malik had a unique and beautiful source for finding the Sunnah. It is called **‘Amal Ahl al-Madīnah** (عَمَل أَهْل المَدِينَة), which means **the practice of the people of Madinah**.

Imam Malik argued:
* The Prophet ﷺ lived in Madinah for ten years.
* He taught thousands of Sahabah how to pray, eat, buy, and sell.
* When the Prophet ﷺ passed away, the Sahabah did not leave Madinah; they stayed and practiced Islam every single day.
* They taught their children, who taught their children (Imam Malik's teachers).
* Therefore, the collective, daily behavior of the entire city of Madinah is like a physical, living Hadith transmitted by thousands of people to thousands of people!

> **[Mālikī Fiqh Flag: Lived Practice over Single Hadith]**
> Because of this, if Imam Malik found a single Hadith narrated by one person (*Khabar al-Wāḥid*) that went against the continuous, public practice of the entire city of Madinah, he would follow the practice of Madinah instead. He famously said: *"A transmission by thousands of people from thousands of people is more reliable than one person from another."*

## Glossary of Key Arabic Terms

1. **Niyyah (نِيّة):** Intention. The purpose behind an action, located in the heart.
2. **Ikhlāṣ (إِخْلَاص):** Sincerity. Doing actions solely for the sake of Allah.
3. **Riyā’ (رِيَاء):** Showing off. Seeking the praise of people through religious deeds.
4. **Kasb (كَسْب):** Acquisition. The Ash‘arī belief that humans choose and acquire their actions, while Allah is the Creator of those actions.
5. **Kalām (كَلَام):** Speech. Specifically refers to Allah's attribute of Speech.
6. **Tafseer (تَفْسِير):** Explanation. The science of explaining the meanings of the Quran.
7. **Tanzīh (تَنْزِيه):** Transcendence. Believing Allah is completely free from resembling His creation.
8. **Isnād (إِسْنَاد):** The chain of narrators of a Hadith.
9. **Matn (مَتْن):** The actual text or speech of a Hadith.
10. **Madhhab (مَذْهَب):** A school of Islamic law.
11. **‘Amal Ahl al-Madīnah (عَمَل أَهْل المَدِينَة):** The lived practice of the people of Madinah, used by Imam Malik as a primary legal proof.

## End-of-Book Reflection Challenge

Choose one task that you do every single day (like doing your homework, helping wash the dishes, brushing your teeth, or walking to school). 

1. Write down what your intention (*Niyyah*) usually is when you do this task.
2. Now, rewrite that intention so that the task is transformed into an act of worship (*‘Ibadah*) for Allah. Explain exactly how this change in intention changes how you feel about the task.
