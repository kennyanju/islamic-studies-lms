## 1. Family Fiqh (*Fiqh al-Usrah*): The Sanctuary of Marriage (*Nikah*)

In contemporary secular culture, marriage is often reduced to a temporary civil arrangement or romantic consumer bond. In Islam, marriage is a **sacred covenant (*Mithaqan Ghalidha*)** designed to cultivate spiritual tranquility (*Sakinah*), affection (*Mawaddah*), and mutual mercy (*Rahmah*) (Surah Ar-Rum 30:21).

### The Pillars of Nikah in Maliki Jurisprudence:
In classical Maliki jurisprudence (*Al-Risalah*, *Matn Ibn 'Ashir*), a valid marriage contract rests upon four foundational pillars (*Arkan*):
1. **The Guardian (*Al-Wali*):** Who represents and safeguards the bride's legal, financial, and personal interests.
2. **The Bridal Gift (*Al-Sadaq* / *Mahr*):** An obligatory gift given directly and exclusively to the bride as her independent personal property.
3. **The Two Spouses (*Al-Zawjan*):** Both parties must be free of legal impediments and enter the marriage with genuine consent.
4. **The Formula of Offer and Acceptance (*Al-Sighah*):** Clear, unambiguous verbal agreement indicating immediate mutual commitment (*Al-Ijab wal-Qabul*).

### Essential Legal Conditions (*Shurut*):
* **Witnesses and Public Proclamation (*Al-I'lan*):** The marriage must have two upright witnesses (*Shahiday 'Adl*) and be publicly proclaimed. Imam Malik emphasized that a marriage contracted under a mutual pledge of secrecy (*Nikah al-Sirr*) is legally invalid (*Fasid*), because marriage must be openly distinguished from illicit intimacy.

### Conflict Resolution & Dissolution Rulings:
* **The Arbitration Stage (Surah An-Nisa 4:35):** Appointing one arbiter from the husband's family and one from the wife's family to reconcile differences before considering separation.
* **Talaq (Repudiation):** Initiated by the husband under strict conditions (pronounced once while the wife is in a period of purity without intimacy, followed by the three-month *'Iddah* waiting period).
* **Khul' (Separation by Mutual Agreement):** Initiated by the wife by returning the Mahr or agreed sum if she cannot emotionally or spiritually maintain the marriage.
* **Faskh (Judicial Dissolution):** Granted by an Islamic judge or Shari'ah council to dissolve the marriage immediately if the husband causes physical harm, commits abuse, fails to provide basic financial maintenance (*Nafaqah*), or abandons the home.

## 2. Mirath Mathematics: The Architecture of Islamic Inheritance

Islamic inheritance law (*'Ilm al-Fara'id*) was praised by classical scholars as comprising half of all legal knowledge. It operates on an exact mathematical system directly ordained in the Holy Quran (Surah An-Nisa 4:11-12):

| Qur'anic Share Group | Fractional Shares (*Al-Furud*) | Primary Entitled Heirs Under Specified Conditions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Group 1: Halving Sequence** | **1/2 (Half)** | Single daughter (no sons), single full sister, husband (if wife leaves no children). |
| | **1/4 (One-Fourth)** | Husband (if wife leaves children), wife (if husband leaves no children). |
| | **1/8 (One-Eighth)** | Wife or wives shared equally (if husband leaves surviving children). |
| **Group 2: Thirds Sequence** | **2/3 (Two-Thirds)** | Two or more daughters (no sons), two or more full sisters. |
| | **1/3 (One-Third)** | Mother (if deceased leaves no children and only one sibling). |
| | **1/6 (One-Sixth)** | Father (with children), Mother (with children), Grandparent. |

### Key Principles of Mirath:
1. **The Primary Non-Excludable Heirs:** Five individuals can **never** be completely excluded from inheritance under any circumstances: **The Father, The Mother, The Husband or Wife, The Son, and The Daughter**.
2. **Residuary Heirs (*Al-'Asabah*):** Take the remaining balance of the estate after fixed Qur'anic shares are distributed (such as sons and brothers).
3. **The Socio-Economic Context of Mirath:**  
   * In pre-Islamic Arabia and medieval legal codes, women inherited nothing and were frequently treated as inherited property.
   * Islam granted women independent legal financial ownership over 1,400 years ago.
   * *The "Double Share for Sons" Context:* Because Islamic law imposes **zero financial maintenance obligations on women** (a husband or father must pay 100% of housing, food, clothing, medical care, and family needs, while a woman retains 100% of her personal wealth and inheritance), the brother's share carries heavy legal and financial duties for female relatives, while the sister's share remains protected surplus wealth.

## 3. Contemporary Islamic Bioethics & Medical Jurisprudence

Senior students preparing for higher education must understand the consensus resolutions of major contemporary councils, such as the International Islamic Fiqh Academy (IIFA):

### A. The Beginning of Life & Reproductive Ethics:
* **Embryonic Development:** The Quran outlines developmental stages: Drop (*Nutfah*), Clot (*'Alaqah*), Lump (*Mudghah*), followed by bone development and **Ensoulment (*Nafkh al-Ruh*) at 120 days** (Sahih al-Bukhari 3208, Sahih Muslim 2643).
* **Abortion Rulings:**
  * *After 120 days:* Absolutely prohibited under all circumstances, classified as taking a soul, **except solely to save the established life of the mother** (applying the legal maxim: *"The established life takes precedence over the potential life"*).
  * *Before 120 days:* Prohibited as a general rule, with exceptions permitted only for severe, certified medical trauma or fatal fetal anomalies certified by trustworthy specialists.
* **In Vitro Fertilization (IVF):** Permissible between legally married husband and wife using their own genetic gametes during a valid marriage. Third-party surrogacy, donor sperm, or donor eggs are strictly forbidden because they violate the preservation of lineage (*Hifdh al-Nasl*).

### B. The End of Life: Brain Death and Euthanasia:
* **Brain Death:** The International Islamic Fiqh Academy resolved that the irreversible, complete cessation of all brain-stem functions certified by medical specialists constitutes legal death in Islam, permitting the withdrawal of artificial ventilators.
* **Prohibition of Active Euthanasia:** Assisted suicide or lethal injection is classified as intentional homicide. Human life belongs to Allah alone.
* **Palliative Care:** Withholding futile, painful artificial interventions when death is inevitable and imminent is permissible, focusing on comfort, dignity, and pain management.

### C. Organ Donation:
* Major contemporary fiqh councils (including IIFA and Al-Azhar) permit **organ donation** after verified death, as well as living donation of paired organs (such as a single kidney or liver lobe), based on Surah Al-Ma'idah (5:32):  
  *“And whoever saves a life, it is as though he has saved all of humanity.”*  
  *Essential Conditions:* The donor or next of kin must consent freely; no commercial sale or financial transaction may occur; and living donation must not endanger the donor's own life or health.
