## 1. Family Fiqh (*Fiqh al-Usrah*): The Sanctuary of Marriage (*Nikah*)

In contemporary secular culture, marriage is often reduced to a temporary civil contract or romantic consumer arrangement. In Islam, marriage is a **sacred covenant (*Mithaqan Ghalidha*)** designed to cultivate spiritual tranquility (*Sakinah*), affection (*Mawaddah*), and mutual mercy (*Rahmah*) (Surah Ar-Rum 30:21).

### The Four Pillars of Nikah in Maliki Jurisprudence:
1. **The Consent of the Two Spouses (*Al-Ijab wal-Qabul*):** Mutual, uncoerced consent and formal offer and acceptance.
2. **The Bridal Gift (**Mahr**):** An obligatory gift given by the groom directly and exclusively to the bride as her unshared personal property.
3. **The Bride's Guardian (**Wali**):** Who safeguards her legal interests, conducts background checks on character, and shields her from exploitation.
4. **Public Proclamation (**Al-I'lan**) with Two Upright Witnesses:**  
   *The Maliki Distinction:* Imam Malik ruled that a marriage conducted in secret, even if sworn with two witnesses sworn to confidentiality, is **legally invalid (*Fasid*)**. Marriage must be proclaimed openly to the community to distinguish lawful union from illicit intimacy!

### Conflict Resolution & Dissolution Rulings:
* **The Arbitration Stage (Surah An-Nisa 4:35):** Appointing one arbiter from the husband's family and one from the wife's family to reconcile differences.
* **Talaq (Repudiation):** Initiated by the husband under strict conditions (pronounced once while the wife is in purity without intimacy, followed by the 3-month *'Iddah* waiting period).
* **Khul' (No-Fault Separation):** Initiated by the wife by returning the Mahr or agreed sum if she cannot emotionally or spiritually maintain the marriage.
* **Faskh (Judicial Dissolution):** Granted by an Islamic judge or Shari'ah council to dissolve the marriage immediately if the husband causes physical harm, commits abuse, fails to provide basic financial support (*Nafaqah*), or abandons the home.

## 2. Mirath Mathematics: The Architecture of Islamic Inheritance

Islamic inheritance law (*'Ilm al-Fara'id*) was praised by classical scholars as comprising half of all legal knowledge. It operates on an exact mathematical system directly ordained in the Holy Quran (Surah An-Nisa 4:11–12):

```
       ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
       │             THE SIX QUR'ANIC FRACTIONAL SHARES         │
       │                        (Al-Furud)                      │
       └───────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘
              ┌────────────────────┴────────────────────┐
              ▼                                         ▼
         Group 1: Halving                     Group 2: Thirds
         • 1/2 (Half)                         • 2/3 (Two-Thirds)
         • 1/4 (One-Fourth)                   • 1/3 (One-Third)
         • 1/8 (One-Eighth)                   • 1/6 (One-Sixth)
```

### Key Principles of Mirath:
1. **The Primary Non-Excludable Heirs:** Five individuals can **never** be disinherited under any circumstances: **The Father, The Mother, The Husband or Wife, The Son, and The Daughter**.
2. **Residuary Heirs (*Al-'Asabah*):** Take the remaining balance of the estate after fixed Qur'anic shares are distributed (e.g. sons and brothers).
3. **The Socio-Economic Wisdom of Mirath:**  
   * In 7th-century pre-Islamic Arabia and medieval Europe, women inherited nothing and were themselves treated as inherited property!
   * Islam granted women independent legal financial ownership 1,400 years ago.
   * *The "Double Share for Sons" Context:* Because Islamic law imposes **zero financial obligations on women** (a husband must pay 100% of housing, food, clothing, medical care, and children's needs, while a wife retains 100% of her personal wealth and inheritance), the brother's share carries heavy social and financial obligations for female relatives, while the sister's share is 100% pure surplus savings!

## 3. Contemporary Islamic Bioethics & Medical Jurisprudence

Senior students preparing for university must master the resolutions of the International Islamic Fiqh Academy (IIFA) regarding modern biomedical technology:

### A. The Beginning of Life & Reproductive Ethics:
* **Embryonic Development:** The Quran outlines the stages: Drop (*Nutfah*), Clot (*'Alaqah*), Lump (*Mudghah*), Bones clothed in flesh, and **Ensoulment (*Nafkh al-Ruh*) at 120 days** (4 months).
* **Abortion Rulings:**
  * *After 120 days:* Absolutely prohibited under all circumstances, classified as homicide, **except solely to save the mother's established life** (applying the legal maxim: *"The established life takes precedence over the potential life"*).
  * *Before 120 days:* Prohibited except for severe, certified medical reasons (e.g. fatal congenital abnormalities or severe maternal medical trauma).
* **In Vitro Fertilization (IVF):** Permissible between legally married husband and wife using their own genetic gametes during a valid marriage. Third-party surrogacy, donor sperm, or donor eggs are unanimously forbidden as they violate lineage preservation (*Hifdh al-Nasl*).

### B. The End of Life: Brain Death and Euthanasia:
* **Brain Death:** The International Islamic Fiqh Academy resolved that the irreversible, complete cessation of all brain-stem functions constitutes legal death in Islam, permitting the withdrawal of mechanical ventilators.
* **Prohibition of Active Euthanasia:** Assisted suicide or lethal injection is classified as intentional murder. Human life belongs to Allah, not the patient or doctor.
* **Palliative Care:** Withholding futile, painful artificial interventions when death is imminent is permissible, focusing on comfort and pain relief.

### C. Organ Donation:
* Major fatwa councils (including IIFA and Al-Azhar) permit **organ donation** after death, as well as living donation of paired organs (e.g. a single kidney or liver lobe), based on Surah Al-Ma'idah (5:32):  
  *“And whoever saves a life, it is as though he has saved all of humanity.”*  
  *Conditions:* The donor or family consents freely; no commercial sale or financial profit occurs; the living donor's own health is not severely endangered.
