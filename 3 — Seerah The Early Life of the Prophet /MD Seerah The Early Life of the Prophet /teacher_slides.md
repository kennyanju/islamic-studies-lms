# Presentation Slides: Seerah & Fiqh — Early Life, Revelation, and the Legacy of Madinah
## A Guide for Teachers (Maliki Fiqh & Ash'ari Aqidah)

---

# Slide 1: Welcome & Unit Overview
## The Early Seerah & The Foundations of Knowledge
*   **Subject:** Islamic Studies (Seerah & Fiqh)
*   **Fiqh School:** Maliki (*Madhhab*)
*   **Creed School:** Ash'ari
*   **Core Focus:** The early life of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, the dawn of revelation, the hardship of early dawah, and the compilation of this legacy in Madinah by Imam Malik.
*   **Key Themes:**
    *   Makkah before Islam & the Prophet’s ﷺ character.
    *   The Cave of Hira and the Nature of *Wahy* (Revelation).
    *   The Quiet Years & the Early Believers.
    *   Kindness in Ta'if and the Character of Mercy.
    *   Madinah & Imam Malik: Preserving the Sunnah through *'Amal* (Practice).

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Welcome & Objective:** Welcome the students and present this unit. Explain that Seerah is not just history; it is the study of how the Prophet ﷺ lived, which forms the basis of our faith and practice.
*   **Pedagogical Note:** Explain that we will connect the historical events (*Seerah*) to our theology (*Aqidah*) and jurisprudence (*Fiqh*). Learning a specific school (Maliki) and creed (Ash'ari) helps students build systematic religious literacy.
*   **Discussion Starter:** Ask: *"Why do we study the Prophet's ﷺ life before he received revelation? How does his early life prepare us to trust his message?"*

---

# Slide 2: Makkah Before Islam — The Sacred Sanctuary
## The City of the Ka'bah
*   **Geography:** A dry valley surrounded by barren mountains in the Hijaz region of Arabia.
*   **The Haram (Sanctuary):** 
    *   Makkah was established around the Ka'bah, built by Prophet Ibrahim (as) and Ismail (as).
    *   A place of peace: fighting was historically forbidden in the sanctuary.
*   **Trade Hub:** Located along key caravan routes linking Yemen (south) and Syria (north).
*   **The Tribe of Quraysh:** The custodians of the Ka'bah and the dominant political force in the city.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Contextualizing Makkah:** Explain the physical layout of Makkah. It had no agriculture; its survival depended entirely on trade and the pilgrimage season.
*   **The Sanctuary:** Highlight that despite the paganism, the Arabs still respected the sanctity of the Haram, a remnant of the teachings of Ibrahim (as).
*   **Language Tip:** Explain *Haram* (sacred boundary/sanctuary) and its root meaning: something protected and set apart.

---

# Slide 3: Jahiliyyah — The Age of Ignorance
## Society, Beliefs, and Idols
*   **Jahiliyyah:** Meaning the "Age of Ignorance"—referring to spiritual and moral darkness, not lack of intelligence.
*   **Shirk (Idolatry/Polytheism):**
    *   The Ka'bah, originally built for the worship of Allah alone, was filled with over 360 idols.
    *   Pagan Arabs believed in Allah as the Supreme Creator but worshipped idols as intermediaries (*shufa'a'*).
*   **Social Injustices:**
    *   Tribalism: Might makes right; no centralized law or justice system.
    *   Exploitation of the weak, orphans, and women.
    *   The practice of female infanticide (*wa'd al-banat*).

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Explaining Jahiliyyah:** Clarify that the Arabs of Jahiliyyah were highly intelligent, poetic, and organized in trade, but they lacked divine guidance and moral boundaries.
*   **The Mechanics of Shirk:** Help students understand that the pagans did not think the stone idols created the heavens. They thought the idols were "close" to Allah and could pass prayers to Him.
*   **Discussion:** How does worshipping created things (like money, fame, or statues) lead to social injustice?

---

# Slide 4: [Aqidah Flag] The Fitrah and the Hanifs
## The Light of Intellect and Monotheism
*   **Fitrah (Natural State):** The innate human disposition to recognize the oneness of Allah.
*   **The Hanifs (Monotheists):**
    *   Individuals in Makkah who rejected idols and stayed true to the monotheism of Ibrahim (as) before Islam began.
    *   Examples: Waraqah ibn Nawfal and Zayd ibn 'Amr ibn Nufayl.
*   **Ash'ari Creed Point — Sound Intellect (*Aql*):**
    *   Ash'ari scholars emphasize that even before a Messenger arrives, human intellect (*'Aql*) can recognize that the universe must have a single, wise Creator.
    *   Paganism was a corruption of the *Fitrah* and a failure of sound logical reasoning.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Ash'ari Aqidah Integration:** Explain that according to Ash'ari theology, the intellect is capable of realizing the existence of a Creator, but the specific details of how to worship Him (*Shari'ah*) can only be known through revelation.
*   **The Hanifs:** Emphasize that Zayd ibn 'Amr used to stand by the Ka'bah and say: *"O Quraysh! None of you is upon the religion of Ibrahim except me."* He refused to eat meat slaughtered for idols.
*   **Concept Check:** What is the difference between what we can discover with our mind (*'Aql*) and what we must receive through a Prophet (*Sam'iyyat*)?

---

# Slide 5: The Noble Family of the Prophet ﷺ
## The Lineage of Banu Hashim
*   **The Clan:** Banu Hashim, a respected and noble branch of the Quraysh tribe.
*   **The Grandfather:** Abdul Muttalib, the chief of Makkah, who rediscovered the Well of Zamzam.
*   **The Father:** Abdullah ibn Abdul Muttalib, who passed away before the Prophet ﷺ was born.
*   **The Mother:** Aminah bint Wahb, a noble woman of Madinah (Yathrib) descent.
*   **Birth of the Prophet ﷺ:** Born on the 12th of Rabi' al-Awwal in the Year of the Elephant (approx. 570 CE).

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Lineage:** Emphasize that Allah chose the Prophet ﷺ from the most noble family to ensure his message would not be easily dismissed due to low social status.
*   **Orphanhood:** Discuss the wisdom of the Prophet ﷺ being born an orphan. He did not grow up under the political training of a father, so his wisdom was purely from Allah.
*   **Pronunciation Tip:** Teach the students how to write and pronounce *Banu Hashim* and *Abdul Muttalib* correctly.

---

# Slide 6: Early Childhood — The Desert of Banu Sa'd
## Foster Care and the Opening of the Chest
*   **Halimah al-Sa'diyyah:** The noble desert woman who fostered the young Muhammad ﷺ.
*   **Arab Custom:** Sending infants to the desert to learn pure Arabic, build physical strength, and escape city diseases.
*   **Blessings (*Barakah*):** Halimah’s family experienced abundant milk, healthy crops, and thriving livestock from the moment they took Muhammad ﷺ.
*   **The Cleansing of the Heart (*Shaqq al-Sadr*):**
    *   Angels split open the chest of young Muhammad ﷺ, removed a black portion (the source of Satan's whispers), and washed his heart with Zamzam in a golden basin.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Shaqq al-Sadr Event:** Explain that this was a physical and spiritual preparation. It guaranteed that the Prophet's ﷺ heart was perfectly pure, entirely protected from the whisperings (*waswasah*) of Shaytan.
*   **Ash'ari Aqidah Integration:** Under Ash'ari theology, all Prophets are protected (*ma'sum*) from sin and spiritual impurity. This physical event is a sign (*Mu'jizah*) of that divine protection.
*   **Discussion:** Why do you think growing up in the quiet desert, away from the busy city, is good for a child's heart and mind?

---

# Slide 7: Years of Grief and Uncle's Care
## The Double Orphan
*   **Loss of Aminah (Age 6):** His mother passed away at Abwa' while returning from visiting family in Yathrib (Madinah).
*   **Loss of Abdul Muttalib (Age 8):** His grandfather, who loved him dearly, passed away, leaving him in the care of his uncle.
*   **Abu Talib's Guardianship:**
    *   Though not wealthy, Abu Talib treated Muhammad ﷺ like his own son.
    *   Muhammad ﷺ worked as a shepherd (*Ra'i*) to help support his uncle's family.
*   **The Lesson of Humility:** The Prophet ﷺ later said: *"There was no Prophet except that he shepherded sheep."*

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **The Shepherd's Role:** Discuss why shepherding is the perfect training for a future leader (patience, protection, constant observation, managing a flock without force).
*   **Empathy:** Help students connect with the emotional weight of being a double orphan by age 8. The Prophet ﷺ grew up understanding the pain of the vulnerable.
*   **Concept Check:** What character traits are built when someone has to work hard from a young age?

---

# Slide 8: The Character of Al-Amin
## The Trustworthy and Truthful Youth
*   **Al-Sadiq (The Truthful):** He never told a lie, even in play or jest.
*   **Al-Amin (The Trustworthy):** The people of Makkah trusted him with their most valuable possessions.
*   **Hilf al-Fudul (The League of the Virtuous):**
    *   At around age 20, Muhammad ﷺ participated in an alliance formed to protect foreign traders and the weak from oppression in Makkah.
    *   He ﷺ later said: *"I witnessed a pact in the house of Abdullah ibn Jud'an that I would not exchange for red camels. If I were called to it in Islam, I would respond."*

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Al-Amin Concept:** Emphasize that his enemies later called him a sorcerer or poet, but *never* called him a liar. His character was bulletproof.
*   **Hilf al-Fudul:** Use this pact to explain social justice in Islam. Even before Islam, there were noble traits in human society. Islam validates and perfects good ethics.
*   **Reflection:** How does being known as "truthful" before presenting a message make people react to it?

---

# Slide 9: Rebuilding the Ka'bah
## The Wisdom of a Peacemaker
*   **The Dispute:** When the Prophet ﷺ was 35, a flood damaged the Ka'bah. The tribes rebuilt it but fought over who would place the Black Stone (*Al-Hajar al-Aswad*) in its corner.
*   **The Threat of War:** The chiefs prepared to fight, dipping their hands in blood to seal their oaths.
*   **Muhammad's ﷺ Solution:** 
    *   He asked for a cloak, placed the Black Stone in the center, and had a leader from each major tribe hold an edge of the cloak to lift it together.
    *   He ﷺ then took the Stone with his own noble hands and placed it in its position.
*   **Result:** Absolute peace and tribal unity achieved through brilliant intellect (*Fatanah*).

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Pedagogical Note:** Explain *Fatanah* (intellect/wisdom), which is one of the necessary attributes of all Prophets in Ash'ari theology. A Prophet must be highly intelligent to guide humanity and resolve disputes.
*   **The Black Stone:** Explain that the stone is from Paradise, but it is not worshipped; it is simply a symbol of the Ibrahimic covenant.
*   **Visualizing:** Have students describe the scene of the cloak to understand the layout and the brilliance of the solution.

---

# Slide 10: Marriage to Khadijah bint Khuwaylid
## A Partnership of Love and Support
*   **Khadijah (ra):** A wealthy, noble businesswoman known as *Al-Tahirah* (The Pure One) for her outstanding character.
*   **The Business Journey:** She hired Muhammad ﷺ to lead her caravan to Syria after hearing of his absolute honesty.
*   **The Miracle on the Journey:** Her servant, Maysarah, witnessed clouds shading Muhammad ﷺ from the hot sun and saw his exceptional dealings.
*   **The Proposal:** Impressed by his character, Khadijah (ra) proposed marriage. He was 25 and she was 40 (according to the famous historical reports).
*   **The Legacy:** She was his sole wife until she passed away, and the mother of all his surviving children except Ibrahim.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Khadijah’s Status:** Emphasize the high status of women in business and society that Khadijah (ra) represented, countering misconceptions about pre-Islamic women.
*   **The Relationship:** Highlight that their marriage was built on mutual respect and spiritual compatibility, which would prove essential when revelation descended.
*   **Language Tip:** Introduce the title *Radiya Allahu 'anha* (May Allah be pleased with her).

---

# Slide 11: The Cave of Hira
## Seclusion and the Pursuit of Truth
*   **Tahannuth (Spiritual Seclusion):** As he approached age 40, Muhammad ﷺ felt a deep need to withdraw from the idolatry and chaos of Makkah.
*   **The Location:** Jabal al-Nur (The Mountain of Light), in a tiny cave called Hira, overlooking Makkah.
*   **The Practice:** He would spend consecutive nights in prayer, contemplation, and reflecting on the Creator, bringing minimal food and water.
*   **The Fitrah Reaching Maturity:** This practice showed his heart was completely clean and ready to receive the heavy weight of prophecy.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Meaning of Tahannuth:** Explain that *tahannuth* means worship according to the remnants of the monotheistic Ibrahimic way.
*   **Pedagogical Link:** Discuss the spiritual benefit of quiet reflection (*tafakkur*). In our noisy world, taking time away from screens and distractions is essential for our relationship with Allah.
*   **Concept Check:** Why was the cave the perfect place for preparation rather than a busy marketplace?

---

# Slide 12: The First Revelation — "Iqra!"
## The Dawn of Prophethood
*   **The Event:** In the month of Ramadan, during the Night of Qadr (*Laylat al-Qadr*), Angel Jibril (as) appeared in his true form.
*   **The Command:** Jibril squeezed the Prophet ﷺ tightly and said, *"Iqra!"* (Read/Recite!).
*   **The Response:** The Prophet ﷺ replied, *"Ma ana bi-qari'"* (I am not a reader/reciter).
*   **The Squeezes:** Jibril squeezed him three times until he was completely exhausted, then recited the first five verses of Surah al-'Alaq:
    > *"Recite in the name of your Lord who created..."* (Qur'an 96:1-5)

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **The Squeezes:** Explain that the intense physical squeeze was to show the Prophet ﷺ that this was not a dream, a hallucination, or a whisper of his own mind, but an objective, physical reality.
*   **The Prophetic State:** The Prophet ﷺ was *Ummi* (unlettered), which is a miracle. His ability to recite the Quran without reading or writing proves the text came from Allah, not himself.
*   **Discussion:** Why is it significant that the very first word of the final revelation was "Read" or "Recite"?

---

# Slide 13: [Aqidah Flag] The Nature of Wahy (Revelation)
## Ash'ari Theology of Allah's Speech
*   **What is Wahy?** The direct, secure communication from Allah to His Prophets.
*   **The Ash'ari Understanding of Kalam (Speech):**
    *   Allah's attribute of Speech (*Kalam*) is eternal and uncreated (*Qadim*), existing without letters, sounds, or language.
    *   **The Arabic Qur'an:** The physical letters we write in the Mus-haf and the sounds we recite are created expressions (*'ibarah*) of Allah’s eternal, uncreated attribute of Speech.
*   **Refutation of Deviance:** This protects us from anthropomorphism (attributing human vocal cords or physical voice to Allah) while maintaining the sacredness and divinity of the Qur'an.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Ash'ari Aqidah Integration:** Spend time on this slide. It is a core Ash'ari creed point. Explain that just as Allah's Knowledge is eternal and doesn't look like human thoughts, His Speech is eternal and doesn't look like human sound waves.
*   **Simplifying for Students:** Use the analogy of a thought: You have an idea in your mind (it has no sound or language yet). When you speak it in English or Arabic, the words express that inner thought. Similarly, the Arabic Quran is the perfect, miraculous expression of Allah's eternal, soundless Speech.
*   **Key Question:** Why must we believe the Quran is not just a human book written by Muhammad ﷺ?

---

# Slide 14: Comfort at Home & Waraqah's Witness
## The Role of Support and Prophetic Proofs
*   **The Trembling Prophet ﷺ:** The Prophet ﷺ ran home, shivering with fear, and cried to Khadijah: *"Zammiluni! Zammiluni!"* (Cover me! Cover me!).
*   **Khadijah’s Assurance:** She comforted him with logic, saying:
    > *"Never! By Allah, He will never disgrace you. You keep good relations with family, help the poor, serve guests, and assist those struck by calamity."*
*   **Waraqah ibn Nawfal’s Testimony:**
    *   Waraqah, a Christian monotheist, recognized the event: *"This is the Namus (Angel of Revelation) who came to Musa."*
    *   He warned: *"I wish I were young when your people drive you out."* The Prophet ﷺ was shocked: *"Will they drive me out?"* Waraqah replied: *"Yes, no man came with what you have brought except that he was persecuted."*

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Character as Proof:** Khadijah (ra) used his ethical character as proof that Allah would not allow him to be harmed or deceived by a devil. Good character is a shield against spiritual confusion.
*   **Waraqah’s Wisdom:** Explain that Waraqah recognized the universal pattern of prophethood: truth is always resisted by those who want to maintain their unjust power.
*   **Discussion:** Why did Khadijah go to a scholar (Waraqah) for validation? What does this teach us about the role of knowledge?

---

# Slide 15: The Earliest Believers
## The Pillars of the Early Community
*   **Khadijah bint Khuwaylid (ra):** The first of all creation to believe. She gave her wealth, emotional support, and life for the message.
*   **Abu Bakr al-Siddiq (ra):** The first free adult male. The closest friend of the Prophet ﷺ, known for his immediate belief without hesitation.
*   **Ali ibn Abi Talib (ra):** The first youth (aged around 10). He lived in the Prophet's ﷺ home and accepted Islam immediately.
*   **Zayd ibn Harithah (ra):** The first freed slave. He chose to stay with the Prophet ﷺ rather than return to his biological father.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Diversity of Early Believers:** Note that the first four Muslims represented different segments of society: a noble woman, a wealthy merchant, a young child, and a freed slave. Islam was for everyone from day one.
*   **Ali’s Age:** Highlight to the students that Ali (ra) accepted Islam when he was their age (10 years old). A young person's commitment to truth is powerful and valid.
*   **Concept Check:** Why was the immediate belief of those closest to the Prophet ﷺ the strongest evidence of his truthfulness? (Answer: Because those who live with you know your real character).

---

# Slide 16: The Quiet Years — Al-Da'wah al-Sirriyyah
## The Secret Building of the Ummah
*   **Duration:** The first three years of the message.
*   **Strategy:** The Prophet ﷺ invited those he trusted deeply, avoiding public conflict while the community was tiny.
*   **Dar al-Arqam:**
    *   The house of a young companion, Al-Arqam ibn Abi al-Arqam, located near Mount Safa.
    *   It served as the first secret school of Islam.
    *   Here, the Prophet ﷺ taught the Quran, purified their hearts, and explained the prayers.
*   **The Early Prayers:** The early Muslims would slip away into the valleys surrounding Makkah to pray in secret, avoiding pagan mockery.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Tactical Wisdom:** Explain that the secret phase was not due to fear of death, but to protect the seed of the Ummah until it was strong enough to survive the storm of persecution.
*   **Education First:** Emphasize that the first activity of the Muslim community was study and spiritual purification. Action without knowledge is dangerous.
*   **Pronunciation Tip:** Teach the students the name *Dar al-Arqam* and its significance as the template for Islamic schools today.

---

# Slide 17: Hardship and Persecution
## The Trial of Faith
*   **The Public Call:** After three years, Allah commanded the Prophet ﷺ to proclaim the message openly.
*   **The Backlash:** Quraysh realized Islam threatened their social hierarchy, economic power, and idol worship.
*   **Boycotts and Torture:**
    *   The weak and enslaved Muslims (like Bilal, Sumayyah, and Yasir) were brutally tortured.
    *   The Prophet ﷺ himself was mocked, had camel intestines thrown on his back while praying, and was boycotted in the valley of Abu Talib for three years, leading to extreme starvation.
*   **Patience (*Sabr*):** Throughout this, the Muslims did not fight back physically; they built inner strength.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Meaning of Sabr:** Explain that *Sabr* in the Maliki and Ash'ari tradition is not passive weakness; it is the active restraint of the soul from reacting with anger or despair, keeping firm on obedience to Allah.
*   **First Martyr:** Mention Sumayyah (ra), the first martyr of Islam, who refused to give up her faith despite the torture of Abu Jahl.
*   **Discussion:** Why does Allah allow His beloved servants to go through hardship? How does this clean our intentions?

---

# Slide 18: The Journey to Ta'if
## Seeking a Haven of Hope
*   **The Year of Grief (*'Am al-Huzn*):** In the 10th year of Prophethood, both Abu Talib (his political protector) and Khadijah (his emotional pillar) passed away.
*   **The Decision:** With persecution peaking in Makkah, the Prophet ﷺ travelled 60 miles on foot to the mountain city of Ta'if to seek protection and invite the tribe of Thaqif to Islam.
*   **The Rejection:** 
    *   The three leaders of Ta'if mocked him brutally. One said: *"If Allah sent you, I will tear down the drapes of the Ka'bah!"*
    *   They set the street children and slaves to pelt him with stones as he left the city.
    *   His sandals were soaked with his own blood, and his companion Zayd (ra) was wounded trying to shield him.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Emotional Weight:** Bring the narrative to life. Show the vulnerability of the Prophet ﷺ walking 60 miles up steep mountain roads, only to be rejected and physically beaten by children.
*   **Patience:** Emphasize that despite this physical and emotional low point, he did not lose faith or become angry at Allah.
*   **Reflection:** How do we deal with rejection when we try to do something good?

---

# Slide 19: [Aqidah Flag] The Prayer of Ta'if
## Absolute Servantship ('Ubudiyyah) and Qadar
*   **The Supplication:** Resting in an orchard, bleeding, the Prophet ﷺ made a historic prayer:
    > *"O Allah! To You alone I complain of my weakness, my lack of resources, and my insignificance before mankind... If You are not angry with me, I do not care..."*
*   **Ash'ari Aqidah Point — Contentment with Decree (*Rida bil-Qadar*):**
    *   The Prophet's ﷺ prayer shows that a believer’s concern is only Allah's pleasure, not worldly success.
    *   Hardship is decreed by Allah out of ultimate wisdom (*Hikmah*). It does not mean Allah is angry with us.
    *   **Iftiqar (Neediness):** True faith is realizing our absolute poverty and neediness before Allah's self-sufficiency.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Aqidah Analysis:** Break down the text of the prayer of Ta'if. Point out that the Prophet ﷺ did not ask for the destruction of his enemies; he complained *only* of his own weakness to Allah.
*   **Concept Check:** What is the difference between complaining *about* Allah (which is forbidden) and complaining *to* Allah (which is the essence of worship)?
*   **Key Lesson:** The Prophet ﷺ taught us that success is measured by our obedience, not by whether people accept us.

---

# Slide 20: The Angel of the Mountains
## Mercy Over Retribution
*   **The Divine Response:** Allah sent Jibril (as) along with the Angel of the Mountains (*Malak al-Jibal*).
*   **The Offer:** The Angel of the Mountains said: *"O Muhammad! If you wish, I will crush them between the two volcanic mountains of Makkah (*Al-Akhshabayn*)."*
*   **The Prophetic Decision:** 
    *   The Prophet ﷺ, demonstrating his role as a "Mercy to the Worlds" (*Rahmah lil-'Alamin*), refused:
    *   *"No, rather I hope that Allah will bring from their descendants those who worship Allah alone, associating nothing with Him."*
*   **Outcome:** Decades later, Ta'if became a stronghold of Islam, producing great heroes of the faith.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Character Analysis:** Contrast the absolute power the Prophet ﷺ was offered (the destruction of his enemies) with his choice of mercy and hope. This is the ultimate proof of his prophethood.
*   **Pedagogical Activity:** Ask students: *"How would a normal king or ruler react if they were beaten and then given the power to destroy their attackers? Why did the Prophet ﷺ choose differently?"*
*   **Legacy:** Mention that his hope was fulfilled. The descendants of Ta'if did indeed become Muslims.

---

# Slide 21: Madinah — The Sanctuary of Knowledge
## The Home of Migration (Dar al-Hijrah)
*   **The Shift:** After the hardships of Makkah, Allah opened the hearts of the people of Yathrib (later named Madinatu-n-Nabi — the City of the Prophet).
*   **The Hijrah (Migration):** The migration marked the birth of the first Islamic state, where the Shari'ah was fully implemented and lived.
*   **The Sacred City:**
    *   The Prophet ﷺ declared Madinah a sanctuary (*Haram*) just like Makkah.
    *   He prayed: *"O Allah, make us love Madinah as much as we love Makkah, or even more."*
*   **The Living Sunnah:** For ten years, the Sahabah lived, breathed, and practiced Islam directly under the Prophet's ﷺ daily guidance.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Transition to Madinah:** Explain that Madinah became the laboratory of the Sunnah. Every movement, ruling, and custom of the Prophet ﷺ was observed and copied by thousands of companions.
*   **Contrast:** Makkah was the place of building creed (*Aqidah*); Madinah was the place of building community life, law, and practice (*Fiqh*).
*   **Discussion:** Why is the environment we live in so important for practicing our faith?

---

# Slide 22: Imam Malik ibn Anas
## The Star of the Scholars (Najm al-'Ulama')
*   **Who was Imam Malik?** Born in Madinah in 93 AH (passed 179 AH), less than a century after the Prophet's ﷺ passing.
*   **The School of Madinah:** 
    *   Imam Malik studied under the children and grandchildren of the Sahabah.
    *   He was the undisputed leader of Fiqh in Madinah, known as the Imam of the Abode of Hijrah (*Imam Dar al-Hijrah*).
*   **The Hadith of the Scholar of Madinah:**
    *   The Prophet ﷺ said: *"Soon people will beat the flanks of camels seeking knowledge, and they will not find anyone more knowledgeable than the scholar of Madinah."* (Sunan al-Tirmidhi).
    *   Many classical scholars stated this refers directly to Imam Malik.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Imam Malik's Profile:** Emphasize that Imam Malik did not travel outside Madinah except for Hajj. He stayed in the city because he believed that the pure knowledge of the Prophet ﷺ was preserved there.
*   **Historical Timeline:** Help students visualize the timeline. The Prophet passed in 11 AH. Imam Malik was born in 93 AH. This is a very short chain of transmission (only one or two generations between him and the companions).
*   **Pronunciation:** *Malik ibn Anas* (pronounce correctly, emphasize the short 'a' in Malik).

---

# Slide 23: [Fiqh Flag] The Practice of the People of Madinah
## Amal Ahl al-Madinah as a Source of Law
*   **The Core Maliki Principle:** The collective practice of the scholars and people of Madinah (*'Amal Ahl al-Madinah*) is a primary source of Islamic law.
*   **Why is it authoritative?**
    *   Madinah was home to thousands of Companions.
    *   They passed their daily religious actions (how they prayed, did business, gave charity) down to their children.
    *   Imam Malik argued: A practice practiced by thousands of people, inherited generation from generation, is stronger than a single, isolated written report (*Hadith Ahad*).
*   **Example — Sadl (Draping the Arms in Prayer):**
    *   In the Maliki school, praying with the arms resting naturally at the sides (*Sadl*) is the primary, well-known practice because this was the visible, continuous action of the people of Madinah.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Fiqh Literacy Integration:** This is a vital slide for understanding the Maliki school. Explain the difference between a written text (Hadith) and a living action (*'Amal*).
*   **The Cloak Analogy:** If you want to know how a traditional cloak is worn, do you read one instruction manual, or do you look at how everyone in the town where the cloak was invented wears it? Imam Malik chose to look at the town.
*   **Concept Check:** Why does the Maliki school value *'Amal* so highly compared to other schools?

---

# Slide 24: Al-Muwatta' & Reverence for the Prophet ﷺ
## The First Book of Hadith and Law
*   **Al-Muwatta' (The Approved/Paved Path):** 
    *   Compiled by Imam Malik. It is the first major written book combining Hadith, rulings of the Sahabah, and the practice of Madinah.
    *   Imam Al-Shafi'i said: *"No book has appeared on earth after the Book of Allah that is closer to the truth than the book of Malik."*
*   **Imam Malik’s Extreme Reverence:**
    *   He would never ride a horse or camel in Madinah, saying: *"I am ashamed before Allah to tread with the hoof of a beast upon the earth wherein is buried the Messenger of Allah ﷺ."*
    *   When asked about a Hadith, he would take a ritual bath (*Ghusl*), put on his finest clothes, apply perfume, and burn incense out of respect for the Prophet's ﷺ words.

**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Character of Imam Malik:** Emphasize that seeking Islamic knowledge requires adab (manners) and respect. Knowledge is a light from Allah, and it does not enter a disrespectful heart.
*   **Muwatta' Context:** Explain that *Al-Muwatta'* took 40 years for Imam Malik to refine and compile. He was extremely cautious about what he wrote down.
*   **Discussion:** How does putting on our best clothes and preparing our space help us focus when we study the words of the Prophet ﷺ or the Quran today?

# Slide 25: Summary & Key Lessons
## Walking the Path of Truth and Legacy
*   **Early Makkah:** The Prophet’s ﷺ pristine character (*Al-Amin*) was the foundation that made the community trust his message.
*   **Cave of Hira:** Revelation (*Wahy*) is the eternal Speech of Allah, sending a light of guidance to save us from the darkness of *Jahiliyyah*.
*   **Ta'if:** Hardship is faced with patience (*Sabr*) and absolute reliance on Allah’s decree (*Qadar*), responding with mercy.
*   **Madinah & Imam Malik:** The preservation of Islam relies on both written Hadith and the living practice (*'Amal*) of the community that directly inherited it.
