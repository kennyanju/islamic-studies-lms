## Section 1: Hilf al-Fudul: The League of the Virtuous

When Muhammad ﷺ was around twenty years old, an unjust merchant of Quraysh purchased valuable merchandise from a visiting foreign trader from Yemen, but refused to pay him his rightful price. The foreign merchant climbed the hill of Abu Qubays in Makkah, calling out in verse to the people of the city to help him secure justice.

Several noble clans gathered at the house of 'Abdullah ibn Jud'an and formed a solemn treaty known as **Hilf al-Fudul (The League of the Virtuous)**. They made an oath before Allah:  
> *"Whenever anyone in Makkah, whether a resident citizen or a visiting foreigner, is wronged, we will stand united by their side until their full rights and property are restored."*

Young Muhammad ﷺ participated in this pact. Decades later as the Messenger of Allah, he remembered this treaty with deep admiration and stated:  
> *"I witnessed in the house of Ibn Jud'an a treaty more beloved to me than red camels. If I were invited to a similar pact in Islam, I would certainly respond."* (Recorded in *Al-Sirah al-Nabawiyyah* of Ibn Hisham, Sahih chain).

This noble event teaches us that standing for justice, defending the weak against bullies, and protecting the rights of foreign visitors are sacred duties for every Muslim everywhere.

## Section 2: Business Integrity & Marriage to Sayyidah Khadijah (RA)

In Makkah lived a respected, wealthy, and intelligent businesswoman named **Sayyidah Khadijah bint Khuwaylid**. Hearing of Muhammad's reputation for honesty, she hired him to oversee her trading caravan traveling to the markets of Sham (Syria).

She sent her trusted assistant, **Maysarah**, to travel alongside him and observe his character. Maysarah returned to Makkah with enthusiastic praise:
* Muhammad ﷺ was completely honest in weighing goods, setting prices, and recording every transaction.
* He spoke with gentleness, never raised his voice in disputes, and never used deceptive sales techniques.
* Maysarah noticed miraculous signs of divine favor: wherever Muhammad traveled across the arid desert, clouds provided shade from the scorching sun.

Deeply moved by his noble character and impeccable honesty, Sayyidah Khadijah proposed marriage to him through her friend Nafisah. Muhammad ﷺ accepted, and they entered into a blessed marriage that lasted twenty-five years. Sayyidah Khadijah was his greatest emotional anchor, wise advisor, and devoted partner.

## Section 3: The Wisdom of Placing the Black Stone

When Muhammad ﷺ was thirty-five years old, flash floods caused severe structural cracks in the ancient walls of the Ka'bah. The tribes of Quraysh agreed to rebuild it, using exclusively lawful, pure money (*Halal Tayyib*) and refusing any profits derived from gambling, usury, or injustice.

The clans worked together until the walls reached the level of the **Black Stone (Al-Hajar al-Aswad)**. At that moment, a bitter quarrel erupted. Every tribal chieftain wanted the sole honor of lifting the Black Stone into place. Arguments escalated, swords were unsheathed, and the clans were on the verge of civil war.

An elder named Abu Umayyah ibn al-Mughirah proposed a peaceful solution: *"Let the first person who enters through the gateway of the sanctuary decide the matter between you."*

Moments later, Muhammad ﷺ walked into the courtyard. When the chieftains saw him, they smiled with profound relief and exclaimed:  
> **هَذَا الأَمِينُ، رَضِينَا بِهِ**  
> *"This is Al-Amin (The Trustworthy)! We are pleased with his judgment!"*

With brilliant diplomatic wisdom, Muhammad ﷺ took off his outer cloak and laid it flat on the ground. He placed the Black Stone in the center and called the leader of every clan to take hold of an edge of the cloth. Together, all the chieftains lifted the stone simultaneously to the wall. Then, Muhammad ﷺ lifted the stone with his own hands and placed it into its permanent corner socket. Conflict was avoided, all tribes shared in the honor, and peace was preserved.

## Section 4: Cave Hira and the First Revelation (Iqra'!)

As he approached forty years of age, Muhammad ﷺ felt an increasing need for solitude away from the idol worship, tribal rivalry, and social corruption of Makkah. He would walk to **Mount Nur** (The Mountain of Light) and climb to the quiet sanctuary of **Cave Hira**, carrying provisions of water and barley flour, spending days and nights in prayer and contemplation of the Creator.

One night in the sacred month of Ramadan (610 CE), the Archangel **Jibril** appeared before him in divine majesty. Jibril embraced him with firm, powerful warmth and commanded:  
> **اقْرَأْ** : *"Read!"*  
> Muhammad ﷺ replied with humble truthfulness: *"I cannot read."*

After embracing him three times, Jibril transmitted the opening verses of the Holy Qur'an:

> **اقْرَأْ بِاسْمِ رَبِّكَ الَّذِي خَلَقَ • خَلَقَ الْإِنسَانَ مِنْ عَلَقٍ • اقْرَأْ وَرَبُّكَ الْأَكْرَمُ • الَّذِي عَلَّمَ بِالْقَلَمِ • عَلَّمَ الْإِنسَانَ مَا لَمْ يَعْلَمْ**  
> *Iqra' bi-smi Rabbika alladhi khalaq. Khalaqa al-insana min 'alaq. Iqra' wa Rabbuka al-akram. Alladhi 'allama bil-qalam. 'Allama al-insana ma lam ya'lam.*  
> *"Read in the name of your Lord who created. Created humanity from a clinging clot. Read, and your Lord is the Most Generous, who taught by the pen, taught humanity that which they knew not."* (Surah Al-'Alaq 96:1-5; Sahih al-Bukhari 3, Sahih Muslim 160, Sahih).

Trembling with awe and physical exhaustion, the Prophet ﷺ rushed home to his beloved wife, crying: *"Zammiluni! Zammiluni!"* (Wrap me up! Cover me!). Sayyidah Khadijah wrapped him in a cloak, listened to his experience, and comforted him with immortal words of faith:  
> *"Never! By Allah, Allah will never disgrace you! For you uphold family ties, bear the burdens of the weak, help the poor, honor guests, and assist those afflicted by misfortune."* (Sahih al-Bukhari 3, Sahih).

## Section 5: Curriculum Anchors in Practice

### 1. Qur'an and Tafsir Anchor: Surah Ash-Sharh (94:1-8)
Allah addresses His beloved Messenger:

> **أَلَمْ نَشْرَحْ لَكَ صَدْرَكَ • وَوَضَعْنَا عَنكَ وِزْرَكَ • الَّذِي أَنقَضَ ظَهْرَكَ • وَرَفَعْنَا لَكَ ذِكْرَكَ**  
> *Alam nashrah laka sadrak. Wa wada'na 'anka wizrak. Alladhi anqada zahrak. Wa rafa'na laka dhikrak.*  
> *"Did We not expand for you your chest? And We removed from you your burden which weighed upon your back, and We raised high for you your mention."* (Surah Ash-Sharh 94:1-4).

**Classical Tafsir (*Tafsir Ibn Juzayy al-Kalbi*):** Imam Ibn Juzayy explains that expanding the chest refers to filling the Prophet's heart with divine wisdom, light, patience, and prophetic strength, enabling him to deliver the final guidance to all humanity with perseverance and mercy.

### 2. Tajweed Rule in Action: The Echo Letters (Qalqalah)
*Qalqalah* produces a distinct bouncing tone when one of the five letters of Qalqalah (**ق ط ب ج د**) carries a Sukun:  
* Examples from Surah Al-'Alaq (96:1-5):
  * **اقْرَأْ** (*Iq-ra'*): The letter Qaf carries a Sukun and bounces clearly.
  * **خَلَقَ** (*khalaq*): When stopping at the end of verse 1, the Qaf bounces with *Qalqalah Kubra*.
  * **الْقَلَمِ** (*al-qalam*): When reciting through, the letters flow smoothly without a bounce.

### 3. Classical Text Anchor: *Al-Sirah al-Nabawiyyah* of Ibn Kathir
Imam 'Imad al-Din Ibn Kathir (d. 774 AH) writes regarding the Prophet's early life:
> *"The Messenger of Allah ﷺ grew up among his people possessing every virtue of character, truthfulness of tongue, gentleness of disposition, and generosity of spirit, until all recognized him as Al-Amin. He was beloved to young and old, and his wisdom brought unity where division threatened."*

### 4. Prophetic Hadith Anchor: Hadith 16 of Nawawi's 40
A man came to the Prophet ﷺ and requested: *"Counsel me."* The Prophet ﷺ replied:

> **لَا تَغْضَبْ**  
> *"Do not become angry."*  
> The man repeated his request several times, and the Prophet ﷺ repeated each time: *"Do not become angry."* (Sahih al-Bukhari 6116, Sahih).

**Family Application:** When disagreements happen between siblings or friends, anger often makes the problem worse. By remembering how Muhammad ﷺ resolved the crisis of the Black Stone with calm wisdom, we learn to pause, breathe, control our temper, and find fair solutions that bring people together.
