## Part A: Multiple Choice Questions

### 1. How does the biographical documentation of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ differ fundamentally from that of founders of ancient classical religions?
(A) It is documented through contemporary divine text (the Quran) and rigorous unbroken chains of verified eyewitness transmission (*Isnad*)
(B) It relies on anonymous folklore written down several centuries after his death
(C) It was reconstructed purely from archaeological stone monuments in southern Yemen
(D) It was invented by imperial decree in Baghdad

### 2. In classical Islamic historiography, what is the methodological difference between Hadith compilation and historical *Maghazi* chronicles?
(A) Hadith compilations were written in Persian, while Maghazi were in Syriac
(B) Historical Maghazi required stricter narrator verification than legal Hadith
(C) Maghazi chronicles were restricted only to medical observations
(D) Hadith collections required strict chain validation for legal rulings and creed, whereas Maghazi permitted broader narrative continuity for non-legislative history

### 3. What is the unanimous theological consensus (*Ijma'*) regarding the Prophet's infallibility (*'Ismah*) in delivering revelation (*Al-Tabligh*)?
(A) He was prone to seasonal forgetfulness in legal matters
(B) He was divinely protected by Allah from any error, addition, omission, or deceit in delivering the message
(C) Infallibility began only after the migration to Madinah
(D) It applied only to the Arabic grammar of the Quran, not the legal rulings

### 4. What geopolitical factor characterized the Arabian peninsula in the 6th century compared to the Byzantine and Sasanian empires?
(A) It was heavily occupied and governed by Roman imperial legions
(B) It was completely submerged under ocean waterways
(C) It existed as an uncolonized geopolitical setting, free from imperial domination, preserving linguistic eloquence and chivalric autonomy
(D) It possessed a unified central monarchy ruling from San'a

### 5. Who among the pre-Islamic *Hunafa'* famously refused to worship idols, rejected meat slaughtered for false gods, and died before the open call of Islam?
(A) Zayd ibn 'Amr ibn Nufayl
(B) Abu Jahl ibn Hisham
(C) Umayyah ibn Khalaf
(D) Al-Walid ibn al-Mughirah

### 6. Why does the secular "epilepsy/hallucination" hypothesis fail to explain the historical reality of the Prophet's revelation?
(A) Because people with neurological conditions in antiquity were immediately exiled from Arabia
(B) Because early Arabians did not know what neurological illnesses were
(C) Because neurological seizures produce cognitive degradation and memory collapse, whereas the Quran displays peerless linguistic structure and rigorous legal legislation over 23 years
(D) Because the Prophet ﷺ refused to speak Arabic in public gatherings

### 7. How do Islamic historians refute the allegation that Prophet Muhammad ﷺ copied biblical scriptures from the monk Bahira?
(A) Bahira was illiterate and unable to speak any Semitic languages
(B) The city of Busra had no Christian inhabitants
(C) Bahira converted to Zoroastrianism shortly after the caravan left
(D) The encounter occurred during a brief childhood caravan stop when the Prophet was around 9-12 years old, witnessed openly by his uncle and fellow travelers

### 8. What profound ethical deduction did Sayyidah Khadijah (RA) make to reassure the Prophet ﷺ after the descent of revelation in Cave Hira?
(A) She told him that his wealth would buy him military protection from Quraysh
(B) She swore by Allah that He would never disgrace him because he maintained family ties, helped the poor, honored guests, and stood for truth
(C) She advised him to consult Roman physicians in Antioch
(D) She suggested that he hide the incident from all family members forever

### 9. In Ibn Khaldun's *Muqaddimah*, how is the tribal solidarity (*'Asabiyyah*) of the Arabian desert analyzed in relation to the Prophetic mission?
(A) As a sociological cohesion that, once purified by divine guidance, provided the communal strength and mobility for the truth to be defended
(B) As an evil force that prevented Islam from expanding outside Makkah
(C) As an economic banking cartel that funded early mosques
(D) As a poetic rhythm used exclusively in camel racing

### 10. What does Qadi 'Iyad declare regarding fabricated narratives in his critical study *Al-Shifa*?
(A) He accepts them as authentic historical proof without question
(B) He recommends reciting them during solar eclipses
(C) He claims that angels permitted Satan to write certain verses of the Quran
(D) He decisively proves they are uncorroborated, baseless fabrications, completely lacking sound Isnad and contradicting fundamental creed

## Part D: Senior Seminar Case Study & Reflection

11. Contrast the epistemological rigor of Islamic *Isnad* methodology with the historical-critical standards applied to ancient Greco-Roman or biblical texts.
12. A secular university lecturer claims: "Muhammad's revelations were simply the subconscious expression of 7th-century Arabian social reform." How does the internal textual evidence of the Quran dismantle this assertion?
13. Analyze Sayyidah Khadijah's immediate response to the first revelation: Why does her ethical deduction represent a reasoned proof of the Prophet's sincerity?
14. Using Qadi 'Iyad's *Al-Shifa*, formulate the theological boundaries of Prophetic *'Ismah* regarding temporal matters versus legislative conveyance.

### Reflection Rubric
* **Full Understanding:** Student demonstrates sophisticated grasp of classical historiography, analyzing the structural differences between Isnad criticism and external textual criticism, Khadijah's ethical epistemology, and the orthodox definition of 'Ismah in legislative conveyance.
* **Basic Understanding:** Student gives general observations without distinguishing between historical chronicles and legal hadith standards.

## Teacher Answer Key
1. A - Islamic Seerah is grounded in contemporary revelation and unbroken chains of eyewitness transmission (*Isnad*).
2. D - Classical scholarship distinguished between rigorous standards for creed/law and broader narrative flexibility in historical chronicles.
3. B - Complete consensus affirms absolute infallibility in conveying the divine message (*Tabligh*).
4. C - Arabia was an independent, uncolonized cultural matrix preserving linguistic eloquence and chivalric autonomy.
5. A - Zayd ibn 'Amr lived and died as a pure monotheist following the Abrahamic legacy before prophethood.
6. C - Intellectual coherence and sustained legislative mastery over 23 years preclude neurological pathology.
7. D - A brief childhood encounter during a public caravan journey cannot account for 23 years of transcendent revelation.
8. B - Khadijah deduced that divine benevolence does not disgrace a person dedicated to truth, family ties, and charity.
9. A - Ibn Khaldun explains that divine guidance utilized social solidarity (*'Asabiyyah*) to establish and protect the community.
10. D - Qadi 'Iyad demonstrated that such stories lacked sound Isnad and violated fundamental theology.
