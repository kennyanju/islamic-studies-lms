# Assessment: Prophethood, Revelation, and Madinah's Legacy
## 20 Questions for 13-Year-Olds (Intermediate Fiqh & Aqidah)

This assessment is designed to test your conceptual understanding, vocabulary, and analytical skills regarding the early Seerah, Ash'ari theology, and Maliki jurisprudence.

### Part 3: Short Answer & Theological Analysis
13. **Answer:** The intellect (*'Aql*) can recognize that the universe must have a single Creator through observation and logic. However, the details of how to worship Him (*Shari'ah*) and what is morally pleasing or displeasing to Him can only be known through revelation (*Sama'*).
14. **Answer:** The private phase was designed to build a strong, resilient foundation of early Muslims through deep spiritual and educational training at Dar al-Arqam. Declaring it publicly immediately would have caused the Quraysh to crush the small community before it had the strength and organization to survive.
15. **Answer:** Instead of choosing revenge, which he was fully capable of executing, the Prophet ﷺ chose to look beyond his own physical suffering and pray for the potential guidance of their future children. This shows his care for the long-term guidance of humanity rather than temporary victory.
16. **Answer:** An isolated written report (*Hadith Ahad*) is transmitted by a single line of individuals and is prone to errors, forgetting, or isolation. In contrast, the *'Amal* of Madinah is a continuous, collective action practiced by thousands of people in the same city where the Prophet ﷺ lived and died. It acts as a living, mass-transmitted (*Mutawatir*) tradition that cannot be easily fabricated or changed.

### Part 4: Open-Ended Critical Analysis / Application (Evaluation Rubric)
17. **Refutation Guide:** A student should explain that a Prophet is not chosen randomly, nor is he ordinary. Under the Ash'ari creed, Prophets are created with the most perfect characters and intellects, and are protected from birth by *'Ismah* (infallibility) from sins and repelling traits. They possess necessary attributes like *Al-Sidq* and *Al-Fatanah* to ensure the message is delivered flawlessly and trusted.
18. **Analysis Guide:** *Asabiyyah* meant supporting one's tribe right or wrong, leading to injustice and war. The Prophet's ﷺ early character showed that he valued absolute justice (joining the *Hilf al-Fudul* to protect foreigners) and unified the community (having all tribal chiefs lift the Black Stone cloak). Islam replaces *Asabiyyah* with brotherhood based on faith, piety, and objective justice under Allah's law.
19. **Analysis Guide:** Hardship is not a sign of Allah's anger or abandonment. Since everything happens by *Qadar* (divine decree) with ultimate wisdom, a believer's goal is *Ubudiyyah* (pleasing Allah). By focusing on Allah's pleasure rather than worldly outcomes, personal failures or hardships are reframed as opportunities for spiritual growth and patience.
20. **Analysis Guide:** Today, knowledge is often treated casually, as information to consume on screens. Imam Malik's *Adab* teaches us that sacred knowledge is a light that requires physical and spiritual respect (Wudu, clean clothes, perfume, focused posture). Students can implement this by preparing their space for study, speaking of the Prophet ﷺ with respect, and seeking to implement the knowledge they gain.
