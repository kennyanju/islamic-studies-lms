## Section 1: Standing for Truth: The Call from Mount Safa

For three years following the initial revelation in Cave Hira, the message of Islam was conveyed quietly to trusted family members, close friends, and sincere seekers of truth. Then, Allah revealed the divine instruction to make the call public:

> **وَأَنذِرْ عَشِيرَتَكَ الْأَقْرَبِينَ**  
> *"And warn your closest kinfolk."* (Surah Ash-Shu'ara 26:214).

Prophet Muhammad ﷺ climbed Mount Safa in the center of Makkah and called out the traditional alarm: *"Ya Sabaha!"* (an ancient Arabian cry alerting the tribe to an impending dawn attack). The clans of Quraysh rushed together.

Standing atop the rocks, the Prophet ﷺ asked the gathering:  
> *"If I were to tell you that enemy horsemen were in the valley at the foot of this mountain preparing to attack you, would you believe me?"*  
> Without hesitation, they replied: *"Yes! We have never experienced anything from you except truthfulness (Al-Amin)."*  
> The Prophet ﷺ then proclaimed: *"Then know that I am a warner sent to you before the arrival of a severe punishment. Say: La ilaha illallah (There is no god but Allah) and you will prosper!"*

His uncle Abu Lahab stood up in furious arrogance, clapping his hands and shouting: *"May you perish for the rest of this day! Is it only for this that you have gathered us?"* In response to this public malice, Allah revealed **Surah Al-Masad**, exposing the eternal failure of arrogance and wealth when turned against divine truth.

## Section 2: Steadfastness Under Persecution: The First Martyrs of Islam

As the message gained momentum among the poor, enslaved, and youth of Makkah, the chieftains of Quraysh initiated a systematic campaign of persecution against vulnerable believers who possessed no tribal protection:

* **Sayyidina Bilal ibn Rabah:** An enslaved youth from Abyssinia whose master, Umayyah ibn Khalaf, dragged him at noon into the scorching desert, placing a massive red-hot boulder upon his chest. Through the agonizing pain, Bilal proclaimed a single triumphant word: **"Ahad! Ahad!"** (Allah is One! Allah is One!). Seeing his steadfast faith, Sayyidina Abu Bakr purchased Bilal and emancipated him for the sake of Allah.
* **The Family of Yasir:** Yasir, his wife Sumayyah, and their son 'Ammar were subjected to brutal torture under the Makkan sun. The Prophet ﷺ passed by them with tears of sorrow, comforting them:  
  *"Patience, O family of Yasir! For indeed your promised destination is Paradise!"* (Recorded in *Al-Mustadrak* 5646, graded Sahih by al-Hakim and al-Dhahabi).  
  Sayyidah Sumayyah became the **first martyr in Islamic history**, refusing to renounce Tawhid even when executed by Abu Jahl.

## Section 3: The Migration to Abyssinia (Habashah)

Seeing his followers endure relentless brutality, the Prophet ﷺ directed a group of eighty-three men and nineteen women to seek refuge across the Red Sea in the Christian Kingdom of Aksum (Abyssinia):  
> *"Travel to the land of Abyssinia, for therein is a righteous King (The Negus / Al-Najashi) in whose realm no person is wronged."* (Recorded in *Al-Sirah al-Nabawiyyah* of Ibn Hisham).

Quraysh dispatched two skilled envoys, 'Amr ibn al-'As and 'Abdullah ibn Abi Rabi'ah, loaded with expensive leather gifts to bribe the King's generals and demand the immediate return of the Muslim refugees.

King Negus summoned the Muslims to his royal court. Sayyidina Ja'far ibn Abi Talib stood before the throne and delivered a masterwork of eloquence:  
> *"O King! We were an ignorant people: worshipping idols, eating carrion, committing immoral deeds, severing ties of kinship, and the strong among us devoured the weak. We remained in this state until Allah sent to us a Messenger from our own people, whose lineage, truthfulness, trustworthiness, and purity we knew. He commanded us to worship Allah alone, speak truth, fulfill trusts, uphold family ties, be good to neighbors, and abstain from bloodshed. When our people persecuted us to force us back to idol worship, we sought refuge in your country, choosing your hospitality over all others, hoping we would not be wronged in your presence, O King!"*

When Negus asked to hear some of the revelation, Ja'far recited the opening verses of **Surah Maryam** recounting the miraculous conception of Prophet 'Isa (Jesus) by the Virgin Mary. King Negus wept until his beard was soaked, and his bishops wept until their scrolls were wet with tears. The Negus declared:  
> *"Indeed, this message and that which Jesus brought emanate from the very same lantern of divine light! Go in safety; by God, I will never surrender you to them!"*

## Section 4: The Three-Year Boycott of Shi'b Abi Talib

Frustrated by the royal protection in Abyssinia and the conversions of powerful leaders like Hamzah ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib and 'Umar ibn al-Khattab, the Quraysh signed a comprehensive social and economic boycott against the entire clan of Banu Hashim. They hung the formal parchment inside the Ka'bah:
* No one in Makkah was permitted to trade with Banu Hashim, marry their women, sell them food, or speak with them.
* For three agonizing years (from the 7th to the 10th year of Prophethood), the Prophet ﷺ, his family, and companions were confined to the arid mountain ravine of Shi'b Abi Talib. They survived on tree leaves, wild grass, and dried animal skins while children cried from starvation.
* **The Miraculous End of the Boycott:** As recorded in *Sirat Ibn Ishaq* and *Ibn Hisham*, Allah sent wood-boring insects (termites) that consumed the entire parchment hung inside the Ka'bah, devouring every sentence of injustice, boycott, and oppression, leaving intact only the opening divine phrase: **"Bismika Allahumma"** (In Your Name, O Allah). The boycott collapsed in failure.

## Section 5: The Year of Sorrow and the Valley of Ta'if

In the tenth year of Prophethood, the Prophet ﷺ faced two profound personal tragedies:
1. **Abu Talib:** His protective uncle who had shielded him from Makkan assassination plots passed away.
2. **Sayyidah Khadijah (RA):** His beloved wife, first believer, and constant pillar of comfort passed away just weeks later. This year became known in Seerah as **The Year of Sorrow (*'Am al-Huzn*)**.

With tribal protection removed, the Prophet ﷺ walked fifty miles across rugged mountainous terrain to the mountain city of **Ta'if**, hoping the leaders of the Thaqif tribe would accept Islam and grant protection to the believers. Instead, the chiefs of Ta'if insulted him and incited street mobs and youths to line the mountain path, pelting him with stones until blood soaked his sandals.

Taking shelter in a quiet orchard, the Prophet ﷺ raised his hands in a supplication preserved in classical Seerah chronicles (*Sirat Ibn Hisham* and *Al-Mu'jam al-Kabir* of al-Tabarani):  
> *"O Allah! To You alone I complain of my weakness, my lack of resources, and my helplessness before people. O Most Merciful of those who show mercy! You are the Lord of the oppressed, and You are my Lord... So long as You are not angry with me, I have no concern!"*

Allah dispatched Angel Jibril alongside the Angel of the Mountains, who greeted the Prophet ﷺ and said:  
*"If you wish, I will bring together the two mountains of Makkah (Al-Akhshabayn) upon them!"* (Sahih al-Bukhari 3231, Sahih Muslim 1795, Sahih).  
With boundless prophetic mercy, Muhammad ﷺ replied:  
> *"No! Rather, I hope that Allah will bring forth from their descendants people who will worship Allah alone without associating any partners with Him!"*

## Section 6: Madinah: Preserving the Prophetic Model (Imam Malik and Al-Muwatta')

The trials of Makkah paved the way for the establishment of the prophetic community in Madinah. Decades later, the sacred practices of the Prophet ﷺ were meticulously recorded in Madinah by the great scholar **Imam Malik ibn Anas (93-179 AH)**.

Imam Malik authored **Al-Muwatta'**, the earliest surviving legal and hadith collection in Islamic history. Imam Malik recognized that the daily, living communal practice of the people of Madinah (*'Amal Ahl al-Madinah*) was a mass-transmitted inheritance of the Sunnah directly from the Companions. Learning the Seerah enables us to see how the patience of Makkah blossomed into the legal and spiritual civilization of Madinah.

## Section 7: Curriculum Anchors in Practice

### 1. Qur'an and Tafsir Anchor: Surah Ash-Shu'ara (26:214-216)
Allah commands:

> **وَاخْفِضْ جَنَاحَكَ لِمَنِ اتَّبَعَكَ مِنَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ**  
> *Wakhfid janahaka liman ittaba'aka mina al-mu'minin.*  
> *"And lower your wing of humility and mercy to those believers who follow you."* (Surah Ash-Shu'ara 26:215).

**Classical Tafsir (*Tafsir al-Qurtubi*):** Imam al-Qurtubi explains that "lowering the wing" is an Arabic metaphor for boundless gentleness, compassion, and protective care. The Prophet ﷺ embodied this humility with his Companions, enduring hunger and hardship alongside them during the three-year boycott.

### 2. Tajweed Rule in Action: Major vs Minor Echo (Qalqalah Kubra vs Sughra)
*Qalqalah* is divided into two distinct levels based on where the pause occurs:
1. **Qalqalah Sughra (Minor Echo):** Occurs when a Qalqalah letter carries a Sukun in the middle of a word or sentence. It is light and fluid. Example: **أَنقَضَ** (*anqada*) or **يَجْعَلْ** (*yaj'al*).
2. **Qalqalah Kubra (Major Echo):** Occurs when stopping at the end of a verse or word on a Qalqalah letter. It is pronounced with a firm, resounding bounce. Example: In Surah Al-Masad (111:1), stopping on the letter Ba in **وَتَبَّ** (*wa tabb*), and in verse 2 stopping on **كَسَبَ** (*kasab*).

### 3. Classical Text Anchor: *Al-Shifa* of Qadi 'Iyad
Qadi 'Iyad writes on the Prophet's patience:
> *"Consider the forbearance of the Prophet ﷺ: his people rejected him, drove him out, boycotted his family, stoned him at Ta'if, and yet when given the authority to destroy them, he chose mercy, patience, and hope for their future generations. Such forbearance cannot belong to ordinary mortals; it is the sublime perfection of Prophethood."*

### 4. Prophetic Hadith Anchor: Hadith 18 of Nawawi's 40
The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> **اتَّقِ اللَّهَ حَيْثُمَا كُنْتَ، وَأَتْبِعِ السَّيِّئَةَ الْحَسَنَةَ تَمْحُهَا، وَخَالِقِ النَّاسَ بِخُلُقٍ حَسَنٍ**  
> *"Be conscious of Allah wherever you are, follow a bad deed with a good deed and it will wipe it out, and interact with people with good character."* (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1987, Hasan Sahih).

**Family Application:** When we face difficult moments, misunderstandings at school, or teasing from others, we remember the Prophet's response at Ta'if. Responding to hostility with patience and prayer transforms our character and earns divine reward.
