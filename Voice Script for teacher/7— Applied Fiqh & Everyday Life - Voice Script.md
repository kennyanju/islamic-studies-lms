# Voice Script: Applied Fiqh & Everyday Life
**Topic:** Halal and Haram food choices, modesty and Awrah, everyday manners, trade ethics, and Hajj & Umrah.  
**Audience:** Parents/Teachers presenting to children aged 10-13.  
**Creed/Law Framework:** Ash'ari Aqidah & Maliki Fiqh.  

---

## Slide 1: Welcome & Course Overview
* **Slide Summary:** Introduces the focus on everyday applied fiqh, turning regular actions into spiritual habits.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Smile, stand comfortably, and greet the students with high energy]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Assalamu Alaikum, everyone! Welcome back. Today, we are beginning a super practical unit called **Applied Fiqh and Everyday Life**. 
  
  Now, when we hear the word *Fiqh*, we might think of dry rules or thick textbooks. But *Fiqh* actually means 'deep understanding.' It is our guide on how to live! 
  
  In this unit, we will cover how we make choices about what is Halal and Haram in food, how we dress with modesty, the manners of eating, greeting, and visiting, how we do business honestly, and the sacred journey of Hajj and Umrah. 
  
  Our goal is to move from just knowing the rules to living them as spiritual habits. Every action—even eating a snack or playing a game—can be worship if we do it with the right intention! Let's start this journey!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A smartphone user guide: it's not there to lock you out of the phone; it's there to show you how to use all the cool features and apps correctly so you get the best experience.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the primary goal of this course? (Answer: To transform our daily actions into spiritual habits of worship)."

## Slide 2: Fiqh as an Act of Worship (Creed Context)
* **Slide Summary:** Understanding Allah as the ultimate Lawgiver (Al-Hakim) and the Ash'ari view of Husn and Qubh.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with a clear, reflective tone]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "First, let's understand the theology of law. Who has the right to decide what is allowed and what is forbidden? 
  
  In our creed, we believe that Allah alone is **Al-Hakim**—The Lawgiver. Only He can declare things *Halal* or *Haram*. Our human intellect, or *'Aql*, is incredibly powerful, but its job is to *discover* and understand Allah's laws, not create them. 
  
  In the Ash'ari school, we learn that moral goodness—**Husn**—and badness—**Qubh**—are defined by Allah's revelation, not just our feelings or what society thinks is trendy. When we follow Fiqh, we are directly submitting to Allah's wise design!"
* **Concept Analogy:** Rules of gravity: we didn't invent gravity, and we can't vote to change it. We study gravity using our minds to make sure we don't fall off buildings. Similarly, we study Fiqh to navigate Allah's moral universe safely.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Who is the ultimate Lawgiver in Islam? (Answer: Allah, Al-Hakim)."

## Slide 3: The Scale of Actions (*Al-Aḥkām al-Khamsah*)
* **Slide Summary:** Reviewing the five categories of actions and how to transform permissible acts (Mubah).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Show five fingers; speak methodically]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "To guide our choices, Islamic law places every human action into one of **five categories**, called **Al-Ahkam al-Khamsah**:
  
  1. **Obligatory** (*Fard* / *Wajib*): You must do it (like daily prayers).
  2. **Recommended** (*Mandub* / *Sunnah*): Rewarded for doing, no sin for skipping.
  3. **Permissible** (*Mubah* / *Halal*): Neutral (like eating or playing).
  4. **Disliked** (*Makruh*): Better to avoid, but no punishment for doing.
  5. **Forbidden** (*Haram*): You must avoid it (like stealing).
  
  Here is the secret: most of your day is *Mubah* (neutral). But if you add a clean intention (*Niyyah*), you can transform a neutral habit into a rewarded action! Eating to get strong for prayer makes eating a good deed."
* **Concept Analogy:** A game scoring system: some moves are required (Fard), some give you bonus points (Mandub), some are neutral (Mubah), some lose points (Makruh), and some cause a game-over (Haram).
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "How can we turn a neutral action like sleeping into an act of worship? (Answer: By intending to rest our body to wake up for Fajr prayer)."

## Slide 4: Halal & Haram in Food and Choices
* **Slide Summary:** The default permissibility of food and the definition of Tayyib.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with positivity and appreciation for food]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Allah created a beautiful world for us. In Islamic law, there is a golden rule: *everything is permissible by default* (*Al-Asl fi al-ashya' al-ibahah*), unless Allah explicitly forbids it. 
  
  So, the list of Halal things is huge, and the list of Haram things is very small! 
  
  What is forbidden? Pork, carrion (animals that died naturally), flowing blood, intoxicants like alcohol, and food sacrificed to idols. 
  
  Allah only forbids what is harmful (*Khabā'ith*) to our bodies, minds, or souls. We should also seek **Tayyib**—which means food that is not just legally Halal, but also wholesome, clean, and ethical."
* **Concept Analogy:** A food menu: imagine a giant restaurant menu with hundreds of dishes, where only three items are crossed off as dangerous. That is the ratio of Halal to Haram.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the general default rule for things in this world? (Answer: Everything is permissible by default unless explicitly forbidden)."

## Slide 5: The Fiqh of Slaughtering (*Dhakāh*)
* **Slide Summary:** Anatomical requirements of Dhakah and the Maliki rule on Tasmiyah.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with respect for animal life; emphasize mercy]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "To make land animals lawful to eat, they must go through ritual slaughtering, called **Dhakah**. 
  
  The cut must be swift with a very sharp knife to sever the windpipe, gullet, and the two jugular veins, minimizing any pain. We must say *Bismillah* before the cut.
  
  Here is a specific **Maliki ruling**: saying *Bismillah* is obligatory *only* if you remember. If a slaughterer forgets to say it, the meat is still Halal to eat out of mercy. But if they deliberately refuse to say it, the meat becomes Haram. Other schools have different views, but Malikis take this balanced approach. We must always respect life!"
* **Concept Analogy:** An off-switch: a swift, clean cut acts like a sudden off-switch, stopping any pain signals from reaching the brain, ensuring the animal doesn't suffer.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "In the Maliki school, what is the status of the meat if the slaughterer accidentally forgot to say Bismillah? (Answer: It is still Halal to eat)."

## Slide 6: The Bounty of the Sea: Seafood Rules
* **Slide Summary:** The highly permissive Maliki view on all sea creatures.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with a cheerful, open tone; show flexibility]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "What about seafood? The Quran says: *'Lawful to you is game from the sea...'*
  
  Here is a major **Maliki flag**: our Madhhab has the most open, permissive view on seafood in the world! 
  
  According to Imam Malik, **all** creatures that live exclusively in water are completely Halal. There are no exceptions for their shape or form. Crab, lobster, shrimp, sharks, oysters, and even what they historically called 'sea dogs' or 'sea pigs' (dolphins) are Halal! 
  
  They do not require ritual slaughter, and even dead fish found floating are lawful. Other schools like the Hanafis only allow fish, but our Madhhab is extremely flexible here."
* **Concept Analogy:** An open pass: the sea is treated as a vast, natural bounty where the general rule of permission applies to everything swimming inside it.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Are crabs, lobsters, and oysters Halal to eat in the Maliki school? (Answer: Yes, all water-dwelling creatures are Halal)."

## Slide 7: Modesty (*Ḥayā’*) as a Core Virtue
* **Slide Summary:** Modesty (Haya') as a branch of faith and its application in speech and behavior.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with warmth and sincerity; connect Haya' to the heart]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Modesty—or **Haya'**—is one of the most beautiful virtues in Islam. The Prophet ﷺ said: *'Modesty is a branch of faith.'* 
  
  Haya' is not about feeling bad about yourself. It is a feeling of spiritual shyness and respect before Allah and His creation. 
  
  Because we know Allah is **Al-Raqib** (The Watchful) and **Al-Basir** (The All-Seeing), we naturally want to behave modestly. 
  
  This means modesty in our speech (avoiding bad words), modesty in our behavior (respecting others), and modesty in our dress (covering our *'Awrah*). It is for both boys and girls!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A soft filter: modesty is like a filter on your speech and action, making sure only clean, respectful, and dignified things pass through to the outside.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Is Haya' only about the clothes we wear? (Answer: No, it is also about our speech, behavior, and character)."

## Slide 8: The Dress of Modesty: *‘Awrah* for Men
* **Slide Summary:** Navel-to-knee boundaries and the Maliki division of Awrah Khafifah vs. Mughalladhah.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with clear, practical boundaries for boys]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "What are the physical rules of dress? We use the term **'Awrah** to describe the parts of the body that must be covered in front of others and during prayer.
  
  For men, the general boundary is from the navel to the knee. 
  
  In the **Maliki school**, we have a precise division: the private organs are **'Awrah Mughalladhah** (heavy awrah), and the thighs are **'Awrah Khafifah** (light awrah). The navel and knee themselves are not awrah. 
  
  However, exposing the thighs in public is still strongly disliked (*Makruh*) or forbidden because it violates *Haya'*. For boys, wearing short shorts that show the thighs in public is not acceptable in our Madhhab!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A warning zone: the private parts are the high-security zone (heavy awrah), and the thighs are the buffer safety zone (light awrah) that must still be protected.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "In the Maliki school, are the thighs considered heavy or light 'Awrah for men? (Answer: Light awrah / 'Awrah Khafifah, but exposing them is still disliked/avoided)."

## Slide 9: The Dress of Modesty: *‘Awrah* for Women
* **Slide Summary:** Women's Awrah in public/prayer and the Maliki ruling on exposing feet in Salah.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with respect and clarity regarding women's rulings]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "For women, in front of unrelated men and during prayer, the *'Awrah* is the entire body except the face and the hands from the wrists down. Clothing should be loose and thick, not tight or see-through.
  
  Here is a specific **Maliki nuance** about prayer: a woman's feet are considered light awrah (*'Awrah Khafifah*) in Salah. 
  
  This means if a woman prays with her feet exposed, her prayer is still valid, but it is disliked (*Makruh*). Our school recommends that she cover them, and if she accidentally prayed with them uncovered, it is recommended to repeat the prayer within its time block. It's a balanced position!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A protective wrapping: just like a precious jewel or phone screen is covered in a soft protective case to keep it safe from scratches, modesty covers the body to protect its dignity.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "If a woman prays with her feet exposed, is her prayer valid in the Maliki school? (Answer: Yes, it is valid, but it is Makruh, and recommended to cover them)."

## Slide 10: Modesty in Character and Demeanor
* **Slide Summary:** Lowering the gaze (Ghadd al-Basar) and avoiding pride in dress.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with a focus on self-control]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Covering our physical *'Awrah* is only half the battle. We must also cover our character!
  
  One of the main duties of modesty is **Ghadd al-Basar**—lowering the gaze. This is an obligation for both men and women. It means guarding our eyes from staring at the *'Awrah* of others or looking at inappropriate media. If we see something bad, we look away instantly.
  
  We must also avoid pride in dress—wearing clothes to show off, feel superior to others, or seek fame. Keep your clothes clean, neat, and simple. Arrogance has no place in our hearts."
* **Concept Analogy:** Looking away from a solar eclipse: if you stare at the sun during an eclipse, it will damage your eyes. Similarly, staring at inappropriate things damages the health of your heart.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the Arabic term for guarding the eyes and looking away from inappropriate things? (Answer: Ghadd al-Basar / Lowering the gaze)."

## Slide 11: Manners of Eating: Transforming Fiqh to Habit
* **Slide Summary:** Sunnah etiquettes of eating: Bismillah, right hand, moderation.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Use a warm, practical, everyday tone]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Eating is something we do every day. Let's make it a source of rewards!
  
  Before you eat, wash your hands and make a clean intention: *'I am eating to get strong to worship Allah.'* 
  
  Then, follow these five Sunnah steps:
  1. Say **Bismillah** (In the name of Allah) at the start.
  2. Eat with your **right hand**.
  3. Eat from what is directly in front of you (don't reach across).
  4. Eat in moderation—divide your stomach into a third for food, a third for water, and a third for air.
  5. Say **Alhamdulillah** at the end. 
  Just like that, you get rewarded for eating your lunch!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A charging station: eating is how we plug our body's battery into the wall. By using the Sunnah, we charge both our physical body and our spiritual bank account at the same time.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the rule of thumb for moderation in eating? (Answer: A third of the stomach for food, a third for water, and a third left empty for air)."

## Slide 12: Manners of Greeting: The Key to Love
* **Slide Summary:** Rulings of Salam (initiating vs. responding) and the Sunnah order of greeting.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Smile; speak in a friendly, outgoing tone]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "The ultimate Islamic greeting is: **As-Salamu 'Alaykum wa Rahmatullah wa Barakatuh**—Peace, mercy, and blessings of Allah be upon you.
  
  Saying it first is a highly recommended Sunnah. But responding to it is **obligatory**! 
  
  The Prophet ﷺ gave us a guide for who should greet whom first:
  - The young should greet the elderly.
  - The walking person should greet the sitting person.
  - And the smaller group should greet the larger group.
  
  Saying Salam is a promise that you will not harm the other person with your tongue or hands. It spreads love in our community!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A handshake agreement: saying Salam is like signing a treaty of peace and safety with everyone you meet in your daily life.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Is initiating the Salam obligatory or recommended? What about responding to it? (Answer: Initiating is recommended Sunnah; responding is obligatory)."

## Slide 13: Manners of Visiting: Entering Homes (*Isti’dhān*)
* **Slide Summary:** Seeking permission (Isti'dhan), the 3-time knock rule, and standing to the side.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Act out knocking; speak with respect for privacy]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "When we visit someone's home, we must follow the rules of **Isti'dhan**—seeking permission.
  
  First: knock or ring the bell up to **three times**. If there is no answer, you must leave. Don't keep pounding!
  
  Second: stand to the side of the door, not directly in front of it. This prevents you from looking inside the house when the door opens, protecting their privacy.
  
  Third: when they ask 'Who is it?', say your name clearly. Don't just shout: 'It's me!'
  
  And finally, avoid visiting during rest times like late at night or early morning. Respecting people's homes is a sign of faith."
* **Concept Analogy:** A digital login screen: you enter your username (identify yourself) and password, and if the system doesn't grant access after three attempts, you log off and try again later.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Why should we stand to the side of the door when knocking on a house? (Answer: To protect the privacy of the people inside when they open the door)."

## Slide 14: Introduction to Fiqh of the Marketplace
* **Slide Summary:** Marketplace ethics, mutual consent (Taradin), and major bans (Riba, Gharar).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with an ethical, business-minded tone]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Islam doesn't separate the mosque from the market. The same Allah we worship in prayer is the Allah we must obey when we buy and sell!
  
  Trade (*Tijarah*) is Halal and noble—the Prophet ﷺ was a merchant. But business must follow three rules:
  1. Honesty.
  2. Mutual consent (*Taradin*).
  3. And clear terms.
  
  We are strictly forbidden from **Riba** (usury/interest) because it exploits the poor, and **Gharar** (excessive uncertainty). You cannot sell something that is undefined—like 'the birds in the sky' or a mystery box where you don't know what you are buying. Everything must be transparent."
* **Concept Analogy:** A clear contract: a business deal must be like a transparent glass box where both parties can see exactly what they are getting and agree on the price.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is 'Gharar' in trade? (Answer: Excessive uncertainty or ambiguity in a transaction, which is forbidden)."

## Slide 15: Honesty & Deception in Trade
* **Slide Summary:** The Prophet's ﷺ warning on deception, disclosing defects ('Ayb), and modern fraud.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with serious ethical weight]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "The Prophet ﷺ gave a strict warning: *'Whoever deceives us is not of us.'*
  
  This means if an item has a defect—an **'Ayb**—the seller *must* disclose it. You can never hide the bad parts of a product under the good parts. 
  
  We must also be honest in quantity, using accurate scales. 
  
  And yes, this applies to kids too! Selling a broken toy to a friend, trading video game accounts using lies, or scamming someone online are all forms of forbidden deception. Halal earnings bring barakah; cheating brings ruin."
* **Concept Analogy:** The wet grain story: the Prophet ﷺ put his hand in a pile of grain and felt it was wet underneath. He told the seller: 'Why did you not put the wet grain on top so people could see? Whoever deceives us is not of us!'
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What must a seller do if the product they are selling has a defect ('Ayb)? (Answer: They must disclose it to the buyer)."

## Slide 16: When is a Deal Final? (Khiyar al-Majlis)
* **Slide Summary:** Explaining the Maliki rejection of Khiyar al-Majlis based on the practice of Madinah.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Highlight this distinctive Maliki rule with emphasis]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Here is a famous **Maliki flag** about business: *When is a deal officially final?*
  
  In other schools, they have *Khiyar al-Majlis*—the option to cancel a sale for any reason as long as you are still standing in the shop. 
  
  But the **Maliki school rejects this**. In our Madhhab, the moment you agree on the price and say 'I buy' and 'I sell', the deal is immediately locked and binding (*Lazim*)! You cannot walk around the store and cancel it just because you haven't stepped outside. 
  
  Why? Because Imam Malik saw that the merchants and scholars of Madinah did not practice this cancel option. Their word was their bond. This teaches us to be serious and decisive before we agree to a deal!"
* **Concept Analogy:** Clicking 'Submit Order' online: the moment you click that button, the order is locked in and processed. You can't cancel it just because you are still browsing the website.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "In the Maliki school, when does a sales contract become binding (Lazim)? (Answer: The moment the verbal agreement/exchange is completed, not when you leave the physical space)."

## Slide 17: Hajj & Umrah: The Sacred Journey
* **Slide Summary:** Definitions of Hajj and Umrah, rulings, and the capability condition (Istitā'ah).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with a tone of longing and sacredness]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Now, let's look at the ultimate journey of a Muslim's life: the pilgrimage to Makkah.
  
  **Hajj** is the annual pilgrimage during the sacred month of Dhu al-Hijjah. It is one of the Five Pillars of Islam, obligatory once in a lifetime for those who are able.
  
  **Umrah** is the minor pilgrimage, which can be performed at any time of the year. In the Maliki school, Umrah is a Confirmed Sunnah. 
  
  To go, you must have **Istitā'ah**—capability. This means having the health, safe roads, and enough savings to go without leaving your family in debt or without food."
* **Concept Analogy:** A visa: you can only enter a country if you have the visa and funds. Allah grants a spiritual 'visa' of obligation only to those who have the physical and financial ability.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the difference between Hajj and Umrah? (Answer: Hajj is obligatory once a life during Dhu al-Hijjah; Umrah is a voluntary/Sunnah minor pilgrimage at any time)."

## Slide 18: State of Ihram & Maliki Positions on Preparation
* **Slide Summary:** The state of Ihram, rules, and the Maliki view on Hajj as an immediate duty ('Alā al-Fawr).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with focus; outline the restrictions clearly]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "To start Hajj or Umrah, you must enter a state of sacred consecration called **Ihram**. 
  
  Outwardly, men wear two simple white sheets, and women wear their regular modest clothes. Inwardly, you make the intention and recite the *Talbiyah*: *'Labbayk Allahumma Labbayk...'* 
  
  Once in Ihram, you cannot cut your hair, clip your nails, use perfume, or wear hats (for men). 
  
  Here is a key **Maliki ruling**: Hajj is an **immediate obligation** (*'Ala al-Fawr*). If you have the health and the money, you must go; you cannot delay it to old age. Delaying it without an excuse is a sin!"
* **Concept Analogy:** Logging into a secure testing portal: once you log in (enter Ihram), you cannot close the screen, open other tabs (restrictions), or leave the desk until the test (Hajj) is submitted.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "In the Maliki school, can you delay Hajj to old age if you are wealthy and healthy today? (Answer: No, it is an immediate duty ('Ala al-Fawr))."

## Slide 19: The Rites of Tawaf (Purity and Continuity)
* **Slide Summary:** Circumambulation rules and the Maliki requirement of Wudu continuity.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak methodically; show a circular motion with your hand]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Once in Makkah, we perform **Tawaf**—walking seven times counter-clockwise around the Ka'bah, starting at the Black Stone.
  
  In the **Maliki school**, having **Wudu** is an absolute condition for Tawaf to be valid. 
  
  And we require strict **Continuity** (*Muwalah*). If your Wudu breaks during the fourth round, you cannot just go wash and resume. Your Tawaf is broken! You must go make Wudu and restart the entire Tawaf from round one. This teaches us to maintain focus and respect the purity of this sacred act."
* **Concept Analogy:** A relay race: you must run all seven laps in one continuous go. If you drop the baton or step off the track to wash, you have to restart the race from the starting block.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What happens if a person's Wudu breaks on round five of Tawaf in the Maliki school? (Answer: They must make Wudu and restart the entire Tawaf from round one)."

## Slide 20: Sa'y, Arafat, and Stoning the Jamarat
* **Slide Summary:** Sa'y (Rukn), Wuquf 'Arafah, and the Maliki precision in stoning the Jamarat.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with narrative flow; show precision]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "After Tawaf, we perform **Sa'y**—walking seven times between the hills of Safa and Marwah, commemorating the search of Hajar (ra) for water. In our school, Sa'y is an absolute Pillar (*Rukn*).
  
  Then comes the peak of Hajj: **Wuquf 'Arafah**—standing at Mount Arafat. The Prophet ﷺ said: *'Hajj is Arafat.'* 
  
  Finally, we stone the **Jamarat** pillars representing Satan. Here is a precise **Maliki rule**: the pebbles must land exactly in the basin. If a pebble bounces off and misses the target, it doesn't count. You must throw another one. This teaches us mindfulness and precision in our daily actions!"
* **Concept Analogy:** Archery: it doesn't matter how hard you pull the bow string; if the arrow misses the target board completely, you get zero points and must shoot another arrow to score.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the peak pillar of Hajj? (Answer: Wuquf 'Arafah / Standing at Mount Arafat)."
