# Voice Script: Foundations of Belief
**Topic:** Who is Allah? Names and Attributes, Pillars of Iman, Angels, Holy Books & Prophets, Qadar, and Maliki/Ash'ari Literacy.  
**Audience:** Parents/Teachers presenting to children aged 10-13.  
**Creed/Law Framework:** Ash'ari Aqidah & Maliki Fiqh.  

---

## Slide 1: Introduction to Aqidah & Fiqh
* **Slide Summary:** Introduction to the definitions of Aqidah and Fiqh, and the concept of Madhhabs.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Smile, look at the students, and speak with warmth and energy]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Assalamu Alaikum, everyone! Welcome! I am so excited to start this journey with you. Today, we are going to explore the two most important branches of our relationship with Allah: **Aqidah** (*Creed*) and **Fiqh** (*Jurisprudence*). 
  
  Think of *Aqidah* as the roots of a giant tree. You can't see them, but they anchor the tree to the ground. *Aqidah* is what we believe in our hearts—about Allah, His Angels, and the unseen. 
  
  *Fiqh*, on the other hand, is like the branches, leaves, and sweet fruit of that tree. It is the practical guide of *how* we live—how we perform Wudu, how we pray, and how we treat others. 
  
  We will be learning these through the lens of two classical schools of thought: the **Ash'ari** school of creed and the **Maliki** school of law. Having a school of thought—a *Madhhab*—is like having a map when climbing a mountain. It is a mercy that helps us navigate our faith with deep scholarship!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A tree: Aqidah is the invisible root system holding us firm, and Fiqh is the visible fruit-bearing branches (our actions).
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "If I believe in Angels in my heart, is that Aqidah or Fiqh? What about performing Wudu?"

## Slide 2: Who is Allah? The Core Principle of Tawhid
* **Slide Summary:** Core principles of Tawhid, and the names Al-Wahid and Al-Ahad.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Lower voice tone to create a sense of awe; speak slowly]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Let's start with the most important question in the universe: *Who is Allah?* The answer is summed up in one word: **Tawhid**. 
  
  Tawhid means the uncompromising belief that Allah is One. He is *Al-Wahid* and *Al-Ahad*—The Unique, the absolute One. He has no parents, no children, no partners, and no equals. 
  
  In the Ash'ari school, we believe that the very first obligation of a Muslim who reaches maturity is to get to know Allah. Not just by repeating words, but by using our minds and looking at the creation around us to realize there is a Creator."
* **Concept Analogy:** If you see a beautifully baked cake, you know a baker made it, even if you didn't see them. The universe is our proof of the Creator.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What Surah in the Quran is known as the ultimate summary of Tawhid? (Answer: Surah al-Ikhlas)"

## Slide 3: Knowing Allah through His Necessary Attributes (Sifat al-Wajibah)
* **Slide Summary:** Understanding the 20 necessary attributes of Allah in Ash'ari creed.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Point to the columns on the slide; speak clearly and methodically]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "To know Allah, we learn about His attributes. Ash'ari scholars organized these into 20 necessary attributes called **Sifat al-Wajibah**. 
  
  Some of these attributes are *existential*, like **Wujud**—the fact that Allah exists. Others are *qualitative*, like His power (*Qudrah*), His will (*Iradah*), and His knowledge (*'Ilm*). 
  
  Allah is Alive (*Hayy*), hears everything (*Sami'*), sees everything (*Basir*), and speaks (*Mutakallim*). When we call Allah 'Hearing' or 'Seeing', we must remember His hearing and seeing are not like ours. He doesn't need ears or eyes. He is completely free of any physical limits."
* **Concept Analogy:** Our sight is limited by darkness and walls. Allah's sight is absolute and has no limits.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Does Allah need tools like eyes or ears to see and hear? Why not?"

## Slide 4: Transcending Space and Time (Mukhalafah lil-Hawadith)
* **Slide Summary:** Declaring Allah free of any resemblance to creation (Tanzih).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Use open hand gestures; speak with clarity and conviction]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "This brings us to a beautiful concept called **Mukhalafah lil-Hawadith**. It means that Allah is completely different from created things. 
  
  Allah created space and time. Because He created them, He cannot be contained by them! Allah is not a physical body. He doesn't occupy space. He doesn't sit on a physical chair, and He doesn't live in a physical place. 
  
  As the Quran says in Surah ash-Shura: *'There is nothing like unto Him.'* If you can imagine it in your mind, Allah is different from that!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A game developer creates a digital world. The developer does not live inside the computer monitor; they are completely outside of the game's rules.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "If someone asks 'Where is Allah?', what is the correct way to explain His existence? (Answer: He exists without being limited by a place or direction)."

## Slide 5: The Names of Allah (Al-Asma' al-Husna)
* **Slide Summary:** The 99 Names of Allah and how we apply them in our daily lives.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Show warmth and appreciation]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Allah has revealed ninety-nine beautiful names to us, known as **Al-Asma' al-Husna**. The Prophet ﷺ told us that whoever memorizes and guards these names will enter Paradise. 
  
  But guarding them doesn't just mean repeating them like a list. It means understanding their meanings and calling upon them. 
  
  When you need food or help, you ask **Al-Razzaq** (The Provider). When you make a mistake and feel bad, you turn to **Al-Ghaffar** (The Repeatedly Forgiving). We build our relationship with Allah through these names."
* **Concept Analogy:** Calling a doctor when you are sick, or a teacher when you want to learn; we call upon the specific attribute of Allah that we need in our lives.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Which Name of Allah would you call upon if you were feeling scared and needed protection? (e.g., Al-Hafiz - The Protector)"

## Slide 6: Ash'ari Reading of Sacred Texts: Tafwid and Ta'wil
* **Slide Summary:** Exploring how orthodox Sunni creed reads metaphorical language in the Quran.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak in a scholarly yet accessible tone; use hands to represent a balance]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Sometimes, the Quran uses words like the 'Hand' (*Yad*) of Allah or His 'Face' (*Wajh*). Since we know Allah is not a physical body, how do we understand this? 
  
  Orthodox Sunni scholars have two beautiful, valid paths:
  
  First: **Tafwid** [taf-weed]. This is the path of leaving the meaning to Allah. We say, 'I believe in the word as Allah revealed it, but I know it does not mean a physical body part, and Allah knows best what it truly means.'
  
  Second: **Ta'wil** [ta-weel]. This is the path of metaphorical interpretation. In Arabic, 'hand' can mean power or favor. So we interpret 'Yad' as Allah's Power. Both paths protect us from comparing Allah to humans, which is a major error called *Tashbih*."
* **Concept Analogy:** If someone says 'lend me a hand,' they don't want you to cut off your hand; they want your help. Arabic also uses metaphors this way!
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What do the two paths of Tafwid and Ta'wil agree on? (Answer: That Allah does not have a physical body or parts)."

## Slide 7: The Six Pillars of Iman (Overview)
* **Slide Summary:** Introduction to the Six Pillars of Iman via the Hadith of Jibril.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with enthusiasm and set a structure]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Now let's look at the foundation of our faith: the **Six Pillars of Iman**. 
  
  In the famous Hadith of Jibril, the Angel Jibril came in the form of a man in shining white clothes to ask the Prophet ﷺ about the religion. When he asked about *Iman* (faith), the Prophet ﷺ replied that it is to believe in six things: 
  
  Allah, His Angels, His Books, His Messengers, the Last Day, and Qadar—the Divine Decree, both its good and its evil. 
  
  Remember, *Islam* is about our outer actions, like praying and fasting. *Iman* is about our inner conviction."
* **Concept Analogy:** A building needs pillars to stand. If you take away the pillars, the roof falls. These six beliefs support our entire spiritual life.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the difference between Islam and Iman? (Answer: Islam is outer action, Iman is inner belief)."

## Slide 8: Iman in Allah and His Attributes of Power, Will, and Knowledge
* **Slide Summary:** Understanding how Knowledge, Will, and Power interact.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Use interlocking hand motions or circle gestures]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "In Ash'ari theology, three attributes explain how everything happens in our universe.
  
  First: **Al-'Ilm** (Divine Knowledge). Allah knows everything eternally. Nothing is hidden from Him.
  
  Second: **Al-Iradah** (Divine Will). Allah chooses to make things happen in a specific way, at a specific time.
  
  Third: **Al-Qudrah** (Divine Power). Allah brings things into existence out of nothing.
  
  Think about a leaf falling from a tree. Allah's *'Ilm* knows it will fall at exactly 3:00 PM. His *Iradah* wills it to fall at 3:00 PM rather than 4:00 PM. His *Qudrah* is the power that makes the leaf drop. They all work together perfectly."
* **Concept Analogy:** A builder who knows the blueprint (Knowledge), decides to start building today (Will), and uses their physical energy to build the wall (Power).
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Does Allah learn new things as time passes? (Answer: No, His Knowledge is eternal and complete)."

## Slide 9: Angels: Creation, Nature, and Characteristics
* **Slide Summary:** The creation, nature, and lack of free will of Angels.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with a sense of wonder]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Let's talk about the second pillar: **Angels**, or *Mala'ikah*. 
  
  Angels are noble creations of Allah made of light. They don't have physical genders—they aren't male or female. They don't eat, drink, sleep, or get tired. 
  
  And unlike us, they do not have free will. They cannot choose to disobey. As the Quran says, *'They do what they are commanded.'* They are in a state of constant, perfect obedience to Allah."
* **Concept Analogy:** Like the solar system following physical laws perfectly without ever straying, angels carry out Allah's commands without error.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Is Iblis (Satan) a fallen angel in Islam? (Answer: No, he is a Jinn, created from smokeless fire, and he has free will)."

## Slide 10: The Major Angels and Their Duties
* **Slide Summary:** The names and roles of Jibril, Mika'il, Israfil, and Malik al-Mawt.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Count the four major angels on your fingers]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Allah has created countless angels, but we are told the names and duties of the major ones. 
  
  First, **Jibril**—the Archangel of Revelation, who brought the Quran to the Prophets. 
  
  Second, **Mika'il**—responsible for rain, plants, and distributing sustenance. 
  
  Third, **Israfil**—who will blow the Trumpet to signal the end of this world and the Day of Resurrection. 
  
  And fourth, **Malik al-Mawt**—the Angel of Death, who takes our souls when our life on earth is complete. 
  
  They are like a perfectly organized team, executing Allah's orders with absolute precision."
* **Concept Analogy:** Like officers in a company with specific roles, the angels are assigned critical tasks to maintain the universe.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the Quranic name of the Angel of Death? (Answer: Malik al-Mawt)."

## Slide 11: The Angels Who Accompany Us
* **Slide Summary:** The Kiraman Katibin and Al-Hafazah.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Touch your shoulders gently; speak in a reassuring and warm tone]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Did you know you have angels with you right now? 
  
  On your shoulders are the **Kiraman Katibin**—the Honorable Recorders. The angel on your right writes down your good deeds instantly. The angel on your left writes down your bad deeds. But here is the beautiful mercy of Allah: the angel on the left holds the pen up for a few hours. If you repent and say *Astaghfirullah*, they write nothing!
  
  We also have **Al-Hafazah**—the Guardian Angels. They protect us from harm and accidents unless Allah has decreed that something must happen. We are never truly alone!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A personal security team and a recording diary that highlights your achievements and gives you a chance to delete your mistakes before they are saved.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What does the angel on the left do if we make a mistake but immediately ask for forgiveness? (Answer: They don't write it down)."

## Slide 12: The Holy Books (Kutub) and the Divine Speech (Kalam)
* **Slide Summary:** The Kutub and the Ash'ari distinction of Kalam Nafsi vs. Kalam Lafzi.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Raise a copy of the Quran respectfully or point to a slide visual]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "The third pillar is belief in the **Holy Books**. Allah sent scriptures to guide humanity: the *Suhuf* to Ibrahim, the *Tawrah* to Musa, the *Zabur* to Dawud, the *Injil* to 'Isa, and the final *Quran* to Muhammad ﷺ.
  
  In the Ash'ari school, we make a beautiful distinction: 
  
  Allah's attribute of Speech is **Kalam Nafsi**—His eternal, inner attribute of Speech, which doesn't have sounds or letters. 
  
  The Arabic words we recite and the physical pages we hold are **Kalam Lafzi**—created letters and sounds that *point to* that uncreated Divine Speech. We treat the physical book with utmost respect because of what it represents."
* **Concept Analogy:** When you think of an idea in your head, it has no sound or language. But when you write it down, you use ink and letters to represent that idea.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Is Allah's attribute of Speech created? (Answer: No, His attribute of Speech is eternal, but the ink and paper of our books are created)."

## Slide 13: Prophets and Messengers (Anbiya' and Rusul)
* **Slide Summary:** The definitions of Nabi and Rasul, and the continuity of the Prophetic chain.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with enthusiasm; show a timeline flow with your hands]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "The fourth pillar is belief in the **Prophets and Messengers**. 
  
  Is there a difference between a *Nabi* and a *Rasul*? Yes! 
  
  A **Nabi** is a prophet chosen by Allah to receive guidance, usually to follow an existing law. A **Rasul** is a messenger who is given a *new* book or a new set of laws. So, every *Rasul* is a *Nabi*, but not every *Nabi* is a *Rasul*.
  
  Allah sent thousands of Prophets, and they all brought the exact same core message: *Worship Allah alone and do good deeds.* We must believe in all of them!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A Nabi is like a teacher who teaches the standard school curriculum. A Rasul is like the principal who introduces a brand new curriculum.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Can a person be a Muslim if they believe in Prophet Muhammad ﷺ but reject Prophet Musa (Moses)? (Answer: No, belief in all Prophets is an all-or-nothing pillar)."

## Slide 14: The Infallibility of the Prophets ('Ismah)
* **Slide Summary:** Infallibility and necessary qualities of Prophets.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with respect and clear emphasis]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Because Prophets are our role models, Allah protected them. This protection is called **'Ismah**—Infallibility. 
  
  They are protected from committing major or minor sins, lying, or breaking promises. Every Prophet must have four qualities: 
  
  **Sidq** (truthfulness), **Amanah** (trustworthiness), **Tabligh** (delivering the message completely), and **Fatanah** (wisdom). 
  
  If a Prophet could commit a sin, then Allah would be ordering us to follow someone doing wrong, which cannot be. Prophets are the ultimate clean mirrors reflecting Allah's guidance."
* **Concept Analogy:** A lighthouse must always have a bright, clean light. If the glass is dirty or the light turns off, ships will crash. Prophets must be clean role models.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What are the four necessary qualities of every Prophet? (Answer: Sidq, Amanah, Tabligh, and Fatanah)."

## Slide 15: Believing in Qadar (Divine Decree)
* **Slide Summary:** The four stages of Qadar: Knowledge, Writing, Will, and Creation.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak in a calming, comforting tone]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Now, let's talk about the sixth pillar: **Qadar**, the Divine Decree. Qadar is the belief that Allah knows and measures everything that happens.
  
  There are four stages of decree:
  1. **Knowledge ('Ilm):** Allah knows everything eternally.
  2. **Writing (Kitabah):** Allah ordered the Pen to write everything in the Preserved Tablet, the *Lawh al-Mahfuz*.
  3. **Will (Mashi'ah):** Everything happens only if Allah wills it.
  4. **Creation (Khalq):** Allah creates everything.
  
  Knowing this brings peace to our hearts. When things go wrong, we know it was decreed, and we trust Allah's plan."
* **Concept Analogy:** The Preserved Tablet is like the ultimate master plan of the universe. Nothing can surprise the One who wrote it.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Does Qadar mean we are forced to do things against our will? (Answer: No, the Pen wrote what we *would* choose to do, based on Allah's eternal knowledge)."

## Slide 16: Human Choice and the Ash'ari Concept of Kasb (Acquisition)
* **Slide Summary:** Resolving the debate between free will and predestination using Kasb.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Use a hands-on physical metaphor]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "If Allah creates everything, why are we responsible for our actions? The Ash'ari answer is a brilliant concept called **Kasb**—Acquisition.
  
  Allah creates the physical ability and the action itself. This is **Khalq** (Creation). But we choose where to direct our intention. This is **Kasb** (Acquisition).
  
  Imagine you decide to give money to charity. Allah creates the money, the movement of your hand, and the ability. You choose to do it. You 'acquire' the deed because of your intention. That's why we are rewarded or punished."
* **Concept Analogy:** A keyboard and computer: Allah creates the computer system, the electricity, and the keyboard. But *you* choose which keys to press to type your message.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "In the action of writing a homework assignment, what is Allah's role and what is the human's role? (Answer: Allah creates the physical ability/paper/ink, the human chooses to write)."

---

## Slide 17: Qadar is Not an Excuse for Bad Choices!
* **Slide Summary:** Accountability and why Qadar cannot justify sins.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak firmly; raise a hand as a stop sign]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "We must make one thing very clear: *we can never use Qadar as an excuse for bad behavior.*
  
  If a student doesn't study, fails an exam, and says, 'Well, it was written in my Qadar that I would fail,' they are making a big mistake. They chose not to study!
  
  There is a famous story about Caliph 'Umar. A thief was brought to him and said, 'I only stole because it was in my Qadar!' 'Umar replied, 'And we are only cutting off your hand because it is in our Qadar!' 
  
  We look at Qadar *after* things happen to have patience, never *before* to excuse our laziness."
* **Concept Analogy:** If you jump into a puddle of mud, you cannot blame the rain for making your pants dirty. You made the choice to jump.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "When should we think about Qadar—before we make a choice, or after something out of our control happens? (Answer: After, as a source of patience)."

---

## Slide 18: Why We Learn Schools of Thought (Madhhabs)
* **Slide Summary:** The role and history of the four Sunni Madhhabs.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with respect and appreciation for diversity]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Why do different scholars sometimes have different details in their rulings? This is where **Madhhabs** come in.
  
  A *Madhhab* is a systematic school of law. The four major Sunni schools are the **Hanafi**, **Maliki**, **Shafi'i**, and **Hanbali** schools. 
  
  They agree on ninety-nine percent of Islam! They only differ on secondary details of practice. The Imams of these schools were great teachers who studied with and respected each other. Following a school is like following a qualified doctor—it ensures we practice Islam correctly."
* **Concept Analogy:** A trunk of a tree (unity in core beliefs) with different branches (valid interpretations of secondary details).
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Are the differences between Madhhabs a sign of division or a sign of mercy and scholarship? (Answer: Mercy and scholarship)."

## Slide 19: The Maliki Madhhab: The Practice of Madinah and Sadl
* **Slide Summary:** Imam Malik, 'Amal Ahl al-Madinah, and Sadl in Salah.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Stand up to demonstrate the posture of Sadl (arms at sides)]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Let's focus on the **Maliki school**. It was founded by Imam Malik, who lived in Madinah—the city of the Prophet ﷺ. 
  
  Imam Malik used a unique methodology called **'Amal Ahl al-Madinah**—the continuous practice of the people of Madinah. He argued that if the children and grandchildren of the Companions in Madinah all did something, it is a living proof of the Sunnah.
  
  One famous practice is **Sadl**—praying with your arms resting naturally at your sides. While other schools fold their hands, Imam Malik observed the scholars of Madinah praying with their arms down. Both *Sadl* and *Qabd* (folding) are valid, and we respect both!"
* **Concept Analogy:** If you want to know how a traditional recipe is made in a specific village, you look at how the villagers have made it for generations, not just a single written note.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Why did Imam Malik prefer praying with arms at the sides (Sadl)? (Answer: Because it was the living practice of the people of Madinah)."

## Slide 20: More Maliki Rulings on Salah
* **Slide Summary:** The recitation of the Basmalah and Sujud al-Sahw (Qabli vs. Ba'di).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Point to the two types of sujud al-sahw on the slide]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Let's look at two other practical Maliki rulings.
  
  First, the **Basmalah** (*Bismillahir-Rahmanir-Rahim*). In the Maliki school, we start directly with *Al-Hamdu lillahi Rabbil-'Alamin* aloud or silently in fard prayers. This is because Malikis view Al-Fatihah as starting with the first verse of praise.
  
  Second, **Sujud al-Sahw** (Prostration of Forgetfulness). If you make a mistake in prayer:
  - If you *missed* a Sunnah action, you make sujud **Qabli** (before the Taslim).
  - If you *added* something extra by mistake, you make sujud **Ba'di** (after the Taslim).
  It's a beautiful, logical system!"
* **Concept Analogy:** If you forgot a step in a recipe, you add it before serving (Qabli). If you accidentally put too much salt, you serve an extra glass of water after the meal (Ba'di).
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "If I forget to recite a second Surah in prayer, do I perform Qabli or Ba'di sujud? (Answer: Qabli, because something was missed)."
