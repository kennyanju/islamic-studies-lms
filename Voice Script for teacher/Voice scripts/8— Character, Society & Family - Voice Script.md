# Voice Script: Character, Society & Family (Akhlaq & Mu'amalat)
**Topic:** Humility, honesty, family ties, neighbor rights, peer pressure, digital ethics, justice, and Maliki/Ash'ari principles in social interactions.  
**Audience:** Parents/Teachers presenting to children aged 10-13.  
**Creed/Law Framework:** Ash'ari Aqidah & Maliki Fiqh.  

---

## Slide 1: Welcome & Course Overview
* **Slide Summary:** Introduces the focus on character (Akhlaq) and social dealings (Mu'amalat) as the mirror of the soul.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Smile, greet the class with warmth and open arms]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Assalamu Alaikum, everyone! Welcome back. Today, we are starting a beautiful new unit called **Character, Society & Family**.
  
  So far, we have studied how to perform Wudu and how to stand in prayer. But did you know that your prayer is incomplete if you go home and treat your parents, siblings, or classmates poorly? 
  
  In this unit, we will explore **Akhlaq**—which is our internal character—and **Mu'amalat**—which is how we interact with others. We will look at honesty, humility, managing anger, the rights of parents and neighbors, choosing friends, social media safety, and how Maliki law protects society. 
  
  Think of your character as the outer mirror of your inner soul. Let's start with a big question: *Can a person have a perfect Salah if they treat their family poorly? why is there a connection?* Let's find out!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A smartphone's hardware and software: your prayers and Wudu are like the high-speed processor (hardware), but your character is the operating system (software) that makes the apps run smoothly and allows you to communicate with the world.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the difference between Akhlaq and Mu'amalat? (Answer: Akhlaq is internal character; Mu'amalat is social interactions and transactions)."

## Slide 2: Creed Foundation: What Makes an Action "Good"?
* **Slide Summary:** Exploring Divine Command Ethics (Al-Husn wal-Qubh) in Ash'ari creed.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak in a slow, philosophical, and reasoning tone]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Let's start with a deep question: *How do we know what is actually good or bad?*
  
  In our **Ash'ari creed**, we learn a rule called Divine Command Ethics—or **Al-Husn wal-Qubh al-Shar'iyyan**. This means that moral goodness and badness are decided directly by Allah's revelation, not just by our feelings or what is popular in society. 
  
  Our mind, or *'Aql*, can figure out that truth is useful. But our mind alone cannot determine what Allah rewards or punishes. We tell the truth and respect our parents because *Allah commanded us to do so*. When we submit to His command, our daily kindness becomes a powerful act of worship!"
* **Concept Analogy:** Traffic laws: the government sets speed limits to keep roads safe. Even if you think driving fast is fun, the law defines what is safe and legal, and you follow it because of the authority behind it. Similarly, we follow moral laws because of Allah's authority.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "According to Ash'ari creed, what determines whether an action is morally good or bad in the sight of Allah? (Answer: Allah's revelation / Divine Command)."

## Slide 3: Perfecting Character: The Prophet’s Mission & Human Choice
* **Slide Summary:** The Prophet's ﷺ mission to perfect character and the Ash'ari concept of Kasb.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with inspiration; emphasize personal accountability]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "The Prophet ﷺ famously said: *'I was sent only to perfect noble character.'* 
  
  This tells us that character is the crown of our religion. But how do we build it? 
  
  This connects back to our Ash'ari concept of **Kasb**—Acquisition. Allah creates the ability to speak or react, but *we* choose to acquire either honesty or anger. 
  
  We cannot blame a bad temper on 'how Allah made us'! We have the free will to intend and choose good character, and when we make that choice, Allah creates the good character in us. Because we choose, we are fully accountable (*Mukallaf*) before Him. Good character is an active choice!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A keyboard shortcut: the computer creates the letters on the screen, but you are the one choosing to press the key for capital 'A' (patience) instead of lower-case 'a' (anger).
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Can we excuse our bad behavior by saying 'this is just how Allah made me'? Why not? (Answer: No, because through Kasb, we are responsible for choosing to acquire good character)."

## Slide 4: Honesty (Sidq): The Root of Trust
* **Slide Summary:** The definition of Sidq, its three levels, and the Maliki legal consequence of lying.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with conviction; count the three levels on your fingers]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "The root of all trust is **Sidq**—Honesty. The Prophet ﷺ said: *'Truthfulness leads to righteousness, and righteousness leads to Paradise.'*
  
  Honesty has three levels:
  1. **Truth in Speech** (*Sidq al-Lisan*): Never telling lies, even when joking to make people laugh.
  2. **Truth in Action** (*Sidq al-'Amal*): Making sure your outer behavior matches your inner heart—no hypocrisy!
  3. **Truth in Intention** (*Sidq al-Niyyah*): Doing good solely for Allah, not for show.
  
  Here is a **Maliki legal flag**: in our Madhhab, if someone is caught lying in public, they lose their upright legal character (*'Adalah*), and their testimony is forever rejected by a judge! Lying ruins your credibility in this world and the next."
* **Concept Analogy:** A transparent window: a clean window lets light pass through perfectly (Sidq). If you paint a fake scene on the glass (lying), it looks confusing and blocks the real light from entering.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What happens to a person's legal status in a Maliki court if they are caught lying? (Answer: Their credibility ('Adalah) is ruined, and their testimony is rejected)."

## Slide 5: Humility (Tawadu'): Defeating the Ego
* **Slide Summary:** Tawadu' vs. Kibr (pride) and the Prophet's ﷺ humble model.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Lower your posture slightly; speak in a gentle, warm tone]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Another beautiful character trait is **Tawadu'**—Humility. Tawadu' is recognizing that every talent, grade, or luxury you have is a gift from Allah, not because you are superior to others.
  
  The opposite is **Kibr**—Pride. The Prophet ﷺ defined pride as: *'rejecting the truth and looking down on people.'* The Quran warns us not to walk on the earth with pride, because we can't crack the earth or reach the heights of the mountains! 
  
  Look at the Prophet ﷺ: he was the leader of Arabia, yet he milked his own goats, repaired his own shoes, and served his family at home. He showed us that true greatness lies in humility."
* **Concept Analogy:** A sponge vs. a stone: a sponge is soft and sits low, absorbing water (blessings). A hard stone sits high and proud, but water runs off it, leaving it dry. Humility absorbs Allah's blessings.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "How did the Prophet ﷺ define pride (Kibr)? (Answer: Rejecting the truth and looking down on people)."

## Slide 6: Controlling Anger: The Power of Hilm
* **Slide Summary:** Controlling anger (Ghadab) through Kadhm al-Ghaydh and Hilm, and practical steps.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Demonstrate the breathing and physical changes; speak calmly]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "We all get angry sometimes. Anger—**Ghadab**—is a spark from Satan that clouds our mind. 
  
  But the Prophet ﷺ said: *'The strong person is not the one who can wrestle others down; the strong person is the one who controls himself in moments of anger.'*
  
  We have two main tools: **Kadhm al-Ghaydh** (suppressing anger) and **Hilm** (forbearance/calmness).
  
  If you feel yourself heating up, follow these three Sunnah steps:
  1. Seek refuge in Allah (*A'udhu billah*).
  2. Change your physical posture: if standing, sit down; if sitting, lie down.
  3. Perform Wudu: cool the fire of anger with water. It works!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A smoke detector: when fire breaks out, the alarm screeches. But instead of letting the house burn, you press the reset button (changing posture/Wudu) to clear the smoke and restore peace.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What physical actions did the Prophet ﷺ recommend to cool down our anger? (Answer: Say 'A'udhu billah', sit or lie down, and make Wudu)."

## Slide 7: Creed Connection: Anger, Patience, and Divine Decree
* **Slide Summary:** Connecting everyday patience to the belief in Qadar (Divine Decree).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with a reassuring, faith-focused tone]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Did you know that controlling your anger is directly connected to the sixth pillar of Iman—belief in **Qadar**? 
  
  Under our creed, everything that happens—even accidents, delays, or minor losses—is created by Allah's will and knowledge. 
  
  When someone spills water on your homework, or a game lags and you lose, reacting with explosive rage is like reacting against what Allah has decreed! 
  
  Instead, when we practice patience (*Sabr*) and say, *'Qaddara Allahu wa ma sha'a fa'ala'* (Allah has decreed, and what He wills, He does), we instantly restore our inner peace and submit to His wisdom."
* **Concept Analogy:** A storm: you can't stop the rain from falling (Qadar), and screaming at the clouds won't stop the storm. You put on a raincoat (patience) and wait for the sun to return.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What phrase should a believer say when a frustrating accident happens out of their control? (Answer: 'Qaddara Allahu wa ma sha'a fa'ala')."

## Slide 8: Character and the Law: The Concept of Muru'ah
* **Slide Summary:** The concept of Muru'ah (chivalry) and how it affects legal standing in the Maliki school.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with focus on dignity and social code]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Let's look at a fascinating concept in Maliki law: **Muru'ah**. 
  
  *Muru'ah* means legal chivalry, dignity, and honorable behavior. It means acting in a way that respects local customs and avoids foolishness.
  
  In the Maliki school, a witness in court must have upright character (*'Adalah*). But if you lack *Muru'ah*, your character is ruined and a judge will reject your testimony! 
  
  What destroys *Muru'ah*? Screaming in public, playing foolish pranks, lying for clout online, or dressing inappropriately. In our Madhhab, bad manners aren't just rude—they have direct legal consequences!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A driver's license: if you keep speed-racing and acting foolishly on the road, the government takes away your license ( testimony status) because you proved you aren't responsible.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the meaning of 'Muru'ah' in the Maliki school? (Answer: Chivalry, dignity, and honorable behavior that respects custom)."

## Slide 9: Rights of Parents (Birr al-Walidayn)
* **Slide Summary:** The Quranic pairing of parents' rights with Allah's worship, and Birr.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with high respect and soft emotion]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Now let's talk about the most important people in our lives: our parents. 
  
  In the Quran, Allah says: *'And your Lord has decreed that you worship none but Him, and to parents, good treatment.'* Look at that sequence! Allah pairs worshiping Him directly with honoring parents.
  
  This duty is called **Birr al-Walidayn**—active goodness and respect. 
  
  Allah forbids us from saying even the smallest sigh of frustration—**'Uff'**—to them. The only time we do not obey them is if they command us to commit a sin. Even then, we must still treat them with gentle kindness and support in worldly matters."
* **Concept Analogy:** A two-part lock: you need both the key of worshiping Allah and the key of treating parents with *Birr* to unlock the gate of Paradise.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Are we allowed to say 'Uff' (sigh of annoyance) to our parents when they ask us to do chores? (Answer: No, this is strictly forbidden in the Quran)."

## Slide 10: Legal Rights of Parents in Maliki Law
* **Slide Summary:** Fiqh rules of parental permission for voluntary acts, Nafaqah, and court testimony.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Point out the legal details on the slide methodically]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "In Maliki law, *Birr* has concrete legal rules:
  
  First, **Parental Permission**: you *must* get permission from your parents before going on voluntary travel or long-term study away from home if it will make them lonely or sad. Serving them is a higher duty (*Fard*) than voluntary travels.
  
  Second, **Financial Support** (*Nafaqah*): if parents become poor, it is a strict legal obligation on their children to pay for their food, housing, and medicine. 
  
  Third, in court, a child's testimony in favor of their parents is disqualified because of natural bias. These rules protect the sacred parent-child bond!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A financial safety net: just as parents support their children when they are weak and young, the law reverses the net, requiring children to support their parents when they are weak and elderly.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Why does Maliki fiqh require parental permission for voluntary travel? (Answer: Because serving parents is a higher obligation—Fard—than voluntary actions)."

## Slide 11: Keeping Family Ties (Silat al-Rahim)
* **Slide Summary:** The concept of Rahim, the danger of Qat' al-Rahim, and how to maintain ties.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with warmth; encourage connection with relatives]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "The word **Rahim** literally means 'womb.' **Silat al-Rahim** means connecting and maintaining relationships with blood relatives—grandparents, aunts, uncles, and cousins.
  
  Severing these ties—**Qat' al-Rahim**—is a major sin! The Prophet ﷺ warned: *'The severer of family ties will not enter Paradise.'*
  
  How do we maintain ties? By calling them, visiting them, sharing gifts, and helping them. 
  
  And here is the true test: the Prophet ﷺ said that maintaining ties isn't just returning a call to a relative who is nice to you. True connection is when a relative cuts you off or is rude, and you *still* make the effort to connect for the sake of Allah!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A bridge: if a storm knocks down a bridge (argument/silence), you don't just leave it broken. You rebuild the bridge to keep the two sides connected.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the meaning of 'Silat al-Rahim'? (Answer: Connecting and maintaining ties with blood relatives)."

## Slide 12: Rights of Neighbors (Huquq al-Jiwar)
* **Slide Summary:** The advice of Jibril, the Maliki 40-house radius rule, and neighborly duties.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with neighborly warmth and civic responsibility]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "The Prophet ﷺ said: *'Angel Jibril kept advising me to treat neighbors kindly until I thought he would make them my heirs.'* 
  
  How far does the neighborhood go? In the **Maliki school**, the neighborhood extends to **forty houses** in every direction—front, back, left, and right! 
  
  Our duties to neighbors are:
  - Guarding their safety: never harming them with noise, garbage, or smells.
  - Generosity: sharing food.
  - Protecting their privacy.
  
  The Prophet ﷺ gave a warning: *'He is not a believer whose neighbor is not safe from his harm.'* Let's make sure our neighbors are happy with us!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A safety bubble: you draw a circle around your house covering forty homes. Inside this bubble, everyone must feel safe, respected, and supported by you.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "How far does the neighborhood boundary extend in the Maliki school? (Answer: Forty houses in every direction)."

## Slide 13: Property & Neighborhood Rights in Maliki Law
* **Slide Summary:** Prevention of harm (Da'f al-Darar) and the right of pre-emption (Shuf'ah).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Use a clear, legal-mindset tone]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "In Maliki Fiqh, we have strict property laws to protect neighbors.
  
  First, the **Prevention of Harm** (*Da'f al-Darar*). A property owner is legally blocked from using their own land in a way that hurts their neighbor—like building a high wall that blocks their sunlight, or opening a window that looks directly into their private yard. 
  
  Second, **Pre-emption** (*Shuf'ah*). If you share property or an access pathway, and you want to sell it, you must offer it to your neighbor/partner first. This prevents unwanted strangers from causing harm to the neighborhood. Our law values peace!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A building permit: you can't just build whatever you want on your land if it causes a safety risk or ruins the privacy of the house next door. Your rights end where your neighbor's harm begins.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the legal right of 'Shuf'ah' (Pre-emption)? (Answer: The requirement to offer shared property/pathway to your neighbor/partner first before selling it to a stranger)."

## Slide 14: Choosing Companions: The Ethics of Friendship
* **Slide Summary:** The Prophet's ﷺ perfume seller vs. blacksmith analogy, and the influence of a Khalil.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with inspiration; emphasize peer influence]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Let's talk about friends. The company you keep shapes your soul!
  
  The Prophet ﷺ gave a beautiful analogy:
  - A **good friend** is like a perfume seller. Even if you don't buy perfume, just sitting with him makes you smell good.
  - A **bad friend** is like a blacksmith working a bellows. He either burns your clothes with sparks, or leaves you smelling like bad smoke.
  
  The Prophet ﷺ said: *'A person is on the religion of his close friend (*Khalil*), so let each of you look to whom he takes as a close friend.'* Choose friends who pull you closer to Allah, not away from Him!"
* **Concept Analogy:** Secondhand smoke: even if you aren't smoking yourself, sitting in a smoky room causes your clothes and hair to smell like smoke. Bad company desensitizes your heart to wrong actions.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the difference between a perfume seller friend and a blacksmith friend? (Answer: The perfume seller influences you with goodness; the blacksmith leaves you smelling bad or burned)."

## Slide 15: Friendship in Court: Maliki Rulings on Witness Testimony
* **Slide Summary:** Why Maliki judges reject the testimony of a close friend due to bias (Tuhmah).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Raise a finger to point out a unique court rule]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Here is another fascinating **Maliki flag**: *Can a close friend testify for you in court?*
  
  In our Madhhab, a person's testimony in favor of their very close friend is **rejected** by the judge! 
  
  Why? Because the legal system recognizes that the bond of close friendship is so strong that you might exaggerate or overlook the truth out of loyalty. 
  
  True Islamic friendship does not mean lying for your friend. It means helping them stand on the truth, even if the truth is against them. Loyalty to justice (*'Adl*) always comes before loyalty to a friend!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A conflict of interest: a referee is not allowed to referee a game where their best friend or sibling is playing, because they might be biased, even if they try to be fair.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Why does a Maliki judge reject a witness's testimony in favor of their close friend? (Answer: Due to suspicion of bias (Tuhmah) caused by close friendship)."

## Slide 16: Confronting Peer Pressure
* **Slide Summary:** Dealing with peer pressure and strategies to guard the Deen.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with empowerment and strength]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Peer pressure is the social force that pushes you to conform to fit in. But as Muslims, our goal is **Hifdh al-Din**—guarding our faith.
  
  How do we stand strong?
  1. **Define your boundaries early:** if everyone knows you pray and don't listen to bad music, they won't ask you to join in.
  2. **The power of 'No':** saying no is a sign of *Muru'ah* (dignity) and *Izzah* (pride in Islam). It shows you are a leader, not a follower.
  3. **Seek better company:** if a group makes you feel bad for practicing your faith, walk away. You deserve better!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A shield wall: when a group stands together with shields locked, the wind can't knock them down. Locking hands with righteous friends protects you from the pressure of the crowd.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What are the three strategies to resist peer pressure? (Answer: Define boundaries early, use the power of 'No', and seek good company)."

## Slide 17: Social Media & The Muslim Mindset
* **Slide Summary:** Applying the Five Necessities of Shariah to social media (mind, honor).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak in a modern, practical tone; mention common apps]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "We live in a digital age. But did you know that the rules of Islam apply online too? 
  
  The Shariah protects five things: Religion, Life, Mind, Honor, and Wealth. 
  
  On social media, we must guard:
  - Our **Mind** (*Hifdh al-'Aql*): avoiding doom-scrolling and addictive, time-wasting content that ruins our focus on Salah.
  - Our **Honor** (*Hifdh al-'Ird*): banning gossip, spreading rumors, cyberbullying, or sharing screenshots to humiliate others.
  
  Our golden rule is simple: *If you wouldn't say it to someone's face in front of their parents, do not type it online!*"
* **Concept Analogy:** A public bulletin board: posting something online is like pasting a giant poster on the main street of your town for everyone, including your parents and teachers, to see forever.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What are the two key necessities we must protect when using social media? (Answer: The Mind/Intellect ('Aql) and Honor/Dignity ('Ird))."

## Slide 18: Blocking the Paths to Harm: Sadd al-Dhara'i'
* **Slide Summary:** Applying the Maliki principle of Sadd al-Dhara'i' to digital life.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Use a warning, proactive tone]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "In Maliki Fiqh, we have a brilliant principle called **Sadd al-Dhara'i'**—which means 'blocking the means to evil.' 
  
  If a permissible action almost always leads to a sin, the Maliki school rules that the action itself becomes forbidden.
  
  How do we apply this online? 
  - If following a certain account keeps leading you to see inappropriate things, you must unfollow or block it (*Sadd al-Dhara'i'*).
  - If a chat group keeps descending into cyberbullying, you must leave the group.
  We don't wait for the sin to happen; we block the path early!"
* **Concept Analogy:** Closing a gate: if you know there is a muddy puddle on the pathway, you close the gate to that path and take another dry road, keeping your clothes clean.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What does the Maliki legal principle of 'Sadd al-Dhara'i'' mean? (Answer: Blocking the pathways/means that lead to evil or sin)."

## Slide 19: Justice ('Adl) and Equity (Qist)
* **Slide Summary:** Nuance between 'Adl (strict equality) and Qist (equity), and avoiding oppression (Zulm).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with a focus on fairness and balance]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Islam stands for fairness. We learn about two words: **'Adl** and **Qist**.
  
  *'Adl* means strict justice—putting everything in its proper place and giving everyone their legal due. *Qist* is equity—ensuring fairness in treatment, taking into account individual needs. 
  
  The opposite of justice is **Zulm**—oppression. Allah says in a Hadith Qudsi: *'O My servants! I have forbidden oppression for Myself, and I have made it forbidden among you, so do not oppress one another.'* 
  
  In our creed, Allah is Al-'Adl. Oppression is impossible for Him because everything belongs to Him."
* **Concept Analogy:** The shoe sizes: giving a 5-year-old and a 15-year-old the exact same size 10 shoe is 'equal' ('Adl under strict definition), but it is unfair. Giving them sizes that fit them perfectly is equity (Qist).
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the difference between 'Adl and Qist? (Answer: 'Adl is strict justice/equality; Qist is equity/fairness based on individual circumstances)."

## Slide 20: The Common Good: Maslahah Mursalah
* **Slide Summary:** Maslahah Mursalah as a Maliki source of law, and everyday application.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with civic duty and inspiration]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Finally, let's look at a famous Maliki source of law: **Maslahah Mursalah**—which means 'Unrestricted Public Interest.'
  
  This means creating rules that benefit society and prevent harm, even if there is no specific Quranic verse about it.
  
  Think about traffic lights or school safety rules. They aren't in the Quran, but they protect human life and order, which is the goal of Shariah. Therefore, obeying them is a religious duty! 
  
  A good Muslim is always a force for good, looking for ways to benefit the community. Let's make our world a better place!"
* **Concept Analogy:** Recycling or litter laws: there isn't a specific verse about plastic bottles, but keeping the town clean protects public health (*Maslahah*), so we do it as a duty.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What does the principle of 'Maslahah Mursalah' mean? (Answer: Unrestricted public interest / taking actions that benefit society even if not explicitly in the texts)."
