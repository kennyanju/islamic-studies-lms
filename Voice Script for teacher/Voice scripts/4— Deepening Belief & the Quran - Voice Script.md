* **Slide Summary:** The concept of Niyyah and how it transforms habits ('Adah) into worship ('Ibadah).

* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] Let's look at the very first step in our belief: **Niyyah**, which means intention. [pause] The Prophet sallallahu alayhi wa sallam taught us a rule: *'Actions are only by intentions, and every person will have only what they intended.'*

## Slide 5: Action & Creation — The Ash‘arī Concept of Kasb (Acquisition)
* **Slide Summary:** Resolving predestination vs. free will using the Ash'ari concept of Kasb.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak methodically; use hands to show creation vs choice]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] Here is a big question that confuses many people: *If Allah creates everything, who is responsible when I do something?* 
  
  The Ash'ari school has a beautiful answer called **Kasb**—which means Acquisition. [pause] Allah is the only Creator. [pause] He creates your physical muscles, the ability to move, and the action itself. [pause] This is **Khalq** (Creation). [pause] But *you* choose where to direct your intention and will. [pause] This is **Kasb** (Acquisition). [pause] Because you choose to acquire the deed, you are responsible. [pause] This middle path protects us from thinking we have no choice (like a leaf in the wind), and also from thinking we create things independently of Allah."
* **Concept Analogy:** Driving a car: Allah creates the engine, the gasoline, the roads, and the car's movement. But you are the driver steering the wheel. You are responsible for where the car goes.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Who creates the action, and who chooses to acquire it? (Answer: Allah creates the action, the human chooses to acquire it)."

## Slide 8: Ash‘arī Theology: Al-Kalām al-Nafsī vs. Al-Kalām al-Lafẓī
* **Slide Summary:** The Ash'ari distinction of Kalam Nafsi (eternal speech) and Kalam Lafzi (created letters/sounds).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak in a clear, educational, theological tone]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] In Ash'ari theology, we learn a very precise distinction about the Quran. [pause] Allah's attribute of Speech is **Kalam Nafsi**—His eternal, inner attribute of Speech, which doesn't have sounds, letters, or a language like Arabic. [pause] It is beyond human description, just like His knowledge. [pause] The physical words we recite, the ink we write, and the paper we hold are **Kalam Lafzi**—created letters and sounds that *point to* that eternal Speech of Allah. [pause] We respect the printed Quran because of what it represents—the uncreated Speech of Allah. [pause] This helps us understand that Allah's speech doesn't require vocal cords, while keeping the text sacred."
* **Concept Analogy:** Music sheets: the paper and printed ink notes are created, but the melody they represent is an abstract idea that exists in a different way.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Is Allah's attribute of Speech created? (Answer: No, the attribute itself is eternal, but the physical Arabic letters and ink on paper are created expressions of it)."

## Slide 18: How Hadith Were Collected: Isnad and Matn
* **Slide Summary:** The anatomy of a Hadith (Isnad and Matn) and evaluation criteria.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Draw a chain and text box in the air as a visual aid]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] How did scholars know which Hadith were real and which were made up? They developed a highly rigorous science to check two parts of every Hadith:
  
  1. [pause] The **Isnad**: The chain of narrators leading back to the Prophet sallallahu alayhi wa sallam (e.g., A heard from B, who heard from C, who heard the Prophet sallallahu alayhi wa sallam say...).
  2. [pause] The **Matn**: The actual text of the Hadith. [pause] Scholars performed massive background checks on every narrator in the chain! They checked: Is this person honest? Do they have a strong memory? Did they actually meet the person they claim to have heard the Hadith from? 
  
  Based on this, they classified Hadiths as *Sahih* (authentic), *Hasan* (good), *Da'if* (weak), or *Mawdu'* (fabricated)."
* **Concept Analogy:** A certificate of authenticity: when you buy a rare collectable, you get a document tracing every single owner back to the factory to prove it is not a fake. An Isnad is that certificate of authenticity.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What are the two parts of a Hadith? (Answer: Isnad—the chain of narrators, and Matn—the actual text)."

## Slide 21: Imam Malik's Methodology: ‘Amal Ahl al-Madīnah
* **Slide Summary:** Explaining 'Amal Ahl al-Madinah as a source of law and why it takes priority.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with emphasis; show a gesture of a large group]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] Imam Malik's signature methodology was **'Amal Ahl al-Madinah**—the practice of the people of Madinah. [pause] He argued that because Madinah was where the Prophet sallallahu alayhi wa sallam lived and established the law for ten years, and where thousands of Companions lived and died, their daily customs were a physical, living Sunnah. [pause] He famously said: *'A mass-transmission of a thousand people inheriting a practice from a thousand people is stronger than a single person narrating a text from a single person.'* 
  
  This is why in the Maliki school, if a single Hadith says one thing, but the entire city of Madinah did another, we follow the city's practice because it represents the actual, continuous Sunnah!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A town custom: if you want to know how a traditional game is played, you don't just read one player's notes; you watch how the whole town has played it for generations.
