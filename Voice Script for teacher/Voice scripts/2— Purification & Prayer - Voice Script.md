# Voice Script: Purification & Prayer (Taharah & Salah)
**Topic:** Water and Purity, Wudu, Ghusl, Tayammum, and Salah (with a focus on Maliki Fiqh).  
**Audience:** Parents/Teachers presenting to children aged 10-13.  
**Creed/Law Framework:** Ash'ari Aqidah & Maliki Fiqh.  

---
**Model Tuning:** ElevenLabs v3 with native expressive audio tags ([warmly], [thoughtful], [reverent], [pause], [short pause]).

## Slide 3: The Creed (Aqidah) Context: Why Do We Worship?
* **Slide Summary:** Explores the concept of 'Ubudiyyah (servantship) and Allah being Al-Ghani (independent of our worship).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with sincerity; use open hands to show receiving]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] Before we look at the physical rules of washing, let's look at the *why*. [pause] Why do we pray? 
  
  In Ash'ari creed, we learn a beautiful name of Allah: **Al-Ghani**. [pause] It means The Independent. [pause] Allah is completely free of any need. [pause] He doesn't need our prayers, our Wudu, or our fasting. [pause] If the entire world stopped praying, it wouldn't diminish Allah's majesty by even a speck!
  
  So why do we do it? Because *we* are the needy ones—**Al-Fuqara'**. [pause] We pray because our souls need peace, guidance, and connection. [pause] Worship is our way of saying thank you to Allah for the gift of life, sight, and faith. [pause] Prayer is the ultimate connection between the servant and the Creator."
* **Concept Analogy:** A phone charger: the power grid doesn't need the phone to be plugged in, but the phone desperately needs the charger to stay alive. We plug ourselves into prayer to recharge our souls.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What does Allah's name 'Al-Ghani' mean? (Answer: The Self-Sufficient or Independent One)."

## Slide 4: The Importance of Purity (Taharah)
* **Slide Summary:** The two levels of purity: external (Zahir) and internal (Batin).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Use hands to gesture to the chest, then outward]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] In Islamic law, **Taharah** is a strict requirement for prayer. [pause] If we don't have Taharah, our prayer is not accepted. [pause] But Taharah has two levels:
  First, [short pause] **Outer Purity** (*Taharah al-Zahir*). [pause] This means washing away physical dirt and being in a state of Wudu. [pause] Second, [short pause] **Inner Purity** (*Taharah al-Batin*). [pause] This means keeping our hearts clean from pride, jealousy, and anger. [pause] In the Maliki school, we learn that outer purification removes two things: physical filth, which we call *najasah*, and a spiritual state of impurity, which we call *hadath*. [pause] We need to clean both before we pray."
* **Concept Analogy:** A computer screen. Physical dirt on the screen is like *najasah*—you can just wipe it off. But if the computer is turned off, that is like *hadath*—the screen is clean, but you need to turn the power on (perform Wudu) to make it work.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the difference between Taharah al-Zahir and Taharah al-Batin? (Answer: Zahir is outer cleanliness, Batin is inner cleanliness of the heart)."

## Slide 5: Types of Water — Overview
* **Slide Summary:** Introducing the three legal categories of water in Maliki Fiqh.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Hold up a visual or gesture to the slide columns]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] To clean ourselves, we need water. [pause] But not all liquids that look like water can be used for Wudu! 
  
  In the Maliki school, we classify water into three clear categories:
  1. **Al-Ma' al-Mutlaq**—this is Purifying water.
  2. **Al-Ma' al-Tahir**—this is Pure water, but it cannot be used for Wudu.
  3. **Al-Ma' al-Najis**—this is Impure water. [pause] Let's look at what each of these means, so we always know how to make a valid Wudu."
* **Concept Analogy:** Fuel for a car: you can't put vegetable oil in a gasoline car, even though vegetable oil is clean and edible. Only the right type of liquid makes the car run.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "How many categories of water are there in Maliki fiqh? (Answer: Three)."

## Slide 6: Category 1: Al-Ma' al-Mutlaq (Purifying Water)
* **Slide Summary:** Definition and examples of natural, purifying water.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with clarity; point out the examples on the slide]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] The first category is **Al-Ma' al-Mutlaq**—also known as *Tahur*. [pause] This is water that is in its natural state, just as Allah created it. [pause] It is pure in itself, and it has the power to purify other things. [pause] Examples include rainwater, seawater, well water, river water, and melted snow or hail. [pause] Even if well water has some natural dirt or leaves in it, it is still *Mutlaq* because those are natural. [pause] The Prophet sallallahu alayhi wa sallam said about the sea: *'Its water is purifying.'* So yes, you can do Wudu at the beach!"
* **Concept Analogy:** Raw, unfiltered nature: water that is called simply 'water' without needing another word to describe it.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Is seawater valid for Wudu? (Answer: Yes, it is Al-Ma' al-Mutlaq)."

---

## Slide 7: Category 2: Al-Ma' al-Tahir (Pure but Non-Purifying)
* **Slide Summary:** Understanding what happens when clean substances mix with water.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Use a conversational, reasoning tone]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] The second category is **Al-Ma' al-Tahir**. [pause] This is water that was originally purifying, but got mixed with a *pure* substance—like milk, soap, rosewater, or tea. [pause] In the Maliki school, the rule is simple: if the clean substance changes the color, taste, or smell of the water so much that it is no longer called just 'water', it becomes *Tahir*. [pause] Think about it: cup of tea is clean and delicious to drink, but you cannot do Wudu with tea! It has lost its status as simple water. [pause] If it changes the name, it changes the ruling!"
* **Concept Analogy:** Orange juice: it is clean to drink and you can spill it on your clothes without them becoming impure, but it's sticky and is no longer water, so it cannot be used for Wudu.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Can you perform Wudu with clean, filtered apple juice? Why not? (Answer: No, because it is Tahir, not Mutlaq. It is no longer called simple water)."

---

## Slide 8: Category 3: Al-Ma' al-Najis (Impure Water)
* **Slide Summary:** The definition of Najis water, and the unique Maliki view on water volume.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Raise index finger to highlight a key rule; speak methodically]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] The third category is **Al-Ma' al-Najis**—Impure water. [pause] This is water that has been mixed with an impurity like urine or blood. [pause] If the impurity changes the water's color, taste, or smell, it is completely *Najis* and cannot be used. [pause] But here is a unique Maliki ruling: *What if an impurity falls into water but does NOT change its qualities?* 
  
  In the Maliki school, that water actually remains **Tahur** (purifying), whether the volume is large or small! Other schools say that if water is in a small bucket, it automatically becomes impure. [pause] But Imam Malik argued that water is purifying by nature unless the impurity actually overpowers it. [pause] However, if you have other water, it is disliked (*makruh*) to use it."
* **Concept Analogy:** A giant lake: if a leaf falls in, it doesn't change the lake. The Maliki school applies this logical concept even to small amounts of water, focusing on whether a change actually occurred.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "If a tiny drop of blood falls into a bucket of water and the water remains completely clear with no change in smell or taste, can it still be used for Wudu in the Maliki school? (Answer: Yes, but it is makruh if other water is available)."

## Slide 9: Step-by-Step Wudu (Obligations / Fara'id)
* **Slide Summary:** Overview of the seven Fara'id of Wudu in the Maliki school.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Show seven fingers clearly]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] Now let's look at the active steps of Wudu. [pause] In the Maliki school, there are **seven obligations**—called **Fara'id**. [pause] If you miss even *one* of these seven, your Wudu is invalid, even if you did it by mistake! 
  
  Let's name them together:
  1. [pause] Intention (*Niyyah*)
  2. [pause] Washing the Face
  3. [pause] Washing the Arms
  4. [pause] Wiping the entire head (*Mash al-Ra's*)
  5. [pause] Washing the Feet
  6. [pause] Rubbing (*Dalk*)
  7. [pause] Continuity (*Muwalat*)
  
  Let's explore some of these in detail, especially the ones that are unique to our Madhhab."
* **Concept Analogy:** A passcode: if your phone passcode has 7 numbers, and you enter 6 of them correctly but leave one out, the phone will not unlock. Every single number is required.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "How many obligatory acts (Fara'id) are there in Maliki Wudu? (Answer: Seven)."

## Slide 10: Focus on Wudu Fara'id: Niyyah, Face, and Arms
* **Slide Summary:** Rules of Intention, Face washing, and Arm washing.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Point to your heart, face, and arms to show the physical limits]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] Let's look at the first three:
  
  First: **Intention** (*Niyyah*). [pause] In the Maliki school, you must make the intention in your heart at the *exact moment* you begin Wudu. [pause] If you wash your hands out of habit, and only intend Wudu when washing your face, the initial hand wash doesn't count!
  
  Second: **Washing the Face**. [pause] This is from the normal hairline to the bottom of the chin, and from earlobe to earlobe. [pause] If you have a thin beard, you must rub the skin underneath. [pause] Third: **Washing the Arms**. [pause] This is from your fingertips all the way up to and including the elbows. [pause] Don't skip the elbows—they must be washed!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A frame: just like a painting must fit perfectly within its frame, your washing must cover the exact boundaries of the face and arms.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Does the intention for Wudu have to be spoken aloud? (Answer: No, it is made silently in the heart)."

## Slide 11: Wiping the Entire Head (Mash al-Ra's)
* **Slide Summary:** Explaining the requirement of wiping the entire head and comparing it to other schools.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Demonstrate the head-wiping motion slowly]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] Here is a major difference between the schools: **Wiping the Head** (*Mash al-Ra's*). [pause] In the Maliki school, it is an absolute obligation to wipe your **entire head**—from the front hairline all the way to the nape of the neck. [pause] Why? Because of how scholars interpreted the Arabic letter *Ba'* in the Quranic command *'Wamsahu bi-ru'usikum'*. [pause] Imam Malik saw it as emphasis, meaning 'wipe your heads completely.' Meanwhile, Imam al-Shafi'i interpreted it as 'wipe a part of your head,' which is why in the Shafi'i school, even wiping three hairs is enough. [pause] To do it the Maliki way, we place our wet hands at the front, slide them all the way to the back. [pause] Sliding them back to the front is a Sunnah, not a Fard!"
* **Concept Analogy:** Dusting a table: you don't just dust a tiny corner; you wipe the whole surface to ensure it is clean.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Is wiping the back-to-front motion of the head a Fard or a Sunnah? (Answer: A Sunnah; the front-to-back wipe is the Fard)."

## Slide 12: Rubbing (Dalk) and Continuity (Muwalat)
* **Slide Summary:** Detailing Dalk (rubbing) and Muwalat (continuity) as Maliki obligations.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Act out rubbing your arm as you explain]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] Two very distinct Maliki obligations are **Dalk** (Rubbing) and **Muwalat** (Continuity).
  
  **Dalk** means passing your hand over the body part while washing it. [pause] In the Maliki school, just letting water run over your arm under the tap without rubbing it is not considered washing. [pause] You must physically rub it!
  
  **Muwalat** means doing Wudu in one continuous go. [pause] You cannot wash your face, pause to talk on the phone for 10 minutes until your face dries, and then continue. [pause] If a limb dries under normal weather because of a long pause, you must start over."
* **Concept Analogy:** Washing dishes: you don't just hold a plate under running water; you scrub it with a sponge (Dalk) to get it clean.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "If someone stands under a shower and lets water run over them without rubbing their skin, is their Wudu valid in the Maliki school? (Answer: No, because they missed the obligation of Dalk)."

## Slide 13: Sunnan and Fada'il of Wudu
* **Slide Summary:** Sunnahs and recommended acts of Wudu.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with enthusiasm; clarify the difference in terms]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] Besides the Fara'id, Wudu has **Sunnahs** (acts the Prophet sallallahu alayhi wa sallam did regularly) and **Fada'il** (recommended virtues). [pause] Some Sunnahs include: washing hands to the wrists at the start, rinsing the mouth (*Madmadah*), sniffing water (*Istinshaq*), and wiping the ears. [pause] Here is a cool fact: in the Maliki school, keeping the Wudu in order (*Tarteeb*) is a Sunnah, not a Fard! In other schools, if you wash your feet before your face, your Wudu is invalid. [pause] In our school, it is valid but disliked. [pause] We also have recommended acts, like saying *Bismillah* at the start, using a tooth-stick (*Siwak*), and washing each part three times instead of just once."
* **Concept Analogy:** A standard sandwich vs. one with extra toppings. The basic sandwich is valid and fills you up (Fara'id), but the extra toppings (Sunnahs and Fada'il) make it taste much better!
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Is keeping the correct order (Tarteeb) of Wudu a Fard or a Sunnah in the Maliki school? (Answer: A Sunnah)."

## Slide 14: Ghusl — The Ritual Bath
* **Slide Summary:** Definition of Ghusl and when it is required.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak in a respectful, mature tone]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] Sometimes, a person enters a state of major ritual impurity, which we call *Hadath al-Akbar*. [pause] Wudu is not enough to clear this; we need **Ghusl**—the ritual bath. [pause] Ghusl means washing the entire body with purifying water with a specific intention. [pause] It is required in four main situations: major impurity due to intimacy, the completion of menstruation (*Hayd*) for women, the completion of post-natal bleeding (*Nifas*), or upon the death of a believer. [pause] Ghusl is a blessing that allows us to return to our full connection in prayer and enter the mosque."
* **Concept Analogy:** A full system reboot. Wudu is like closing a frozen app, but Ghusl is like restarting the whole computer system to make it run fresh.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the difference between Hadath al-Asghar and Hadath al-Akbar? (Answer: Asghar is minor impurity needing Wudu; Akbar is major impurity needing Ghusl)."

## Slide 15: Steps and Obligations of Ghusl
* **Slide Summary:** The five obligations of Ghusl in the Maliki school and the Sunnah method.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak clearly; highlight the requirement of hair permeation]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] In the Maliki school, Ghusl has **five obligations**:
  1. [pause] The Intention to lift the major impurity.
  2. [pause] Continuity (*Muwalat*)—not stopping halfway.
  3. [pause] Rubbing (*Dalk*)—yes, you must rub your entire body with your hands!
  4. [pause] Washing the entire body with water.
  5. [pause] Permeating the hair (*Khilal al-Sha'r*)—making sure water reaches the roots of all hair. [pause] To perform it the Sunnah way, wash your hands first, clean your private parts, perform Wudu, pour water over your head three times rubbing the scalp, and then wash your right side and then your left side, ensuring no spot is left dry."
* **Concept Analogy:** Painting a wall: you must ensure the paint covers every single corner and crack, otherwise the job is incomplete.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Do women have to undo tight braids for Ghusl in the Maliki school? (Answer: No, as long as the water can reach the roots of the hair)."

## Slide 16: Tayammum — Purification with Earth
* **Slide Summary:** The permission and spiritual meaning of Tayammum.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Lower voice; speak with warmth and reflection]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] What if we need to pray, but there is no water? Or what if we are so sick or injured that using water would harm us? 
  
  Allah, out of His immense mercy, gave us **Tayammum**—dry purification using clean earth. [pause] This teaches us a beautiful spiritual lesson. [pause] In Ash'ari Aqidah, we know that water doesn't purify us because of its chemistry; it purifies us because *Allah commanded it to*. [pause] If Allah commands earth to purify us when water is gone, it works! Purity is an act of obedience, showing that Allah wants ease for us, not hardship."
* **Concept Analogy:** A backup power generator: when the main power line (water) goes down, the backup generator (earth) keeps the lights on.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "When is Tayammum allowed? (Answer: When there is no water, or when using water would cause physical harm)."

## Slide 17: The Steps and Substance of Tayammum
* **Slide Summary:** Defining Sa'id and the steps of Tayammum in the Maliki school.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Demonstrate the strikes and wipes physically]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] What substance can we use for Tayammum? In our Madhhab, we use **Sa'id**—which is anything that is part of the natural surface of the earth. [pause] This includes clean soil, sand, rocks, pebbles, or even dust. [pause] Here is how we do it:
  1. [pause] Make the **Intention** to make prayer permissible.
  2. [pause] Perform the **First Strike** by gently patting the earth or stone with both palms.
  3. **Wipe your face** completely once.
  4. [pause] Perform the **Second Strike** (which is Sunnah) and **wipe your hands** and forearms. [pause] Wiping to the wrists is Fard, and wiping up to the elbows is Sunnah. [pause] It is quick, clean, and simple!"
* **Concept Analogy:** Hand sanitizer: a quick, dry alternative to soap and water that allows you to keep going when you are on the move.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Can you make Tayammum on a clean, solid rock in the Maliki school? (Answer: Yes, because it is part of the natural surface of the earth)."

## Slide 19: Prayer Times & Cosmic Rhythms
* **Slide Summary:** Understanding how prayer times connect to the movement of the sun.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Point to a solar diagram or slide visual]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] Allah didn't set our prayer times by artificial clocks; He linked them to the **cosmic rhythms** of the sun!
  
  - Subh starts at true dawn and ends at sunrise.
  - Zuhr starts when the sun passes its highest point.
  - 'Asr starts when the shadow of an object equals its height.
  - Maghrib starts immediately after the sun sets.
  - 'Isha starts when the red twilight disappears from the sky. [pause] This connects our souls to the natural universe. [pause] In the Maliki school, we learn that each prayer has a *preferred* time when we should pray, and a *necessary* time for emergencies. [pause] We should always try to pray in the preferred window!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A train timetable: there is a preferred time when the train runs smoothly, and an emergency backup schedule if there is a delay.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What event in nature marks the start of the Maghrib prayer? (Answer: Sunset)."

## Slide 20: How We Stand in Prayer — The Position of the Arms
* **Slide Summary:** Introduces Qiyam and the concept of Sadl vs. Qabd.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Stand up to show the posture of Sadl (arms relaxed at sides)]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] When we stand for prayer, this is called **Qiyam**—which is an obligatory pillar of Salah. [pause] But where do we place our arms? 
  
  Some Muslims fold their hands over their chest or belly. [pause] This is called **Qabd**. [pause] But in the Maliki school, the preferred and Sunnah way for obligatory prayers is **Sadl**—letting your arms rest naturally at your sides. [pause] If you have never seen this before, it might surprise you! But it is a deeply authentic Sunnah. [pause] Let's stand up together and try it. [pause] Notice how relaxed and natural it feels to stand before Allah with your arms at your sides."
* **Concept Analogy:** Standing at attention: when soldiers stand respectfully before a leader, they stand with their arms naturally at their sides.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the term for praying with arms resting at the sides? (Answer: Sadl)."

## Slide 21: Understanding Sadl (Arms at the Sides)
* **Slide Summary:** The background of Sadl and the methodology of 'Amal Ahl al-Madinah.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with authority and respect for scholarship]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful] Why do Malikis perform Sadl? 
  
  It goes back to **'Amal Ahl al-Madinah**—the practice of the people of Madinah. [pause] Imam Malik lived in the city of the Prophet sallallahu alayhi wa sallam. [pause] He saw thousands of the children and grandchildren of the Companions praying. [pause] They all prayed with their arms at their sides. [pause] Imam Malik argued that this continuous, public practice was a stronger proof of how the Prophet sallallahu alayhi wa sallam prayed at the end of his life than a single written narration. [pause] He did allow clasping (*qabd*) in voluntary prayers if someone's arms got tired. [pause] Remember: both *sadl* and *qabd* are correct and valid Sunni ways. [pause] We respect everyone!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A living tradition: like learning a family game by watching your parents and grandparents play it, rather than just reading a rulebook.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Why did Imam Malik prefer the practice of the people of Madinah? (Answer: Because it was a living, continuous tradition inherited directly from the Companions)."
