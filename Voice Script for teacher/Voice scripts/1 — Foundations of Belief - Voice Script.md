# Voice Script: Foundations of Belief
**Topic:** Who is Allah? Names and Attributes, Pillars of Iman, Angels, Holy Books & Prophets, Qadar, and Maliki/Ash'ari Literacy.  
**Audience:** Parents/Teachers presenting to children aged 10–13.  
**Creed/Law Framework:** Ash'ari Aqidah & Maliki Fiqh.  
**Model Tuning:** ElevenLabs v3 with native expressive audio tags ([warmly], [thoughtful], [reverent], [pause], [short pause]).

## Slide 4: Knowing Allah through His Necessary Attributes (Sifat al-Wajibah)
* **Slide Summary:** Understanding the 20 necessary attributes of Allah across four categories (Nafsiyyah, Salbiyyah, Ma'ani, and Ma'nawiyyah).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with structured clarity; count categories cleanly]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[methodical, clear] To truly know Allah, scholars organized what the Quran and reason teach us into twenty necessary attributes, called **Sifaat al-Waajibah**. [pause] In Ash'ari creed, these twenty attributes are arranged into four clear groups. [pause] First: **Nafsiyyah**—the Personal Attribute, which is **Wujud**—the eternal Existence of Allah. [pause] Second: **Salbiyyah**—the Negative or Exclusionary attributes, which negate any flaw from Allah. [short pause] These include His beginninglessness (*Qidam*), [short pause] His endlessness (*Baqa'*), [short pause] His absolute difference from creation (*Mukhalafah*), [short pause] His self-subsistence (*Qiyam bi-Nafsih*), [short pause] and His absolute Oneness (*Wahdaniyyah*). [long pause] Third: **Sifaat al-Ma'ani**—the Qualitative attributes. [short pause] These are Allah's Divine Power (*Qudrah*), [short pause] Will (*Iradah*), [short pause] Knowledge (*'Ilm*), [short pause] Life (*Hayat*), [short pause] Hearing (*Sam'*), [short pause] Sight (*Basar*), [short pause] and Divine Speech (*Kalam*). [long pause] And fourth: **Sifaat al-Ma'nawiyyah**—the Active State attributes, [short pause] meaning that Allah is perpetually and actively Powerful, [short pause] Willful, [short pause] Knowing, [short pause] Living, [short pause] Hearing, [short pause] Seeing, [short pause] and Speaking. [pause] Remember: [short pause] Allah's hearing and seeing are not like ours. [short pause] He does not need ears, eyes, or instruments. [short pause] His attributes are perfect, eternal, and without limits."
* **Concept Analogy:** Our vision requires light, eyes, and distance. Allah's sight encompasses every atom in total darkness without any organ or limitation.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What are the four categories of the 20 necessary attributes in Ash'ari creed? (Answer: Nafsiyyah, Salbiyyah, Ma'ani, and Ma'nawiyyah)."

## Slide 7: Ash'ari Reading of Sacred Texts: Tafwid and Ta'wil
* **Slide Summary:** How Sunni scholarship approaches apparent anthropomorphic verses without compromising transcendence.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Adopt an intellectual, reassuring tone; illustrate balance]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[scholarly, thoughtful] In the Quran, we sometimes encounter phrases like the 'Hand'—*Yad*—of Allah, [short pause] the 'Face'—*Wajh*—of Allah, [short pause] or Allah 'establishing' over the Throne—*Al-Istiwa'*. [pause] Since we know beyond doubt that Allah is free from having a physical body or human parts, [short pause] how do orthodox Sunni scholars read these texts? [long pause] They follow two valid, noble methodologies: [pause] First: **Tafweed**—the way of the early generations, the *Salaf*. [short pause] In Tafweed, we affirm the sacred wording as revealed, [short pause] we strip away any physical or bodily meaning, [short pause] and we say: 'Allah knows best the true reality of His words.' [long pause] Second: **Ta'weel**—the method formalized by later scholars, the *Khalaf*. [short pause] Ta'weel provides a figurative, metaphorical interpretation consistent with classical Arabic language. [pause] For example: [short pause] 'Hand' is understood as Divine Power (*Qudrah*) or Divine Generosity (*Ni'mah*). [pause] And *Istiwa'* over the Throne is understood as Supreme Sovereignty and Control (*Istila'*). [pause] Both Tafweed and Ta'weel share the exact same foundation: [short pause] they completely protect our belief from *Tashbeeh*—[short pause]the error of comparing Allah to human beings."
* **Concept Analogy:** When someone says, 'Could you lend me a hand?', you don't expect them to literally cut off their hand; you know from language that they are offering help.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What do both Tafwid and Ta'wil agree on? (Answer: That Allah does not have a physical body, limbs, or human qualities)."

## Slide 17: Human Choice and the Ash'ari Concept of Kasb (Acquisition)
* **Slide Summary:** Resolving the relationship between Divine omnipotence and human accountability through Kasb.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Engage the audience intellectually; emphasize the moral responsibility]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[thoughtful, engaging] A crucial question often arises: [short pause] If Allah creates everything, why are we held accountable for our actions? [pause] The Ash'ari tradition provides an elegant, profound answer known as the doctrine of **Kasb**—Acquisition. [long pause] We distinguish between two realities: [pause] **Khalq**—Creation. [short pause] Allah alone creates the physical power, the circumstances, and the bodily movement. [pause] And **Kasb**—Acquisition. [short pause] The human being exercises their intention, willingness, and moral choice to direct that energy toward good or evil. [long pause] Think of giving charity: [short pause] Allah created the money, your arm muscles, and the paper notes. [short pause] But *you* directed your heart and intention to give that money to the poor. [pause] You 'acquired' that moral deed! [pause] This is exactly what Allah declares in Surah al-Baqarah: [short pause] *Laha ma kasabat wa 'alayha maktasabat*—[short pause]'For every soul is the good it acquired, [short pause] and against it is the evil it accrued.' [pause] We are rewarded and held accountable based on the choices of our hearts."
* **Concept Analogy:** A driver behind the steering wheel of an automobile: the manufacturer engineered the engine, wheels, and fuel, but the driver decides whether to steer toward safety or off the road.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the difference between Khalq and Kasb? (Answer: Khalq is Allah creating the power and action; Kasb is the human acquiring the deed through intention and choice)."

## Slide 18: Qadar is Not an Excuse for Bad Choices!

## Slide 21: More Maliki Rulings on Salah
* **Slide Summary:** Maliki rulings on beginning Al-Fatihah without vocalizing the Basmalah in Fard prayers, and the structure of Sujud al-Sahw.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with clear, practical instruction; contrast Qabli and Ba'di]`
* **Narrative Script (ElevenLabs v3 Ready):**
  "[practical, clear] Let us look at two additional distinctive rulings in Maliki Fiqh that every student should know. [pause] First: **The Recitation of the Basmalah**. [pause] The Imam begins recitation immediately with: [short pause] *Al-Hamdu lillahi Rabbil-'Alamin*. [pause] This is because in the Maliki tradition, the Basmalah is viewed as a sacred blessing opening the Surah, rather than an integral verse of Al-Fatihah itself. [long pause] Second: **Sujud al-Sahw**—the Prostrations of Forgetfulness. [pause] Maliki fiqh provides an exceptionally clear rule: [pause] If you *omitted* an emphasized sunnah from your prayer, [short pause] you perform two prostrations **Qabli**—[short pause]*before* making the final Taslim. [pause] It is a remarkably logical, elegant system of worship."
* **Concept Analogy:** A repair manual: if you accidentally forgot a bolt before closing the machine, you insert it before finishing (Qabli); if you ended up with an extra piece on the bench, you address it after the run is complete (Ba'di).
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "If someone accidentally missed reciting the Tashahhud in prayer, do they perform prostrations Qabli or Ba'di? (Answer: Qabli, because a sunnah element was omitted)."
