## A Guide for Teachers (Maliki Fiqh & Ash'ari Aqidah)

# Slide 1: Welcome & Course Overview
## Purification & Prayer: The Journey of the Soul
*   **Subject:** Islamic Studies (Fiqh and Aqidah)
*   **Fiqh School:** Maliki (*Madhhab*)
*   **Creed School:** Ash'ari
*   **Core Focus:** Understanding how to purify ourselves externally and internally to stand before our Creator.
*   **Key Themes:**
    *   Water and Purity (*Taharah*)
    *   Ablution (*Wudu*) and Ritual Bathing (*Ghusl*)
    *   Dry Ablution (*Tayammum*)
    *   The Five Daily Prayers (*Salah*)
    *   The Posture of Standing in Prayer (*Sadl*)

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Welcome & Objective:** Welcome the students. Explain that this unit will cover how we prepare physically and spiritually to meet Allah in prayer.
*   **Pedagogical Note:** Highlight that we are learning the rulings according to the **Maliki school** of jurisprudence (*fiqh*) and the **Ash'ari school** of theology (*aqidah*). Explain that learning a specific school helps build "fiqh literacy"—understanding that while there is diversity in Islamic practice (*ikhtilaf*), each school is built on sound, systematic interpretations of the Quran and Sunnah.
*   **Discussion Starter:** Ask students: *"Why do you think we wash ourselves before talking to Allah? What does cleaning the body do for the heart?"*

# Slide 2: The Creed (Aqidah) Context: Why Do We Worship?
## Understanding 'Ubudiyyah (Servantship)
*   **Allah is Al-Ghani (The Independent):**
    *   Allah does not *need* our prayers or our worship.
    *   He is completely free of any need (*istighna'*).
*   **We are the Needy ones (*Al-Fuqara'*):**
    *   We pray because we are in need of Allah's mercy, guidance, and peace.
*   **Prayer as Gratitude (*Shukr*):**
    *   Worship is our natural response to the blessings of existence, sight, life, and faith.
*   **The Pillar of Religion (*'Imad al-Din*):**
    *   Prayer is the ultimate connection (*silah*) between the servant (*'abd*) and the Creator.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Ash'ari Creed Integration:** Explain the concept of *Al-Ghani* (The Rich/Self-Sufficient). Under Ash'ari theology, Allah's attributes include absolute independence. His commands (like prayer) are not for His benefit, but are out of His mercy to guide us. 
*   **Concept check:** Make sure students understand that when they pray, they are receiving the benefit, not Allah.
*   **Language Tip:** Introduce the terms *'Ubudiyyah* (the state of being a loving servant to Allah) and *Al-Ghani* (The One who has no needs).

---

# Slide 3: The Importance of Purity (Taharah)
## External and Internal Cleanliness
*   **What is Taharah?**
    *   Linguistically: Cleanliness and purification.
    *   Lawfully (*Shar'an*): Removing ritual impurity (*hadath*) and physical filth (*najasah*).
*   **The Two Levels of Purity:**
    1.  **Outer Purity (*Taharah al-Zahir*):** Clean clothes, clean body, and clean prayer area.
    2.  **Inner Purity (*Taharah al-Batin*):** Purifying the heart from pride, jealousy, and disbelief (*shirk*).
*   **Key Maliki Principle:** Purification is a strict requirement (*shart*) for the validity of prayer.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Explanation:** Distinguish between *hadath* (an invisible spiritual state of impurity, like after sleeping or using the bathroom) and *najasah* (a physical substance like urine or blood). 
*   **Pedagogical Tip:** Use the analogy of a computer screen: *Najasah* is like dust on the screen (you can see it and wipe it off), while *hadath* is like the screen being turned off (it's clean, but you need to press the power button—perform Wudu—to make it active).
*   **Reflection:** How does physical cleanliness help us focus mentally and spiritually?

---

# Slide 4: Types of Water — Overview
## The Legal Categories of Water
*   Water is the primary tool for physical purification.
*   In the Maliki school, water is classified into three main categories:
    1.  **Al-Ma' al-Mutlaq / Al-Tahur:** Purifying water.
    2.  **Al-Ma' al-Tahir:** Pure, but non-purifying water.
    3.  **Al-Ma' al-Najis:** Impure water.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** We must know which water is valid for Wudu and Ghusl.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Overview:** Introduce the idea that not all liquids that look like water can be used for ritual purification.
*   **Fiqh Literacy:** Clarify the distinction between "pure" (*tahir*) and "purifying" (*tahur*). A substance can be clean in itself (like orange juice) but you cannot use it to remove ritual impurity (*hadath*).
*   **Activity:** Show three transparent cups of liquid (1. Plain tap water, 2. Water mixed with milk/tea, 3. Water with a drop of vinegar or dirt). Ask students to predict which ones can be used for Wudu.

---

# Slide 5: Category 1: Al-Ma' al-Mutlaq (Purifying Water)
## Pure in Itself and Purifying for Others
*   **Definition:** Water that remains in its natural state, just as Allah created it.
*   **Examples:**
    *   Rainwater (*ma' al-matar*)
    *   Sea water (*ma' al-bahr*)
    *   Well water (*ma' al-bi'r*)
    *   River water (*ma' al-nahr*)
    *   Melted snow or hail
*   **Status:** It is **Tahur** (both pure to touch/drink and valid for Wudu and Ghusl).

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Detailing the Ruling:** Even if well water contains some natural minerals or leaves that fell into it naturally, it is still classified as *Mutlaq* because these changes are natural and unavoidable.
*   **Hadith Reference:** Mention the Prophet's (pbuh) statement about the sea: *"Its water is purifying, and its dead meat is halal"* (Muwatta Malik).
*   **Key Question:** What makes well water or rain water different from apple juice, even though both are clean? (Answer: Well water is in its natural state and is called simply "water" without qualifiers).

---

# Slide 6: Category 2: Al-Ma' al-Tahir (Pure but Non-Purifying)
## When Pure Things Mix with Water
*   **Definition:** Purifying water (*mutlaq*) that has been mixed with a **pure** substance (e.g., milk, soap, rosewater, honey).
*   **The Key Maliki Ruling:**
    *   If the pure substance **changes** one of the three qualities of the water (color, taste, or smell) significantly so that it is no longer called just "water", it becomes **Tahir**.
*   **Examples:** Tea, coffee, rosewater, chicken soup.
*   **Status:** You **cannot** use it for Wudu or Ghusl, but it is clean to drink or cook with.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Maliki Specific Detail:** If a very small amount of soap or flour falls into water and doesn't change its name (people still look at it and call it "water", not "soap-water" or "dough-water"), it remains *Tahur* (purifying). The critical threshold is whether the *name* of the water has changed.
*   **Pedagogical Practice:** Emphasize this rule: "If it changes the name of the water, it changes its ruling."
*   **Discussion:** Why is it mercy that we cannot use juice or tea for Wudu? Imagine trying to pray with sticky juice on your arms!

---

# Slide 7: Category 3: Al-Ma' al-Najis (Impure Water)
## When Impurity Mixes with Water
*   **Definition:** Water that has been altered by an impurity (*najasah*), such as urine, blood, or sewage.
*   **The Maliki View on Water volume:**
    *   If the impurity changes the color, taste, or smell of the water, it is **Najis** (unanimously agreed).
    *   *What if an impurity falls into water but does NOT change its qualities?*
        *   **Maliki school:** The water remains **Tahur** (purifying) regardless of whether the volume is large or small. However, if the water is small in volume, it is disliked (*makruh*) to use if other water is available.
        *   *Contrast:* Other schools (like the Shafi'i school) rule that if the water is less than *Qullatayn* (approx. 200 liters), it automatically becomes *najis* even if it does not change.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Fiqh Literacy Focus:** Draw attention to this unique Maliki ruling. Imam Malik argued that water is purifying by default and only becomes impure if the impurity actually overpowers the water's qualities (taste, smell, color).
*   **Terminology:** *Qullatayn* refers to two large clay jars. Explain that while Shafi'is use this as a strict threshold, Malikis focus on the actual alteration (*taghyir*) of the water.
*   **Application:** If a drop of blood falls into a small bucket of water, and the water does not change color, smell, or taste, the Maliki ruling is that it is still valid for Wudu, though if you have another clean bucket, it is better (*makruh* to use the first).

---

# Slide 8: Step-by-Step Wudu (Obligations / Fara'id)
## The Core Pillars of Wudu
*   To have a valid Wudu, you must perform the **seven obligations (*Fara'id*)** according to the Maliki school:
    1.  **Intention (*Niyyah*)**
    2.  **Washing the Face**
    3.  **Washing the Arms** (including elbows)
    4.  **Wiping the ENTIRE Head (*Mash al-Ra's*)**
    5.  **Washing the Feet** (including ankles)
    6.  **Rubbing (*Dalk*)**
    7.  **Continuity (*Muwalat / Fawr*)**
*   Missing any of these seven invalidates the Wudu.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Introduction to Fara'id:** Explain that *Fara'id* (plural of *Fard*) are the absolute requirements of Wudu. If you miss one, even by mistake, your Wudu is not complete and your prayer is invalid.
*   **Memory Tool:** Encourage students to write down or memorize the number **7** for Maliki Wudu obligations. (Other schools have fewer; e.g., Shafi'is have 6, Hanafis have 4).
*   **Pronunciation:** Pronounce *Dalk* (dal-lam-kaf) and *Muwalat* (meem-waw-alif-lam-alif-ta) clearly.

---

# Slide 9: Focus on Wudu Fara'id: Niyyah, Face, and Arms
## Starting the Wudu Correctly
*   **1. Intention (*Niyyah*):**
    *   Must be made in the heart at the very moment you begin washing.
    *   Formulated as: intending to perform Wudu for prayer, or to remove ritual impurity.
*   **2. Washing the Face (*Ghasl al-Wajh*):**
    *   From the normal hairline to the bottom of the chin, and from earlobe to earlobe.
*   **3. Washing the Arms (*Ghasl al-Yadayn*):**
    *   From the fingertips up to and including the elbows.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Niyyah Details:** In the Maliki school, the intention must coincide with the start of the Wudu. If you wash your hands at the beginning as a habit without intending Wudu, and only make the intention when washing your face, the initial hand washing does not count as part of Wudu.
*   **Face Details:** Remind students that washing the beard is required. If the beard is thin, the skin underneath must be washed. If it is thick, washing the exterior is sufficient.
*   **Elbows:** Emphasize that the elbow is *included* in the wash, not excluded.

---

# Slide 10: Wiping the Entire Head (Mash al-Ra's)
## A Key Maliki Distinction
*   **4. Wiping the Head (*Mash*):**
    *   You must wipe your hands over the **entire head**—from the front hairline all the way to the nape of the neck.
*   **The Maliki View:**
    *   Wiping the *entire* head is an absolute obligation (*Fard*).
*   **Comparison with Other Schools:**
    *   **Shafi'i:** Wiping even a few hairs is sufficient.
    *   **Hanafi:** Wiping a quarter of the head is required.
    *   **Hanbali:** Wiping the entire head is also obligatory.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Fiqh Literacy:** Point out this major difference. Explain that the difference comes from how scholars interpreted the Arabic letter *Ba'* in the Quranic verse: *"Wamsahu bi-ru'usikum"* (Wipe [part of or all] your heads). 
    *   Imam Malik interpreted the *Ba'* as extra emphasis (*za'idah*), meaning "Wipe your heads [entirely]." 
    *   Imam al-Shafi'i interpreted it as *tab'eed* (partitive), meaning "Wipe a part of your heads."
*   **Demonstration:** Show how to place wet thumbs on the temples and index fingers on the front hairline, then slide the hands back to the neck. Note: The return motion (from back to front) is *Sunnah*, not *Fard*.

---

# Slide 11: Rubbing (Dalk) and Continuity (Muwalat)
## Distinctive Maliki Obligations
*   **6. Rubbing (*Dalk*):**
    *   Passing the hand over the body part while washing it, or immediately after pouring water, before it dries.
    *   *Why?* Merely letting water run over the skin is not considered "washing" (*ghasl*) in Maliki fiqh; it must be rubbed.
*   **7. Continuity (*Muwalat* / *Fawr*):**
    *   Performing the steps of Wudu one after another without any long interruption that would allow the previous limb to dry under normal weather conditions.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Rubbing (*Dalk*):** Explain that this is unique to the Maliki school. If someone just stands under a faucet and lets water pour over their arms without rubbing them, their Wudu is invalid according to Imam Malik.
*   **Continuity (*Muwalat*):** Explain that if someone starts Wudu, washes their face, and then stops to answer a phone call for 10 minutes until their face dries, they must start Wudu all over again.
*   **Exception:** If someone runs out of water and goes to get some immediately, they can continue where they left off, provided the delay was not unreasonably long.

---

# Slide 12: Sunnan and Fada'il of Wudu
## Enhancing Our Purification
*   **Sunnahs of Wudu (High Sunnahs):**
    *   Washing the hands to the wrists at the beginning.
    *   Rinsing the mouth (*Madmadah*).
    *   Sniffing water into the nose (*Istinshaq*) and blowing it out (*Istinthar*).
    *   Returning the wipe of the head (from the neck back to the front).
    *   Wiping the ears inside and out.
    *   Renewing the water for the ears.
    *   **The Order (*Tarteeb*):** Keeping the obligatory actions in their correct order. (In Maliki fiqh, *tarteeb* is a Sunnah, not a Fard, but skipping it deliberately is disliked).
*   **Recommended Acts (*Fada'il* / *Mustahabbat*):**
    *   Saying *Bismillah* at the start.
    *   Using the tooth-stick (*Siwak*).
    *   Washing three times (the first wash is Fard, second and third are Fada'il).

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Clarifying Tarteeb:** Note that in the Shafi'i and Hanbali schools, *Tarteeb* is a Fard (obligation). In the Maliki school, it is a Sunnah. This means that if someone accidentally washes their feet before wiping their head, their Wudu is still technically valid, though they have missed a Sunnah. They should correct it if they remember during the Wudu.
*   **Class Activity:** Ask students: *"If Wudu order is a Sunnah, why should we still try to keep it in order?"* Explain that following the Sunnah brings immense reward and aligns us with the practice of the Prophet (pbuh).

---

# Slide 13: Ghusl — The Ritual Bath
## Restoring Major Ritual Purity
*   **What is Ghusl?**
    *   Washing the entire body with purifying water (*mutlaq*) with a specific intention.
*   **When is it Required?**
    1.  **Major Impurity (*Janabah*):** Due to marital intimacy or physical emissions.
    2.  **Completion of Menstruation (*Hayd*):** For women.
    3.  **Completion of Post-Natal Bleeding (*Nifas*):** For women.
    4.  **Death:** Washing the deceased believer (*Ghusl al-Mayyit*).
*   **Status:** An absolute obligation to perform prayer and enter the mosque.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Pedagogical Sensitivity:** Discuss these topics maturely. Frame Ghusl as a blessing that transitions a believer from a state of ritual restriction back to full connection in worship.
*   **Vocabulary:** Define *Hadath al-Akbar* (major ritual impurity, which requires Ghusl) versus *Hadath al-Asghar* (minor ritual impurity, which only requires Wudu).

---

# Slide 14: Steps and Obligations of Ghusl
## The Maliki Method of Ghusl
*   **The Obligations (*Fara'id*) of Ghusl (5 items):**
    1.  **Intention (*Niyyah*):** To lift the major impurity.
    2.  **Continuity (*Muwalat*):** Not pausing so long that part of the body dries.
    3.  **Rubbing (*Dalk*):** Rubbing the entire body with the hand.
    4.  **Washing the entire body:** Ensuring water reaches every part.
    5.  **Permeating the hair (*Khilal al-Sha'r*):** Rubbing water to the roots of all hair (head, beard, etc.).
*   **How to do it (Sunnah Method):**
    *   Wash hands, wash private parts, perform Wudu (except feet, or complete it), pour water over the head three times rubbing the scalp, wash the right side of the body, then the left side, ensuring no spot is missed.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Dalk in Ghusl:** Emphasize that rubbing is required. If a person just stands under a shower without rubbing their skin with their hands, their Ghusl is invalid according to the Maliki school.
*   **Difficult Areas:** Remind students to pay attention to hidden areas like the navel, underarms, backs of knees, and between the toes.
*   **Hair:** Explain that women do not need to undo tight braids for Ghusl in the Maliki school, *provided* the water can reach the roots of the hair. If the water cannot reach the roots, the braids must be undone.

---

# Slide 15: Tayammum — Purification with Earth
## Allah's Mercy When Water is Unavailable
*   **What is Tayammum?**
    *   Dry purification using clean, natural earth.
*   **When is it Allowed?**
    *   **Lack of Water:** No water is nearby, or the water available is only enough for drinking.
    *   **Inability to Use Water:** Due to severe illness, wound, or extreme cold that would cause harm, and there is no way to heat it.
*   **Spiritual Lesson:**
    *   Tayammum teaches us that purification is primarily a spiritual act of obedience. If the physical means (water) are missing, we still submit to the ritual using earth.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Spiritual Insight (Ash'ari):** Connect this to Aqidah. Allah is the Creator of both water and earth. He is the One Who purifies us; the water and earth are merely means (*asbab*). When the primary means (water) is removed, Allah makes the secondary means (earth) valid. This shows that the power to purify comes from Allah's command, not the physical properties of water.
*   **Quranic Verse:** Mention Surah al-Ma'idah (5:6): *"And if you are ill or on a journey... and find no water, then perform Tayammum with clean earth..."*

---

# Slide 16: The Steps and Substance of Tayammum
## What is "Sa'id" and How is it Wiped?
*   **The Ground Substance (*Sa'id*):**
    *   In the Maliki school, *Sa'id* is defined as anything that is part of the natural surface of the earth.
    *   This includes: Clean soil/dirt, sand, natural stone/rocks, pebbles, and dust.
*   **The Step-by-Step Method:**
    1.  **Intention (*Niyyah*):** Intending to make prayer permissible (*istibahat al-salah*).
    2.  **First Strike:** Gently pat the earth/stone with both palms.
    3.  **Wipe the Face:** Wipe the entire face once.
    4.  **Second Strike (Sunnah) / Wipe the Hands:** Wipe the hands and forearms. In the Maliki school, wiping to the wrists is Fard, and wiping up to the elbows is Sunnah.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Maliki Scope of Sa'id:** Point out that Malikis are very flexible about the substance. You can make Tayammum on a solid rock or clean sand, whereas some other schools require soil that has loose dust that clings to the hands.
*   **First Strike / Second Strike:** Clarify that hitting the ground once is Fard. Wiping up to the wrists is Fard. Hitting a second time and wiping up to the elbows is Sunnah in the Maliki school.
*   **Wiping Method:** Show how to wipe the right arm first (using the left hand) and then the left arm (using the right hand).

---

# Slide 17: The Five Daily Prayers (Salah)
## The Pillar of Religion (Imad al-Din)
*   **Why is Salah the Pillar?**
    *   The Prophet (pbuh) said: *"The head of the matter is Islam, its pillar is the prayer, and its peak is Jihad"* (Tirmidhi).
*   **The Metaphor of the Pillar:**
    *   Just like a tent collapses if the main central pole (*'imad*) is pulled out, a person's practice of Islam cannot stand without Salah.
*   **The Five Obligatory Prayers (*Al-Salawat al-Khams*):**
    1.  **Subh / Fajr** (Dawn)
    2.  **Zuhr** (Midday)
    3.  **'Asr** (Afternoon)
    4.  **Maghrib** (Sunset)
    5.  **'Isha** (Night)

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Discussion:** Why do you think prayer is called a "pillar"? What happens to our day when we miss a prayer?
*   **Term Check:** Explain that in North Africa and West Africa (heavily Maliki regions), the dawn prayer is often called **Subh** for the two obligatory units, while the term **Fajr** is sometimes used specifically for the highly recommended two Sunnah units performed just before it. We will use them interchangeably for the obligatory dawn prayer here, but mention this regional terminology.

---

# Slide 18: Prayer Times & Cosmic Rhythms
## Aligning Our Day with Creation
*   Each prayer has a specific window of time linked to the movement of the sun:
    *   **Subh (Fajr):** Starts at true dawn (*al-fajr al-sadiq*) and ends at sunrise.
    *   **Zuhr:** Starts when the sun passes its highest point (zenith/*zawal*) and ends when the shadow of an object equals its height.
    *   **'Asr:** Starts when Zuhr ends and ends when the sun sets (preferred time ends when sun turns yellow).
    *   **Maghrib:** Starts immediately after sunset and ends when the red twilight (*al-shafaq al-ahmar*) disappears.
    *   **'Isha:** Starts when the red twilight disappears and ends at dawn (preferred time ends at the first third of the night).

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Understanding Times:** In Maliki fiqh, there are two types of time for each prayer:
    1.  *Ikhtiyari* (Preferred time): The window in which you should pray under normal circumstances.
    2.  *Daruri* (Necessary/Emergency time): The window for those with valid excuses (like sleeping, illness, menstruation ending) to pray before the prayer is considered missed (*qada'*).
*   **Cosmic Connection:** Point out that Islam connects our worship directly to nature. We look at the sun and shadows, connecting our souls to the natural cycles created by Allah.

---

# Slide 19: How We Stand in Prayer — The Position of the Arms
## Standing (Qiyam) and Sadl
*   **Qiyam (Standing):**
    *   Qiyam is an obligatory pillar (*Rukn*) of the obligatory prayers.
*   **Where do we put our arms?**
    *   There are two main practices among Muslims during standing:
        1.  **Sadl:** Letting the arms rest naturally at the sides of the body.
        2.  **Qabd:** Clasping the right hand over the left hand/wrist on the chest or below the navel.
*   **The Maliki View:**
    *   In the dominant Maliki opinion, **Sadl** is the preferred and sunnah way to stand during obligatory prayers (*Fara'id*).

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Clarification:** Many students may see Muslims clasping their hands and assume this is the only way to pray. Explain that **Sadl** is a very ancient, valid practice.
*   **Diversity in Fiqh:** Emphasize that all four Sunni schools are correct. Shafi'is, Hanafis, and Hanbalis perform *Qabd* (with slight variations on placement). Malikis perform *Sadl*. 
*   **Class Activity:** Ask students to try both postures. Ask: *"How does it feel to stand in Sadl? Does it feel relaxed and natural?"* Explain that both ways are inherited from the Prophet (pbuh).

---

# Slide 20: Understanding Sadl (Arms at the Sides)
## The Background of the Maliki Practice
*   *Why do Malikis perform Sadl?*
    1.  **The Practice of Madinah (*'Amal Ahl al-Madinah*):** Imam Malik lived in Madinah, the city of the Prophet (pbuh). He observed that the scholars and descendants of the Companions prayed with their arms at their sides.
    2.  **The Mudawwanah:** The primary textbook of Maliki fiqh quotes Imam Malik saying that he disliked clasping the hands in obligatory prayers, preferring that the arms rest naturally.
*   **Nafl (Voluntary) Prayers:**
    *   Imam Malik permitted clasping (*qabd*) in voluntary prayers if the prayer is long and the person needs to rest their arms.

---
**Teacher Notes:**
*   **Contextual Depth:** Explain the concept of *'Amal Ahl al-Madinah* (the practice of the people of Madinah). For Imam Malik, the continuous, inherited practice of thousands of people in Madinah who learned directly from their fathers (who learned from the Companions) was a stronger source of law than a single isolated narration (*hadith ahad*).
*   **Respecting Differences:** Reiterate that we do not look down on others who pray differently. A Maliki student standing in *sadl* next to a Shafi'i student in *qabd* are both praying correctly according to authentic traditions.
