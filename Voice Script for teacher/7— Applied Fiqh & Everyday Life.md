# Applied Fiqh & Everyday Life: Slide Deck for Teachers

This slide deck is designed to guide teachers through the core topics of daily applied fiqh. It emphasizes practical habits, vocabulary, and flags specific rulings from the Maliki school of jurisprudence and the Ash'ari school of creed.

---

## Slide 1: Welcome & Course Overview
### Visual Outline
*   **Topic:** Applied Fiqh & Everyday Life
*   **Key Areas Covered:**
    1.  *Ḥalāl* and *Ḥarām* in food and choices.
    2.  Modesty (*Ḥayā’*) and boundaries of dress (*‘Awrah*).
    3.  Social manners: Eating, greeting, and visiting.
    4.  Marketplace ethics: Honesty and contracts.
    5.  Introduction to the sacred rites of Hajj and Umrah.
*   **Goal:** Moving from knowing rulings to living them as spiritual habits.

### Speaker Notes
*   Welcome the students. Explain that fiqh is not just a set of dry rules to memorize; it is the practical guide to aligning our everyday actions with the will of Allah, our Creator.
*   Introduce the concept that every action we take—from what we eat to how we greet a friend or buy a snack—is an opportunity for worship (*‘Ibādah*) if done with the right intention (*Niyyah*).
*   Mention that throughout this unit, we will study these concepts through the lens of the Maliki school of jurisprudence (*Madhhab*) and the Ash'ari school of creed (*‘Aqīdah*), so students understand the consistency and lineage of their practice.

## Slide 2: Fiqh as an Act of Worship (Creed Context)
### Visual Outline
*   **The Lawgiver (*Al-Ḥākim*):** Allah alone has the right to declare things permissible (*Ḥalāl*) or forbidden (*Ḥarām*).
*   **Human intellect (*‘Aql*):** Understands and discovers Allah's laws but does not create them.
*   **Our Purpose:** Aligning our choices with the Divine Command (*Al-Ḥukm al-Shar‘ī*).
*   **Creed Focus (Ash'ari):** Goodness (*Ḥusn*) and badness (*Qubḥ*) of actions are defined by revelation (*Shar‘*), not just independent human logic.

### Speaker Notes
*   *Ash'ari Connection:* Explain to the students that in Ash'ari theology, we believe that ultimate moral goodness and badness are determined by Allah's revelation, not merely by what human intellect finds pleasant or unpleasant. Allah is the Creator of all causes and effects, and His wisdom is absolute.
*   Therefore, when we follow fiqh, we are submitting to Allah's decree. Fiqh means "deep understanding"—specifically, understanding our responsibilities before Allah.
*   Encourage students to see that studying fiqh is a way of getting closer to Allah by learning what pleases Him.

## Slide 3: The Scale of Actions (*Al-Aḥkām al-Khamsah*)
### Visual Outline
*   **1. Obligatory (*Farḍ* / *Wājib*):** Rewarded for doing, punished for omitting.
*   **2. Recommended (*Mandūb* / *Mustaḥabb* / *Sunnah*):** Rewarded for doing, no punishment for omitting.
*   **3. Permissible (*Mubāḥ* / *Ḥalāl*):** Neutral; no inherent reward or punishment (unless accompanied by intention).
*   **4. Disliked (*Makrūh*):** Rewarded for avoiding, no punishment for doing.
*   **5. Forbidden (*Ḥarām*):** Rewarded for avoiding, punished for doing.

### Speaker Notes
*   Review the five rulings of sacred law (*Al-Aḥkām al-Khamsah*).
*   *Key Concept:* Emphasize that most things in our daily lives start as *Mubāḥ* (permissible). For example, eating an apple or sleeping.
*   Show students how a *Mubāḥ* action can be transformed into a rewarded action (*Mandūb* or *Wājib*) through *Niyyah* (intention). For example, eating to gain strength to worship Allah, or sleeping to rest the body for morning prayer (*Fajr*).
*   This illustrates how a Muslim's entire day can become worship.

## Slide 4: Halal & Haram in Food and Choices
### Visual Outline
*   **General Rule:** Everything is permissible by default (*Al-Aṣl fī al-ashyā’ al-ibāḥah*) unless explicitly prohibited by revelation.
*   **What makes food *Ḥalāl*?**
    *   Cleanliness, safety, and source.
    *   Lawful animals slaughtered according to Islamic guidelines.
*   **What is *Ḥarām*?**
    *   Pork, carrion (dead meat), flowing blood, intoxicants (like alcohol), and food sacrificed to other than Allah.

### Speaker Notes
*   Discuss the primary Quranic text: "O you who believe! Eat of the good things that We have provided for you..." (Surah al-Baqarah, 2:172).
*   Explain that Allah only forbids things that are harmful (*khabā’ith*) to our physical bodies, our minds, or our spirits.
*   Introduce the concept of *Ṭayyib* (wholesome/pure), explaining that food should not only be legally halal but also clean, ethical, and wholesome.

---

## Slide 5: The Fiqh of Slaughtering (*Dhakāh*)
### Visual Outline
*   **Slaughtering (*Dhakāh*):** The method to make land animals lawful to eat.
*   **The Cut:** Must sever the windpipe (*Ḥulqūm*), the gullet (*Marī’*), and the two jugular veins (*Wadajayn*).
*   **The Name of Allah (*Tasmiyah*):** Saying *Bismillah* before the cut.
*   **Maliki Nuance on *Tasmiyah*:**
    *   It is obligatory (*Wājib*) if remembered.
    *   If forgotten, the animal is still *Ḥalāl* to eat.
    *   If intentionally left out, the meat is *Ḥarām*.

### Speaker Notes
*   Explain the anatomical requirements of *Dhakāh*: The cut must be swift with a sharp knife to minimize pain, showing mercy to the animal.
*   > [!NOTE] **Maliki Ruling:** The Maliki school holds that the *Tasmiyah* (saying *Bismillah*) is a condition of validity for slaughter, but only if one remembers it. If a Muslim forgets to say it, the meat is halal out of mercy and excuse. However, if they intentionally refuse to say it, the meat cannot be eaten. Contrast this with the Shafi'i school (where *Tasmiyah* is recommended, so even if intentionally left out, it is halal though disliked) and the Hanafi school (similar to Malikis, but with slightly different definitions).
*   Teach students to check their sources of meat to ensure it is certified halal.

## Slide 6: The Bounty of the Sea: Seafood Rules
### Visual Outline
*   **Quranic Basis:** "Lawful to you is game from the sea and its food..." (Surah al-Ma'idah, 5:96).
*   **Maliki Position on Sea Creatures:**
    *   **Extremely permissive:** All creatures that live exclusively in water are *Ḥalāl*.
    *   No exceptions for shape or form (e.g., sea dogs, sea pigs, crabs, sharks, and shellfish).
    *   No requirement for ritual slaughter for sea animals.
    *   Even dead sea creatures found floating are lawful.

### Speaker Notes
*   > [!NOTE] **Maliki Ruling:** The Maliki school has the most open position regarding seafood. Unlike the Hanafi school (which only permits fish and considers other sea creatures like crabs or lobsters disliked or forbidden), or other schools that have exceptions for certain creatures, the Maliki school permits *all* water-dwelling animals without exception.
*   Imam Malik was once asked about "sea pigs" (porpoises/dolphins) and he replied that they are halal because they are from the sea.
*   Explain that this shows the flexibility and mercy of the law, varying according to geography and cultures.

## Slide 7: Modesty (*Ḥayā’*) as a Core Virtue
### Visual Outline
*   **Definition:** *Ḥayā’* is spiritual modesty, shyness, and a feeling of restraint before Allah and His creation.
*   **Prophetic Hadith:** "Modesty is a branch of faith (*Īmān*)."
*   **Rooted in Creed:** Knowing that Allah is *Al-Raqīb* (The All-Watchful) and *Al-Baṣīr* (The All-Seeing).
*   **Actionable Modesty:**
    *   Modesty in speech (avoiding foul language).
    *   Modesty in behavior (respectful posture and manners).
    *   Modesty in dress (covering the *‘Awrah*).

### Speaker Notes
*   Connect *Ḥayā’* to *‘Aqīdah* (creed): If a student truly believes that Allah sees and hears everything, they will naturally develop *Ḥayā’* in their hearts.
*   Emphasize that modesty is not about feeling ashamed of ourselves, but about recognizing our dignity as creations of Allah and showing respect to our Creator and others.
*   Explain that modesty is for both males and females; it is not a female-only concept.

---

## Slide 8: The Dress of Modesty: *‘Awrah* for Men
### Visual Outline
*   ***‘Awrah*:** The parts of the body that must be covered in front of others and during prayer.
*   **General male limit:** From the navel (*Surrah*) to the knee (*Rukbah*).
*   **Maliki Specifics:**
    *   The navel and the knee themselves are *not* part of the *‘Awrah*.
    *   The thigh is considered *‘Awrah Khafīfah* (light/minor awrah).
    *   The private parts (front and back) are *‘Awrah Mughallaẓah* (heavy/major awrah).
    *   Exposing the thigh in public is strongly disliked (*Makrūh*) and must be avoided.

### Speaker Notes
*   > [!NOTE] **Maliki Ruling:** In the Maliki school, the *‘Awrah* of a man is divided into "heavy" (*Mughallaẓah*)—the private organs—and "light" (*Khafīfah*)—the thighs and surrounding area. While the navel and knee are not included, exposing the thighs in public is still forbidden or highly disliked depending on the context, and it is a breach of *Ḥayā’*.
*   For prayer, a man must cover at least his heavy awrah for the prayer to be valid, but covering from navel to knee is required to avoid sin, and wearing a garment over the shoulders is highly recommended (*Mandūb*).
*   Teach boys that wearing short shorts that reveal the thighs violates the basic rules of Islamic dress code.

---

## Slide 9: The Dress of Modesty: *‘Awrah* for Women
### Visual Outline
*   **In front of unrelated men (*Ajānib*):** The entire body except the face and the hands (from the wrists down).
*   **In the Prayer (*Ṣalāh*):** Same as above (face and hands exposed).
*   **Maliki Nuance on the Feet:**
    *   The feet are considered *‘Awrah Khafīfah* (light awrah) in prayer.
    *   If a woman prays with her feet exposed, her prayer is valid, but it is disliked (*Makrūh*).
    *   It is recommended (*Mustaḥabb*) to repeat the prayer within its time block if the feet were uncovered.

### Speaker Notes
*   Explain the general ruling: Modest clothing for women must be loose (not skin-tight) and thick (not see-through).
*   > [!NOTE] **Maliki Ruling:** The Maliki school holds that a woman's feet are part of the light *‘Awrah* (*‘Awrah Khafīfah*). Unlike other schools (like the Hanafi school which says feet are not awrah at all, or the Shafi'i/Hanbali schools which say feet are heavy awrah and prayer is invalid if uncovered), the Malikis take a middle path: the prayer is valid if they are uncovered, but she has left a recommendation and it is preferred to cover them or repeat the prayer if the time of that prayer has not expired.
*   Remind students that dress codes protect dignity and foster a society based on character, not physical attraction.

---

## Slide 10: Modesty in Character and Demeanor
### Visual Outline
*   **Spiritual Modesty:** The outward dress is a reflection of the inner heart.
*   **Lowering the Gaze (*Ghaḍḍ al-Baṣar*):**
    *   An obligation for both men and women.
    *   Guarding the eyes from looking at the *‘Awrah* of others or looking with desire.
*   **Avoiding Arrogance in Dress:**
    *   Do not wear clothes to show off, feel superior, or seek fame (*Thiyāb al-Shuhrah*).
    *   Keep clothes clean, neat, and simple.

### Speaker Notes
*   Teach students that covering the physical *‘Awrah* is incomplete without covering the spiritual *‘Awrah* of bad character.
*   Discuss *Ghaḍḍ al-Baṣar*: It means not staring at people, avoiding inappropriate media, and looking away immediately if we accidentally see something inappropriate.
*   Explain that wearing expensive brands just to make others feel poor or look down on them is a major sin of the heart (*Kibr* / pride).

## Slide 11: Manners of Eating: Transforming Fiqh to Habit
### Visual Outline
*   **Intention (*Niyyah*):** Eating to gain strength to worship Allah.
*   **Sunnah Actions of Eating:**
    1.  Washing hands before and after.
    2.  Saying *Bismillah* (In the name of Allah) at the start.
    3.  Eating with the right hand.
    4.  Eating from what is directly in front of you.
    5.  Praising Allah at the end: Saying *Alhamdulillah*.
*   **Moderation:** Not filling the stomach completely (leaving space for food, water, and air).

### Speaker Notes
*   Explain that eating is an action we perform multiple times a day. If we apply the Sunnah, we earn thousands of rewards every week just by eating!
*   Remind them of the Hadith: "If one of you eats, he should eat with his right hand, and if he drinks, he should drink with his right hand, for Satan eats and drinks with his left hand" (Muslim).
*   Introduce the rule of moderation: A third for food, a third for drink, and a third for air. This promotes physical health and spiritual alertness.

## Slide 12: Manners of Greeting: The Key to Love
### Visual Outline
*   **The Islamic Greeting:** *As-Salāmu ‘Alaykum wa Raḥmatullāhi wa Barakātuh* (Peace, mercy, and blessings of Allah be upon you).
*   **Rulings of *Salām*:**
    *   Initiating the greeting: Recommended Sunnah (*Sunnah Mu’akkadah*).
    *   Responding to the greeting: Obligatory (*Farḍ Kifāyah* for a group, *Farḍ ‘Ayn* for an individual).
*   **Who greets whom? (Sunnah Order):**
    *   The young greet the elderly.
    *   The walker greets the sitter.
    *   The smaller group greets the larger group.

### Speaker Notes
*   Explain the meaning of *As-Salāmu ‘Alaykum*: You are offering safety and peace to the other person, promising that you will not harm them with your tongue or your hand.
*   Discuss the reward system: Saying *As-Salāmu ‘Alaykum* gives 10 rewards; adding *wa Raḥmatullāh* gives 20; adding *wa Barakātuh* gives 30.
*   Encourage students to spread peace (*Ifshā’ al-Salām*) to those they know and those they do not know, as instructed by the Prophet ﷺ.

---

## Slide 13: Manners of Visiting: Entering Homes (*Isti’dhān*)
### Visual Outline
*   **Seeking Permission (*Isti’dhān*):** Never enter a home without explicit permission.
*   **The Three-Time Rule:** Knock or ring the bell up to three times. If there is no answer, you must leave.
*   **Standing to the Side:** Do not stand directly in front of the door (to avoid looking inside when it opens).
*   **Identifying Yourself:** When asked "Who is it?", state your name. Do not just say "Me!" or "It's me!"
*   **Visiting Times:** Avoid visiting during times of rest (early morning, afternoon rest, late at night).

### Speaker Notes
*   Read Surah an-Nur (24:27): "O you who believe! Do not enter houses other than your own until you have asked permission and greeted those in them..."
*   Explain the wisdom: Homes are sanctuaries of privacy. People dress comfortably inside and may not want others to see them. Standing to the side of the door protects their privacy.
*   Address the common habit of saying "Me!" when someone knocks. Tell them the Prophet ﷺ disliked this because it does not clarify who is at the door.

## Slide 14: Introduction to Fiqh of the Marketplace
### Visual Outline
*   **Trade (*Tijārah*):** Halal by default, and a noble profession (the Prophet ﷺ was a merchant).
*   **Core Principles of Islamic Commerce:**
    1.  Honesty and truthfulness.
    2.  Mutual consent (*Tarāḍin*).
    3.  Clear terms (No cheating or deception).
*   **Major Prohibitions:**
    *   *Ribā* (Usury/Interest).
    *   *Gharar* (Excessive uncertainty or ambiguity in a transaction).

### Speaker Notes
*   Explain that Islam is a complete way of life. It does not separate the mosque from the market. The same God we worship in prayer is the God we must obey when buying and selling.
*   Introduce *Ribā* (usury/interest) and explain that it is strictly forbidden because it exploits the poor and makes money from money without real work.
*   Introduce *Gharar* (ambiguity) and explain that you cannot sell something you do not own, or something that is undefined (like "the birds in the sky" or "the fish in the sea").

---

## Slide 15: Honesty & Deception in Trade
### Visual Outline
*   **The Prophetic Warning:** "Whoever deceives us is not of us."
*   **Honesty in Quality:**
    *   Disclosing any defects (*‘Ayb*) in the item being sold.
    *   Never hiding the bad parts of the product under the good parts.
*   **Honesty in Quantity:**
    *   Using accurate scales and measurements.
    *   Quranic warning to the "defrauders" (*Al-Muṭaffifīn*).
*   **Cheating in Games & Online:** Account trading, buying fake items, and scamming are all forms of forbidden transaction.

### Speaker Notes
*   Share the story of the Prophet ﷺ passing by a food seller. He put his hand inside a pile of grain and felt wetness. He asked, "What is this, O seller?" The man said, "It was rained upon." The Prophet ﷺ said, "Why did you not place the wet grain on top so people could see it? Whoever deceives us is not of us."
*   Apply this to the modern world: selling a broken video game, using fake photos to sell a toy online, or lying about how old an item is are all forms of haram deception.

---

## Slide 16: When is a Deal Final? (Khiyar al-Majlis)
### Visual Outline
*   **Concept:** *Khiyār al-Majlis* is the option to cancel a sale as long as the buyer and seller are still in the same physical space.
*   **Maliki Position:**
    *   **The Maliki school rejects *Khiyār al-Majlis*.**
    *   A contract is binding (*Lāzim*) the moment the verbal agreement (Offer and Acceptance) is completed.
    *   You cannot cancel the deal just because you are still in the shop, unless there is a defect or a prior condition of return (*Khiyār al-Shart*).
*   **Proof:** The practice of the people of Madinah (*‘Amal Ahl al-Madīnah*).

### Speaker Notes
*   > [!NOTE] **Maliki Ruling:** This is a very famous point of difference in fiqh. Other schools (Shafi'i and Hanbali) say that if you buy an item, you can change your mind and cancel the transaction for any reason as long as you are still standing in the store. However, the Maliki school holds that once the words "I sell this to you" and "I buy it" are spoken (or money is exchanged), the contract is immediately locked and binding (*Lāzim*).
*   *Why?* Imam Malik observed that the scholars and people of Madinah did not practice this "option of the session" in their markets. For Malikis, the practice of Madinah (*‘Amal Ahl al-Madīnah*) is a powerful source of law because it represents the continuous, inherited practice of the Companions.
*   Explain that this teaches us to be decisive and serious before we agree to buy or sell anything.

## Slide 17: Hajj & Umrah: The Sacred Journey
### Visual Outline
*   **Definitions:**
    *   *Ḥajj:* The annual pilgrimage to Makkah during the sacred month of *Dhū al-Ḥijjah* (a Pillar of Islam).
    *   *‘Umrah:* The minor pilgrimage which can be performed at any time of the year.
*   **The Rulings:**
    *   *Ḥajj* is obligatory once in a lifetime for those who are able.
    *   *‘Umrah* is a confirmed Sunnah (*Sunnah Mu’akkadah*) in the Maliki school.
*   **Capability (*Istiṭā‘ah*):** Having the physical health, safe passage, and financial means.

### Speaker Notes
*   Explain the difference between Hajj and Umrah. Hajj is one of the Five Pillars of Islam.
*   Discuss *Istiṭā‘ah* (capability). Hajj is only required of those who can afford it without going into debt or leaving their families without support.
*   Explain that Hajj is a journey of the soul, erasing all past sins if accepted by Allah (*Ḥajj Mabrūr*).

## Slide 18: State of Ihram & Maliki Positions on Preparation
### Visual Outline
*   ***Iḥrām*:** The state of sacred consecration.
*   **Outward clothing:** Two white sheets for men; regular modest dress for women.
*   **Inward state:** Intentional entry, reciting the *Talbiyah*.
*   **Maliki Rulings on *Iḥrām*:**
    *   **The obligation of Hajj is immediate (*‘Alā al-Fawr*):** Once you are able, you must go; you cannot delay it to old age without sin.
    *   Ghusl for *Iḥrām* is highly recommended, even for women who are not praying.

### Speaker Notes
*   Discuss the rules of *Iḥrām*. Once in *Iḥrām*, certain actions become forbidden: cutting hair, clipping nails, using scented perfume, hunting animals, and covering the head (for men) or face (for women).
*   > [!NOTE] **Maliki Ruling:** The Maliki school holds that Hajj is an immediate obligation (*‘Alā al-Fawr*). If a person has the health and money to go, they are not allowed to say, "I will wait 10 years until I am older." Delaying it without a valid excuse is considered a sin.
*   Contrast this with schools that allow delaying (*‘Alā al-Tarakhī*). This teaches us to prioritize Allah's commands.

## Slide 19: The Rites of Tawaf (Purity and Continuity)
### Visual Outline
*   ***Ṭawāf*:** Circumambulating the Ka'bah seven times counter-clockwise, starting at the Black Stone (*Al-Ḥajar al-Aswad*).
*   **Maliki Requirements for *Ṭawāf*:**
    1.  **Purity (*Ṭahārah*):** Being in Wudu and free of physical impurity is a condition of validity (*Sharṭ Ṣiḥḥah*).
    2.  If Wudu breaks during *Ṭawāf*, the *Ṭawāf* is invalidated and must be restarted.
    3.  **Continuity (*Muwālāh*):** The seven rounds must be completed without long interruptions.

### Speaker Notes
*   Explain that *Ṭawāf* is like prayer; it requires the same state of physical and ritual cleanliness.
*   > [!NOTE] **Maliki Ruling:** In the Maliki school, ritual purity (Wudu) is an absolute condition for *Ṭawāf*. If your Wudu breaks during the 4th round, you cannot just go make Wudu and resume from the 5th. You must go make Wudu and restart the entire *Ṭawāf* from the beginning (unless the break was extremely brief and you made Wudu immediately nearby, but the general rule is strict continuity).
*   This highlights the importance of maintaining focus and preparation before starting this major act of worship.

---

## Slide 20: Sa'y, Arafat, and Stoning the Jamarat
### Visual Outline
*   ***Sa‘y*:** Walking seven times between the hills of *Ṣafā* and *Marwah*.
    *   Maliki Position: *Sa‘y* is a Pillar (*Rukn*); Hajj is incomplete without it.
*   **Standing at Arafat (*Wuqūf ‘Arafah*):** The core pillar of Hajj. Spending time there on the 9th of Dhu al-Hijjah.
*   **Stoning the *Jamarāt*:** Throwing pebbles at the pillars representing Satan.
    *   Maliki Position: Pebbles must land in the basin. If a pebble misses, it does not count and must be re-thrown.

### Speaker Notes
*   Explain the historical connection of *Sa‘y*: It commemorates the struggle of Hajar, the mother of Prophet Ismail, searching for water in the desert.
*   Explain that *Wuqūf* at Arafat is the peak of Hajj. The Prophet ﷺ said, "Hajj is Arafat." It is a day of intense Dua and forgiveness.
*   > [!NOTE] **Maliki Ruling:** For the stoning of the *Jamarāt*, the Maliki school is precise. The pebble must actually hit the target area (the basin/pillar). If you throw it and it bounces off and lands outside without touching the target zone, or if you miss completely, you cannot ignore it. You must throw another pebble to ensure the count of seven is accurate. This teaches precision and mindfulness in our actions.

## Slide 21: Conclusion: Transforming Fiqh into Habit
### Visual Outline
*   **Knowledge + Action:** Fiqh is meant to shape our daily routine.
*   **Habit Formation:**
    *   Practice modesty in your language today.
    *   Be honest in your transactions, even in video games and school projects.
    *   Remember Allah before eating and pray for the ability to visit His House (Makkah).
*   **The Heart of Fiqh:** Seeking the pleasure of Allah in everything we do.

### Speaker Notes
*   Summarize the presentation. Remind the students that knowing the rules is only the first step. The real goal is to put them into practice until they become second nature—habits that we perform without even thinking, yet still get rewarded for.
*   Challenge the students: Choose one manner we discussed today (like greeting everyone with Salām or knocking three times and standing to the side) and implement it consciously for the next week.
*   Ask if there are any questions before transitioning to the student handouts and activities.
