# Voice Script: Fasting, Zakah, & Community Responsibility
**Topic:** Ramadan and the fiqh of fasting, Zakat al-Fitr, Zakah on wealth, Sadaqah, and Eid celebration.  
**Audience:** Parents/Teachers presenting to children aged 10-13.  
**Creed/Law Framework:** Ash'ari Aqidah & Maliki Fiqh.  

---

## Slide 1: Welcome & Course Overview
* **Slide Summary:** Introduces the transition from individual physical worship to financial, communal, and social acts of worship.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Smile, extend arms in a welcoming gesture, and speak with high energy]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Assalamu Alaikum, everyone! Welcome to our next exciting unit: **Fasting, Zakah, and the Community**. 
  
  In our previous lessons, we learned how to prepare our bodies through Wudu and stand in Salah. Now, we are shifting from individual worship to acts of worship that bind our entire community together. 
  
  We will learn about the spiritual purpose of fasting, the precise Maliki rules of Wudu and food during Ramadan, how *Zakat al-Fitr* cleans our fasts at the end of the month, how *Zakah on wealth* brings financial justice to the poor, and finally, how to celebrate Eid with pure joy. 
  
  Let's start with a reflection: *When we fast or give charity, who benefits more? Is it ourselves, the poor, or Allah?* Let's find out!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A web: individual threads (prayers) are strong, but when you weave them together (fasting and charity), they create a massive web of support that holds up the entire community.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What are the two major pillars of Islam we are covering in this course? (Answer: Fasting/Siyam and Zakah)."

## Slide 2: Creed (Aqidah) Context: Why Do We Fast?
* **Slide Summary:** Exploring Divine Independence (Istighna') vs. human dependency (Iftiqar).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak in a slow, reflective, and humble tone]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Before we look at the rules of what breaks a fast, let's look at the theology. Why do we fast?
  
  In our creed, we know that Allah is **Al-Ghani**—The Self-Sufficient. He does not need our hunger, our thirst, or our money. He is completely independent of all creation. 
  
  Instead, *we* are the needy ones—**Al-Fuqara'**. We depend on Allah for every single breath and heartbeat. When we fast, and our stomachs rumble, it is a physical reminder of our absolute dependency—our **Iftiqar**—on Allah for our food and life. Fasting is also a secret worship. Unlike prayer, there is no physical action. Only Allah knows if you are truly fasting!"
* **Concept Analogy:** The air we breathe: the atmosphere doesn't need humans to breathe it, but we would die in seconds without it. We need Allah's provision; He does not need our worship.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Why is fasting called a 'secret worship'? (Answer: Because there are no visible movements; only Allah and the person know if they are truly fasting)."

## Slide 3: The Spirit of Fasting (Siyam)
* **Slide Summary:** Fasting as a shield (Turs) and the two levels of fasting.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Use a reassuring and protective tone; act out holding a shield]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "The Prophet ﷺ taught us: *'Fasting is a shield.'* 
  
  Just like a shield blocks arrows in battle, fasting protects us from making bad choices in this world and blocks the Fire in the next. 
  
  But there are two levels of fasting:
  First: **Fasting of the stomach**—holding back from food and drink.
  Second: **Fasting of the limbs and heart**—holding back our tongue from lying or arguing, our eyes from looking at bad things, and our hearts from jealousy. 
  
  The Prophet ﷺ warned us that if we don't give up lying and bad behavior, Allah has no need of us leaving our food and drink! Fasting is the ultimate training for **Ikhlas** (sincerity)."
* **Concept Analogy:** A software firewall: it doesn't just block big virus downloads; it monitors all the small incoming traffic to keep the system clean and safe. Fasting is a firewall for the soul.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What did the Prophet ﷺ say fasting is like? (Answer: A shield)."

## Slide 4: Establishing Ramadan: Moon Sighting (Hilal)
* **Slide Summary:** Visual moon sighting rules vs. astronomical calculations in the Maliki school.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Point up with your finger as if looking at the night sky]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "How do we know when Ramadan starts? The Prophet ﷺ said: *'Fast when you see it [the crescent moon], and break your fast when you see it.'*
  
  In the Maliki school, we establish the start of Ramadan in one of three ways:
  1. A reliable sighting of the crescent moon (*Hilal*) by two upright male witnesses.
  2. A sighting by a large crowd of people, making it impossible to lie.
  3. Or completing thirty days of the previous month, Sha'ban.
  
  In our Madhhab, we do not use computer math or astronomical calculations to start Ramadan. We stick to the physical, visual sighting taught by the Prophet ﷺ. It is a beautiful communal tradition!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A referee's whistle: even if everyone knows the clock is ticking, the play only officially starts when the referee blows the whistle. Sighting the moon is that official whistle.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Can we start Ramadan based on computer predictions alone in the Maliki school? (Answer: No, we must have physical visual sighting or complete 30 days of Sha'ban)."

## Slide 5: The Maliki View on Global vs. Local Sighting
* **Slide Summary:** Maliki global sighting rule vs. Shafi'i local sighting.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Spread your arms wide to show distance and global unity]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Here is a really cool difference between the schools: *Does a moon sighting in one country count for another?*
  
  In the **Maliki school**, the answer is yes! We believe in **Global Sighting**. If the moon is sighted reliably in one land, it becomes obligatory for Muslims in all other lands to start fasting, as long as the news reaches them and they share the night. This favors global Muslim unity!
  
  In contrast, the **Shafi'i school** advocates **Local Sighting**. They say if the moon is sighted in one city, it only binds people within a local radius of about eighty-three kilometers. Both opinions are respected, but the Maliki view connects us all globally."
* **Concept Analogy:** A school bell: in a large school, when the main bell rings, all classrooms start the lesson together (global), rather than each classroom ringing its own clock (local).
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the difference between global and local moon sighting? (Answer: Global means one sighting binds all lands; local means it only binds the nearby region)."

## Slide 6: The Pillars of Fasting (Arkan al-Siyam)
* **Slide Summary:** The two pillars of fasting: Intention (Niyyah) and Abstinence (Imsak).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak clearly; list the two pillars with emphasis]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "To make a fast valid, it must stand on two primary pillars, or **Arkan**:
  
  First: **The Intention** (*Niyyah*). You must decide in your heart that you are fasting for the sake of Allah.
  
  Second: **Abstinence** (*Imsak*). You must refrain from food, drink, and anything entering the stomach cavity from the first light of true dawn (*Fajr*) all the way until sunset (*Maghrib*).
  
  Fasting is obligatory for every sane, mature Muslim who is physically able, and women must be clear of their monthly bleeding. That's the baseline!"
* **Concept Analogy:** The walls of a box: if you have a top and a bottom but no sides (abstinence), the box cannot hold anything. The pillars contain and protect the fast.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "When does the fast actually start—at sunrise or at dawn (Fajr)? (Answer: At true dawn/Fajr)."

---

## Slide 7: Deep-Dive: The Intention (Niyyah) in Maliki Fiqh
* **Slide Summary:** The Maliki rule of Niyyah Wahidah (single intention for consecutive fasts).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Show a single finger; speak with a tone of clarification]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Let's focus on a major Maliki ruling about the intention: **Niyyah Wahidah** (a single intention).
  
  In the Maliki school, because Ramadan is a continuous, consecutive fast, you only need to make **one single intention** on the night before the first day of Ramadan to cover the entire month! You don't have to worry if you forget to make the intention on night fifteen. 
  
  However, if your fast is broken—like if you get sick, travel, or have your period—you *must* make a new intention the night before you start fasting again. Other schools require a brand new intention every single night, but Imam Malik viewed the whole month as one single package of worship."
* **Concept Analogy:** Buying a season pass: you pay once at the gate to enter the park for the whole season (single intention), rather than buying a new ticket every single morning you walk in.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "If a person has to stop fasting on day ten of Ramadan because they traveled, what must they do when they return to fast on day twelve? (Answer: Make a fresh intention the night before)."

---

## Slide 8: Invalidators of Fasting: Qada' (Makeup Only)
* **Slide Summary:** Actions that break the fast requiring a makeup day (Qada') without sin.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Use a teaching, analytical tone; write Qada' on the board]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Sometimes the fast is broken. In Maliki fiqh, if a fast is broken in certain ways, you only owe **Qada'**—which means you make up that single day after Ramadan. 
  
  Here is a major Maliki difference: if you eat or drink **by mistake or forgetfulness**, your fast *is* broken in our school, and you must make it up, but you have no sin. In other schools, forgetful eating doesn't break the fast. 
  
  Other things that require Qada' only include: throwing up deliberately, getting nose drops or ear drops into your throat, or failing to make your intention before Fajr. You are not in trouble, but you must replace that day."
* **Concept Analogy:** A flat tire: if you get a puncture by accident, your trip is paused, and you must change the tire (Qada'). You aren't in trouble with the police, but you still have to fix the tire to finish the journey.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "If Zayd forgets he is fasting and drinks milk in the Maliki school, what is the ruling? (Answer: His fast is broken; he must fast a makeup day (Qada') after Ramadan, but he has no sin)."

## Slide 9: Major Violations: Qada' and Kaffarah (Expiation)
* **Slide Summary:** Deliberate violations that trigger both Qada' and Kaffarah (expiation).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with seriousness and weight]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "But what if someone deliberately and voluntarily breaks their fast during Ramadan without a valid excuse, like eating a sandwich or drinking juice? 
  
  In the Maliki school, this is a massive violation! It triggers both Qada' (making up the day) AND **Kaffarah**—which is a severe expiation or penalty to repair the spiritual gravity of violating Ramadan. 
  
  We require *Kaffarah* for *any* deliberate breaking of the fast, whether by eating, drinking, or intimacy. Other schools limit *Kaffarah* only to breaking the fast through intimacy. Our Madhhab protects the sacredness of Ramadan very strictly!"
* **Concept Analogy:** Breaking a contract: if you accidentally spill water on a contract, you print a new copy (Qada'). But if you deliberately tear up the contract in front of the business partner, you face a major penalty (Kaffarah) for disrespecting the agreement.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the term for the penalty paid for deliberately breaking the Ramadan fast? (Answer: Kaffarah)."

## Slide 10: The Maliki Hierarchy of Kaffarah
* **Slide Summary:** The three options of Kaffarah, with feeding 60 poor people as the preferred option.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with appreciation for social justice]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "If someone triggers Kaffarah, what is the penalty? There are three options:
  1. Feeding sixty poor people.
  2. Fasting two consecutive months.
  3. Freeing a slave (which isn't applicable today).
  
  In the Maliki school, **feeding sixty poor people** is the *most preferred* option! Other schools say you *must* fast two months, and you can only feed the poor if you are too sick to fast. 
  
  Why did Imam Malik prefer feeding the poor? Because fasting only benefits you, but feeding sixty hungry people benefits the whole community! This shows the deep social justice built into our Madhhab."
* **Concept Analogy:** A community service order: instead of sitting in a quiet room as punishment (fasting), a judge orders you to help clean up the local park and feed the homeless, benefiting everyone.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the highly preferred method of Kaffarah in the Maliki school? (Answer: Feeding sixty poor people)."

## Slide 11: Recommended & Disliked Acts of Fasting
* **Slide Summary:** Sunnahs (Suhoor, Iftar) and Makruhat (tasting food, deep rinsing).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with practical advice and warmth]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "To get the most out of our fasting, we should follow the Sunnah:
  - Eat **Suhoor** (the pre-dawn meal) and delay it until just before Fajr to keep your strength.
  - **Hasten to break your fast** (*Iftar*) the moment sunset happens. The Prophet ﷺ said: *'People will remain in goodness as long as they hasten to break their fast.'*
  
  We should also avoid disliked acts (*Makruhat*):
  - Avoid tasting food unless necessary (like a chef, who must spit it out immediately).
  - Avoid sniffing water too deeply during Wudu, because water might slip down your throat.
  - And avoid complaining about being hungry or angry. Fasting is about patience!"
* **Concept Analogy:** Extra credit: you can pass an exam with a C grade (just doing the pillars), but doing the recommended acts is like answering the extra credit questions to get an A+.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Why should we avoid sniffing water too deeply into the nose during Wudu while fasting? (Answer: Because water might enter the stomach and break the fast)."

## Slide 12: Zakat al-Fitr: The Charity of Fasting
* **Slide Summary:** Purpose, obligation, and communal role of Zakat al-Fitr.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with warmth; emphasize community care]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "At the end of Ramadan, we pay a special obligatory charity called **Zakat al-Fitr**. 
  
  This charity is obligatory on every single Muslim—man, woman, and child—who has enough food for Eid day. Parents pay it on behalf of their children.
  
  Why do we pay it? The Prophet ﷺ said it does two things: it cleans our fasting from any mistakes or idle talk we made during Ramadan, and it feeds the poor. It ensures that no member of our community goes hungry on the day of Eid. It connects our worship directly to caring for others!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A clean-up crew: after building a beautiful house (our fasts in Ramadan), the clean-up crew sweeps away any dust and wood shavings (our minor mistakes) so the house is perfectly ready to show.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Who is responsible for paying Zakat al-Fitr for children? (Answer: Their parents or guardians)."

---

## Slide 13: Zakat al-Fitr: Timing & The Maliki Rules
* **Slide Summary:** Due times, best times, and forbidden times for Zakat al-Fitr.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Point to a timeline or slide visual; speak with structure]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "When do we pay Zakat al-Fitr? Timing is everything!
  
  - It becomes **obligatory** at sunset on the last day of Ramadan.
  - The **best time** (*Mustahabb*) is on Eid morning, after Fajr but *before* going to the Eid prayer.
  - You are permitted to pay it **one or two days before Eid** so charity groups have time to distribute it.
  
  If you delay it until after the Eid prayer, it is disliked (*Makruh*). If you delay it past Eid day, it is strictly forbidden (*Haram*)! It stays as a debt on your shoulders that you must pay immediately, but the special reward of Zakah is lost."
* **Concept Analogy:** Sending a birthday gift: if you send the gift a day early, it's fine. If you bring it on the birthday, it's perfect. But if you bring it a month late, the party is over and the moment is lost.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the absolute deadline to pay Zakat al-Fitr without it becoming a sin? (Answer: Before Eid day ends; delaying it past Eid day is Haram)."

## Slide 14: Zakat al-Fitr: Amounts & Measurements
* **Slide Summary:** Volumetric measurement of Sa' and Mudd, and modern weight equivalents.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Hold hands together as a scoop to demonstrate]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "How much do we have to pay? The amount is exactly **One Sa'** of food.
  
  What is a *Sa'*? In the Maliki school, a *Sa'* is a volumetric measure made of **four Amdad** (singular **Mudd**). 
  
  A *Mudd* is the scoop of two average-sized hands held together. 
  
  Because different grains have different weights, one *Sa'* of wheat, rice, or dates weighs about **2.1 to 2.5 kilograms** (around 5 pounds). Imam Malik lived in Madinah and used the exact measuring cups handed down from the families of the Companions, ensuring our measurements are extremely close to the Prophet's ﷺ scale!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A measuring cup: just like a baking recipe requires exactly one cup of flour, the Prophet ﷺ set a spiritual recipe of exactly one Sa' of food to feed a family.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "How many Amdad (double handfuls) make up one Sa'? (Answer: Four)."

## Slide 15: Zakat al-Fitr: Staple Foods vs. Cash
* **Slide Summary:** The debate on paying in food (Maliki default) vs. cash (Hanafi default).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with balance and scholarly appreciation]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "What do we pay? In the **Maliki school**, you must pay Zakat al-Fitr using the **common staple foods** of the country—like rice, wheat, dates, or raisins. Paying in cash is *not* valid in our classical school because the Prophet ﷺ specified food.
  
  In contrast, the **Hanafi school** allows paying in **cash**, arguing that money is more useful for the poor today. 
  
  To balance this, many modern Maliki charities allow you to give them cash, and they use that cash to buy the actual rice or wheat to distribute to the poor on Eid morning. Both approaches ensure the poor are fed!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A gift card vs. the actual toy: some people prefer giving the exact item (food), while others prefer giving the money so the recipient can choose (cash). Both seek to bring joy.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Why does the classical Maliki school require food instead of cash? (Answer: To follow the exact physical practice described in the Hadiths)."

## Slide 16: Zakah on Wealth (Zakah al-Mal)
* **Slide Summary:** Meaning of Zakah and the theology of wealth as a trust (Amanah).
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak in a slow, ethical, and inspiring tone]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Now let's look at the third pillar of Islam: **Zakah al-Mal**—Zakah on wealth.
  
  Linguistically, Zakah means 'growth' and 'purification'. 
  
  In our creed, we believe that **wealth is a trust** (*Amanah*). Allah is the true Owner of everything, and we are just caretakers. Zakah is not a voluntary tip; it is the legal right of the poor that Allah has placed inside our bank accounts. 
  
  When we pay it, we prune our wealth, cleaning it from greed and materialism. Just like pruning a plant makes it look smaller but helps it grow back stronger, Zakah purifies and grows our wealth!"
* **Concept Analogy:** Pruning a tree: you cut off some branches (giving 2.5% of your money), which looks like you have less, but it actually causes the tree to grow back healthier, stronger, and full of sweet fruit.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What does the word 'Zakah' mean linguistically? (Answer: Growth and Purification)."

---

## Slide 17: Who Must Pay Zakah al-Mal?
* **Slide Summary:** Conditions of obligation, and the Maliki ruling on children's wealth.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Raise your finger for the comparison between Malikis and Hanafis]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Who has to pay Zakah on wealth? Five conditions must be met:
  1. Being Muslim.
  2. Being free.
  3. Having full ownership of the money.
  4. The money reaches a minimum amount called **Nisab**.
  5. A full lunar year (**Hawl**) passes while your money stays above the Nisab.
  
  Here is a major Maliki rule: **Zakah is obligatory on children and insane people!** If a child inherits a large sum of money above Nisab, their guardian must pay Zakah from it on their behalf. Hanafis disagree, saying only adults pay. But Malikis say Zakah is the right of the poor linked to the *wealth*, not the age of the person."
* **Concept Analogy:** A parking ticket or tax: if a car is parked illegally, the car gets a ticket, regardless of whether an adult or a child owns the car. The obligation is tied to the asset.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Does a wealthy child owe Zakah on their savings in the Maliki school? (Answer: Yes, their guardian must calculate and pay it on their behalf)."

---

## Slide 18: Nisab, Hawl, & The Categories of Zakah
* **Slide Summary:** Calculating 2.5% Zakah, Gold/Silver Nisabs, and the harvest rule.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with a clear, mathematical focus]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "How do we calculate Zakah? For cash, gold, and silver, the rate is **2.5%**—which is one-fortieth of your wealth.
  
  The minimum threshold, or **Nisab**, is based on:
  - Gold: Eighty-five grams of pure gold.
  - Silver: Five hundred and ninety-five grams of silver.
  If your savings are above this value for a whole lunar year (*Hawl*), you pay 2.5%.
  
  But note: agricultural crops and fruits do *not* require waiting a year. You pay their Zakah immediately on the day of harvest. As the Quran says: *'Give its due on the day of its harvest.'*"
* **Concept Analogy:** A tax threshold: you only pay if your income crosses a certain limit. Nisab is that boundary line of financial comfort.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "If you have savings above Nisab, what percentage do you pay as Zakah? (Answer: 2.5%)."

## Slide 19: The Recipients of Zakah (Asnaf)
* **Slide Summary:** The eight categories of Zakah recipients and who is excluded.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Point to the list on the slide; speak methodically]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Where does our Zakah money go? The Quran restricts it to exactly **eight categories** of people:
  1. The poor (*Fuqara'*).
  2. The needy (*Masakin*).
  3. The Zakah collectors.
  4. Reconciling hearts.
  5. Freeing captives.
  6. Debtors (those in halal debt who can't pay).
  7. In the cause of Allah.
  8. Stranded travelers.
  
  Remember, you can never give Zakah to your own parents, grandparents, children, or spouse because you are already legally required to take care of them with your regular money!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A locked trust fund: the money is legally set aside only for specific people who need it. You cannot tap into it for other projects or family members who are already your responsibility.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "Can you give your Zakah to your own mother or father? Why not? (Answer: No, because supporting them is already your personal legal obligation, not charity)."

## Slide 20: Sadaqah: Voluntary Kindness & Social Responsibility
* **Slide Summary:** Voluntary Sadaqah, communal responsibility (Fard Kifayah), and social justice.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Speak with inspiration; show a gesture of a helping hand]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "Zakah is obligatory, but we also have **Sadaqah**—voluntary charity. The word *Sadaqah* comes from *Sidq* (truthfulness), because giving away your money voluntarily proves the truth of your faith!
  
  Sadaqah is huge in scope. The Prophet ﷺ said: *'Every good deed is a charity.'* Smiling at your brother, removing a branch from the road, or helping someone carry their bags is Sadaqah.
  
  We also have a collective duty: **Fard Kifayah**. Ensuring no one in our neighborhood starves is a duty on the whole town. If a poor person starves and the townspeople knew, the entire community shares the sin. Islam builds a network of care!"
* **Concept Analogy:** A building code: the government requires a basic safety structure (Zakah), but the community adds beautiful gardens, parks, and playgrounds (Sadaqah) to make it a great place to live.
* **Check-for-Understanding Question:** "What is the difference between Fard 'Ayn (individual duty) and Fard Kifayah (communal duty)? (Answer: Fard 'Ayn must be done by everyone; Fard Kifayah is done by some to clear the duty for the whole community)."

## Slide 21: Eid al-Fitr: Etiquette and Celebration
* **Slide Summary:** Eid al-Fitr prayer (Confirmed Sunnah), extra Takbeers, and Sunnah practices.
* **Delivery Direction:** `[Smile, speak with joy and celebration]`
* **Narrative Script:**
  "After the spiritual marathon of Ramadan, Allah gifts us a day of joy: **Eid al-Fitr**! 
  
  In the Maliki school, the Eid prayer is a **Confirmed Sunnah** prayed in congregation. It is two Rak'ahs. In the first Rak'ah, we make six extra Takbeers before reciting. In the second Rak'ah, we make five extra Takbeers.
  
  The Sunnahs of Eid are: taking a bath (*Ghusl*), wearing your absolute best clothes, eating an odd number of dates before leaving the house, and walking to the prayer place by one path and returning by another. Let's celebrate our worship with joy!
  
  Jazakum Allahu Khayran, and Eid Mubarak to you all!"
* **Delivery Tip:** `[Lead the students in reciting the Eid Takbeerat together to end the class on a high note.]`
