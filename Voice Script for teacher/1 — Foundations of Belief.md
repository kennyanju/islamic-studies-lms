# Presentation Slides: Islamic Creed (Aqidah) & Law (Fiqh) for Teachers
**Topic:** Who is Allah? Names and Attributes, Pillars of Iman, Angels, Holy Books & Prophets, Qadar, and Maliki/Ash'ari Literacy.
**Target Audience:** Parents / Teachers presenting to children aged 10-13.
**Perspective:** Ash'ari Aqidah & Maliki Fiqh.

---

## Slide 1: Introduction to Aqidah & Fiqh
* **Visual Guide / Layout:** A simple, elegant title slide. Background showing a traditional manuscript design or geometric patterns. Emphasize the keywords: *Aqidah* (Creed) and *Fiqh* (Jurisprudence).
* **Slide Content:**
  * **Welcome to our Islamic Studies Journey!**
  * **Aqidah (عَقِيدَة):** The firm belief in our hearts about Allah, the Prophets, and the unseen.
  * **Fiqh (فِقْه):** The practical rules of how we worship and live our lives.
  * Why do we learn specific schools of thought (*Madhhabs*)?
    * To build deep legal and theological literacy.
    * To appreciate the rich diversity of Islamic scholarship.
* **Teacher Notes:**
  * Start the class by explaining that Islam is not flat or uniform in every detail of practice and sub-theology. Scholars have preserved different, valid paths.
  * Introduce the *Ash'ari* (أَشْعَرِي) school of theology and the *Maliki* (مَالِكِي) school of law. Explain that these are followed by millions of Muslims, particularly in North and West Africa, parts of the Middle East, and historically in Muslim Spain (Al-Andalus).
  * Emphasize to the students that seeing differences in how people pray (like where they place their hands) is a sign of mercy and scholarly depth, not division.

---

## Slide 2: Who is Allah? The Core Principle of Tawhid
* **Visual Guide / Layout:** A starry night sky or a beautiful nature background. Central word: *Tawhid* (تَوْحِيد) in clean, prominent text.
* **Slide Content:**
  * **Tawhid (تَوْحِيد):** Uncompromising belief in the Oneness of Allah.
  * Allah is *Al-Wahid* (الْوَاحِد) and *Al-Ahad* (الأَحَد) – The One, The Unique.
  * He has no partners, no parents, no children, and no equals.
  * Knowing Allah is the very first obligation (*Wajib*) for a Muslim.
* **Teacher Notes:**
  * Explain that *Tawhid* is the foundation of everything. In the Ash'ari school, the first obligation upon a person who reaches the age of mental maturity (*Bulugh*) is to know Allah through reason and revelation.
  * Discuss Surah al-Ikhlas (Chapter 112) as the ultimate summary of Tawhid. Highlight the word *Al-Samad* (الصَّمَد) – the Self-Sufficient Master upon whom all creation depends, while He depends on nothing.

---

## Slide 3: Knowing Allah through His Necessary Attributes (Sifat al-Wajibah)
* **Visual Guide / Layout:** A table or split column layout showing the Arabic terms on one side and English translations on the other.
* **Slide Content:**
  * **Sifat al-Wajibah (الصِّفَات الْوَاجِبَة):** The attributes that logically must belong to Allah.
  * Ash'ari scholars identify **20 necessary attributes** divided into groups:
    1. *Nafsiyyah* (Personal): **Wujud** (Existence).
    2. *Salbiyyah* (Negative/Divesting): Attributes that deny what is impossible for Allah (e.g., having a beginning or end).
    3. *Ma'ani* (Existential/Qualitative): Power, Will, Knowledge, Life, Hearing, Sight, Speech.
    4. *Ma'nawiyyah* (Active/State): Being Powerful, Being Willful, etc.
* **Teacher Notes:**
  * Explain that "negative attributes" (*Salbiyyah*) do not mean "bad." They mean attributes that negate or strip away any qualities of imperfection from Allah. For example, *Qidam* (Beginninglessness) negates that Allah was created.
  * Keep it simple for younger students: Allah is alive (*Hayy*), knows everything (*'Alim*), can do all things (*Qadir*), wills everything (*Murid*), hears everything (*Sami'*), sees everything (*Basir*), and speaks (*Mutakallim*). We will explore these one by one.

---

## Slide 4: Transcending Space and Time (Mukhalafah lil-Hawadith)
* **Visual Guide / Layout:** Graphic illustrating a timeline and a globe, with a barrier or indicator showing Allah is completely outside and beyond these concepts.
* **Slide Content:**
  * **Mukhalafah lil-Hawadith (مُخَالَفَتُهُ لِلْحَوَادِثِ):** Absolute difference from created things.
  * Allah is not a physical body (*Jirm*). He does not occupy space.
  * Allah created space and time; therefore, He is not contained by them.
  * *"There is nothing like unto Him, and He is the Hearing, the Seeing."* (Surah ash-Shura, 42:11)
* **Teacher Notes:**
  * This is a cornerstone of Ash'ari theology: *Tanzih* (تَنْزِيه), which means declaring Allah free of any resemblance to His creation.
  * Explain that everything we can imagine in our minds is a creation. Since Allah is the Creator, He cannot look like, sound like, or be like anything we can imagine. 
  * If a student asks, "Where is Allah?", the teacher should explain: Allah exists without a place (*Makan*). He created "above" and "below," so He is not physically inside or limited by directions.

## Slide 5: The Names of Allah (Al-Asma' al-Husna)
* **Visual Guide / Layout:** A collage of beautiful calligraphy of some of the 99 Names, such as *Al-Khaliq* (The Creator), *Al-Rahman* (The Merciful), and *Al-Hakim* (The Wise).
* **Slide Content:**
  * **Al-Asma' al-Husna (الأَسْمَاء الْحُسْنَى):** The Most Beautiful Names of Allah.
  * The Prophet ﷺ said: *"Allah has ninety-nine names, one hundred less one. Whoever guards/memorizes them will enter Paradise."* (Sahih al-Bukhari)
  * We use these Names to understand Allah's actions and how He cares for us.
  * Examples:
    * **Al-Ghaffar (الْغَفَّار):** The Repeatedly Forgiving.
    * **Al-Razzaq (الرَّزَّاق):** The Provider.
* **Teacher Notes:**
  * Emphasize that memorizing the Names is not just repeating them like a list; it means understanding their meanings and living by them.
  * Connect the Names to daily life: when we make a mistake, we call upon *Al-Ghaffar*; when we need help or food, we call upon *Al-Razzaq*; when we see the beautiful organization of the universe, we praise *Al-Hakim*.

## Slide 6: Ash'ari Reading of Sacred Texts: Tafwid and Ta'wil
* **Visual Guide / Layout:** A scale balance showing two valid paths: *Tafwid* (leaving the meaning to Allah) and *Ta'wil* (metaphorical interpretation).
* **Slide Content:**
  * How do we understand Quranic words like the "Hand" (*Yad*) or "Face" (*Wajh*) of Allah?
  * **Option A: Tafwid (تَفْوِيض) - The Way of the Salaf (Early Scholars)**
    * We say: We believe in the word as Allah revealed it, but we do not give it a physical meaning, and we say "Allah knows best what it means."
  * **Option B: Ta'wil (تَأْوِيل) - The Way of the Khalaf (Later Scholars)**
    * We interpret the word metaphorically using proper Arabic rules.
    * *Yad* (Hand) = Power (*Qudrah*) or Favor (*Ni'mah*).
    * *Istiwa'* (Establishing over the Throne) = Domination/Control (*Istila'*).
* **Teacher Notes:**
  * This slide is crucial for theological literacy. It teaches students that the Quran contains metaphorical language.
  * Explain that both *Tafwid* and *Ta'wil* are valid, orthodox Sunni paths. They both agree on one primary rule: **Allah does not have physical body parts.** 
  * Warn against *Tashbih* (تَشْبِيه) (anthropomorphism / comparing Allah to humans) and *Tajsim* (تَجْسِيم) (giving Allah a physical body). These are rejected in Sunni Aqidah.

---

## Slide 7: The Six Pillars of Iman (Overview)
* **Visual Guide / Layout:** An infographic showing six pillars supporting a beautiful structure labeled *Iman* (Faith).
* **Slide Content:**
  * **Iman (إِيمَان):** Faith or belief.
  * The Hadith of Jibril: The Prophet ﷺ was asked about Iman and replied that it is to believe in:
    1. **Allah** (اللّٰه)
    2. His **Angels** (مَلَائِكَة)
    3. His **Books** (كُتُب)
    4. His **Messengers** (رُسُل)
    5. The **Last Day** (الْيَوْم الْآخِر)
    6. **Qadar** (قَدَر) - Divine Decree, both its good and its evil.
* **Teacher Notes:**
  * Remind the students of the difference between *Islam* (submission/physical actions like praying and fasting) and *Iman* (internal belief/conviction of the heart).
  * Use the Hadith of Jibril as the reference point. In it, Angel Jibril came in the form of a man in white clothing to teach the Sahabah (Companions) their religion.

---

## Slide 8: Iman in Allah and His Attributes of Power, Will, and Knowledge
* **Visual Guide / Layout:** Interlocking circles or gears showing *Qudrah* (Power), *Iradah* (Will), and *'Ilm* (Knowledge) working together.
* **Slide Content:**
  * Under Ash'ari Aqidah, three key attributes explain how Allah acts in the universe:
  * **Al-'Ilm (الْعِلْم) - Divine Knowledge:**
    * Allah knows everything eternally.
    * He knows what was, is, will be, and what *could* have been.
  * **Al-Iradah (الإِرَادَة) - Divine Will:**
    * Allah chooses to make things happen in a specific way, at a specific time, with specific qualities.
  * **Al-Qudrah (الْقُدْرَة) - Divine Power:**
    * Allah brings things from non-existence into existence.
* **Teacher Notes:**
  * Help students understand the relationship: Allah's *'Ilm* knows that a leaf will fall from a tree at 3:00 PM. His *Iradah* wills that this specific leaf falls at 3:00 PM rather than 4:00 PM. His *Qudrah* is the power that physically makes the leaf drop.
  * Emphasize that Allah's knowledge does not change; He does not "learn" new things. His knowledge is eternal.

---

## Slide 9: Angels: Creation, Nature, and Characteristics
* **Visual Guide / Layout:** Bright light rays against a deep background. Emphasize that angels are not human-like babies with wings, but majestic creations of light.
* **Slide Content:**
  * **Angels (Malā'ikah - مَلَائِكَة):** Noble creations of Allah made from light (*Nur*).
  * They do not have physical genders (they are neither male nor female).
  * They do not eat, drink, sleep, or get tired.
  * They do not possess free will in the way humans do:
    * *"They do not disobey Allah in what He commands them, and they do what they are commanded."* (Surah at-Tahrim, 66:6)
* **Teacher Notes:**
  * Clarify common cultural misconceptions: Angels in Islam are not "people who died and got wings." They are a completely different species created before humans.
  * Discuss their size and power. For example, Jibril is described as having 600 wings, blocking the horizon.
  * Contrast them with Jinn, who are made of smokeless fire (*Marij min Nar*) and do have free will to choose between good and evil. (Iblis was a Jinn, not a fallen angel, in Sunni theology).

---

## Slide 10: The Major Angels and Their Duties
* **Visual Guide / Layout:** Four columns or cards, each representing one of the major angels with their primary responsibility listed below.
* **Slide Content:**
  * **Jibril (جِبْرِيل):** The Archangel of Revelation (*Wahy*). Delivered scripture to the Prophets.
  * **Mika'il (مِيكَائِيل):** Responsible for rain, vegetation, and distributing sustenance (*Rizq*) by Allah's command.
  * **Israfil (إِسْرَافِيل):** The blower of the Trumpet (*Sur*) to signal the end of the world and the Day of Resurrection.
  * **Malik al-Mawt (مَلَكُ الْمَوْت):** The Angel of Death (often named *'Izra'il* in tradition) who extracts souls when their time ends.
* **Teacher Notes:**
  * Emphasize that these angels do not act independently; they are perfectly organized soldiers executing the orders of Allah.
  * Discuss how *Mika'il* is associated with mercy because rain brings life to dry land, showing Allah's care for all living things.
  * Clarify that the name *'Izra'il* is popular in Islamic literature and cultural tradition, but in the Quran, he is referred to as *Malik al-Mawt* (The Angel of Death). Both are acceptable to mention.

## Slide 11: The Angels Who Accompany Us
* **Visual Guide / Layout:** An illustration of a child walking, with two soft light shapes on either side representing the recording angels, and others around them for protection.
* **Slide Content:**
  * **Kiraman Katibin (كِرَامًا كَاتِبِينَ) - The Honorable Recorders:**
    * Two angels sit on our shoulders to write down our deeds.
    * The angel on the right writes good deeds immediately.
    * The angel on the left writes bad deeds, but delays writing to give us time to repent (*Tawbah*).
  * **Al-Hafazah (الْحَفَظَة) - The Protectors:**
    * Guardian angels that protect us from accidents and harm, unless Allah decrees that something should happen.
* **Teacher Notes:**
  * In literature, the recording angels are sometimes referred to as *Raqib* (رَقِيب - Watcher) and *'Atid* (عَتِيد - Ready). In Ash'ari theology, these are descriptions of their qualities (that they are always present and ready to record), rather than their unique names.
  * Tell the students the beautiful mercy of the left-shoulder angel: Hadith reports that when a Muslim does a bad deed, the angel on the left raises his pen for a few hours. If the person asks Allah for forgiveness, nothing is written down!

## Slide 12: The Holy Books (Kutub) and the Divine Speech (Kalam)
* **Visual Guide / Layout:** Images representing scrolls and books. Text highlighting the names of the books and the prophets who received them.
* **Slide Content:**
  * We believe in the original scriptures sent by Allah:
    * **Suhuf (صُحُف):** Scrolls of Ibrahim (Abraham).
    * **Tawrah (تَوْرَاة):** Torah of Musa (Moses).
    * **Zabur (زَبُور):** Psalms of Dawud (David).
    * **Injil (إِنْجِيل):** Gospel of 'Isa (Jesus).
    * **Quran (قُرْآن):** Final Revelation to Muhammad ﷺ.
  * **Ash'ari Theological Distinction:**
    * **Kalam Nafsi (كَلَام نَفْسِي):** Allah's eternal, inner attribute of Speech, which has no letters, sounds, or language.
    * The physical Quran we hold, read, and hear consists of created letters and sounds that *point to* that uncreated Divine Speech.
* **Teacher Notes:**
  * Explain the historical context: Sunni scholars had a major debate about whether the Quran is created or uncreated. 
  * The Ash'ari school resolved this with a beautiful distinction: Allah's attribute of Speech (*Kalam*) is eternal and uncreated, just like His Knowledge. However, the Arabic words we print on paper, the sound waves created when we recite, and the ink we use are created things. We must respect the printed Quran because of what it represents—the eternal Speech of Allah.

## Slide 13: Prophets and Messengers (Anbiya' and Rusul)
* **Visual Guide / Layout:** A chain link timeline showing the continuity of Prophets from Adam to Muhammad ﷺ, emphasizing they all brought the same message of Tawhid.
* **Slide Content:**
  * **Nabi (نَبِي) - Prophet:** A man chosen by Allah to receive guidance, usually following an existing law (*Shari'ah*).
  * **Rasul (رَسُول) - Messenger:** A Prophet who is given a *new* book or a new set of laws (*Shari'ah*).
  * Every *Rasul* is a *Nabi*, but not every *Nabi* is a *Rasul*.
  * Total number: Tens of thousands of Prophets were sent, but **25 are mentioned by name** in the Quran.
  * Core message: Worship Allah alone and do good deeds.
* **Teacher Notes:**
  * Ask students to name some of the 25 Prophets in the Quran (e.g., Adam, Nuh, Ibrahim, Ismail, Ishaq, Ya'qub, Yusuf, Ayub, Musa, Harun, Dawud, Sulayman, Yunus, Zakariyyah, Yahya, 'Isa, Muhammad).
  * Explain that belief in all Prophets is an all-or-nothing pillar. We cannot believe in Muhammad ﷺ but reject Musa or 'Isa; rejecting one Prophet is like rejecting all of them.

---

## Slide 14: The Infallibility of the Prophets ('Ismah)
* **Visual Guide / Layout:** Icons representing clean virtues: Truth, Trust, Intelligence, and a shield representing protection from sin.
* **Slide Content:**
  * **'Ismah (عِصْمَة):** Infallibility. The protection of Prophets from committing sins.
  * Ash'ari theology states that Prophets are protected from:
    * Major sins (*Kaba'ir*).
    * Minor sins that show a lack of character or base behavior.
    * Lying or breaking promises.
  * Four necessary qualities of every Prophet:
    1. **Sidq (صِدْق):** Complete truthfulness.
    2. **Amanah (أَمَانَة):** Absolute trustworthiness.
    3. **Tabligh (تَبْلِيغ):** Delivering the message without hiding anything.
    4. **Fatanah (فَطَانَة):** Sharp intellect and wisdom.
* **Teacher Notes:**
  * Why must Prophets be infallible? Because they are our ultimate role models. If they committed sins (like stealing or lying), then Allah would be commanding us to follow people who do bad things, which is logically impossible for the Just Creator.
  * Sometimes the Quran describes a Prophet making a mistake (like Prophet Yunus leaving his people early). Ash'ari scholars explain that these were not *sins* but choices between two good options where they chose the less optimal one, or small slips of judgment (*Zallah*) that Allah corrected immediately to teach humanity.

---

## Slide 15: Believing in Qadar (Divine Decree)
* **Visual Guide / Layout:** An image of a pen writing on a scroll, representing the *Lawh al-Mahfuz* (Preserved Tablet).
* **Slide Content:**
  * **Qadar (قَدَر):** Allah's eternal measurement and decree of all things.
  * Four aspects of Qadar (often called the stages of decree):
    1. **'Ilm (Knowledge):** Allah knows everything eternally.
    2. **Kitabah (Writing):** Allah ordered the Pen (*Al-Qalam*) to write everything that will happen in the Preserved Tablet (*Al-Lawh al-Mahfuz*).
    3. **Mashi'ah (Will):** Everything happens only if Allah wills it.
    4. **Khalq (Creation):** Allah is the sole Creator of all things, including actions.
* **Teacher Notes:**
  * Introduce the concept of the *Lawh al-Mahfuz* (اللَّوْح الْمَحْفُوظ). Clarify that the "writing" of the decree does not mean we are forced to do things we don't want to do. The Pen wrote what we *would choose* to do, based on Allah's eternal knowledge.
  * Explain that Qadar is a source of peace for a believer. When something bad happens that is out of our control (like a natural storm or getting sick), we know it was decreed, and we say, *Qaddarallahu wa ma sha'a fa'ala* (Allah has decreed, and what He wills, He does).

## Slide 16: Human Choice and the Ash'ari Concept of Kasb (Acquisition)
* **Visual Guide / Layout:** A user interface metaphor: Allah creates the computer system and the keyboard (Power/Creation), but the human chooses which buttons to press (Acquisition).
* **Slide Content:**
  * If Allah creates everything, who is responsible for our actions?
  * **The Ash'ari Answer: Kasb (كَسْب) - Acquisition.**
    * **Creation (Khalq):** Allah creates the ability and the physical action.
    * **Acquisition (Kasb):** The human directs their intention and choice toward that action.
  * *Example:* You decide to give charity. Allah creates the movement of your hand and the money. You "acquire" the deed because you intended and chose to do it.
  * *"For it (the soul) is what it earned (Kasabat), and against it is what it accrued."* (Surah al-Baqarah, 2:286)
* **Teacher Notes:**
  * This is one of the most famous theological debates in Islamic history.
  * Contrast the Ash'ari view with two extremes:
    1. *Jabriyyah* (Determinists): They believed humans have no choice at all, like feathers in the wind. (Rejected!)
    2. *Mu'tazilah* (Rationalists): They believed humans create their own actions independently of Allah. (Rejected!)
  * The Ash'ari middle path is *Kasb*: Allah is the only Creator of effects, but humans are responsible because they have the freedom to intend and choose.

---

## Slide 17: Qadar is Not an Excuse for Bad Choices!
* **Visual Guide / Layout:** A stop sign or warning icon. Side-by-side text contrasting "Excusing choices" vs. "Taking responsibility."
* **Slide Content:**
  * **We cannot use Qadar to justify our sins!**
  * If someone steals money and says, *"It was written in my Qadar, so don't blame me!"* - this is a misuse of belief.
  * **The Law School Analogy:**
    * If you skip studying and fail an exam, you cannot blame the teacher for writing "Fail" on your report card. You made the choice to skip studying.
  * **Use Qadar for comfort in calamity, not as an excuse for laziness or sin.**
* **Teacher Notes:**
  * Share the famous story of 'Umar ibn al-Khattab: A thief was brought to him and said, "I only stole because it was decreed by Allah." 'Umar replied, "And we only cut off your hand because it was decreed by Allah!" This shows that Qadar does not bypass justice or accountability.
  * Teach students that we only look at Qadar *after* something happens (to accept it with patience), never *before* it happens (as an excuse to do wrong).

---

## Slide 18: Why We Learn Schools of Thought (Madhhabs)
* **Visual Guide / Layout:** A map of the Muslim world showing different colors for different schools, or a tree with different branches showing unity in the trunk.
* **Slide Content:**
  * Why do different scholars have different opinions on small details?
  * **What is a Madhhab (مَذْهَب)?** A systematic method of understanding the Quran and Sunnah.
  * The four major Sunni schools of law:
    * **Hanafi** (حَنَفِي)
    * **Maliki** (مَالِكِي)
    * **Shafi'i** (شَافِعِي)
    * **Hanbali** (حَنْبَلِي)
  * They agree on 99% of the core of Islam. Their differences are in the secondary details of practice.
* **Teacher Notes:**
  * Explain that the founders of these schools were great Imams who loved and respected each other. Imam Shafi'i was a student of Imam Malik, and Imam Ahmad was a student of Imam Shafi'i.
  * Reiterate that having a Madhhab is like having a guide who has mapped out the mountain. It is safer to follow an expert map than trying to climb without any training.

---

## Slide 19: The Maliki Madhhab: The Practice of Madinah and Sadl
* **Visual Guide / Layout:** An illustration of a person praying with their hands resting naturally at their sides (*Sadl*), contrasted with hands folded on the chest (*Qabd*).
* **Slide Content:**
  * **Imam Malik ibn Anas (d. 179 AH):** The Imam of Madinah, the city of the Prophet ﷺ.
  * **'Amal Ahl al-Madinah (عَمَل أَهْل الْمَدِينَة):**
    * Imam Malik looked at how the children and grandchildren of the Sahabah in Madinah practiced Islam as a living proof of the Sunnah.
  * **Sadl (سَدْل) vs. Qabd (قَبْض) in Salah:**
    * **Sadl:** Praying with arms hanging naturally at the sides. This is the preferred position in fard (obligatory) prayers in the Maliki school.
    * Why? Because Imam Malik observed the people of Madinah praying this way.
    * Both Sadl and Qabd are valid Sunni practices.
* **Teacher Notes:**
  * Address a common point of confusion for kids: "Why does that person pray differently?" 
  * Explain that in the Maliki school, *Sadl* is the primary position because it was the continuous, public practice of the city of Madinah. If the Prophet ﷺ had ordered everyone to fold their hands, the entire city of Madinah would not have agreed on praying with their hands down. Therefore, it is a highly respected Sunnah.
  * Note: In Maliki fiqh, it is also permitted to fold hands (*Qabd*) in nafl (voluntary) prayers, but *Sadl* is preferred in fard prayers.

## Slide 20: More Maliki Rulings on Salah
* **Visual Guide / Layout:** Checklist format detailing specific Maliki rulings on Salah.
* **Slide Content:**
  * **Recitation of the Basmalah (بِسْمِ اللّٰهِ الرَّحْمٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ):**
    * In the Maliki school, we do not recite the *Basmalah* aloud or silently before Al-Fatihah in the fard prayers. We start directly with *"Al-Hamdu lillahi Rabbil-'Alamin."*
  * **Making Sujud al-Sahw (Prostration of Forgetfulness):**
    * **Ba'di (بَعْدِي - After):** If you added something extra to the prayer by mistake, you make sujud *after* the Taslim.
    * **Qabli (قَبْلِي - Before):** If you missed a sunnah act in the prayer, you make sujud *before* the Taslim.
* **Teacher Notes:**
  * Explain that not reciting the *Basmalah* in fard prayers is another practice of the people of Madinah. Malikis believe Al-Fatihah is the start of the recitation, and the Basmalah is a separator between Surahs, not a verse of Al-Fatihah itself.
  * Introduce *Sujud al-Sahw* (سُجُود السَّهْو) simply. The distinction between *Qabli* (before Taslim for omission) and *Ba'di* (after Taslim for addition) is a beautiful, logical system unique to the Maliki school.

## Slide 21: Summary & Reflection
* **Visual Guide / Layout:** Summary list on the left, a thought-bubble illustration with the word "Reflection" on the right.
* **Slide Content:**
  * **What we have learned today:**
    * Allah is Unique and not like His creation (*Mukhalafah*).
    * The Six Pillars of Iman anchor our hearts.
    * Angels surround us, recording deeds and protecting us.
    * Qadar is Allah's decree, but we are responsible for our choices (*Kasb*).
    * Scholar differences, like *Sadl* in Maliki fiqh, are a mercy.
  * **Reflection Question:**
    * *How does knowing that Allah creates our physical abilities, but we choose how to use them (Kasb), change the way you think about your daily choices?*
* **Teacher Notes:**
  * Conclude the lesson by asking the students to sit quietly for two minutes to think about the reflection question.
  * Invite two or three students to share their answers. Emphasize that every choice we make (e.g., helping a friend, studying, speaking kindly) is a physical movement created by Allah, but our soul is what decided to do it, and that is what Allah rewards.
