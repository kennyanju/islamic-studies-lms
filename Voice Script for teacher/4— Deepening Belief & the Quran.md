## Slide 2: Unit 1: Tawheed in Daily Life — The Role of Intention (Niyyah)
### Slide Content
* **The Foundation of Action:**
  * Arabic Term: *Niyyah* (نِيّة) - Intention or purpose.
  * Hadith: *"Actions are only by intentions, and every person will have only what they intended..."* (Bukhari & Muslim).
* **Why does intention matter?**
  * It transforms ordinary habits (*‘ādah*) into acts of worship (*‘ibādah*).
  * Example: Eating to gain strength to worship Allah vs. eating purely for sensory pleasure.
* **The Seat of Intention:**
  * The heart (*al-qalb*). Stating the intention out loud is recommended in some circumstances to aid the heart, but the true locus is internal.

### Teacher Notes
* **Aims:** Help students understand that Islam is not just about external movements, but internal alignment.
* **Discussion Prompt:** "If someone sweeps the floor to get pocket money vs. sweeping the floor to make their parents happy and please Allah, what is the difference in reward?"
* **Mālikī Fiqh Flag:** In the Mālikī school, the *niyyah* is a strict pillar (*rukn*) for the validity of purification (*ṭahārah*) and prayer (*ṣalāh*). Without a specific intention at the very beginning of the act, the worship is invalid.

## Slide 3: Sincerity (Ikhlāṣ) vs. Showing Off (Riyā’)
### Slide Content
* **Sincerity (*Ikhlāṣ* - إِخْلَاص):**
  * Purifying one's actions so they are done *only* for the sake of Allah (*Lillāh*).
* **The Threat of Ostentation (*Riyā’* - رِيَاء):**
  * Doing good deeds so people will praise you, notice you, or think you are religious.
  * Often called the "minor shirk" (*ash-shirk al-asghar*) because it associates human approval with Allah's pleasure.
* **The Qur'anic Warning:**
  * *"So woe to those who pray, [but] who are heedless of their prayer - those who make show [of their deeds]"* (Surah Al-Mā‘ūn, 107:4-6).

### Teacher Notes
* **Aims:** Cultivate self-reflection and watchfulness (*murāqabah*) over one's heart.
* **Pedagogical Tip:** Use the analogy of a fake coin—it looks shiny on the outside but is worthless inside. Similarly, an action done with *riyā’* has no weight in the sight of Allah.
* **Ash‘arī Creed Flag:** Ash‘arīs emphasize that Allah does not accept actions that have partners associated with Him. The purity of belief (*Tawḥeed*) directly requires purity of action (*ikhlāṣ*).

## Slide 4: Action & Creation — The Ash‘arī Concept of Kasb (Acquisition)
### Slide Content
* **Who Creates Our Actions?**
  * **Allah is the Creator of everything**, including our physical bodies and our choices.
  * Qur'an: *"And Allah created you and that which you do."* (Surah Aṣ-Ṣāffāt, 37:96).
* **The human role: *Kasb* (كَسْب):**
  * Transliteration: *Kasb* (Acquisition).
  * Definition: While Allah creates the power and the act, the human being *chooses* and *directs* their will (*al-irādah al-juz’iyyah*) towards the action.
* **Responsibility:**
  * Because we choose to acquire the act (good or bad), we are held accountable for it.

### Teacher Notes
* **Aims:** Introduce the middle-path theology of the Ash‘arī school regarding free will and predestination.
* **Ash‘arī Creed Flag (Critical Concept):** 
  * The Ash‘arīs navigate between two extremes: the *Qadariyyah* (who claimed humans create their own actions independently of Allah) and the *Jabariyyah* (who claimed humans have no choice at all, like feathers in the wind). 
  * Ash‘arism teaches that Allah is the sole *Khāliq* (Creator) of the action, but the human is the *Mūtasib* (Acquirer). We intend and choose; Allah creates the action in accordance with our choice. This ensures complete divine sovereignty and complete human moral responsibility.

## Slide 6: Unit 2: The Quran — Divine Speech and Revelation
### Slide Content
* **What is the Quran?**
  * The speech of Allah (*Kalāmullāh*), revealed to Prophet Muhammad ﷺ through the Angel Jibreel (Gabriel).
  * The miracle that remains unchanged and protected by Allah.
* **How it was revealed:**
  * Over a period of **23 years**.
  * Revealed in portions: some in Makkah (focusing on Creed and Tawheed) and some in Madinah (focusing on law, community, and social structures).
* **The Qur'anic Witness:**
  * *"Indeed, it is We who sent down the Quran and indeed, We will be its guardian."* (Surah Al-Ḥijr, 15:9).

### Teacher Notes
* **Aims:** Set the historical and theological foundation of the Quran.
* **Pedagogical Tip:** Contrast the Quran with other scriptures which were altered over time. Highlight the miracle of Quranic memorization (*ḥifẓ*) as a primary method of preservation.
* **Discussion:** Why do you think the Quran was revealed gradually over 23 years instead of all at once? (To strengthen the Prophet's ﷺ heart and allow the Sahabah to implement the laws step-by-step).

## Slide 7: Ash‘arī Theology: Al-Kalām al-Nafsī vs. Al-Kalām al-Lafẓī
### Slide Content
* **Divine Attribute of Speech (*Kalām*):**
  * In Ash‘arī theology, Speech is an eternal, uncreated attribute of Allah.
* **The Distinction:**
  1. ***Al-Kalām al-Nafsī* (الكَلَام النَّفْسِي):**
     * The internal, eternal Speech of Allah. It has no beginning, no end, and does not consist of letters, sounds, or languages (like Arabic or Hebrew).
  2. ***Al-Kalām al-Lafẓī* (الكَلَام اللَّفْظِي):**
     * The created expression of that eternal Speech. This refers to the physical words we recite, the ink we write, and the sound waves we hear. It is in Arabic, which is created by Allah to allow humans to comprehend His eternal message.
* **Our Belief:** The Quran we hold in our hands points to and represents the uncreated Speech of Allah.

### Teacher Notes
* **Aims:** Teach correct creedal formulation regarding the nature of the Quran, preventing errors like total physical anthropomorphism or, conversely, denying the Quran is the Speech of Allah.
* **Ash‘arī Creed Flag (Critical Concept):**
  * The historical debate on whether the Quran is created or uncreated caused much confusion. The Ash‘arī scholars resolved this systematically. 
  * The *Attribute* of Speech itself (*al-Kalām al-Nafsī*) is eternal and uncreated, just like Allah's Knowledge and Power. 
  * The *physical recitation*, the paper, and the ink (*al-Kalām al-Lafẓī*) are created by Allah. However, we do not call the Quran "created" without qualification, because the message and the meaning it carries is the eternal Speech of Allah.

## Slide 8: Period of Revelation — Oral Transmission & Scribes
### Slide Content
* **The Primary Method: Oral Preservation**
  * The Prophet ﷺ memorized the Quran instantly when Jibreel recited it.
  * He ﷺ would immediately recite it to the Sahabah (Companions), who would memorize it.
  * The Quran was preserved primarily in the chests of men (*ṣudūr al-rijāl*).
* **The Secondary Method: Written Fragments**
  * The Prophet ﷺ appointed specific scribes of revelation (*Kuttāb al-Waḥy*), such as Zayd ibn Thābit.
  * They wrote the verses on available materials: parchment (*raq*), palm leaves (*‘usub*), shoulder bones of camels (*aktāf*), and flat stones (*likhāf*).
  * This occurred in the direct presence of the Prophet ﷺ to ensure absolute accuracy.

### Teacher Notes
* **Aims:** Detail the dual-track preservation system (memorization + writing) during the life of the Prophet ﷺ.
* **Discussion:** Why was oral preservation more reliable than written fragments in ancient Arabia? (Paper was scarce, but the Arabs had highly developed, legendary memories for poetry and genealogies).
* **Historical context:** Mention that the text was written in the early Arabic script, without dots (*naqṭ*) or vowel marks (*tashkeel*), which was read according to the oral tradition.

## Slide 9: Compilation of the Mushaf: Abu Bakr & Uthmanic Collections
### Slide Content
* **First Phase: Abu Bakr's Caliphate (11-13 AH)**
  * Trigger: The Battle of Yamamah, where many memorizers (*huffāẓ*) of the Quran were martyred.
  * Action: Caliph Abu Bakr (advised by Umar) commissioned Zayd ibn Thābit to collect all written fragments and compile them into one master manuscript (*Ṣuḥuf*).
  * Criterion: Every written piece had to be verified by two witnesses who heard it directly from the Prophet ﷺ.
* **Second Phase: Uthman's Caliphate (25-35 AH)**
  * Trigger: Expansion of the empire led to regional differences in pronunciation and dialects.
  * Action: Caliph Uthman ordered the master manuscript to be copied into standard codices (*Maṣāḥif*, sing. *Muṣḥaf*) written in the Qurayshi dialect.
  * Standardization: The *Rasm al-‘Uthmānī* (Uthmanic orthography) became the sole authorized script. All other individual personal notebooks were destroyed to preserve unity.

### Teacher Notes
* **Aims:** Provide a clear timeline of the preservation of the written text.
* **Pedagogical Tip:** Emphasize that the Uthmanic compilation did not change the Quran; it standardized the writing to prevent arguments about dialectal differences (*Ahruf*).
* **Mālikī Flag:** The Mālikī school (and the consensus of Sunni scholars) maintains that the Uthmanic script (*Rasm ‘Uthmānī*) is sacred and must be preserved in all written copies of the Quran; we cannot update the spelling rules of the Quran to match modern Arabic grammar rules.

## Slide 10: Unit 3: Introduction to Tafseer (Quranic Exegesis)
### Slide Content
* **What is Tafseer?**
  * Arabic Term: *Tafseer* (تَفْسِير) - Explanation, interpretation, or uncovering.
  * Science: The scholarly study of the meanings, context, and rulings of Quranic verses.
* **Sources of Tafseer:**
  1. The Quran itself (explaining one verse by another).
  2. The Sunnah (the Prophet ﷺ explaining verses to his companions).
  3. The statements of the Sahabah (Companions) and Tābi‘ūn (Successors).
  4. Sound Arabic grammar and linguistic analysis.
* **Warning:** It is forbidden to interpret the Quran based on personal, uneducated whim (*tafseer bi'r-ra’y al-maḍmūm*).

### Teacher Notes
* **Aims:** Establish the intellectual authority required to explain the Word of Allah.
* **Pedagogical Tip:** Use the analogy of a specialized medical text. Just as you wouldn't let a random person interpret a brain surgery manual, we do not let untrained people interpret the text of Allah's revelation without checking the scholars.
* **Core Rule:** Tafseer requires mastery of over 15 classical disciplines, including lexicography, grammar, theology (*‘aqīdah*), jurisprudence (*fiqh*), and historical contexts of revelation (*asbāb al-nuzūl*).

## Slide 11: Surah Al-Ikhlāṣ: Text and Translation
### Slide Content
```text
بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ
1. قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ
2. اللَّهُ الصَّمَدُ
3. لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ
4. وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ
```
* **Translation:**
  1. Say, "He is Allah, [who is] One.
  2. Allah, the Self-Sufficient Besought of all.
  3. He neither begets nor is born,
  4. Nor is there to Him any equivalent."
* **Context of Revelation (*Asbāb al-Nuzūl*):**
  * The polytheists of Makkah came to the Prophet ﷺ and asked: "Describe the lineage of your Lord." Allah revealed this Surah in response.

### Teacher Notes
* **Aims:** Introduce the text, pronunciation, and translation of Surah Al-Ikhlāṣ.
* **Pedagogical Tip:** Recite the Surah with the students, paying close attention to Tajweed rules (e.g., the Qalqalah at the end of *aḥad*, *ṣamad*, *yulad*).
* **Theological Import:** Highlight that this short Surah represents one-third of the Quran (*thuluth al-Quran*) because the Quran deals with three primary themes: Creed (*‘Aqīdah*), Law (*Fiqh*), and Stories/Exhortations. This Surah fully covers the first theme.

## Slide 12: Surah Al-Ikhlāṣ: Ash‘arī Creed & Tanzīh (Transcendence)
### Slide Content
* **Core Theology: *Tanzīh* (تَنْزِيه):**
  * Definition: Declaring Allah free from any resemblance to His creation (transcendence).
  * Ash‘arī Principle: Allah is not a body, does not occupy space, is not bound by time, and has no physical parts.
* **Analyzing Verse 1: *Aḥad* (أَحَد):**
  * Allah is absolute Unity. He is not composed of parts, and He has no partners or equals.
* **Analyzing Verses 3 & 4: Rejecting Likeness:**
  * *"He neither begets nor is born"* directly refutes polytheism and any idea that Allah has a physical lineage or physical offspring.
  * *"Nor is there to Him any equivalent"* aligns with the core verse: *"There is nothing like unto Him"* (Surah Ash-Shūrā, 42:11).

### Teacher Notes
* **Aims:** Connect Surah Al-Ikhlāṣ to the foundational concepts of Ash‘arī creed.
* **Ash‘arī Creed Flag (Critical Concept):**
  * The Ash‘arī school strongly emphasizes *tanzīh* (absolving Allah of human-like qualities). 
  * Any verse or Hadith that seems to imply physical body parts (like hand, face, or sitting) must not be understood literally (*ḥaqīqī*). They are understood either:
    * Through *Tafwīḍ*: Accepting the words but consigning their true meaning and modality (*kayf*) to Allah, while firmly denying physical resemblance.
    * Through *Ta’wīl*: Explaining the terms linguistically (e.g., "Hand" meaning power or favor, "Face" meaning pleasure or existence) according to the usage of classical Arabic.

## Slide 13: Understanding Allah's Name: Aṣ-Ṣamad (الصَّمَد)
### Slide Content
* **What does *Aṣ-Ṣamad* mean?**
  * One of Allah's Beautiful Names, appearing only once in the Quran.
* **Classical Explanations:**
  * **The Master of All:** The Supreme Leader who possesses perfect mastery, to whom all of creation turns for their needs.
  * **The Self-Sufficient:** He who does not need anyone or anything, but everyone and everything needs Him.
  * **The One without Parts:** Linguistically, it refers to that which is solid, having no cavities, showing that Allah does not eat, drink, or have internal organs.
* **Creed Connection:**
  * Since Allah is *Aṣ-Ṣamad*, He is free from decay, change, or need.

### Teacher Notes
* **Aims:** Unpack this unique, profound Name of Allah.
* **Discussion Prompt:** "If a person is hungry, they need food. If they are tired, they need sleep. What does it mean for Allah to be completely free of all needs (*Ghanī*) while we are completely dependent (*Faqīr*)?"
* **Sources:** Refer to classical Tafseer works (e.g., Tafseer al-Qurṭubī - a major Mālikī/Ash‘arī source) where *Aṣ-Ṣamad* is explained as the Lord whose sovereignty is absolute and complete.

## Slide 14: Surah Al-‘Aṣr: Text and Translation
### Slide Content
```text
بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ
1. وَالْعَصْرِ
2. إِنَّ الْإِنسَانَ لَفِي خُسْرٍ
3. إِلَّا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالْحَقِّ وَتَوَاصَوْا بِالصَّبْرِ
```
* **Translation:**
  1. By time,
  2. Indeed, mankind is in loss,
  3. Except for those who have believed and done righteous deeds and advised each other to truth and advised each other to patience.
* **Imam al-Shāfi‘ī’s Quote:**
  * *"If Allah had revealed only this Surah as a proof to His creation, it would have been enough for them."*

### Teacher Notes
* **Aims:** Introduce the text, pronunciation, and translation of Surah Al-‘Aṣr.
* **Key Vocabulary:**
  * *Al-‘Aṣr* (العَصْر): Time, or specifically the late afternoon period.
  * *Khusr* (خُسْر): Absolute loss or ruin.
  * *Tawāṣaw* (تَوَاصَوْا): Mutually advising/exhorting one another.

## Slide 15: Surah Al-‘Aṣr: The Formula for Escaping Loss
### Slide Content
* **The Divine Oath:**
  * Allah swears by *Time* (*Al-‘Aṣr*) to show its immense value. Time is the capital asset of human life; once spent, it can never be recovered.
* **The Four Conditions of Success:**
  1. **Faith (*Īmān*):** Correct belief in Allah and His Messenger.
  2. **Righteous Actions (*‘Amal Ṣāliḥ*):** Doing good deeds sincerely (*ikhlas*) according to the law (*Shari‘ah*).
  3. **Advising to Truth (*Tawāṣī bi’l-Ḥaqq*):** Supporting truth, justice, and correct knowledge in the family and community.
  4. **Advising to Patience (*Tawāṣī bi’ṣ-Ṣabr*):** Encouraging one another to remain firm during trials, constant in worship, and away from sins.

### Teacher Notes
* **Aims:** Practical moral and ethical application of the Surah.
* **Pedagogical Tip:** Create a link back to Unit 1 (Intention). Doing righteous actions (*‘Amal Ṣāliḥ*) requires *niyyah* to be valid. 
* **Ash‘arī Creed Note:** *Īmān* (faith) is the root, and actions (*‘amal*) are its branches. While actions are necessary for the completeness and health of faith, a person who fails in actions but has correct faith is still within the fold of Islam, though sinful. This is a key counterpoint to extremist views (like the Kharijites) who claimed that committing major sins nullifies faith completely.

## Slide 16: Unit 4: Hadith and Sunnah — Definitions and Collection
### Slide Content
* **Definitions:**
  * **Sunnah (سُنَّة):** The lived practice, character, rulings, and path of the Prophet ﷺ.
  * **Hadith (حَدِيث):** The written or oral report detailing what the Prophet ﷺ said, did, approved of, or looked like.
* **Authority of the Sunnah:**
  * The Quran commands us to follow the Sunnah.
  * *"He who obeys the Messenger has obeyed Allah..."* (Surah An-Nisā’, 4:80).
  * We cannot understand how to pray, pay Zakah, or perform Hajj without the Sunnah.

### Teacher Notes
* **Aims:** Clarify the vocabulary and explain the absolute authority of the prophetic model.
* **Pedagogical Tip:** Ask students: "Does the Quran specify exactly how many units (*rak‘ahs*) to pray for Dhuhr?" (No, the Quran commands prayer, but the Prophet ﷺ taught the details: "Pray as you have seen me pray").
* **Key Distinction:** A Hadith is a *report* of the Sunnah, but the Sunnah itself is the broader *normative practice*.

## Slide 17: How Hadith Were Collected: Isnad and Matn
### Slide Content
* **The Anatomy of a Hadith:**
  1. ***Isnād* (إِسْنَاد):** The chain of narrators leading back to the Prophet ﷺ (e.g., A heard from B, who heard from C, who heard from the Prophet ﷺ).
  2. ***Matn* (مَتْن):** The actual text or speech of the report.
* **The Evaluation Criteria:**
  * Hadith scholars developed the science of biography (*‘Ilm al-Rijāl*) to check:
    * Is the narrator trustworthy (*‘Adl*)?
    * Do they have a strong memory (*Ḍābiṭ*)?
    * Did the narrators actually meet each other (connected chain - *Muttaṣil*)?
    * Are there hidden defects (*‘Ilal*) or contradictions?
* **Classifications:**
  * *Ṣaḥīḥ* (Authentic), *Ḥasan* (Good), *Ḍa‘īf* (Weak), and *Mawḍū‘* (Fabricated).

### Teacher Notes
* **Aims:** Demystify how Hadith collections were compiled with scientific rigor.
* **Pedagogical Tip:** Use the "telephone game" analogy, but show how Hadith scholars prevented corruption by demanding a complete background check on every single person in the chain.
* **Mālikī Fiqh Flag:** While the authenticity of a chain (*isnād*) is highly important, the Mālikī school has additional criteria for acting upon a Hadith. If a single narration (*khabar al-wāḥid*) contradicts the established, continuous practice of Madinah (*‘Amal Ahl al-Madīnah*), the practice of Madinah is preferred (discussed in Slide 20).

## Slide 18: Unit 5: Legal Frameworks — Why Muslims Follow a Madhhab
### Slide Content
* **What is a *Madhhab* (مَذْهَب)?**
  * Plural: *Madhāhib* (مَذَاهِب).
  * Definition: A school of Islamic law named after a master jurist (*Mujtahid*).
* **The Four Canonical Sunni Madhhabs:**
  1. **Hanafi** (founded by Imam Abu Hanifah, d. 150 AH)
  2. **Maliki** (founded by Imam Malik, d. 179 AH)
  3. **Shafi'i** (founded by Imam al-Shafi'i, d. 204 AH)
  4. **Hanbali** (founded by Imam Ahmad ibn Hanbal, d. 241 AH)
* **Why follow one?**
  * The Quran commands: *"So ask the people of the message if you do not know."* (Surah An-Naḥl, 16:43).
  * A Madhhab is a structured, tested methodology of extracting rulings from the Quran and Sunnah, protecting us from misinterpreting texts on our own.

### Teacher Notes
* **Aims:** Explain the concept of legal schools and normalize diversity within Sunni Islam.
* **Pedagogical Tip:** Compare a Madhhab to a medical specialty or a legal jurisdiction. You don't perform surgery by self-diagnosing via textbooks; you follow established medical protocols. Similarly, we follow established legal protocols in Fiqh.
* **Diversity Concept:** The differences between Madhhabs are in secondary legal details (*furū‘*), not core theological beliefs (*uṣūl*). All four schools are correct paths to the truth.

## Slide 19: Imam Malik ibn Anas: The Scholar of Madinah
### Slide Content
* **Who was Imam Malik?**
  * Born: 93 AH in Madinah. Died: 179 AH in Madinah.
  * Title: *Imām Dār al-Hijrah* (The Leader of the Abode of Migration).
  * Respect for the Prophet ﷺ: He would never ride a horse or camel in Madinah out of respect for the ground in which the Prophet ﷺ is buried, and he would always perform Wudu and wear his best clothes before teaching Hadith.
* **His Major Work:**
  * ***Al-Muwaṭṭa’* (المُوَطَّأ):** Meaning "The Approved/Smoothed Path." It is the earliest surviving written collection of Hadith and legal rulings, compiled over 40 years.

### Teacher Notes
* **Aims:** Profile the founding father of the Mālikī school.
* **Pedagogical Tip:** Highlight Imam Malik’s absolute love and reverence for the Prophet ﷺ and the city of Madinah. 
* **Historical quote:** Imam al-Shafi'i said of him: "When the scholars are mentioned, Malik is the shining star."

## Slide 20: Imam Malik's Methodology: ‘Amal Ahl al-Madīnah
### Slide Content
* **The Concept:** *‘Amal Ahl al-Madīnah* (عَمَل أَهْل المَدِينَة)
  * Translates to: **The practice of the people of Madinah**.
* **Why Imam Malik relied on this:**
  * Madinah was where the Prophet ﷺ lived for the last 10 years of his life, established the Shari'ah, and died.
  * Thousands of Sahabah lived there, and they passed their daily practices down to their children (the Tābi‘ūn).
  * Imam Malik argued: The continuous, daily practice of thousands of people in Madinah is a **mass-transmitted (mutawātir) physical Sunnah**.
* **Formula:**
  * Malik famously stated: *"A mass-transmission from a thousand people to a thousand people is stronger than a single person narrating from a single person (Hadith Āḥād)."*

### Teacher Notes
* **Aims:** Introduce the signature epistemological methodology of the Mālikī school.
* **Mālikī Fiqh Flag (Critical Concept):**
  * This slide explains why the Mālikī school sometimes rules differently from what is written in a specific individual Hadith. 
  * If a single Hadith narrates that the Prophet ﷺ did X, but Imam Malik observed that *none* of the scholars or general population of Madinah practiced X, he concluded that the Hadith was either abrogated (*mansūkh*), misunderstood, or rare, and that the continuous public practice (*‘Amal*) represented the final, actual Sunnah of the Prophet ﷺ.
  * Example: Praying with arms down at the sides (*sadl*). Imam Malik observed the people of Madinah praying like this, indicating it was the continuous practice passed down from the Sahabah, despite single hadiths describing clasping the hands (*qabḍ*).

  * Differentiate the uncreated speech of Allah from physical letters.
  * Understand the rigorous science of Hadith.
